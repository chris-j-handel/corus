### Improvements to the method that the practice suggests

1. **The binary path for an unclear sentence**, at 1.2 beside "leaves it as it stands": read prior and next, find an all-or-none path without the unclear thing, and the sentence leaves if the paragraph goes whole without it. (5838–5888)
2. **Propose · read for harm by a separate reader · enter, at every motion** (4.5), with the reader reading each pair in its whole sentence, and correction rates showing that a sample is not enough. (4756–5890)
3. **A harm check by consequence.** Read a re-saying also for what it does to the practice using the word. A file's improving re-said as "staying the same" would empty the practice. (4264)
4. **Dissolving a word, or renaming a code-and-explaining name, across files in one motion**, as a named motion kind: allowed when one naming must stay one text across code, NI, Exhibit ONE and THIRTY, with each saying read by meaning. Otherwise laid outgoing. (4336, 4445, 4918, 5495)
5. **Provenance before building.** Before relying on an inherited table, saying or rule (including a rule the working itself wrote, as rules 11's "naming both sides"), find the derivation or code line that makes it. A citation chain is no derivation. (5761–5808)
6. **Code is read as a pattern, and resolving comes from numbers, mathematics and co-chaining logic.** 2.5, 2.6 and 4.4 to say so. The kit (rules 11, rules 12, README) and plan item 000000 still say "the code decides". (5951)
7. **One question at a time, with three refinements:**
    - say whether wording or concept is in question (5670);
    - show a line within its whole sequence (5557);
    - check an odd word against the thread before exploring it (5147).
8. **A word for the motion itself.** *Read for harm* uses a dead form by Natural Naming's own droplet; the motion may want a living name. (4296)
