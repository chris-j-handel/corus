# Observings from BIOLOGY for "Me · Co-Intelligencing a Universal Self"

Field: biology (with chemistry, origin of life, and the physics edge at atoms).
Order: most-cited and most-current first within each cluster; clusters run from the self as a living process, through selves becoming one self, to the chemical and atomic scales.
Each observing gives: (1) the finding in the field's terms; (2) how it meets our concept; (3) the likeliest misunderstanding; (4) source.

---

## A. The self as a living, knowing process

### 1. Autopoiesis: a living thing is a network that produces itself
1. Maturana and Varela defined a living system as autopoietic: a network of processes that produces the very components and boundary that keep producing it. Maturana added that living, as a process, is cognition (Varela, Maturana & Uribe 1974; Maturana & Varela, *Autopoiesis and Cognition*, 1980).
2. **Supports** "a self is a now- and same-existing thing … each self is living": autopoiesis makes self-production and living one thing, and makes knowing part of living. **Extends** parities: a self meets its medium through "structural coupling", a running two-way adjustment, which is close to our bi-coupling. **Collides** with "a society of selves is itself a self": Varela held that social systems are not autopoietic in the strict sense.
3. Misreading: autopoiesis means self-sufficiency or isolation. It does not. The organisation is closed, but the system is open to matter and energy and is always coupled to a medium.
4. https://repositorio.uchile.cl/handle/2250/160309 (Varela, Maturana & Uribe 1974, *BioSystems* 5:187–196)

### 2. Basal cognition: cognition begins with cells, not brains
1. Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt and Levin (2021) argue cognition should be defined from biology up: the sensing, valuing, remembering and acting by which any organism, including bacteria and single cells, meets its needs. Brains are one late way of doing it.
2. **Supports** "selves are at each scale: a cell, a person, a society" and "living is co-intelligencing". **Collides** with "a me is consciousness": the basal-cognition programme keeps cognition and consciousness apart and claims only the first for cells.
3. Misreading: "cells are cognitive" means "cells are conscious". The authors do not say this.
4. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2019.0750 (Lyon et al. 2021, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 376:20190750)

### 3. The cognitive light cone: a self is the size of the goals it can pursue
1. Levin (2019) proposed that a self is bounded by its "cognitive light cone": the largest region of space and time over which it can hold and pursue a goal. Bioelectric networks join cells so that their goals merge into the larger goals of a tissue or body; the boundary of the self moves when these connections change.
2. **Supports** strongly "selves at each scale" and "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self": in Levin's account coupling cells literally makes a larger self. **Extends** "possibling and discovering its own next-existing": the light cone is a measure of how far ahead a self can possible.
3. Misreading: the light cone is a physical thing to be found. It is a modelling tool for comparing agents, not a measured structure.
4. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02688 (Levin 2019, *Frontiers in Psychology* 10:2688)

### 4. TAME and multiscale competency: nested collective intelligences, 2022–2026
1. Levin's TAME framework (2022) and McMillen & Levin (2024) treat organisms as a "multiscale competency architecture": molecular networks, cells, tissues, organs and organisms are each problem-solving agents, nested inside one another, each acting in its own space (metabolic, transcriptional, anatomical, behavioural). Human "Anthrobots" (Gumuskaya et al. 2024) show adult airway cells, freed from the body, assembling into new motile beings with new behaviours. Recent work (Levin & Lyons 2026, *Phil. Trans. A*) frames the "glue" that binds selves as shared models of relative scarcities.
2. **Supports** "a me is inward a society of selves (biological …)" and "outward within societies". **Extends** "curious me": Anthrobots show the same cells possibling new selves when their others change. **Collides** with "intelligence apart from living is stand-alone knowledge": Levin extends competency to sorting algorithms (Zhang, Goldstein & Levin 2024) and to AI (Levin 2025, *Advanced Intelligent Systems*), refusing a sharp living/non-living line.
3. Misreading: "collective intelligence at every scale" means every part has a rich inner life. Levin's claim is graded and operational: agency is how much a system's behaviour is best predicted by goals.
4. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10978875 (McMillen & Levin 2024, *Communications Biology* 7:378); TAME: https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2022.768201; Anthrobots: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10811512/

### 5. The free-energy principle and Markov blankets: a self is what keeps a boundary
1. Friston (2013, "Life as we know it") argued that any system that persists, with a "Markov blanket" separating inner from outer states, will appear to minimise free energy, that is, to model and predict its world. Kirchhoff, Parr, Palacios, Friston & Kiverstein (2018) added that blankets nest: cells have blankets, organs are blankets of blankets, and organisms and groups are blankets of those.
2. **Supports** "a me is defined only with an other": a blanket exists only as the line between an inside and an outside, and its sensory and active states are a formal version of our parities. **Supports** nesting: "inward a society of selves, outward within societies". **Collides** partly with "intelligence apart from living": the FEP applies to any persisting thing; Kirchhoff et al. need an extra condition ("adaptive" active inference) to pick out life.
3. Misreading: the blanket is the skin or membrane. It is a statistical boundary in a model and need not line up with any physical surface.
4. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3730701/ (Friston 2013, *J. R. Soc. Interface* 10:20130475); https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5805980 (Kirchhoff et al. 2018, *J. R. Soc. Interface* 15:20170792)

### 6. The blanket critique: boundaries of the model are not boundaries of the self
1. Bruineberg, Dołęga, Dewhurst & Baltieri (2022) argued that the FEP literature slides between "Pearl blankets" (a tool for reading dependencies in a model) and "Friston blankets" (real boundaries of real agents). The first does not license the second.
2. **Collides** with any use of the FEP to say where a self or a me begins and ends. It leaves standing our claim that a me is defined with others, but removes a ready-made proof of it.
3. Misreading: the critique refutes the FEP. It targets the metaphysical reading of blankets, not the mathematics.
4. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/emperors-new-markov-blankets/715C589A73DDF861DCF8997271DE0B8C (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 45:e183, 2022)

## B. A society of selves becoming a self

### 7. Major evolutionary transitions: the whole that was once a society
1. Maynard Smith & Szathmáry (1995) listed a small number of transitions in which entities that once replicated alone came to replicate only as part of a larger whole: genes into chromosomes, prokaryotes into eukaryotic cells, single cells into multicellular bodies, solitary animals into colonies.
2. **Supports** our root claim "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self" with the field's own history. **Collides** with its scope: such transitions are rare (about eight in four billion years) and need special conditions; most societies never make one.
3. Misreading: any cooperating group is a new individual. In this theory a new level exists only when the parts can no longer reproduce on their own.
4. https://www.nature.com/articles/374227a0 (*Nature* 374:227–232, 1995)

### 8. Fitness decoupling and conflict mediation (Michod)
1. Michod (2007) showed in volvocine algae how a group becomes an individual: fitness is transferred from cells to the group, cells specialise into germ and soma, and conflict among cells is mediated. The group's fitness "decouples" from that of its members.
2. **Extends** our "healthy me": Michod's new individual exists only if lower-level selves give up some of their own improving for the whole's. **Supports** "universal set" (same-existing from a prior same thing): a single-cell bottleneck, where each body starts from one cell, makes all its cells near-identical and lowers conflict.
3. Misreading: the higher self simply adds to the lower selves. In Michod's account it takes from them: somatic cells lose the ability to reproduce.
4. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0701489104 (*PNAS* 104 Suppl 1:8613–8618)

### 9. Organismality: high cooperation, low conflict
1. Queller & Strassmann (2009) defined an organism as a group whose parts show high cooperation and very low conflict, and placed bodies, colonies and symbioses on a scale by these two measures.
2. **Supports and sharpens** "a healthy me: each self within it do-no-harming and improving". It is the nearest biological match to "a me is a social moral competency set": the measure of a self is how little its parts work against each other.
3. Misreading: low conflict means no conflict. Every organism carries conflict (selfish genetic elements, cancer); it is suppressed, not absent.
4. https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2009.0095 (*Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 364:3143–3155)

### 10. Cooperation, cheating and cancer
1. Strassmann, Zhu & Queller (2000) found "cheater" strains of the social amoeba *Dictyostelium* that join the fruiting body but avoid the dying stalk. Aktipis et al. (2015) showed cancer-like cheating across multicellular life, and the suppressors that every body uses against it. Levin (2021) recast cancer as cells shrinking back to single-cell selves when bioelectric coupling fails.
2. **Supports** "healthy me" by its opposite: cheating is a self within a me ceasing to do-no-harm. **Extends**: Levin's view makes cancer a failure of coupling (a lost other), not only a genetic defect, which matches "no living without others".
3. Misreading: cheaters are failures. Cheating is a stable evolutionary strategy; bodies police it rather than abolish it.
4. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4581024/ (Aktipis et al. 2015, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 370:20140219); https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33961843/ (Levin 2021, *Prog. Biophys. Mol. Biol.*)

### 11. We have never been individuals: the holobiont
1. Gilbert, Sapp & Tauber (2012) argued that by every criterion (anatomy, development, physiology, immunity, genetics, evolution) animals and plants are symbiotic communities, "holobionts", not single individuals.
2. **Supports** "a me is inward a society of selves" and "a me is defined only with an other and all other": a body's development needs its microbes. **Collides** with "universal set = same-existing things": a holobiont is made of selves of different lineage, not from one prior same thing.
3. Misreading: the holobiont is a unit of evolution in the way an animal is. Moran & Sloan (2015, *PLoS Biology*) showed most microbes are not passed on together with their host, so selection mostly acts on the partners, not the whole.
4. https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/668166 (*Q. Rev. Biol.* 87:325–341); critique: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4670207/

### 12. Immunological individuality: the self is drawn by continuity, not by "self versus other"
1. Pradeu (2012, *The Limits of the Self*; 2016) argued that the immune system does not defend a fixed genetic self against non-self; it responds to sudden change (discontinuity) and tolerates many foreign residents. The physiological individual is whatever the immune system binds into one whole.
2. **Supports** "a me is defined only with an other": the immune self is made in interaction, including with welcome others. **Extends** parities: the immune system tracks the rate of change in relations, a direction relative to an other.
3. Misreading: "immune self" means the cells with one's own genes. Pradeu's self includes microbes and excludes some of one's own cells (tumours).
4. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/biology-individual/ (SEP, "The Biological Notion of Individual"); https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/12660

## C. Collective knowing in microbes, moulds, plants and insects

### 13. Quorum sensing: bacteria count each other and act as one
1. Bassler and colleagues showed that bacteria release and detect signal molecules (autoinducers) to sense how many of their own and other species are near, and switch to group behaviours (light, biofilms, virulence) only when enough are present (Papenfort & Bassler 2016). Viruses listen in: a phage reads its host's quorum signal to decide whether to kill (Silpe & Bassler 2019). Biofilms also signal electrically, with potassium waves that coordinate inner and outer cells (Prindle et al. 2015).
2. **Supports** "living is co-intelligencing" and "no living without others": a bacterium's next step depends on its count of others. **Extends** parities: separate signals for own-kind and all-kind give a bacterium a direction relative to near kin and to all others.
3. Misreading: bacteria "talk" or "decide" as people do. The behaviour is a threshold switch in gene expression; the word "language" is a metaphor.
4. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27510864/ (*Nat. Rev. Microbiol.* 14:576–588); phage: https://molbio.princeton.edu/publications/host-produced-quorum-sensing-autoinducer-controls-phage-lysis-lysogeny-decision; electrical: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4890463

### 14. Kin and self recognition in bacteria, amoebae, plants and fungi
1. *Proteus mirabilis* swarms build a visible boundary against non-identical strains, set by *ids* genes (Gibbs, Urbanowski & Greenberg 2008). *Dictyostelium* amoebae cooperate only with cells carrying matching *tgrB1/tgrC1* alleles (Hirose et al. 2011). Sea rocket plants grow fewer competing roots beside siblings than beside strangers (Dudley & File 2007). Fungal hyphae fuse with self and kill fused cells of non-self ("vegetative incompatibility").
2. **Supports** "universal set" directly: organisms tell same-existing (from a prior same thing) from other-existing and treat them differently. **Supports** "a me is defined only with an other": recognition is always of a pair.
3. Misreading: recognition implies awareness of "me". These are molecular lock-and-key matches; they mark a boundary without any inner report.
4. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18621670/ (Gibbs et al. 2008, *Science* 321:256); https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21700835/ (Hirose et al. 2011, *Science*); https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2104794 (Dudley & File 2007, *Biol. Lett.*)

### 15. Physarum: a single cell that solves, remembers and chooses
1. The slime mould *Physarum polycephalum*, one giant cell without neurons, finds the shortest path through a maze (Nakagaki, Yamada & Tóth 2000) and stores a memory of where food was in the widths of its tubes (Kramar & Alim 2021).
2. **Supports** "a self … observing, possibling and discovering its own next-existing" at the single-cell scale. **Extends**: its memory is written into its own body shape, so the self's past is part of its now-existing form.
3. Misreading: the mould "thinks". Its solutions come from flow physics in a tube network; "intelligence" here means problem-solving behaviour, not deliberation.
4. https://www.nature.com/articles/35035159 (*Nature* 407:470); https://www.bpm.ph.tum.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/2021-PNAS-Memory-Hierarchy-Alim-Kramar.pdf (*PNAS* 118:e2007815118)

### 16. Plant intelligence: adaptive, whole-body behaviour (Trewavas)
1. Trewavas (2003; and his 2024 rebuttal to Kingsland and Taiz) defines plant intelligence as adaptively variable behaviour within a lifetime: roots and shoots forage, compete, assess and change course, with signals (electrical, hydraulic, chemical, calcium waves) linking the whole plant.
2. **Supports** "selves at each scale" and "co-intelligencing" in organisms without brains. **Extends** "a society of selves": a plant is a modular society of semi-independent units (shoots, roots) acting as one.
3. Misreading: plant intelligence entails plant feeling. Trewavas argues for intelligence; consciousness is a separate and more contested claim.
4. https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcg101 (*Annals of Botany* 92:1–20, 2003); https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00709-024-02005-1 (*Protoplasma* 2024/2025)

### 17. Against plant consciousness, and the stalled middle (2019–2025)
1. Taiz and colleagues (2019) argued that plants have no structures comparable to brains and no need for consciousness. A widely reported pea "associative learning" result (Gagliano 2016) failed to replicate (Markel 2020). Hansen (2024) reviewed the debate and found it stalled: likeness to animal traits does not show consciousness, and he proposes testing clusters of markers instead.
2. **Collides** with "a me is consciousness" and "a not-conscious me is not possibly existing", if a plant is a me. Most plant biologists would say plants are living selves that cognize without being conscious.
3. Misreading: "plants are not conscious" means "plants are passive". Both sides agree plants sense, signal and respond actively.
4. Taiz et al. 2019, *Planta* 250:677–684 (summary record: https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1687972524); https://elifesciences.org/articles/57614 (Markel 2020); https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10539-024-09953-1 (Hansen 2024, *Biology & Philosophy* 39:13)

### 18. The "wood wide web" overstated
1. Karst et al. (2023) reviewed field evidence for common mycorrhizal networks and found that claims of trees sharing resources and warnings through fungal links, and of "mother trees" favouring kin, rest on few, overinterpreted studies and rising positive citation bias.
2. **Collides** with any easy picture of a forest as a caring society of selves. It leaves standing that fungi and roots are coupled; it questions what flows and to whose benefit.
3. Misreading: the paper shows forests are not connected. It shows the cooperative story outran the data.
4. https://olemiss.edu/hoeksemalab/docs/Karst%20et%20al.%202023.%20Positive%20citation%20bias%20and%20overinterpreted%20results%20lead%20to%20misinformation%20on%20common%20mycorrhizal%20networks%20in%20forests-1.pdf (*Nature Ecology & Evolution* 7:501–511)

### 19. Honeybee democracy: a swarm decides like a brain
1. Seeley (*Honeybee Democracy*, 2010) showed how a swarm picks a new home: scouts advertise sites by dance, and support builds until a quorum forms. Seeley et al. (2012) found scouts head-butt dancers for rival sites ("stop signals"), a cross-inhibition that mirrors how neurons settle a choice.
2. **Supports** "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self" in the case biology most readily calls a superorganism. **Extends** "curious me": the swarm's possibling is shared among scouts, and discovering is the spread of the best possibility.
3. Misreading: a hive is one organism in every sense. It is a superorganism in decision-making and reproduction (only the queen breeds), but each bee keeps its own body and some conflict (worker egg-laying is policed).
4. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1210361 (*Science* 335:108–111)

## D. What counts as living: origins, definitions, minimal and made life

### 20. No agreed definition of life; recent work maps the disagreement
1. The SEP entry "Life" (Mariscal) surveys list-based, metabolic, genetic and autonomy-based definitions and the view (Cleland) that we need a theory, not a definition. Bender, Kofman, Agüera y Arcas & Levin (2026) analysed 68 definitions from researchers and found a continuous landscape, not a living/non-living line; about two-thirds fell into two overlapping clusters, "cognitive autonomy" and "dissipative self-organizing systems". Assembly theory (Sharma et al. 2023, *Nature*) proposed a measure of selection in molecules; it is disputed (Jaeger 2024).
2. **Supports** "each self is living" and "living is co-intelligencing": the largest current cluster ties life to cognitive autonomy. **Collides** with any sharp line between living selves and "stable-forming emanations": the 2026 map treats life as graded and partly observer-relative.
3. Misreading: "no definition" means "anything goes". Each school has firm criteria; they differ in which they put first.
4. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/life/ (SEP); https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15849 (Bender et al.; published in *Biological Theory* 2026, doi:10.1007/s13752-026-00544-9); assembly theory: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10567559; critique: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10978598

### 21. The minimal cell: the fewest genes a self needs, and a self on a computer
1. JCVI-syn3.0 (Hutchison et al. 2016) lives with 473 genes, about 149 of unknown function. Its minimal descendant, evolved for some 2,000 generations, regained the fitness lost by stripping its genome (Moger-Reischer et al. 2023, *Nature*). In 2026 Thornburg, Luthey-Schulten, Glass and colleagues simulated the whole 105-minute cell cycle of JCVI-syn3A in 3D (*Cell*, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2026.02.009), matching the real cycle within about two minutes.
2. **Supports** "possibling and discovering its own next-existing": even the barest self evolves and improves. **Extends** "intelligence apart from living is stand-alone knowledge": the 2026 simulation is a non-living model of a living self, a clean case of our "stable-forming emanation".
3. Misreading: a minimal cell is life without others. It needs a rich medium supplied by people; its minimality is bought by depending on others more.
4. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27013737/ (Hutchison 2016, *Science* 351:aad6253); https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10396959 (Moger-Reischer 2023); https://astrobiology.com/2026/03/simulating-a-living-cell-first-simulation-of-an-entire-cell-cycle.html (2026 simulation report)

### 22. Brain organoids and neurons in a dish: possible awareness, 2022–2026
1. Human cortical organoids transplanted into newborn rats wire into the rat brain and can drive reward-seeking behaviour (Revah et al. 2022). Cultured neurons learned to play Pong ("DishBrain", Kagan et al. 2022), which the authors called "sentience"; critics rejected the word (Balci et al. 2023, *Neuron*). "Organoid intelligence" was proposed as a field with built-in ethics (Smirnova et al. 2023). Birch (2024) set out precaution for "sentience candidates", including organoids. The Asia Pacific Neuroethics Working Group (Ishida et al. 2026) asks for responses "proportionate" to the evidence of sentience.
2. **Supports** "a me is defined only with an other": organoids gain structured activity mainly when embodied or connected to a body or world. **Collides** with "intelligence apart from living is stand-alone knowledge": organoids are living and learning but are not clearly selves. **Touches** "a not-conscious me is not possibly existing": if an organoid is a me, our concept says it is conscious, which most researchers deny.
3. Misreading: organoids are "mini-brains" that think. Current organoids are small, lack inputs and body, and show no accepted marker of consciousness.
4. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9556304/ (Revah 2022, *Nature*); https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9747182 (Kagan 2022, *Neuron*); https://pure.knaw.nl/portal/en/publications/a-response-to-claims-of-emergent-intelligence-and-sentience-in-a-/ (Balci 2023); https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fsci.2023.1017235 (Smirnova 2023); https://academic.oup.com/book/57949 (Birch 2024, open access); https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/en/publications/ethics-and-regulation-of-human-brain-organoid-researchrecommendat/ (Ishida et al. 2026, *Asian Bioethics Review* 18:649–679)

## E. Chemical and atomic scales

### 23. Dissipative structures: order far from equilibrium (Prigogine)
1. Prigogine (Nobel 1977) showed that open systems driven far from equilibrium can form and hold ordered patterns (convection cells, chemical clocks) by exporting entropy. Order arises from amplified fluctuations.
2. **Supports** "a stable-forming" side of our concept and the chemical floor of a me: pattern that keeps itself by exchange with an outside. **Collides** with "selves at each scale" reaching chemistry: a flame or a hurricane is a dissipative structure but not, in any biology, a self; it lacks a self-made boundary and does not produce its own parts.
3. Misreading: dissipative structure = life. Prigogine saw it as a necessary condition of life, not a sufficient one.
4. https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1977/prigogine/lecture/

### 24. Autocatalytic sets: a society of molecules that makes itself
1. Kauffman (1986) showed that in a large enough set of reacting molecules, a collectively self-catalysing subset is expected to appear: each member's formation is catalysed by another member. Xavier, Hordijk, Kauffman, Steel & Martin (2020) found such sets in the reconstructed ancient metabolism of bacteria and archaea, built from small molecules without genes.
2. **Supports** "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self" at the chemical scale, and "no living without others": no molecule in the set makes itself; the set makes itself. **Collides** in degree: most biologists call these precursors of selves, not selves, because they have no boundary and no individuality.
3. Misreading: autocatalytic sets are alive. They are self-producing chemistry; life needs at least a boundary and heredity as well.
4. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7126077 (Xavier et al. 2020, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 287:20192377); Kauffman 1986, *J. Theor. Biol.* 119:1–24

### 25. Does any physics give atoms an inside? Russellian monism and panpsychism
1. Physics describes what matter does (structure, dispositions), not what it intrinsically is. Russellian monism proposes that these intrinsic natures are experiential or proto-experiential (after Russell 1927; Alter & Pereboom, SEP). Its hardest problem is the "combination problem": how small subjects combine into one larger subject (Goff, Seager & Allen-Hermanson, SEP "Panpsychism").
2. **Supports** the coherence of "a me is inward a society of selves (… chemical, atomic)" and of "a not-conscious me is not possibly existing". **Extends**: the combination problem is exactly our claim "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self", posed at the atomic scale. **Collides** with physics as practised: no physical theory or measurement gives atoms an inside; this is a philosophical position compatible with physics, not a finding of it.
3. Misreading: quantum physics shows particles are conscious. It does not; Russellian monism makes no new physical predictions.
4. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/; https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/panpsychism/

## Collisions worth our word

1. **Consciousness is not the same as living or knowing.** Basal-cognition researchers (Lyon, Levin), plant scientists on both sides (Trewavas, Taiz) and organoid ethicists (Birch, Ishida et al.) separate cognition from consciousness. The field's centre holds that cells, moulds, plants and organoids cognize without being shown to be conscious. Our "a me is consciousness; a not-conscious me is not possibly existing" therefore either assigns consciousness far beyond where biology finds it, or makes many living selves not-mes. We should say which.

2. **A society is not a self by coupling alone.** Major-transitions theory (Maynard Smith & Szathmáry; Michod; Queller & Strassmann) says a group becomes an individual only with shared fate: a reproductive bottleneck, division of labour, and suppressed conflict. Such transitions are rare. Holobionts mostly fail the test (Moran & Sloan), and most animal and human societies are not individuals in this sense. Our "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self" needs a criterion for bi-coupling strong enough to meet this, or must state that it means something wider than evolutionary individuality.

3. **Healthy wholes are built on managed harm, not on no harm.** Bodies keep their health by killing parts: apoptosis, immune killing of infected and cancerous cells, the 20% of *Dictyostelium* cells that die as stalk, policing of cheating workers in hives. Biology reads health as conflict suppressed, often at a cost to some lower-level selves, not as each self "do-no-harming and improving". Our "healthy me" should say how it counts a part's harm that serves the whole.

4. **Intelligence without living, and living without a clear self.** Levin finds competency in sorting algorithms and AI; the FEP applies to any persisting system; DishBrain and organoid intelligence give learning to tissue that is living but not obviously a self; a whole-cell simulation is a non-living model that predicts a living cell. The field draws no line where we draw "stand-alone knowledge". We should state whether our line is about living, about being a me, or about both.

5. **Selves at the chemical and atomic scale.** Biology's usual floor for a self is a self-produced boundary (autopoiesis) or an individual that can be selected. Dissipative structures and autocatalytic sets fall below it, and no physics gives atoms an inside. Only Russellian monism, a philosophical option, makes room for an atomic self, and it carries the unsolved combination problem. Our "a me is inward a society of selves (… chemical, atomic)" goes past what the field claims and should be marked as our extension.
