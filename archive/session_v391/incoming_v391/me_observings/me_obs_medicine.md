# Observings from Medicine and Health, for "Me · Co-Intelligencing a Universal Self"

Field: medicine and health (immunology, oncology, microbiome, psychoneuroimmunology, interoception, psychiatry, public health, ageing, clinical neurology, anaesthesia, death determination).
Order: most-cited and most-current first within each cluster; clusters run from "no living without others" outward to "consciousness at the edges of the me".
Each entry: **Observing** / **Meets** / **Likely misreading** / **Source**.

## A. No living without others: social connection as a health variable

### 1. Social relationships predict survival as strongly as smoking does
- **Observing.** A meta-analysis of 148 studies (about 308,000 people) found that people with stronger social relationships had about 50% greater odds of survival over follow-up (OR 1.50). A follow-up meta-analysis of 70 studies (3.4 million people) found social isolation, loneliness and living alone raised mortality risk by about 29%, 26% and 32%. Julianne Holt-Lunstad, Timothy Smith, Bradley Layton, 2010; Holt-Lunstad et al., 2015.
- **Meets.** Supports "there is no living without others" and "a me is defined only with an other". It puts a number on it: being cut off from the outward society shortens the inward society's living.
- **Likely misreading.** That the effect is about having *many* contacts. Both objective isolation and subjective loneliness count separately, and quality matters; a me is not healthier by headcount.
- **Source.** 2010: https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316 ; 2015: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25910392/

### 2. US Surgeon General's advisory: loneliness as a public-health epidemic (2023)
- **Observing.** Vivek Murthy's advisory *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation* (2023), with Holt-Lunstad as lead scientific editor, reported that about half of US adults report loneliness and equated the mortality effect of disconnection with smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day. It named social connection a determinant of health and set out a national strategy across individuals, communities, health systems and governments.
- **Meets.** Supports and extends: it treats society-scale connection as part of personal health, close to our "a healthy me: each self within it, at each scale, do-no-harming and improving", read outward.
- **Likely misreading.** That loneliness is a private feeling to be fixed by the individual. The advisory places responsibility at every scale (policy, design of places, workplaces), which is closer to our multi-scale self than to self-help.
- **Source.** https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf

### 3. WHO Commission on Social Connection: global figures (2025)
- **Observing.** The WHO Commission on Social Connection reported (30 June 2025) that about 1 in 6 people worldwide are affected by loneliness and that loneliness is linked to over 871,000 deaths a year, roughly 100 an hour.
- **Meets.** Supports at the scale of humanity: a society of selves whose disconnection shows up in its members' deaths. Extends our "society is itself a self" into global health governance.
- **Likely misreading.** That a causal per-death count is established. The figure is a modelled attributable estimate from observational data.
- **Source.** https://www.who.int/news/item/30-06-2025-social-connection-linked-to-improved-heath-and-reduced-risk-of-early-death

### 4. Social connection as a critical factor for mental and physical health (2024 review)
- **Observing.** Holt-Lunstad's 2024 review in *World Psychiatry* gathers evidence that social connection has structural, functional and quality components, each independently tied to cardiovascular disease, dementia, depression and mortality, and argues for treating it as a clinical vital sign.
- **Meets.** Supports our "parities": connection has distinct directions (structure, function, quality) relative to each other, not one quantity.
- **Likely misreading.** That "social" means only human-to-human. The review is human-scale; our concept extends the same structure inward (cells, microbes).
- **Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11403199/

### 5. Social determinants of health: the gradient (Marmot, WHO CSDH 2008)
- **Observing.** The WHO Commission on Social Determinants of Health (chair Michael Marmot) reported in *Closing the Gap in a Generation* (2008) that health follows a social gradient: the conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work and age, and the distribution of power and money, drive large, avoidable inequalities in life expectancy.
- **Meets.** Supports "a me is outward within societies": health is not a property of the body alone. Extends "social moral competency" toward justice: a society that harms some of its selves is not a healthy me.
- **Likely misreading.** That social determinants are external "causes" acting on a separate individual. In our terms the individual's health is partly constituted by the society it is within.
- **Source.** https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/43943

## B. The immunological self: who is self, who is other

### 6. Burnet: immunity as self/not-self discrimination (1949, 1959, Nobel 1960)
- **Observing.** Frank Macfarlane Burnet proposed that the immune system learns, during development, to recognise the body's own constituents as "self" and to attack "not-self"; clonal selection theory (1957–59) gave the mechanism, and his Nobel lecture (1960) framed immunology as the science of self and not-self.
- **Meets.** Collides in part. Burnet defines self by *exclusion* of the other, fixed early in life. Our "me" is defined *with* an other and all other, and keeps changing.
- **Likely misreading.** That immunology's "self" is a metaphysical self. Burnet's self is an operational category (what the immune system tolerates), and immunologists themselves now dispute it.
- **Source.** https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1960/burnet/lecture/

### 7. Matzinger's danger model (1994, 2002)
- **Observing.** Polly Matzinger argued that the immune system does not respond to "foreign" as such but to danger: alarm signals released by damaged or stressed cells. Harmless foreign things (food, fetus, commensal microbes) are tolerated; harmful self (tumours, damaged tissue) can be attacked.
- **Meets.** Supports "do-no-harming" as the operative rule: the immune response tracks harm, not membership. Collides with any reading of "me" as a fixed membership list.
- **Likely misreading.** That "danger" is a moral category. It is a molecular one (damage-associated molecular patterns); the fit to our ethics is structural, not literal.
- **Source.** 1994: https://www.doi.org/10.1146/annurev.iy.12.040194.005015 ; 2002 Science "The danger model: a renewed sense of self", doi 10.1126/science.1071059 (open copy listed by JCI: https://www.jci.org/references/scholar/39091/B2)

### 8. Pradeu's continuity and discontinuity theory of immunity (2006, 2012, 2013)
- **Observing.** Thomas Pradeu (with Edgardo Carosella, 2006) proposed that the immune system responds to strong *discontinuity* in the molecular patterns it interacts with, not to "non-self"; slow, continuous change (commensals, fetus, tumours that grow slowly) is tolerated. Pradeu, Jaeger and Vivier (2013) refined this to a "discontinuity theory" based on the speed of change. Pradeu's *The Limits of the Self* (2012) argues the organism is a heterogeneous, immunologically defined individual including its microbes.
- **Meets.** Strongly supports "same-existing from a prior-existing same thing": the immune self is defined by continuity through change, not by origin. Supports "a me is inward a society of selves" (microbes included in the organism).
- **Likely misreading.** That Pradeu abolishes the self. He replaces a self/other boundary with a criterion of continuity; the organism remains an individual, but a mixed one.
- **Source.** SEP, "Philosophy of Immunology" (Pradeu): https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/immunology/ ; 2006: https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/3062 ; 2013: https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/10046/

### 9. Peripheral immune tolerance and regulatory T cells (Nobel Prize 2025)
- **Observing.** The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went to Mary Brunkow, Fred Ramsdell and Shimon Sakaguchi for discovering peripheral immune tolerance: regulatory T cells (Tregs) and the FOXP3 gene actively restrain immune attack on the body's own tissues. When this fails (e.g. FOXP3 mutation, IPEX syndrome), severe autoimmunity follows.
- **Meets.** Supports "a healthy me: each self within it do-no-harming". Tolerance is not passive absence of attack but an ongoing, active restraint by some cells on others. Autoimmunity is a failure of social moral competency at the cellular scale.
- **Likely misreading.** That autoimmunity is "the self attacking itself" in a person-level sense. It is one cell population harming others within a shared body; the "self" is plural.
- **Source.** https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2025/press-release/

## C. Cancer: a cell parting from its tissue society

### 10. Cancer as cheating in multicellular cooperation (Aktipis et al. 2015; Aktipis 2020)
- **Observing.** C. Athena Aktipis and colleagues (2015) surveyed cancer-like phenomena across the tree of life and framed multicellularity as cooperation resting on five foundations: inhibition of proliferation, control of cell death, division of labour, resource allocation and care of the extracellular environment. Cancer is cheating on each. Her book *The Cheating Cell* (2020) extends this.
- **Meets.** Strongly supports "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self" and "a healthy me: each self within it, at each scale, do-no-harming". Cancer is a cell that keeps living and possibling for itself while ceasing to co-intelligence with its tissue.
- **Likely misreading.** That the cancer cell is "selfish" in an intentional or moral sense. The framing is evolutionary: selection within the body favours cells that defect.
- **Source.** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4581024/ ; book: https://newbooksnetwork.com/the-cheating-cell

### 11. Tissue organization field theory (Sonnenschein and Soto, 1999–2008)
- **Observing.** Carlos Sonnenschein and Ana Soto argue that cancer is a disease of tissue organisation, not of single mutated cells: proliferation is the default state of cells, and the tissue's field of relations normally restrains it. Disrupted stroma–epithelium relations can produce cancer; restoring them can normalise tumour cells.
- **Meets.** Extends our concept strongly: health of a cell is a property of its relations within the tissue society, not of the cell alone. This is "a me is defined only with an other" at the tissue scale.
- **Likely misreading.** That TOFT is the consensus. The somatic mutation theory remains dominant; TOFT is a respected minority view, and many now combine both.
- **Source.** https://www.pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18472276/

### 12. Fetal microchimerism: cooperation and conflict inside the mother (Boddy, Aktipis et al. 2015)
- **Observing.** Cells from a fetus cross the placenta and persist in the mother's tissues for decades. Amy Boddy, Athena Aktipis and colleagues (2015) review evidence that these cells can aid wound healing and lactation or contribute to autoimmune disease and cancer, and frame this as cooperation and conflict between mother and offspring.
- **Meets.** Supports "a me is inward a society of selves" literally: a mother's body carries her child's living cells. Extends "parities": mother and child have changing directions relative to each other, within one body.
- **Likely misreading.** That microchimerism is rare or exotic. It is near-universal after pregnancy; what is uncertain is its net health effect.
- **Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4712643 ; ASU summary: https://news.asu.edu/content/fetal-cells-influence-moms-health-during-pregnancy-%E2%80%94-and-long-after

## D. The holobiont: the inward society includes other species

### 13. "We have never been individuals" (Gilbert, Sapp, Tauber 2012)
- **Observing.** Scott Gilbert, Jan Sapp and Alfred Tauber argue that animals and plants are holobionts, host plus symbionts, by every criterion of individuality (anatomical, developmental, physiological, immunological, genetic, evolutionary). Symbionts are needed for normal development and immune maturation.
- **Meets.** Strongly supports "a me is inward a society of selves (biological, chemical, atomic)" and "there is no living without others". The other is not only outside; it is constitutive.
- **Likely misreading.** That the holobiont is a unit of selection in the same way as an organism. That claim is contested; the developmental and physiological dependence is not.
- **Source.** https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/668166

### 14. Revised count: about as many bacterial as human cells (Sender, Fuchs, Milo 2016)
- **Observing.** Ron Milo's group revised the old "10 bacteria per human cell" figure to about 38 trillion bacteria and 30 trillion human cells in a reference adult, a ratio near 1.3:1, with most human cells being red blood cells.
- **Meets.** Supports the inward-society claim with numbers: by cell count, the me is roughly half others.
- **Likely misreading.** That cell count measures contribution. Bacterial cells are tiny (about 0.2 kg in total); their weight is in function, not mass.
- **Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27541692/

### 15. The microbiota–gut–brain axis (Cryan et al. 2019)
- **Observing.** John Cryan, Ted Dinan and colleagues reviewed evidence that gut microbes signal to the brain via the vagus nerve, immune mediators and metabolites (e.g. short-chain fatty acids, tryptophan metabolites), affecting stress responses, mood and behaviour in animals, with emerging human data.
- **Meets.** Extends: the inward society takes part in the me's feeling and behaving, not only its digestion. Links the holobiont to our "a me is consciousness".
- **Likely misreading.** That microbes "control" mood in humans. Most causal evidence is from germ-free or treated animals; human causal evidence is still limited.
- **Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31460832/

## E. Psychoneuroimmunology and allostasis: the body regulating with others

### 16. Conditioned immunosuppression: the founding of psychoneuroimmunology (Ader and Cohen 1975)
- **Observing.** Robert Ader and Nicholas Cohen showed that rats given saccharin paired with an immunosuppressant later suppressed their immune response to saccharin alone. The immune system could learn through the nervous system.
- **Meets.** Supports "a me is a self observing, possibling, discovering": the inward society anticipates. Supports co-intelligencing among scales (nervous and immune selves coupling).
- **Likely misreading.** That "the mind controls immunity" at will. The effect is associative learning in a bidirectional system, not voluntary control.
- **Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1162023/

### 17. Social ties, immunity and gene expression (Cohen et al. 1997; Cole et al. 2007)
- **Observing.** Sheldon Cohen and colleagues (1997) exposed volunteers to a cold virus; people with more diverse social roles were about four times less likely to develop a cold. Steve Cole, Louise Hawkley, John Cacioppo and colleagues (2007) found chronically lonely people show a distinct leukocyte gene-expression profile: up-regulated inflammation, down-regulated antiviral and antibody genes (later called the "conserved transcriptional response to adversity").
- **Meets.** Strongly supports "a me is defined only with an other" down to gene expression: the outward society shapes the inward cell society.
- **Likely misreading.** That loneliness "damages genes". It changes which genes are expressed, which is reversible in principle.
- **Source.** 1997: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9333253 ; 2007: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17854483/

### 18. Allostasis: stability through change, by prediction (Sterling and Eyer 1988; McEwen 1998; Sterling 2012)
- **Observing.** Peter Sterling and Joseph Eyer (1988) proposed allostasis, "stability through change": the brain predicts needs and adjusts physiology in advance, rather than only correcting deviations (homeostasis). Bruce McEwen (1998) added allostatic load: the wear from chronic or poorly ended stress responses. Sterling (2012) restated allostasis as predictive regulation.
- **Meets.** Supports "a self observing, possibling and discovering its own next-existing": regulation is anticipation of the next state. Supports "same-existing": stability is kept by changing.
- **Likely misreading.** That allostasis replaces homeostasis wholesale. Many physiologists treat it as an extension; the dispute is partly terminological.
- **Source.** McEwen 1998: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9428819/ ; Sterling 2012 (Physiology & Behavior 106:5–15) abstract: https://www.qigonginstitute.org/abstract-print/16743

## F. Interoception and the bodily self

### 19. Interoception and the anterior insula (Craig 2002, 2009)
- **Observing.** A. D. (Bud) Craig described a homeostatic afferent pathway carrying the condition of all body tissues to the posterior insula, re-represented in right anterior insula as a basis for subjective feeling and, he argued, for a sentient self across moments.
- **Meets.** Supports "a me is consciousness" grounded in the inward society: feeling is the body's tissues reported to the whole.
- **Likely misreading.** That the self "lives in" the insula. Craig's claim is about a necessary substrate and integration, not a seat.
- **Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12154366/

### 20. Interoceptive inference and the embodied self (Seth 2013; Seth, Suzuki, Critchley 2012)
- **Observing.** Anil Seth proposed that emotion and the sense of being a self arise from the brain's predictions about the body's internal state (interoceptive inference), with the purpose of keeping the organism alive. Seth, Suzuki and Critchley (2012) modelled "conscious presence" as successful suppression of interoceptive prediction error, and its loss as depersonalisation. Popularised in *Being You* (2021).
- **Meets.** Strongly supports "a self observing, possibling and discovering its own next-existing", and "a me is consciousness" tied to living. Supports our claim that intelligence apart from living is a different thing: in Seth's view consciousness is tied to being a living, self-maintaining body.
- **Likely misreading.** That Seth's "self" is a mere illusion. He calls it a controlled hallucination, but a functional, life-serving one, not nothing.
- **Source.** 2013 (Trends Cogn Sci): https://sro.sussex.ac.uk/id/eprint/49586/ ; 2012: https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2011.00395/full

## G. Disorders of the self in psychiatry

### 21. Depersonalisation-derealisation disorder, and self-disorders in schizophrenia
- **Observing.** Depersonalisation-derealisation disorder (persistent feeling of being detached from one's self, body or world, with intact reality-testing) has a prevalence of about 1% in community samples (systematic review: Yang, Millman, David, Hunter 2023). Louis Sass and Josef Parnas (2003) argue that schizophrenia is at root a disorder of minimal self-experience: diminished self-affection and hyper-reflexivity.
- **Meets.** Extends and tests "a me is consciousness": the self can be present yet felt as absent. A me can be conscious but alienated from its own parities. Partly collides: a person with depersonalisation is fully conscious yet reports "no me".
- **Likely misreading.** That depersonalisation means the self is gone. Patients remain aware that the feeling is unreal; it is a change in the felt quality of selfhood, not its absence.
- **Source.** Prevalence: https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10150602/ ; Sass and Parnas 2003: https://dc2.philarchive.org/rec/SASSCA

## H. What health is: definitions and clinical stance

### 22. WHO 1948 definition and Huber's "positive health" (2011, 2016)
- **Observing.** The WHO Constitution (1946, in force 1948) defines health as "a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity". Machteld Huber and colleagues (BMJ 2011) argued "complete" makes most people unhealthy most of the time and proposed health as "the ability to adapt and to self manage in the face of social, physical and emotional challenges". Their 2016 study operationalised "positive health" in six dimensions: bodily functions, mental functions, meaningfulness, quality of life, social participation, daily functioning.
- **Meets.** Huber supports our concept closely: health as a capacity of a living self to keep possibling with others, not a static complete state. Our "healthy me" adds a moral criterion (do-no-harming, improving) at each inward scale, which neither definition has.
- **Likely misreading.** That "adaptation" means acceptance of any conditions. Huber's critics note the risk of shifting burden onto individuals; our concept places the do-no-harming duty on every scale, society included.
- **Source.** WHO: https://www.who.int/about/governance/constitution ; Huber 2011: https://www.bmj.com/content/343/bmj.d4163.full ; Huber 2016: https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/6/1/e010091

### 23. Biopsychosocial and relationship-centred medicine (Engel 1977; Beach and Inui 2006)
- **Observing.** George Engel (1977) argued that the biomedical model's reduction of illness to molecular deviation could not explain illness as lived; he proposed a biopsychosocial model, a hierarchy of systems from cell to society. Mary Catherine Beach, Thomas Inui and the Relationship-Centered Care Research Network (2006) went further: the relationship between patient and clinician (and among clinicians, and with community) is itself the moral and therapeutic unit of care, with both parties changed by it.
- **Meets.** Supports and extends: Engel's systems hierarchy mirrors our selves at each scale; relationship-centred care is close to "co-intelligencing", where healing is a bi-coupling, not a delivery.
- **Likely misreading.** That patient-centred means patient-sovereign. Relationship-centred care stresses reciprocity and mutual influence, not one party's wishes overriding the other's.
- **Source.** Engel: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/847460/ ; Beach and Inui: https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/relationship-centered-care-a-constructive-reframing/

## I. Curiosity, wellbeing and ageing

### 24. Curiosity, meaning, wellbeing and survival (Kashdan and Steger 2007; Swan and Carmelli 1996; Sakaki et al. 2018; Whatley et al. 2025)
- **Observing.** Todd Kashdan and Michael Steger (2007) found in daily-diary data that on days people felt more curious they reported more meaning and life satisfaction, and trait-curious people gained more. Gary Swan and Dorit Carmelli (1996) found, in a 5-year follow-up of older men in the Western Collaborative Group Study, that higher curiosity was associated with lower mortality. Michiko Sakaki, Ayano Yagi and Kou Murayama (2018) proposed curiosity as a key to adaptive ageing, linking it to dopaminergic memory systems. Whatley, Murayama, Sakaki and Castel (2025, n = 1,218) found trait curiosity falls with age but state curiosity (interest in specific new information) rises with age.
- **Meets.** Supports "a curious me: its possibling shared with others and its discovering of next possibilities" and suggests that curiosity is a health variable, not only a trait. The 2025 result fits "possibling" as situational and renewable.
- **Likely misreading.** That curiosity causes longevity. The mortality data are observational; Kashdan's work is also mostly within-person and correlational. Our concept says curiosity is *shared*; most measures here are individual.
- **Source.** Kashdan and Steger: https://stafforini.com/works/kashdan-2007-curiosity-pathways-wellbeing/ ; Swan and Carmelli: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8893314/ ; Sakaki et al.: https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/75889/1/SakakiYagiMurayama_2018.pdf ; Whatley et al.: https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0320600.html

## J. Consciousness at the edges of the me

### 25. Covert awareness in disorders of consciousness (Owen 2006; Claassen 2019, 2022; Bodien 2024 NEJM)
- **Observing.** Adrian Owen and colleagues (2006) showed by fMRI that a woman diagnosed as vegetative could follow instructions to imagine playing tennis or walking through her house. Jan Claassen's group found EEG signs of command-following in about 15% of unresponsive acutely brain-injured patients (NEJM 2019), and in a 193-patient follow-up those with covert consciousness recovered faster (41% vs 10% good recovery at one year; Lancet Neurology 2022). Yelena Bodien and colleagues (NEJM 2024; 353 adults, six sites) found cognitive motor dissociation in about 25% of patients with no behavioural response to commands.
- **Meets.** Supports "a not-conscious me is not possibly existing" in an unexpected way: a me can persist, conscious, while the outward society cannot see it. Collides with any reading that ties the me to visible co-intelligencing: here the bi-coupling is cut on one side.
- **Likely misreading.** That 25% of "vegetative" patients are fully aware. Task-based activation shows covert command-following, a sign of some awareness, not proof of rich experience; a negative test does not prove unconsciousness.
- **Source.** Owen 2006: https://www.coma.uliege.be/publications/detecting-awareness-in-the-vegetative-state ; Claassen 2019: https://www.cuimc.columbia.edu/news/hidden-consciousness-detectable-eeg-just-days-after-brain-injury ; Claassen 2022: https://www.cuimc.columbia.edu/news/hidden-consciousness-detected-eeg-predicts-recovery-unresponsive-patients ; Bodien 2024: https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645 and https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/one-in-four-patients-in-vegetative-or-minimally-conscious-state-able-to-perform-cognitive-tasks

### 26. Anaesthesia: unresponsiveness is not unconsciousness (Alkire, Hudetz, Tononi 2008; Sanders et al. 2012)
- **Observing.** Michael Alkire, Anthony Hudetz and Giulio Tononi (2008) reviewed how general anaesthetics abolish consciousness, most consistently by disrupting cortical integration and effective connectivity rather than switching off a single centre. Robert Sanders, Tononi, Steven Laureys and Jamie Sleigh (2012) separated unconsciousness, disconnection from the environment, and unresponsiveness: isolated forearm technique studies show some patients respond to commands during apparent general anaesthesia, and dreaming (disconnected consciousness) is common.
- **Meets.** Extends "a me is consciousness": consciousness can be present while disconnected from others (dreaming under anaesthesia), which tests "a me is defined only with an other". Supports the view that the me is an integration of many selves; anaesthesia works by breaking that integration.
- **Likely misreading.** That anaesthesia is like sleep, or that it "turns off" the self cleanly. It is a pharmacological, graded and variable disconnection; the self returns because the inward society (cells, tissues) keeps living throughout.
- **Source.** Alkire et al. 2008: https://centerforsleepandconsciousness.psychiatry.wisc.edu/wp-publications/rn147 ; Sanders et al. 2012: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3311716

### 27. Death determination: brain death guidelines, legal unrest, and post-mortem brain revival (2019–2023)
- **Observing.** The 2023 AAN/AAP/CNS/SCCM consensus guideline (David Greer and colleagues) defines brain death/death by neurologic criteria as permanent loss of function of the brain, including the brainstem, causing coma, brainstem areflexia and apnoea; it does not require loss of all hypothalamic function. The US Uniform Law Commission paused its revision of the Uniform Determination of Death Act in 2023 after failing to agree on wording. Nenad Sestan's group (Vrselja et al., Nature 2019) restored circulation and some cellular functions in pig brains four hours after death (BrainEx) without restoring organised brain activity.
- **Meets.** Collides productively. Medicine defines death of the *person* by loss of whole-brain function while many inward selves (cells, organs) keep living, sometimes for transplant. This tests our claim that a me is consciousness *and* a society of living selves: when the integrating consciousness ends, the inward society does not die at once.
- **Likely misreading.** That BrainEx shows the dead can be revived. Cells were partly restored; no sign of awareness was seen, and the study blocked neural activity by design.
- **Source.** Guideline: https://www.organdonationalliance.org/toolbox/pediatric-and-adult-brain-death-death-by-neurologic-criteria-consensus-guideline/ ; UDDA pause: https://nyulangone.org/news/brain-injury-expert-says-important-changes-still-needed-law-defining-death-despite-reform-pause ; BrainEx: https://news.yale.edu/2019/04/17/scientists-restore-some-functions-pigs-brain-hours-after-death

## Collisions worth our word

1. **Self by exclusion vs self by relation.** Burnet's immunology, still taught as the default, defines the self by what it attacks: the not-self. Our "me is defined only with an other" says the other is constitutive. Matzinger and Pradeu side with us on mechanism (harm and discontinuity, not foreignness), but clinical immunology still runs on self/non-self language. We should say plainly that our "other" includes what the immune system rightly attacks.
2. **Is the cancer cell a self?** By our definition, a cancer cell observes, possibles and discovers its next-existing; it is living. Yet it ceases to co-intelligence with its tissue and harms it. Medicine treats it as a defector to be removed, not a self with a claim. Our concept must say whether "each self do-no-harming" licenses removing a harming self, and at which scale the moral claim sits.
3. **Consciousness without visible others, and living without consciousness.** Covert awareness (25% CMD) and dreaming under anaesthesia show a me conscious but cut off from co-intelligencing. Conversely, the brain-dead body, the cell culture and the transplanted organ are living without consciousness. Medicine separates "alive" (organism or cells) from "conscious" (person). Our "a not-conscious me is not possibly existing" holds only if we say clearly that a me is not the same as a living body, and that living selves without a me exist.
4. **Depersonalisation: conscious, yet no felt me.** Psychiatry reports people fully conscious who sincerely say "I am not here". This collides with "a me is consciousness: one definition" unless we allow a me to exist while its own felt sense of itself is impaired. The field would say consciousness and self-experience can come apart.
5. **Health without a moral criterion.** WHO and Huber define health as well-being or adaptive capacity; neither says each inward self must do no harm and improve. Medicine treats harm-doing tissues as disease, but does not call a healthy person a "social moral competency set". Our moral definition of health will read to clinicians as a category shift; we should state it as a definition of our own, consistent with but stronger than Huber.
