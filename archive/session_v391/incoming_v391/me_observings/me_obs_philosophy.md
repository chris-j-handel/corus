# Observings: philosophy of consciousness and the self

Field: philosophy of consciousness and the self, with the cognitive science of the self.
For: "Me · Co-Intelligencing a Universal Self".
Gathered: 23 October 2026.

Order: current consciousness science first (2023–2026), then the self in cognitive science, then life and mind, then relational selves, then metaphysics. Within each group the most-cited or most-current work comes first.

Key to "touches": **U** universal set · **S** self (living, at each scale, bi-coupling) · **L** living is co-intelligencing; stand-alone knowledge is non-living · **M** me (unique set, parities, inward and outward, defined with other) · **C** a me is consciousness · **H/K** healthy and curious me (social moral competency).

## A. Consciousness science now (2023–2026)

### 1. The IIT vs GNWT adversarial collaboration found no winner (Cogitate Consortium, *Nature*, 2025)
- **Observing.** Proponents of integrated information theory (IIT) and global neuronal workspace theory (GNWT) agreed, before any data, what would count against each theory. They then tested 256 people in 7 labs with fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG. IIT's prediction of sustained synchrony in posterior cortex was not found. GNWT's predicted "ignition" when a percept ends was not found, and the prefrontal cortex held only some of the conscious content. A second large experiment is being finished.
- **Meets.** *Collides gently, on C.* The field has no settled, tested criterion for where consciousness is present. If we say "a me is consciousness," we take on that open question. We cannot borrow a measure from neuroscience to settle it.
- **Likely misreading.** That science has "disproved" consciousness theories, or shown that consciousness cannot be measured. It tested specific neural predictions. Both theories survive in revised form.
- **Source.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1 · PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40307561/

### 2. The indicator-properties method for AI consciousness (Butlin, Long et al., 2023; *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 2025)
- **Observing.** Nineteen researchers, among them Chalmers, Bengio, Birch and Fleming, take the main neuroscientific theories (recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order, predictive processing, attention schema, agency and embodiment). They derive "indicator properties" from them and check AI systems against the list. Their working assumption is computational functionalism. Their 2023 verdict: no current system is a strong candidate, but there are no obvious technical barriers to building one. The 2025 paper sharpens this into a method for setting how confident to be.
- **Meets.** *Collides with L and C.* The method makes consciousness depend on functional organisation, not on being alive. A non-living system could therefore meet it. It *extends* M on one point: the "agency and embodiment" indicators ask whether a system models its own coupling with an environment. That is close to our parities.
- **Likely misreading.** That the report says AI is, or will soon be, conscious. It gives a checklist under one contested assumption. It also states that systems do not currently score high.
- **Source.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011 · 2023 report: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708

### 3. Biological naturalism: consciousness may need life (Seth, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 2025)
- **Observing.** Anil Seth argues against computational functionalism. He holds that consciousness may depend on properties of living systems: multiscale self-maintenance, autopoiesis and predictive regulation of the body to stay alive. If so, a large language model, however capable, is unlikely to be conscious. BBS published the paper with many open peer commentaries.
- **Meets.** *Supports L and C strongly.* This is the nearest the field comes to our claim that intelligence apart from living is a non-living emanation, and that a me is conscious because it lives. It also *supports S*: the self is first a living body regulating itself.
- **Likely misreading.** That Seth proves machines cannot be conscious. He argues it is unlikely for current architectures and leaves open that it could be possible for artificial living systems.
- **Source.** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/conscious-artificial-intelligence-and-biological-naturalism/C9912A5BE9D806012E3C8B3AF612E39A

### 4. Taking AI welfare seriously, and early evidence of model introspection (Long, Sebo, Butlin, Chalmers, Birch et al., 2024; Lindsey/Anthropic, 2025–26)
- **Observing.** Long and colleagues argue there is a "realistic possibility" that some near-future AI systems will be conscious or robustly agentic, and so morally significant. They urge labs to assess and prepare now. Separately, Anthropic researchers planted concepts directly into a model's internal activations and found that models sometimes, unreliably, noticed and named them. They call this a limited form of introspective awareness.
- **Meets.** *Collides with L.* Our concept classes a trained model as stand-alone knowledge. The field increasingly asks the question about the *running system in interaction*, not about the stored weights. That distinction could *extend* our concept rather than break it. The weights would be an emanation. Whether a deployed system coupled with users is a self is a separate question we have not yet answered. It also *touches H*: if the possibility is real, do-no-harm reaches to such systems.
- **Likely misreading.** That "introspective awareness" means felt self-awareness. The authors explicitly do not claim phenomenal consciousness.
- **Source.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01828

### 5. A moratorium on synthetic suffering (Metzinger, 2021)
- **Observing.** Thomas Metzinger argues for a global moratorium until 2050 on research that risks creating artificial conscious experience. His reason is that such systems could suffer in large numbers, with no way for them to report it or for us to stop it.
- **Meets.** *Extends H.* It applies do-no-harm across the line between living and artificial before anyone knows whether that line holds.
- **Likely misreading.** That Metzinger thinks current AI suffers. He argues from risk and uncertainty.
- **Source.** https://zenodo.org/records/7034864

### 6. Could a large language model be conscious? (Chalmers, 2023)
- **Observing.** David Chalmers weighs reasons for and against. Reasons against include missing recurrence, global workspace, unified agency, senses and a body. He estimates under 10% that the LLMs of the time were conscious. He thinks extended successors ("LLM+" systems with senses, bodies and memory) could reach much higher credence within a decade.
- **Meets.** *Collides with L, partly.* For Chalmers the obstacles are architectural, not life itself. *Supports M*, though: several of his obstacles are about coupling with a world and with others, which is our "defined only with an other."
- **Likely misreading.** That a 10% estimate is a finding. It is a stated personal credence.
- **Source.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07103

### 7. Integrated information theory and its exclusion postulate (Tononi, 2004; IIT 4.0, Albantakis et al., 2023)
- **Observing.** IIT says consciousness *is* a maximally irreducible cause-effect structure, measured by integrated information (Φ). Its exclusion postulate says that among overlapping candidate systems, only the one with maximal Φ is conscious. Elements and supersets of that maximum are not separately conscious. IIT also predicts that conventional digital computers have negligible Φ whatever they compute.
- **Meets.** *Supports C and L* in two ways. Consciousness is identified with an intrinsic, unified existence, and stand-alone computation is not conscious. *Collides sharply with S*, though. Under exclusion, a person and the society they belong to cannot both be conscious subjects. Our "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self," joined to "a me is consciousness," is what IIT forbids.
- **Likely misreading.** That IIT is a "panpsychism of everything." It grants consciousness only to local maxima of Φ, not to every aggregate.
- **Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10581496/ · Tononi 2004: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC543470/

### 8. The "pseudoscience" dispute over IIT (Fleming et al. open letter, 2023; Klincewicz et al., *Nature Neuroscience*, 2025)
- **Observing.** More than 100 researchers signed a 2023 open letter calling IIT pseudoscience. The 2025 *Nature Neuroscience* piece argues that IIT's core claims cannot be tested even in principle. Defenders reply that the adversarial-collaboration data show it is testable.
- **Meets.** *A caution on C and U.* An identity claim ("consciousness is X") that fixes its own axioms first draws this charge. Our "a me is consciousness: one definition" will draw the same charge unless we say what would count against it.
- **Likely misreading.** That the field has thrown IIT out. It is contested, still funded and still tested.
- **Source.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-01881-x

### 9. Global workspace: consciousness as broadcast (Baars, 1988; Dehaene, Changeux et al.; Mashour et al., *Neuron*, 2020)
- **Observing.** Many specialised, unconscious processors compete. Content becomes conscious when it wins entry to a limited-capacity workspace and is "broadcast" to all of them. In the brain this shows up as a sudden, non-linear "ignition" across prefrontal and parietal networks.
- **Meets.** *Supports M (inward society) and L.* Inside one me, consciousness is many sub-processes sharing what one of them has. That is co-intelligencing within. *Collides with C at small scales.* The workspace is a high-level architecture, so a cell or a single circuit has none.
- **Likely misreading.** That the workspace is a place or a central viewer, a "Cartesian theatre." Baars's metaphor is a theatre, but the claim is about availability across processes.
- **Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8770991/

### 10. The hard problem (Chalmers, 1995)
- **Observing.** The "easy problems" are explaining discrimination, report, integration and control. The hard problem is explaining why any of that is accompanied by experience, why there is "something it is like." Chalmers argues that functional explanation alone cannot close this gap.
- **Meets.** *Collides with C as a definition.* Saying "a me is consciousness" names the two as one. It does not explain why a living, co-intelligencing set is felt from within. Readers from this field will ask whether we have solved, dissolved or set aside the hard problem.
- **Likely misreading.** That defining me as consciousness *answers* the hard problem. It states an identity. It does not explain it.
- **Source.** https://consc.net/papers/facing.pdf

## B. The self in cognitive science

### 11. The minimal self and pre-reflective self-awareness (Gallagher, 2000; Zahavi, 2005; Gallagher & Zahavi, SEP)
- **Observing.** Every experience comes with a "for-me-ness," a first-person givenness that needs no reflection, no concept of self and no narrative. Gallagher separates the *minimal self* (sense of ownership and sense of agency in the moment) from the *narrative self* (extended across time in stories). Zahavi argues that the minimal self is real and is the condition of any further self.
- **Meets.** *Supports C directly.* For this tradition, any conscious experience already has a minimal self, so a not-conscious me is not possible. *Collides with M*, in that the minimal self is not constituted by others. It is there before social relation.
- **Likely misreading.** That "pre-reflective" means unconscious. It means conscious but not attended to as an object.
- **Source.** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-consciousness-phenomenological/ · Gallagher 2000: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1364-6613(99)01417-5

### 12. Multidimensional self: experience, empathy, shame (Zahavi, *Self and Other*, 2014)
- **Observing.** Zahavi keeps the minimal experiential self but adds dimensions that only exist through others. Shame shows that I can be given to myself *as seen by another*. Empathy is a direct, perception-like grasp of another's experience, not an inference.
- **Meets.** *Supports M and H.* A me has an inner core and outward layers that only exist with an other. Shame and empathy are where social moral competency is felt.
- **Likely misreading.** That Zahavi has become a pure social constructivist. He holds both levels.
- **Source.** https://cfs.ku.dk/news/self-and-other/

### 13. The self as centre of narrative gravity (Dennett, 1992)
- **Observing.** A self is like a centre of gravity: a useful abstraction, not a physical part. Brains spin stories as spiders spin webs, and the self is what those stories are about. It is real as a pattern, not as a hidden thing.
- **Meets.** *Supports S as process* (a self is made over time, by doing). *Collides with C.* Dennett denies any extra inner "what it is like" beyond functions and reports. For him our C would be either trivial or an illusion.
- **Likely misreading.** That "abstraction" or "fiction" means the self does not matter or does not exist. Dennett means it exists the way an equator does.
- **Source.** https://web-archive.southampton.ac.uk/cogprints.org/266/1/selfctr.htm

### 14. Narrative self-constitution, and the Episodic objection (Schechtman, 1996; G. Strawson, 2004)
- **Observing.** Marya Schechtman holds that a person makes themselves a person by building an autobiographical narrative that others can recognise and that fits social practice. Galen Strawson replies that many people are "Episodic." They do not live their lives as a story, and they are not ethically or psychologically lacking for it.
- **Meets.** *Supports M and K* (Schechtman): the me is made with others, and its next chapter is a possibling. *Collides with S* (Strawson): if selves exist in brief, non-narrative episodes, a self does not need to see its own story in order to live. Our "observing … its own next-existing" should not be read as requiring a life story.
- **Likely misreading.** That a curious me must be a self-narrating me.
- **Source.** https://metapsychology.net/?p=238 · Strawson: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9329.2004.00264.x

### 15. The self-model theory: no one is there (Metzinger, *Being No One*, 2003)
- **Observing.** There are no selves, only systems that run a transparent *phenomenal self-model*. The system cannot see the model as a model, so it experiences itself as a self. The model is the system's tool for controlling itself as a whole.
- **Meets.** *Collides with S and M on what exists.* For Metzinger a "me" is the content of a representation, not a living set. It *supports C* in a narrower way: the conscious self is a model-in-use, so wherever there is a self-model there is self-experience.
- **Likely misreading.** That "model" means stand-alone knowledge in our sense. Metzinger's self-model is a live process inside a living system, not a stored record.
- **Source.** https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780262633086

### 16. Protoself, core self, autobiographical self (Damasio, 1999, 2010; Damasio & Carvalho, 2013)
- **Observing.** Antonio Damasio stacks the self in layers. The protoself is brainstem mapping of the body's state, with primordial feelings. The core self is a pulse of "this is happening to me" when an object changes the organism. The autobiographical self is memory-based identity. All of it rests on homeostasis, and feelings report how life-regulation is going.
- **Meets.** *Supports S, M (inward society) and H.* The me is built up from bodily life, and "feeling well" is the felt state of good regulation, close to our healthy me. *Collides with C at the base.* Damasio's protoself is not itself conscious. He allows unconscious layers of self, which our "a not-conscious me is not possible" rules out.
- **Likely misreading.** That the three selves are three little agents. They are levels of one organism's regulation.
- **Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23329161/ · *Self Comes to Mind* (2010): https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780307378750

### 17. The beast-machine self (Seth & Tsakiris, 2018)
- **Observing.** The most basic self is the brain's predictive control of the body's interior: heartbeat, gut and chemistry. It works to keep the organism alive. Experience of self is a "controlled hallucination" in the service of staying alive, not of describing the world.
- **Meets.** *Supports S and L.* The self is first a living thing predicting its own next state, which is close to our "possibling … its own next-existing."
- **Likely misreading.** That "hallucination" means the self is unreal or false.
- **Source.** https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Being_a_beast_machine_the_somatic_basis_of_selfhood/23461235

## C. Life and mind

### 18. Autopoiesis: living as self-production and cognition (Maturana & Varela, 1980)
- **Observing.** A living system is autopoietic. It is a network that continually produces the components that produce it, and it makes its own boundary. "Living systems are cognitive systems, and living as a process is a process of cognition." Systems shape each other through *structural coupling* while each keeps its own organisation.
- **Meets.** *Strong support for S and L.* Each self is living. Cognition belongs to living. Coupling is how selves co-shape. Our "bi-coupling" is close to structural coupling. *Collides with S on the society claim.* Maturana denied that human societies are themselves autopoietic unities. Luhmann's social systems are autopoietic, but they are made of communications, not persons.
- **Likely misreading.** That "operational closure" means isolation. It means self-determined organisation, and it needs constant open exchange.
- **Source.** https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-94-009-8947-4

### 19. Deep continuity of life and mind; enactivism (Varela, Thompson & Rosch, 1991; Thompson, *Mind in Life*, 2007)
- **Observing.** Mind is "enacted" by an organism's sense-making as it keeps itself going in a world. Where there is life there is mind, and the properties of mind are richer forms of the properties of life. *The Embodied Mind* drew on Buddhist practice to argue that the self is a process without a fixed core.
- **Meets.** *Supports S, L and the inward-outward M.* *Collides with C at the cell scale.* Thompson grants sense-making and a minimal "selfhood" to cells, but not human-style consciousness. Our "a me is consciousness" at each scale goes further than enactivists do.
- **Likely misreading.** That "mind in life" means every living thing is conscious as we are.
- **Source.** https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780262529365 · *Mind in Life*: https://www.bookshare.org/browse/book/1624082

### 20. Participatory sense-making (De Jaegher & Di Paolo, 2007)
- **Observing.** When two agents interact, the interaction process can gain its own autonomy. It can keep itself going and shape what each participant makes sense of, beyond what either intends. Social understanding is done *together*, not only inside each head.
- **Meets.** *Strong support for L and M.* This is the enactivist form of our "co-intelligencing": a couple's interaction has its own sense-making dynamics. It *partly supports S on societies*. The interaction is autonomous, though De Jaegher and Di Paolo stop short of calling it a conscious self.
- **Likely misreading.** That the interaction "is" a third mind with experiences.
- **Source.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-007-9076-9

### 21. Selves at every scale: TAME and the cognitive light cone (Levin, 2022)
- **Observing.** Michael Levin's experiments show that cells and tissues pursue goals, such as rebuilding a body shape after injury. Bioelectric networks bind cells into larger selves with larger goals, which he calls a bigger "cognitive light cone." Cancer is a cell's light cone shrinking back to the single cell, so that it defects from the collective.
- **Meets.** *Strongest support for S.* There are selves at each scale, and a collective of selves that couple becomes a self with its own goals. It also *supports H*. Cancer is a vivid case of one inward self ceasing to do-no-harm, and the larger me becoming unhealthy.
- **Likely misreading.** That Levin claims cells are conscious. He claims graded agency and cognition. He is cautious, and agnostic, about experience.
- **Source.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10346 · https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2022.768201

### 22. Nested Markov blankets of life (Kirchhoff, Parr, Palacios, Friston & Kiverstein, 2018)
- **Observing.** Under the free-energy principle, any self-organising system is set apart from its world by a statistical boundary (a Markov blanket). Blankets nest: cells inside organs inside organisms inside groups, each level keeping itself going by active inference. Critics argue that these blankets are modelling tools, not real boundaries (Bruineberg et al., *BBS* 2022).
- **Meets.** *Supports S and M*: inward and outward nesting, and a self defined by its exchanges across a boundary. *Collides* in that the formalism applies to non-living systems too, so it does not mark life or consciousness alone.
- **Likely misreading.** That a Markov blanket is a physical membrane.
- **Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5805980/ · critique: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/emperors-new-markov-blankets/715C589A73DDF861DCF8997271DE0B8C

### 23. Extended mind and the parity principle (Clark & Chalmers, 1998)
- **Observing.** If an outside resource (Otto's notebook) plays the role that memory would play inside the head, it is part of the cognitive process. That is the "parity principle." Cognition can extend into tools and other people.
- **Meets.** *Supports M outward and L.* A stand-alone emanation, such as a notebook, a book or a model, becomes part of a living me when it is coupled into its co-intelligencing. *Collides with C.* Clark and Chalmers extend cognition and belief, not consciousness. Chalmers has said that consciousness probably does not extend.
- **Likely misreading (naming).** Their "parity" means *functional equivalence* of inner and outer. Our "parity" means *a changing direction relative to an other*. Same word, different idea. We should flag this in the text.
- **Source.** https://www.academia.edu/178449/The_Extended_Mind

## D. Relational and social selves

### 24. The I and the me (James, 1890; Mead, 1934)
- **Observing.** William James split the self into the *I* (the knower) and the *Me* (all that a person can call theirs: body, clothes, family, home, reputation, inner states). There are material, social and spiritual Me's, and a man "has as many social selves as there are individuals who recognize him." G. H. Mead then argued that the self arises only in social interaction. The "me" is the organised attitudes of others taken into oneself (the "generalised other"). The "I" is the novel response that can change them.
- **Meets.** *Supports M strongly.* A me includes body, kin and society, and it exists only with others. Mead's "I" is close to our *possibling* and *discovering*, and his "me" to the other-built set. *Collides with S on scale.* Mead reserves selves for beings with language and role-taking, not cells.
- **Likely misreading.** That our "me" is James's or Mead's Me, which is the object side, the self as known. Ours is the whole living conscious set, the I and the Me together. Also, Mead's "me" is the conventional, conforming part, which our me is not.
- **Source.** James, *Principles*, ch. 10: https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin10.htm · Mead: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mead/

### 25. Recognition (Hegel, 1807; Honneth, 1992)
- **Observing.** In the *Phenomenology of Spirit*, self-consciousness "achieves its satisfaction only in another self-consciousness." One becomes a self through being recognised by another whom one recognises in turn. Axel Honneth turns this into three spheres (love, rights, esteem). Misrecognition in any of them is a moral injury that damages the self.
- **Meets.** *Supports M and H.* A me exists only with an other, and harm to a self includes failing to recognise it. *Collides* in tone. For Hegel, recognition is won through struggle, and the master–servant stage shows unequal, distorted forms. Co-living is not automatically mutual.
- **Likely misreading.** That recognition is simply being noticed. It is a mutual, normative standing.
- **Source.** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/recognition/

### 26. Ubuntu: a person is a person through other persons (Mbiti, 1969; Metz, 2007)
- **Observing.** In sub-Saharan thought, *umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu*. Personhood is achieved through relationships of identity (sharing a way of life) and solidarity (caring for others' good). Thaddeus Metz states it as a moral theory: an act is right insofar as it honours and promotes such harmonious relationships.
- **Meets.** *Strong support for M and H.* A me is a social moral competency set, and do-no-harm and improving are what being a person consists in.
- **Likely misreading.** That ubuntu dissolves the individual into the group. It also *collides on degree*. Many ubuntu accounts treat personhood as something one can have more or less of, which sits uneasily with "each self" having equal standing.
- **Source.** https://philarchive.org/rec/METTAA

### 27. Care ethics and the relational self (Gilligan, 1982; Noddings, 1984; Held, 2006)
- **Observing.** Moral life starts in relations of dependence and care, not in autonomous choosers. The self is relational. Moral competence is attentive, responsive maintenance of the relationships one lives in.
- **Meets.** *Supports H and K.* Do-no-harming and improving, practised as care, at every scale from couple to society.
- **Likely misreading.** That care ethics is "only for private life." Held and others extend it to politics.
- **Source.** https://iep.utm.edu/care/

## E. Metaphysics of self and consciousness

### 28. Anatta and the no-self debate (Buddhist Abhidharma; Siderits, Thompson & Zahavi eds., 2011; Thompson, 2020)
- **Observing.** Buddhist philosophy holds that there is no permanent, unitary self (*ātman*). There is a stream of five aggregates arising in dependence on causes (*pratītyasamutpāda*). Evan Thompson's middle view is that the self is real as a process of "I-making," not as a substance. He keeps a minimal subjectivity.
- **Meets.** *Supports U and S.* Something now-existing that arises from a prior-existing same stream is close to dependent origination, and the self is a process. *Collides with M*: Buddhist critics would treat a "unique set" as the clinging the path loosens.
- **Likely misreading.** That "no-self" means no one experiences, or no one is responsible. It denies a fixed owner, not experience or agency.
- **Source.** https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-indian-buddhism/ · *Self, No Self?*: https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780199593804

### 29. Panpsychism and the combination problem (G. Strawson, 2006; Goff, 2019; Chalmers, 2016)
- **Observing.** Strawson argues that real physicalism entails panpsychism: experience is a basic feature of physical things. Goff, in *Galileo's Error*, develops this view. The main obstacle is the combination problem: how do many micro-subjects make up one macro-subject, such as a person, rather than a crowd of them?
- **Meets.** *Supports C at every scale:* if each self is conscious, cells and atoms included, a not-conscious me is impossible. *Collides with S on societies.* Most panpsychists deny that every aggregate (a rock, a crowd, a nation) is a subject. Our "a society of selves bi-coupling is itself a self" is a combination claim. We would need an account of which couplings combine and which do not.
- **Likely misreading.** That panpsychism says rocks and spoons are conscious. It usually says their *constituents* have proto-experience.
- **Source.** https://consc.net/papers/combination.pdf · Strawson and replies: https://ndpr.nd.edu/news/consciousness-and-its-place-in-nature-does-physicalism-entail-panpsychism/ · Goff: https://penguinrandomhouse.com/books/599229/galileos-error/

## Collisions worth our word

1. **Can a society and its members both be conscious?** IIT's exclusion postulate (7) says no: only the maximum of integrated information is a subject. The panpsychist combination problem (29) says there is no settled account of how many subjects make one. Mead, Maturana and De Jaegher (18, 20, 24) give interactions and societies their own dynamics, but not their own consciousness. To hold both "a society is a self" and "a me is consciousness," we need to say what kind of consciousness a couple or a society has. The alternative is to say "self" at that scale does not require the same consciousness as at the person scale.

2. **Is there a not-conscious self?** Damasio's protoself (16), Dennett's narrative self (13) and Levin's cell-level agency (21) all allow self-organisation and goal-pursuit without experience. Gallagher and Zahavi's minimal self (11) agrees with us that every experience is self-given, but not that every self experiences. "A not-conscious me is not possibly existing" is a stronger claim than most of the field makes. We should say whether "consciousness" is meant in a wider sense than phenomenal experience.

3. **Can non-living systems be conscious?** The indicator-properties method (2), Chalmers (6) and Long et al. (4) treat consciousness as a matter of functional organisation, so a non-living AI could in principle qualify. Seth (3) and IIT (7) side with us for different reasons. The field's live question is the *running, coupled* AI system, not the stored model. Our "a trained AI model is stand-alone knowledge" meets the field best if we say clearly whether the deployed system in use is still only an emanation.

4. **Is a me a thing or a process, or a model?** Metzinger (15) and Dennett (13) say no one is there: there is a self-model or a narrative abstraction. Buddhist anatta (28) says no fixed self, only a stream. Our "a me is a unique universal set" will read to them as a reification. Our strongest reply is from the same field: enactivism and autopoiesis (18, 19) make the self a real, living process. We should say "a me" in that sense.

5. **Is selfhood first private or first relational?** Phenomenologists (11) hold that a minimal self comes before any relation to others. Mead, Hegel, ubuntu and care ethics (24–27) hold that self is made with others. "A me is defined only with an other" sides with the relational camp. We need to say whether the inner givenness of experience also needs an other, or only the me as a whole set does.
