## Chemistry's societies (C)

**Opening: nucleation**

C1. *(7 · 11)* In a supersaturated sodium chloride solution in a carbon nanotube cone, filmed at atomic resolution at 25 frames a second, cuboid crystals of some tens of NaCl units emerge from a disordered mixture of the ions; the times of emerging spread about their mean at a normal distribution (Nakamuro and colleagues, Journal of the American Chemical Society 143:1763, 2021, at a news account and its title).

C2. *(7 · 11)* Calcium carbonate solutions carry stable clusters of ions about two nanometres across before any crystal nucleates, even below saturation (Gebauer, Völkel and Cölfen, Science 322:1819, 2008, at its title and the field's account); the clusters are described as liquid-like ionic polymers (Demichelis and colleagues, Nature Communications 2011, at its title); a 2024 paper names the cluster pathway inconsistent with calcium carbonate's kinetics (Crystal Growth and Design 2024, at its title).

C3. *(7 · 11 · built)* A supersaturated sodium acetate solution continues liquid until a seed or a disturbance meets it, then crystallizes through its whole volume from that place, warming (the field's account, not fetched this session).

C4. *(7 · 11)* A supercooled melt or supercooled water, at nucleation, warms back toward its melting point as the crystal grows, the field's recalescence (the field's account, at an encyclopedia page's title).

**Parting**

C5. *(7 · 11)* A mixture made unstable parts into two phases everywhere at once, with no barrier, forming intertwined structures that coarsen, the field's spinodal decomposition; a mixture only metastable parts at a few discrete places over a barrier, the field's nucleation and growth (the field's account, at an encyclopedia page).

**Merging and releasing at one scale**

C6. *(7 · 11)* In a suspension of small crystals or an emulsion, smaller particles dissolve and their material redeposits onto larger ones, the field's Ostwald ripening, named at surface energy and the Kelvin equation (the field's account, at an encyclopedia page).

C7. *(7 · 11)* Surfactant molecules above a critical concentration associate into micelles; single molecules leave and rejoin a micelle within microseconds, while whole micelles form and dissolve over milliseconds to seconds (Aniansson and Wall, 1974, the field's account at a topic page, figures not fetched this session).

**One hand from many**

C8. *(7 · 11)* Sodium chlorate crystallizes in left- and right-handed crystals: from an unstirred solution in about equal numbers; from a stirred solution nearly all crystals of one sample share one hand, the hand differing from sample to sample (Kondepudi, Kaufman and Singh, Science 250:975, 1990, at its title and the field's account).

C9. *(7 · 11)* Equal amounts of left- and right-handed sodium chlorate crystals ground continuously in their saturated solution turn wholly to one hand along a sigmoidal course; the field names Ostwald ripening, attrition and fragments joining crystals of their own hand, *enantiospecific aggregation* (Viedma, Physical Review Letters 2005, at an encyclopedia's account).

**Locking: chemical oscillators**

C10. *(7 · 11)* Belousov–Zhabotinsky droplets held in a row in oil couple through species crossing the oil; the field reports neighbours oscillating anti-phase, in phase, and patterns where some droplets stay still while their neighbours oscillate, depending on droplet size, spacing and the species crossing (Toiya, Vanag and Epstein, Angewandte Chemie 2008; Delgado and colleagues, Soft Matter 7:3155, 2011, at their titles and the field's account, contents not fetched this session).

C11. *(7 · 11 · 23)* About a hundred thousand catalytic particles, each a Belousov–Zhabotinsky oscillator, suspended in a solution they exchange with, synchronize as their density rises either gradually or suddenly, switching on at a density, which way set by the rate of exchange with the solution (Taylor, Tinsley, Wang, Huang and Showalter, Science 323:614, 2009, at its abstract's account); in the field's account, slow exchange gives the gradual and fast exchange the sudden (not fetched this session).

**Receding, then the snap**

C12. *(7 · 11)* In the iodine clock, iodine forms throughout while thiosulfate turns each iodine back as it forms; when the thiosulfate is used up the solution turns dark blue at once (Landolt, 1886; the field's account, at an encyclopedia page).

C13. *(below 5 · 7)* Hydrogen and oxygen react slowly or explode at sharp limits of pressure and temperature: radicals open in pairs, pass the chain on, open further radicals at a branch, and end where two meet; explosion where branching outruns ending (the field's account, not fetched this session).

**A pattern while fed**

C14. *(7 · 11)* The chlorite–iodide–malonic acid reaction, fed continuously in a gel, holds spots or stripes at one wavelength of its own for as long as the feed continues (Castets, Dulos, Boissonade and De Kepper, Physical Review Letters 64:2953, 1990, at its title and the field's account).

## The molecule (M)

M1. *(7)* A single pentacene molecule has been imaged by atomic force microscopy near absolute zero, its five rings visible (Gross and colleagues, Science 2009, not fetched this session); at ordinary temperatures bonds vibrate, molecules rotate, and flexible molecules pass between conformations continuously (the field's account).

M2. *(7)* Ammonia's nitrogen passes through the plane of its hydrogens to the mirror side and back about twenty-four billion times a second, the field's inversion by tunnelling, on which the first maser was built (Townes, 1954; the field's account).

M3. *(7 · planet)* About three hundred kinds of molecule are detected in interstellar clouds (the field's account, not fetched this session); amino acids are found in the Murchison meteorite (Kvenvolden and colleagues, 1970) and form in the Miller–Urey apparatus (1953); minerals crystallize from magma (the field's accounts).

M4. *(7 · 11)* Molecules of L- and D-aspartic acid arriving at calcite's mirror faces are found remaining in different amounts at the two faces, the field's *selective adsorption* (Hazen, Filley and Goodfriend, PNAS 2001, at its title and the field's account).

## Pharmacology (P)

**The effect with no molecule**

P1. *(37)* Eighty people with irritable bowel syndrome: thirty-seven given pills plainly said to be inert improved more at three weeks than forty-three given no treatment, global improvement 5.0 against 3.9, with less symptom severity and more adequate relief (Kaptchuk and colleagues, PLoS ONE 2010, at its abstract).

P2. *(23 · 37)* Rats given saccharin once, paired with cyclophosphamide, later met saccharin alone after an antigen, and their antibody response was suppressed; animals re-exposed to saccharin died (Ader and Cohen, Psychosomatic Medicine 1975, at its title and the field's account).

**Rhythm**

P3. *(29 · 37)* Rhesus monkeys with hypothalamic lesions, making no GnRH of their own: hourly GnRH pulses restored the pituitary's LH and FSH; the same GnRH continuously raised them briefly and then suppressed them; hourly pulses again restored them (Belchetz, Knobil and colleagues, Science 1978, at its title and the field's account). Medicine gives pulsed GnRH to bring on ovulation and continuous GnRH agonists to suppress testosterone (the field's account).

P4. *(23 · 37)* Parathyroid hormone given once daily builds bone; kept high continuously, as in hyperparathyroidism, it removes bone (the field's account).

P5. *(37)* Repeated opioid doses bring tolerance; stopping brings withdrawal with signs opposite to the drug's (the field's account).

P6. *(37)* Rats given heroin repeatedly in one setting, then a large dose, died about half as often when it came in the same setting as in a new one, and heroin-naive rats nearly all died, in the field's account about 32%, 64% and 96% (Siegel and colleagues, Science 1982, at its title; figures not fetched this session).

**Three ways of meeting one receptor**

P7. *(7 · 23)* Receptors and single ion channels open and close all or none with no agonist present, the field's constitutive activity; mutant receptors held at the active face cause disease, TSH-receptor mutations and hyperthyroidism among them (the field's account).

P8. *(7 · 23)* At delta-opioid receptors some antagonists lowered basal activity below baseline, the field's negative intrinsic activity, and others blocked without changing it (Costa and Herz, PNAS 1989, at its title and the field's account).

P9. *(23)* In cells never given morphine, naloxone and naltrexone act as neutral antagonists; in morphine-dependent cells as inverse agonists, precipitating withdrawal, while 6β-naltrexol stays neutral (Wang and colleagues, Journal of Neurochemistry 2001, at its title and the field's account); naltrexone is reported neutral in both states (Divin and colleagues, British Journal of Pharmacology 2009, at its title).

P10. *(23)* One ligand acts as agonist in one system and inverse agonist in another as the receptors' own basal activity differs, the field's protean agonism (Kenakin, FASEB Journal 2001, at its title and the field's account).

**What medicine delivers**

P11. *(23 · 37)* Medicine delivers living societies in blood and organ transplants, bone marrow and stem-cell transplants, faecal microbiota transplants, engineered CAR-T cells, live vaccines, phages, maggots and leeches (the field's accounts).

P12. *(17 · 23)* An mRNA vaccine delivers a sequence; the receiving cells make the protein (the field's account).

**Probes and side effects**

P13. *(23 · 31 · 37)* A cosyntropin pulse followed by no cortisol rise where the adrenal fails; dobutamine stress echocardiography showing wall segments failing to move where the heart is ischaemic; the dexamethasone suppression test not suppressing where an adrenal axis runs autonomously; a naloxone challenge precipitating withdrawal where dependence is carried; ajmaline drawing out the Brugada ECG pattern in hearts hiding it at rest (European Heart Journal, at its title); edrophonium briefly restoring strength in myasthenia gravis; FDG, a glucose-shaped tracer, gathering where cells turn fastest (the field's accounts).

P14. *(23 · 37)* Children with infectious mononucleosis given amoxicillin had a rash at 29.5%, and 23% had a rash with no antibiotic (Chovel-Sella and colleagues, Pediatrics 2013, at its title and the field's account).

## Enantiomers (E)

E1. *(7 · 23 · 37)* Thalidomide's two hands interconvert, with a half-life near twelve hours in buffer and near one hour in serum; only the (S) hand is reported teratogenic, and animal studies with the (R) hand showed no harm despite its ready interconversion, the field's *thalidomide paradox*; the paper proposes the self-disproportionation of enantiomers, the two hands meeting as pairs apart from the single hand (Scientific Reports 2018, "Understanding the Thalidomide Chirality in Biological Processes by the Self-disproportionation of Enantiomers", at passages).

E3. *(7 · 23 · 37)* Ethambutol's (S,S) hand is about 500 times more potent against tuberculosis than its (R,R) hand and 12 times more than the meso form; all three are equally potent at its major side effect, optic neuritis (the field's account, at an encyclopedia page).

E4. *(7 · 37)* (R)-carvone smells of spearmint and (S)-carvone of caraway (the field's account, not fetched this session).

## Shards at their scales, and persisters (S, B)

S1. *(23 · 37)* Fibres longer than about 20 micrometres, asbestos and long carbon nanotubes among them, are incompletely engulfed by macrophages, the field's *frustrated phagocytosis*, with continuing release of inflammatory mediators, inflammation, fibrosis and mesothelioma; short fibres are engulfed and cleared (Oberdörster, 2000, in Particle and Fibre Toxicology 8:15, 2011, at passages).

B1. *(23 · 43)* Watched cell by cell in microfluidic channels, the *Escherichia coli* cells surviving ampicillin, the field's persisters, were cells already not growing, or growing slowly, before the antibiotic arrived; after it was removed they grew again, and their offspring were as susceptible as the rest; persisters formed in a population's resting phase and also arose continuously during growth, the field's type I and type II (Balaban, Merrin, Chait, Kowalik and Leibler, Science 305:1622, 2004, at its title and the field's account).

B2. *(23 · 43)* A bacterial population meeting an antibiotic the field names bactericidal is released fast at first and then slowly, a small fraction surviving long, the field's biphasic killing curve (the field's account).

B3. *(7 · 23)* Persisters are released by aminoglycosides, the field's *killing*, when these are given with certain metabolites that restart the cells' proton-motive force (Allison, Brynildsen and Collins, Nature 2011, the field's account, not fetched this session).

## Lithium (Li)

Li1. *(5 · 37)* Lithium salts calmed mania (Cade, Medical Journal of Australia 1949, the field's account); some seventy-five years on, the field names many candidate places lithium meets, inositol monophosphatase and GSK-3 among them, and no one of them as the whole of its effect (the field's account, not fetched this session).

Li2. *(23 · 37)* Neurons grown from the stem cells of people with bipolar disorder fired hyperactively and showed mitochondrial abnormalities; lithium reversed the hyperexcitability only in neurons from people who respond to lithium (Mertens and colleagues, Nature 527:95, 2015, at its abstract).

Li3. *(37 · 43)* Across randomized trials in mood disorders, lithium against placebo went with fewer suicides, odds ratio 0.13 (0.03 to 0.66), and fewer deaths from any cause, 0.38 (Cipriani and colleagues, BMJ 2013, at its abstract); a later review of clinical trials in adults is titled on the same question (BJPsych Open 2022, at its title, its finding not fetched this session).

Li6. *(below 5 · 5 · 37)* In rats made hyperactive with ketamine, lithium-6 suppressed the hyperactivity longer than lithium-7, the two isotopes differing only at the nucleus, their nuclear spins one and three halves (Ettenberg and colleagues, Pharmacology Biochemistry and Behavior 2020, at a later paper's account and its title); a paper proposes pairs of radicals whose recombination the lithium nucleus's spin changes (Zadeh-Haghighi and Simon, Scientific Reports 2021, at passages).

Z2. *(11 · 23 · 37)* Many older people carry abundant plaques and tangles and show no dementia, and about a third of cognitively normal older adults show amyloid on PET; across people the loss of synapses follows cognitive decline more closely than plaques do (the field's account, not fetched this session).

Z3. *(11 · 23 · 37)* Mice and worms expressing amyloid-β survived lethal infection more often, its oligomers and fibrils entrapping the microbes, the field's *antimicrobial* and *protection*, and infection in the brain seeded plaques around them (Kumar, Moir, Tanzi and colleagues, Science Translational Medicine 2016, at a news account).

Z4. *(23 · 29 · 37)* In mouse models, light and sound flickering at 40 hertz entrained gamma rhythms, lowered amyloid and changed the microglia (Iaccarino, Tsai and colleagues, Nature 2016, at its title and the field's account); a later study reports failing to find the entrainment or the amyloid change (the field's account, not fetched this session).

## Paracetamol (Pa)

Pa1. *(7 · 37)* Paracetamol, in medicine since the 1890s, is taken more widely than any other pain medicine; the field has named it a weak inhibitor of cyclooxygenase, a central acting drug, and a drug of the cannabinoid and TRPV1 receptors, and holds none of these as its whole way (the field's account, not fetched this session).

Pa2. *(7 · 23)* In the nervous system paracetamol's metabolite p-aminophenol is joined to arachidonic acid by fatty acid amide hydrolase, making AM404 (Högestätt and colleagues, Journal of Biological Chemistry 2005, at its title); mice lacking TRPV1 lose paracetamol's relief at some pains (Mallet and colleagues, PLoS ONE 2010, at its title).

Pa3. *(7 · 23)* AM404 formed at the terminals of the neurons the field names pain-sensing meets their sodium channels NaV1.7 and NaV1.8 directly, the field's *block*, and the pain there is relieved (Maatuf and colleagues, PNAS 2025, at its title and a news account).

Pa4. *(37)* In 1,652 people with acute low back pain, regular paracetamol, paracetamol as needed and placebo gave median recovery at 17, 17 and 16 days, with no difference in pain, disability, function or sleep (Williams and colleagues, Lancet 2014, at a news account).

Pa5. *(37 · 43)* A single dose of 1,000 mg lowered people's felt empathy for others' physical and social pain, in two double-blind placebo-controlled experiments (Mischkowski, Crocker and Way, Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience 2016, at its abstract); paracetamol lowered reported hurt feelings from social rejection (DeWall and colleagues, Psychological Science 2010, at its title).

Pa6. *(7 · 23 · 31)* In the liver a small part of paracetamol becomes the reactive NAPQI, met and joined by glutathione as it forms; past a dose the glutathione is used up and NAPQI binds the liver cells' own proteins and the cells die, and N-acetylcysteine, restoring glutathione, prevents the injury when given early (the field's account, not fetched this session).

## Sham surgery (Sh)

Sh1. *(29 · 37)* In 180 people with osteoarthritis of the knee, arthroscopic lavage, arthroscopic debridement and a sham procedure of skin incisions alone improved alike over two years (Moseley and colleagues, New England Journal of Medicine 2002, at an encyclopedia's account and its title).

## Fever (Fv)

Fv1. *(37)* Desert iguanas infected with bacteria seek warmer places and raise their body temperature, a behavioural fever; kept at higher temperatures they survived more often, and given an antipyretic that prevented the fever they died more often (Kluger and colleagues, Science 1975 and 1976, at their titles and the field's account); fish and other ectotherms show the same behavioural fever (Reynolds and colleagues, Nature 1976, at its title).

Fv2. *(23 · 37)* In mouse airway cells the common-cold rhinovirus replicated better at 33 °C, the nose's temperature, than at 37 °C, and the difference lay mainly in the airway cells' own changing, the field's *innate response*, less at the cooler temperature; in cells lacking that changing the virus replicated alike at both (Foxman, Iwasaki and colleagues, PNAS 2015, at a news account).

Fv3. *(37)* In 700 critically ill patients with fever and suspected infection, paracetamol against placebo gave 23 and 22 ICU-free days and 28-day mortality of 13.9% and 13.7% (Young and colleagues, HEAT, New England Journal of Medicine 2015, at a journal-club account).

Fv4. *(37 · 43)* Fever-reducing drugs are estimated to raise yearly influenza cases by about 5% across North America, the fever-reduced shedding more virus and going out while contagious (Earn, Andrews and Bolker, Proceedings of the Royal Society B 2014, at a university account).

## Anaesthesia (A)

A1. *(7 · 23 · 37)* Anaesthetic potency across chemically unrelated agents follows their solubility in olive oil, the field's Meyer–Overton correlation (Meyer, 1899; Overton, 1901; the field's account); in homologous series potency stops rising past a chain length, the field's cutoff (the field's account).
