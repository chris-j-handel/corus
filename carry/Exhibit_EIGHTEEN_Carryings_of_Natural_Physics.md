### Metronomes on a board free to roll

- *Natural observation*: Two pendulum metronomes of about 94 g each, set on a light wooden board of about 58 g resting on two empty beverage cans free to roll, came into step, almost always in phase with a small phase difference; antiphase locking appeared only at altered arrangements, with the board's motion damped (the table wetted under the cans) or at a much higher ticking rate; seven metronomes on one platform were studied briefly; the table alone served for measuring the metronomes' frequency differences, and no separate fixed-table trial is reported ([Pantaleone, 2002, American Journal of Physics 70(10), DOI 10.1119/1.1501118](https://www.uaa.alaska.edu/academics/college-of-arts-and-sciences/departments/physics-and-astronomy/_documents/metro.pdf), read at its full text, the author's copy). Huygens in 1665 found two pendulum clocks on one board suspended on the backs of two chairs settling into antiphase after hundreds of swings (Goldsztein, Nadeau and Strogatz, arXiv 2008.02947, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.02947, a later paper's account of Huygens, read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: Pantaleone names the coupling as passing through the motion of the base, which moves in answer to both pendulums, and models each metronome as a van der Pol oscillator; in his theory "There is no synchronization for uncoupled oscillators"; he suggests that damping of the base favoured the antiphase locking of Huygens's clocks, whose base was very massive and whose frequency differences were much smaller.

### Arriving light extinguished within a medium

### Sideways light from air, and none from an even medium

- *Natural observation*: Scattering by particles much smaller than the wavelength is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the wavelength, blue scattered more than red, and "the diffuse sky seen in daytime is blue"; Rayleigh published two papers on the colour and polarization of skylight in 1871 and showed in 1899 that his equations applied to single molecules ([an encyclopedia's page, "Rayleigh scattering"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_scattering), read at an encyclopedia's page); as the field's account has it, the sky's sideways light is polarized at right angles to the Sun. ### A bubble of gas rising through a liquid, a cap and a ring

- *Natural observation*: The first motion of a sphere-shaped gas bubble from rest in a liquid has an acceleration "equal to twice that of gravity"; the bubble distorts to a mushroom shape and breaks into a small spherical-cap bubble above a large toroidal bubble, in theory and in experiments ([Walters and Davidson, 1963, Journal of Fluid Mechanics 17(3), 321–336](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/abs/initial-motion-of-a-gas-bubble-formed-in-an-inviscid-liquid/C15C747940B551DF31AC40FBA1DE8760), read at its abstract); in computations by a boundary-integral method a bubble at first spherical is penetrated from below by a "gravitationally driven liquid jet" and becomes a toroidal bubble with circulation, a vortex ring that rises and spreads with "rapid oscillations of the ring radius" ([Lundgren and Mansour, 1991, Journal of Fluid Mechanics 224, 177–196](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/abs/vortex-ring-bubbles/D7A750395438407AA8C14E682D4255F8), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: Walters and Davidson treat the liquid as inviscid and discuss the relation of the toroid to the distorted sphere; Lundgren and Mansour have gravity driving a jet of liquid upward through the bubble, a greater surface tension giving a broader jet and a surface tension "too large" giving a jet that "fails to penetrate", a circulation depending only weakly on surface tension, and a model equation of their own that explains the oscillations and the spreading of the ring.
