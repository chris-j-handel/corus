### Two expansion rates, one through a model

- *Natural observation*: The Local Distance Network, one analysis joining several distance methods weighted by their robustness, with Cepheids and the tip of the red-giant branch as primary indicators, the Milky Way, the Large Magellanic Cloud and NGC 4258 as anchors, and type Ia supernovae, surface-brightness fluctuations and megamasers in the Hubble flow, gave a local expansion rate of "H0 = 73.50 ± 0.81 km s−1 Mpc−1", 7.1σ from the 67.24 ± 0.35 got from the anisotropies of the cosmic microwave background and 5.0σ from the 68.51 ± 0.58 got from nucleosynthesis and baryon acoustic oscillations within flat ΛCDM ([H0DN Collaboration: S. Casertano and colleagues, 2026, Astronomy & Astrophysics 708, A166](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/240623/1/aa57993-25.pdf), read at its full text; the news account of 13 April 2026 by A. ### Fewer captures at gallium, and one explaining tested

- *Natural observation*: At the Baksan Experiment on Sterile Transitions a 3.414-megacurie chromium-51 neutrino source was set within two nested volumes of gallium and the production of germanium-71 was measured at the two distances; the rates were similar at both and "20-24% lower than expected, thus reaffirming the anomaly" ([V. Frekers and colleagues, 2022, arXiv 2109.11482, the publishing by its DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.232501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11482), read at its abstract). At MicroBooNE, a liquid-argon time projection chamber, data from two accelerator neutrino beams showed no evidence of muon-neutrino to electron-neutrino transitions and none of electron-neutrino disappearance ([MicroBooNE Collaboration, 2025, Nature 648, 64–69](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09757-7), read at its abstract, also at arXiv 2512.07159).

### A shower model missing a mean, describing a fluctuation

- *Natural observation*: At the Pierre Auger Observatory the number of muons in inclined air showers, zenith angles 62° to 80°, reconstructed with both the surface and the fluorescence detectors from 1 January 2004 to 31 December 2017, was measured at its mean and, for the first time, at its shower-to-shower fluctuations, from 281 events above 4×10^18 eV; the relative fluctuation at 10^19 eV is 0.102 ± 0.029 (stat.) ± 0.007 (syst.), within the range the hadronic interaction models give for proton and iron, and the models' mean number of muons is too low, reconciled with the measuring "by an increase in the average number of muons of 43%, 35%, and 26%" at QGSJET II-04, EPOS-LHC and Sibyll 2.3d ([Pierre Auger Collaboration, 2021, Physical Review Letters 126, 152002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07797), read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: The writers say the fluctuations agree with the air-shower simulations with no rescaling at any model, and that the muon deficit is compatible with "a small deficit at every stage of the shower that accumulates" along the shower's development "rather than a discrepancy in the first interaction"; the result constrains the hadronic interaction models at ultrahigh energies.

### Two hadron patterns, each naming no one account

- *Natural observation*: In high-multiplicity proton–proton collisions at 13 TeV and proton–lead collisions at 5.02 TeV, ALICE measured the elliptic flow of mesons (π±, K±, K0S) and of baryons (p + p̄, Λ + Λ̄) against transverse momentum and found a grouping of the baryons and of the mesons with "a significant splitting (~5σ) at intermediate pT in both p–Pb and pp collisions", the baryons flowing more strongly, as in heavy-ion collisions ([ALICE Collaboration, 2026, Nature Communications 17, 2585](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67795-1), read at its full text; the collaboration's news page, https://alice-collaboration.web.cern.ch/2026-ALICE-alice-sees-new-sign-primordial-plasma-proton-collisions, read at its full text). Schmookler, A. Schmidt, O. Hen, S. Gilad and colleagues, 2019, Nature 566, 354–358](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-0925-9), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: At the first, the writers say a model with hydrodynamically flowing partons and quark coalescence gives the grouping and the splitting, a model lacking coalescence does not, and the AMPT model fails even qualitatively; the results "provide evidence that the system created in high-multiplicity p–Pb and pp collisions includes a stage with hydrodynamically flowing partons, similar to the one observed in heavy-ion collisions", and the news page adds that discrepancies remain, tied to the modelling of the proton's substructure and the collisions' initial geometry. At the second, the writers say the EMC data are explained by a universal modification of nucleons in neutron–proton short-range correlated pairs, give a data-driven modification function, and say that in nuclei with many more neutrons than protons a proton is likelier than a neutron to be in such a pair.
