### Twenty-four weeks, from the second dose

**The file's sentences**: "waited twenty-four weeks past the last infusion so the antibodies had cleared the body, and stopped therapy again", and "interrupted twenty-four weeks after the antibodies cleared".

- *Natural observation*: in a trial's second part, twenty-eight people who had each had placebo, and whose virus had come again within twenty weeks of stopping therapy, were given two doses of two long-acting antibodies twenty weeks apart while on therapy, and therapy was stopped again; a report of the trial has "ART was stopped 24 weeks after the second bnAb dose, not after the antibodies had cleared" (the RIO trial, reported at a conference in 2025 and 2026; read at [one report of it](https://www.aidsmap.com/node/39937) and [another](https://i-base.info/htb/50530), its own publishing not opened).
- *Science explanation*: the twenty-four weeks were chosen for the antibodies to have left the body ahead of the stopping.

## Observings from science, each said as its publishing read has it · v386EOD

Each is from an earlier session's report read whole at this gathering. Its publishing was opened at this gathering, and the two lines say what the page read has: a part said *as the field's account has it* was reached at no page read, and a publishing not opened is named not opened.

### Inert pills said plainly to be inert

- *Natural observation*: Of 80 people with irritable bowel syndrome (70% women, mean age 47), 37 given pills openly described as "placebo pills made of an inert substance, like sugar pills" improved more over three weeks than 43 given no treatment with similar contact with providers: global improvement was 5.2 against 4.0 at day 11 and 5.0 against 3.9 at day 21, with lower symptom severity and more adequate relief at both days, and a trend in quality of life at day 21 (p = .08) ([Kaptchuk, Friedlander, Kelley, Sanchez, Kokkotou, Singer, Kowalczykowski, Miller, Kirsch and Lembo, 2010, PLoS ONE 5(12), e15591](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0015591), read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: The patients were told that such pills had been shown in clinical testing to bring improvement "through mind-body self-healing processes"; the writers only suggest what explains the effect, naming a believable rationale, a positive and realistic expectation, and the ritual of swallowing pills, and they call replication essential.

### A taste paired once with a drug

- *Natural observation*: Rats were conditioned to avoid a taste by pairing saccharin with cyclophosphamide, an immunosuppressive drug; three days later they were injected with sheep red blood cells, and conditioned rats given saccharin again had a weaker antibody response than placebo-treated rats, while nonconditioned rats were unaffected; pairing saccharin with lithium chloride, which does not suppress immunity, gave the taste aversion with no lowering of antibody; as the field's account has it, the pairing was made once, and in earlier work some conditioned animals given saccharin again died ([Ader and Cohen, 1975, Psychosomatic Medicine 37(4), 333–340](https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:1162023), read at its abstract, in an index record's paraphrase).
- *Science explanation*: The paper's title names it "Behaviorally Conditioned Immunosuppression": a taste paired with an immunosuppressing drug later brought a weakened antibody response with no drug given, the field's conditioned response.

### One hormone pulsed and continuous, opposite effects

- *Natural observation*: As the field's account has it, medicine gives pulsed GnRH to bring on ovulation and continuous GnRH agonists to suppress testosterone. Parathyroid hormone analogue injected once daily has "a net effect of stimulating new bone formation", and "chronically elevated PTH will deplete bone stores" (the field's account, read at an encyclopedia's page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teriparatide, which does not name hyperparathyroidism). In rhesus monkeys with hypothalamic lesions that abolish the pituitary's release of gonadotropic hormone, constant infusion of GnRH failed to restore sustained gonadotropin secretion, and the synthetic hormone given once per hour, "the physiological frequency of gonadotropin release in the monkeys", re-established it; opening continuous GnRH in lesioned animals already re-established on hourly GnRH could bring what the writers call "desensitization" or "down regulation" of the processes of gonadotropin release; as the field's account has it, the gonadotropins were LH and FSH, they rose briefly ahead of the suppression, and hourly pulses restored them once more ([Belchetz, Plant, Nakai, Keogh and Knobil, 1978, Science 202(4368), 631–633](https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:100883), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The writers attribute the difference "to the pattern of GnRH delivery rather than to the amounts of this hormone to which the cells of the pituitary are exposed"; for parathyroid hormone the encyclopedia's page says that "intermittent exposure to PTH will activate osteoblasts more than osteoclasts".

### Tolerance, withdrawal, and a dose in a new room

- *Natural observation*: As the field's account has it, repeated opioid doses bring tolerance, and stopping brings withdrawal with effects opposite to the drug's. In animal experiments made to test a model of "overdose" deaths among heroin addicts, heroin-induced mortality was affected by drug-associated environmental cues as well as by pharmacological factors; in Siegel's own later account (2017, read at https://scroll.in/pulse/842652/the-heroin-overdose-mystery-why-where-an-addict-shoots-up-matters), rats given a drug on a number of occasions and then a last dose in a new place died at about twice the rate of rats given it in the usual place; the figures of about 32% in the same setting, 64% in a new one and 96% in heroin-naive rats are as the field's account has it ([Siegel, Hinson, Krank and McCully, 1982, Science 216(4544), 436–437](https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:7200260), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The writers propose that conditioned "drug-anticipatory responses", in addition to pharmacological factors, affect heroin-induced mortality; in Siegel's later account animals learn drug-preparatory responses to cues linked with a drug, the responses reduce the drug's effect, and with those cues absent the tolerance is absent.

### Receptors active with no agonist present

- *Natural observation*: As the field's account has it, receptors and single ion channels open and close all or none with no agonist present, and mutant receptors fixed at the active side cause disease; an encyclopedia's page on the thyrotropin receptor lists reports of gain-of-function mutations with nonautoimmune hyperthyroidism and with hyperfunctioning thyroid adenomas (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyrotropin_receptor). At "delta opioid receptors in membranes of NG108-15 neuroblastoma-glioma cells", two types of competitive antagonist were distinguished: one neither stimulated nor inhibited the GTPase activity of G proteins, and its apparent affinity for the receptor was unchanged by uncoupling of receptor and G proteins with pertussis toxin; the other inhibited GTPase, and its affinity for receptors rose after the uncoupling ([Costa and Herz, 1989, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 86(19), 7321–7325](https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:2552439), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The writers name the second type's effect "negative intrinsic activity" and say that such antagonists "may be a general feature of several classes of neurotransmitters or hormone receptors"; an encyclopedia's page names a receptor's activity with no ligand present its constitutive or basal activity, and a ligand lowering it below the basal level an inverse agonist (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_agonist).

### One ligand neutral in one tissue, inverse in another

- *Natural observation*: In C6 glioma and HEK293 cells expressing mu-opioid receptors, naltrexone, 6β-naltrexol and naloxone "were indistinguishable to the mu-opioid receptor in the opioid-naïve or dependent state and acted as neutral antagonists", and all the antagonists tested precipitated the same degree of cAMP overshoot in opioid-dependent cells (Divin, Bradbury, Carroll and Traynor, 2009, British Journal of Pharmacology 156(7), 1044–1053, read at its abstract, https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:19220294). An earlier report differs: the mu opioid receptor shows spontaneous, agonist-independent G protein coupling in vitro; naloxone and naltrexone were neutral antagonists in untreated cells, "whereas inverse agonistic effects became apparent only after morphine pretreatment"; 6α- and 6β-naltrexol, the naloxols and 6β-naltrexamine were neutral with or without morphine pretreatment; and in mice made dependent on morphine, 6β-naltrexol brought much less withdrawal jumping than naloxone and naltrexone at doses that blocked morphine's pain relief ([Wang, Raehal, Bilsky and Sadée, 2001, Journal of Neurochemistry 77(6), 1590–1600](https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:11413242), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: Wang and colleagues say their result "supports the hypothesis that naloxone-induced withdrawal symptoms result at least in part from suppression of basal signaling activity of MOR in morphine-dependent animals"; Divin and colleagues conclude that forming a constitutively active mu-opioid receptor "is not a requirement" for adenylyl cyclase sensitization; Kenakin's review names ligands that behave as positive and as inverse agonists at one receptor "protean agonists" and gives as the mechanism "the selective microaffinity of ligands for different conformational states of the receptor" (Kenakin, 2001, The FASEB Journal 15(3), 598–611, read at its abstract, https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:11259378).

### Medicine's probes, and a rash with no antibiotic

- *Natural observation*: In primary adrenal failure "the cortisol will not rise during the cosyntropin stimulation test" (the field's account, read at an encyclopedia's page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACTH_stimulation_test); ajmaline, flecainide and procainamide, drugs blocking the sodium current, can "reveal a Type 1 Brugada pattern in susceptible people" who show no diagnostic ECG pattern at rest (the field's account, read at an encyclopedia's page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brugada_syndrome); as the field's account has it, dobutamine stress echocardiography shows wall segments failing to move at an ischaemic heart, dexamethasone does not suppress an adrenal axis acting autonomously, a naloxone challenge precipitates withdrawal at a carried dependence, edrophonium briefly restores strength in myasthenia gravis, and FDG, a glucose-shaped tracer, gathers at the cells changing fastest. Of 238 children in hospital with infectious mononucleosis confirmed by Epstein-Barr virus serology at two tertiary centres in Israel, reviewed in retrospect, 173 were given antibiotics; 57 (32.9%) of the treated had a rash during the illness against 15 (23.1%) of the untreated, a difference the writers give as P = .156, "not significant"; with amoxicillin the rash was at 29.5% (95% confidence interval 18.52 to 42.57), far below the 90% reported for ampicillin in the past ([Chovel-Sella, Ben-Tov, Lahav, Mor, Rudich, Paret and Reif, 2013, Pediatrics 131(5), e1424–e1427](https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:23589810), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The encyclopedia's page names the cosyntropin probe a "stimulation test"; the rash, first reported in the 1960s as "ampicillin rash" and then estimated at 80% to 100% of treated patients, is concluded by the writers to be "much lower than originally reported" with amoxicillin; they give no cause for the rash in the abstract.

### Thalidomide's two hands interconverting

- *Natural observation*: Thalidomide's two hands interconvert: the paper gives, from earlier work, about 12 hours in buffer and about 1 hour in serum for one part in two of a single hand to convert; the (S) hand alone was reported teratogenic by Blaschke and colleagues in 1979, and animal experiments with the (R) hand showed no teratogenic activity despite its ready racemization in the body; on stirring thalidomide of 20% enantiomeric excess in a solvent, the solution was enriched to as much as 98% enantiomeric excess, and the racemate dissolved in water at about 62.5 µg/mL against about 345 to 349 µg/mL for each single hand ([Tokunaga, Yamamoto, Ito and colleagues, with Shibata, 2018, Scientific Reports 8, 17131](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-35457-6), read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: The writers name the puzzle the "thalidomide paradox" and "disclose a hypothesis": a fraction of the thalidomide epimerizes in the body, the racemic part precipitates "in (R/S)-heterodimeric form" and is removed from biological processes, and the single hand remains in solution, the self-disproportionation of enantiomers; the enrichment was shown in water and buffer, and its happening in a living body was not tested.

### Three handed drugs and one handed smell

- *Natural observation*: Ibuprofen's (S) hand is "the more biologically active", and its (R) hand undergoes "a unidirectional chiral inversion" to the (S) hand in the body, by way of acyl-CoA synthetase, 2-arylpropionyl-CoA epimerase (alpha-methylacyl-CoA racemase) and a hydrolase; the page says ibuprofen is metabolized mainly in the liver and does not name the organ of the inversion (the field's account, read at an encyclopedia's page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibuprofen). Ethambutol's (+)-(S,S) hand "is 500 and 12 fold more potent than (−)-(R,R)-ethambutol and the meso-form respectively" against tuberculosis, and "all the three isomers are equipotent in terms of the major side-effect of the drug, optic neuritis" (the field's account, citing Sheldon, 1993, Chirotechnology, read at an encyclopedia's page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethambutol). (R)-(−)-carvone has the sweetish minty smell of spearmint leaves, and (S)-(+)-carvone gives caraway seeds their smell ([Leitereg and colleagues, 1971, Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry 19(4), 785–787](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carvone), read at an encyclopedia's page citing it).
- *Science explanation*: The encyclopedia's pages name (S)-ibuprofen the eutomer, the hand with the wanted activity, and say of carvone that "olfactory receptors must respond more strongly to one enantiomer than to the other"; none is given for ethambutol's equal potency at the eye.

### Long fibres, crystals and wear particles at cells

- *Natural observation*: Crystalline uric acid is named a danger signal to the NLRP3 inflammasome, with a part in gout (the field's account, read at an encyclopedia's page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLRP3); as the field's account has it, cholesterol crystals in arteries and silica in the lung likewise bring inflammation at the place they lodge. Wear particles "worn off the contact surface of the artificial ball and socket joint" bring resorption of living bone around an implant, which can lead to its loosening (the field's account, read at an encyclopedia's page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteolysis). Fibres long enough to be "incompletely engulfed by macrophages during clearance" are given as the more pathogenic, with "the sustained release of inflammatory mediators" possibly leading to inflammatory, fibrotic and carcinogenic outcomes; Oberdörster (2000) is cited as regarding "fibres > 20 μm long as being of sufficient length", and the paper's own cutoff is 15 μm; the paper proposes, after Donaldson and colleagues, that "particles and short fibres may be cleared through stomata" while long fibres are retained at the diaphragm with inflammation and fibrosis, and it concludes that the carbon nanotubes studied "conform to the 'fibre pathogenicity paradigm'", their harm depending on durability and on discrete long fibres ([Osmond-McLeod, Poland, Murphy and colleagues, 2011, Particle and Fibre Toxicology 8, 15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1743-8977-8-15), read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: The paper's background names it "so-called 'frustrated phagocytosis'", macrophages' clearance of long, biopersistent fibres succeeding only in part; the encyclopedia's page gives the bone loss at implants as following from the body's attempts to clear the wear debris.

### Persisters watched cell by cell

- *Natural observation*: Certain metabolic stimuli, glucose and pyruvate among them in the journal's summary, enabled aminoglycosides to kill persisters of Escherichia coli and of Staphylococcus aureus; the effect was specific to aminoglycosides, "does not rely on growth resumption", worked with and without air, treated biofilms of both bacteria, and improved treatment in a mouse model of chronic urinary tract infection (Allison, Brynildsen and Collins, 2011, Nature 473(7346), 216–220, read at its abstract, https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10069). Single cells of Escherichia coli were followed in microfluidic devices: persistence was linked to heterogeneity already present in the population ahead of the antibiotic, with phenotypic switching "between normally growing cells and persister cells having reduced growth rates", and cells regrown from persisters "remain sensitive to the antibiotic", unlike resistant mutants; as the field's account has it, the antibiotic was ampicillin, and such cells formed in a population's resting phase and also arose continuously during growth ([Balaban, Merrin, Chait, Kowalik and Leibler, 2004, Science 305(5690), 1622–1625](https://api.openalex.org/works/pmid:15308767), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: Balaban and colleagues explain persistence by "preexisting heterogeneity in bacterial populations", a phenotypic switch for which they give "a simple mathematical description"; Allison and colleagues give the killing as proceeding "by the generation of a proton-motive force which facilitates aminoglycoside uptake", the persisters being dormant and still "primed for metabolite uptake, central metabolism and respiration"; the two types of persister and the biphasic killing curve are as the field's account has it.

### Donor gut communities at recurrent infection, two trials

- *Natural observation*: Patients with recurrent Clostridium difficile infection received four days of vancomycin, bowel lavage and an infusion of donor faeces through a nasoduodenal tube; 13 of 16 (81%) were free of the diarrhoea without relapse at 10 weeks after the first infusion and 15 of 16 after a second infusion from another donor, against 4 of 13 (31%) with vancomycin alone and 3 of 13 (23%) with vancomycin and bowel lavage, and the trial was stopped after an interim analysis; after the infusion the patients' faecal bacterial diversity rose toward that of the healthy donors, with more Bacteroidetes and Clostridium clusters IV and XIVa and fewer Proteobacteria ([van Nood, Vrieze, Nieuwdorp, Fuentes, Zoetendal, de Vos, Visser, Kuijper, Bartelsman, Tijssen, Speelman, Dijkgraaf and Keller, 2013, New England Journal of Medicine 368, 407-415](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa1205037?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its abstract). In an unblinded noninferiority trial of 116 adults at three centres in Alberta, faecal microbiota given once in oral capsules left 96.2% (51 of 53) free of recurrent infection over 12 weeks, and given once by colonoscopy 96.2% (50 of 52) ([Kao and colleagues, 2017, JAMA 318, 1985-1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29183074/), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: None is given beside the observing; the first paper names the treatment an infusion of donor faeces and the second names it fecal microbiota transplantation.

### Fermented foods, and a child's own mitochondria

- *Natural observation*: Thirty-six healthy adults, 18 in each arm, ate either a diet high in fermented foods or a diet high in fibre for ten weeks; in the fermented-food arm gut microbial diversity rose and rose more with larger intake, and 19 of 93 measured inflammatory serum proteins fell, interleukin-6 among them; in the high-fibre arm diversity was unchanged across the cohort and none of the 19 changed ([Wastyk, Fragiadakis, Perelman, Dahan, Merrill, Yu, Topf, Gonzalez, Van Treuren, Han, Robinson, Elias, Sonnenburg, Gardner and Sonnenburg, 2021, Cell 184, 4137-4153](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.09.30.321448v2.full), read at its preprint's full text, the journal citation at its Crossref record). A paper titled "Autologous mitochondrial transplantation for dysfunction after ischemia-reperfusion injury" describes, in its record's one-line summary, "Intramyocardial injection of autologous mitochondria" ([Emani, Piekarski, Harrild, del Nido and McCully, 2017, Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery 154, 286-289](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.jtcvs.2017.02.018?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its title and its record's one-line summary); a later review by two of its writers says the first patients were children on ECMO after ischaemia and reperfusion of the heart muscle and that this experience "suggests low side effect profile with favorable efficacy in a small group of patients" ([Emani and McCully, 2018, Translational Pediatrics 7, 169-175](https://tp.amegroups.org/article/view/18598), read at its abstract); the mitochondria came from the children's own muscle and ventricular function improved, as the field's account has it.
- *Science explanation*: None is given beside the observing.

### Living things and sequences medicine delivers

- *Natural observation*: Medicine delivers living things in blood and organ transplants, bone marrow and stem-cell transplants, faecal microbiota transplants, engineered CAR-T cells, live vaccines, phages, maggots and leeches, and grafts of skin and cornea, as the field's account has it. A new copy of the SMN1 gene is delivered within adeno-associated virus serotype 9 capsids, as a single intravenous infusion, to children under two years of age with spinal muscular atrophy from mutations in both copies of SMN1; islets isolated from a donor pancreas are infused through the portal vein into the liver of people with type 1 diabetes; an mRNA vaccine delivers its sequence within lipid nanoparticles, the receiving cells, dendritic cells among them, read the mRNA at their ribosomes in the cytoplasm and make the encoded antigen, the body's genomic DNA is unaffected, and the body degrades the mRNA within a few days; the nanoparticle is about a hundred nanometres across, as the field's account has it (no publishing is named; read at encyclopedia pages: [Onasemnogene abeparvovec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onasemnogene_abeparvovec), [Islet cell transplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islet_cell_transplantation), [mRNA vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRNA_vaccine), [Lipid-based nanoparticle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid-based_nanoparticle)).
- *Science explanation*: None is given beside the observing; the encyclopedia describes the spinal muscular atrophy medicine as "providing a new copy of the survival of motor neuron (SMN) gene that produces the SMN protein", and describes lipid nanoparticles as entering cells by receptor-mediated endocytosis and releasing their RNA from acidified endosomes.

### Lithium at mania, and at responders' own neurons

- *Natural observation*: Cade reported "outstanding results" from lithium citrate or lithium carbonate in a small trial of patients with mania, dementia praecox or melancholia, the calming of mania the strongest of them ([Cade, 1949, Medical Journal of Australia 2(36), 349-352, "Lithium salts in the treatment of psychotic excitement"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cade), read at an encyclopedia's page). Hippocampal dentate gyrus granule-cell-like neurons grown from induced pluripotent stem cells of people with bipolar disorder showed mitochondrial abnormalities and hyperactive action-potential firing while young, and lithium reversed the hyperexcitability only in neurons from patients who themselves responded to lithium treatment ([Mertens, Wang, Kim, Yu, Pham, Yang, Zheng, Diffenderfer, Zhang, Soltani, Eames, Schafer, Boyer, Marchetto, Nurnberger, Calabrese, Oedegaard, McCarthy, Zandi, Alda, Nievergelt, the Pharmacogenomics of Bipolar Disorder Study, Mi, Brennand, Kelsoe, Gage and Yao, 2015, Nature 527, 95-99](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature15526), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The encyclopedia's page on the medicine states "Lithium's mechanism of action is not known" and lists glycogen synthase kinase 3β, inositol monophosphatase, pAp-phosphatase, the myo-inositol transporter, NMDA receptors and cyclic AMP signalling among the proposed targets ([Lithium (medication)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_%28medication%29), read at an encyclopedia's page); the Nature paper's writers propose that "hyperexcitability is one early endophenotype of bipolar disorder".

### Lithium and fewer suicides, at trials and in water

- *Natural observation*: Across 48 randomised trials with 6,674 participants with mood disorders, lithium against placebo went with fewer suicides, odds ratio 0.13 (0.03 to 0.66), and fewer deaths from any cause, 0.38 (0.15 to 0.95), and with no clear benefit for deliberate self-harm, 0.60 (0.27 to 1.32) ([Cipriani, Hawton, Stockton and Geddes, 2013, BMJ 346, f3646](https://www.bmj.com/content/346/bmj.f3646), read at its abstract); a later review of 12 randomised trials with 2,578 participants found an odds ratio for suicide of 0.41 (0.03 to 2.49) and called the evidence "inconclusive" ([Nabi, Stansfeld, Plöderl, Wood and Moncrieff, 2022, Epidemiology and Psychiatric Sciences 31, e65](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/36382E3353168D08CD9B568BF4C46F85/core-reader), read at its abstract), and a review of clinical trials in adults is titled on the same question (Riblet and colleagues, BJPsych Open 2022, not opened). Across 15 ecological studies, places with more naturally occurring lithium in public drinking water had lower suicide mortality, pooled standardised regression coefficient -0.27 (-0.47 to -0.08), -0.13 (-0.24 to -0.02) for women and -0.26 (-0.56 to 0.03) for men, with high heterogeneity; the studies covered 1,286 regions, counties and cities in seven countries at trace amounts far below medicine's, as the field's account has it ([Memon, Rogers, Fitzsimmons, Carter, Strawbridge, Hidalgo-Mazzei and Young, 2020, British Journal of Psychiatry 217, 667-678](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/en/publications/association-between-naturally-occurring-lithium-in-drinking-water/), read at its abstract). Maintenance blood levels of lithium lie near 0.6 to 1.0 mmol/L and levels above 1.5 mmol/L are toxic ([Lithium (medication)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_%28medication%29), read at an encyclopedia's page).
- *Science explanation*: The BMJ review's writers suggest that lithium may lower suicide partly by reducing relapse of the mood disorder and partly by decreasing aggression and possibly impulsivity; the drinking-water review's writers call the association protective at the population level and name the ecological design and its fallacy as limits; the encyclopedia names the closeness of the two blood levels a narrow therapeutic index.

### Two lithium isotopes parting at a living animal

- *Natural observation*: Male Sprague-Dawley rats, 78 analysed, ate lithium-6, lithium-7 or naturally occurring lithium at 2.0 mEq/kg each day in a food mash and were made hyperactive with ketamine at 25 mg/kg; on the first trial the three lithium groups lowered the hyperactivity alike, and by the fourth trial "Li-6 animals exhibited significantly greater and more prolonged reductions in hyperactivity compared to either Li-7 and Li", the two isotopes differing at the nucleus alone, with nuclear spins 1 for lithium-6 and 3/2 for lithium-7 ([Ettenberg, Ayala, Krug, Collins, Mayes and Fisher, 2020, Pharmacology Biochemistry and Behavior 190, 172875](https://www.kitp.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/users/mpaf/Ettenberg%20et%20al%202020.pdf), read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: The experiment's writers call the mechanism unclear and mention a difference of nuclear spin as one idea; a later paper proposes ("here we propose") a naturally occurring radical pair of a flavin semiquinone and superoxide, its electron spins entangled, whose recombination the lithium nucleus's spin changes, and calls this a hypothesis open to experimental test ([Zadeh-Haghighi and Simon, 2021, Scientific Reports 11, 12121](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-91388-9), read at its full text).

### An antidepressant's hours and its weeks

- *Natural observation*: Monoaminergic antidepressants, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors among them, have "a delayed onset of action of at least a week" ([Pharmacology of antidepressants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology_of_antidepressants), read at an encyclopedia's page). In mice whose hippocampal neurogenesis was prevented by x-irradiation, the behavioural effects of antidepressants were blocked, and the drugs were fluoxetine and imipramine as the field's account has it ([Santarelli, Saxe, Gross, Surget, Battaglia, Dulawa, Weisstaub, Lee, Duman, Arancio, Belzung and Hen, 2003, Science 301, 805-809](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1126/science.1083328?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its title and its record's one-line summary); new neurons of the adult hippocampus need "approximately three to six weeks to mature" ([Adult neurogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_neurogenesis), read at an encyclopedia's page). Chronic fluoxetine reinstated ocular dominance plasticity in the visual cortex of adult animals, rats as the field's account has it, and promoted recovery of visual function in adult amblyopic animals, shown by electrophysiological and behavioural tests ([Maya Vetencourt, Sale, Viegi, Baroncelli, De Pasquale, O'Leary, Castrén and Maffei, 2008, Science 320, 385-388](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1126/science.1150516?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its title and its record's one-line summary).
- *Science explanation*: The 2003 paper's title names a "Requirement of Hippocampal Neurogenesis for the Behavioral Effects of Antidepressants" and its summary says "disrupting antidepressant-induced neurogenesis blocks behavioral responses to antidepressants"; the encyclopedia notes that the weeks of maturation are about the length of the delay of the drugs' effect, and says of people that "The role of new neurons in human adult brain function thus remains unclear".

### Ketamine's relief within a day

- *Natural observation*: About 70 percent of treatment-resistant patients improved dramatically within a day of a single dose of ketamine in earlier studies of major depression and bipolar depression; in rats a single low dose raised synapse-associated proteins in the prefrontal cortex within 2 hours and raised neuronal spines, budding synapses, within 24 hours, depression-like behaviour of stressed rats was rapidly reversed, and the effects lasted at least a week ([Li, Lee, Liu, Banasr, Dwyer, Iwata, Li, Aghajanian and Duman, 2010, Science 329, 959-964](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/news/science-updates/2010/rapid-antidepressant-works-by-boosting-brains-connections), read at a National Institute of Mental Health account).
- *Science explanation*: Ketamine rapidly activated the enzyme mTOR, which makes the proteins that form synapses, and rapamycin, which blocks mTOR, blocked ketamine's effects; the paper's title names "mTOR-dependent synapse formation" as underlying the rapid antidepressant effects of NMDA antagonists.

### Amyloid cleared, decline slowed by a quarter

- *Natural observation*: In 1,795 people with early Alzheimer's disease, 898 on lecanemab and 897 on placebo, the Clinical Dementia Rating sum of boxes worsened over 18 months by 1.21 points with the drug and 1.66 with placebo, a difference of -0.45 (-0.67 to -0.23), which is 27% of the placebo change; in a substudy of 698 participants brain amyloid fell by 59.1 centiloids more with the drug (-62.6 to -55.6); infusion-related reactions came in 26.4% and amyloid-related imaging abnormalities with oedema or effusions in 12.6% of those on the drug ([van Dyck, Swanson, Aisen, Bateman, Chen, Gee, Kanekiyo, Li, Reyderman, Cohen, Froelich, Katayama, Sabbagh, Vellas, Watson, Dhadda, Irizarry, Kramer and Iwatsubo, 2023, New England Journal of Medicine 388, 9-21](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa2212948?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its abstract, the volume and pages at its Crossref record). Among 2,914 people with normal cognition, amyloid pathology by PET or cerebrospinal fluid rose from 10% at age 50 to 44% at age 90 ([Jansen and colleagues, 2015, JAMA 313, 1924-1938](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1001/jama.2015.4668?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its abstract). Many older people carry abundant plaques and tangles and show no dementia, and across people the loss of synapses follows cognitive decline more closely than plaques do, as the field's account has it.
- *Science explanation*: The trial's writers say lecanemab "reduced markers of amyloid in early Alzheimer's disease" with moderately less decline than placebo and with adverse events, and that longer trials are warranted; the swelling is the trial's amyloid-related imaging abnormalities with oedema or effusions.

### Amyloid-β entrapping microbes, and a 40 hertz flicker

- *Natural observation*: Expression of amyloid-β protected against fungal and bacterial infection in mouse, nematode and cell-culture models of Alzheimer's disease; its soluble oligomers bound carbohydrates of the microbial cell wall, its protofibrils inhibited adhesion of the pathogen to host cells and its fibrils agglutinated and entrapped the microbes, and Salmonella Typhimurium infecting the brains of 5XFAD mice seeded and accelerated amyloid-β deposition that colocalised with the bacteria ([Kumar, Choi, Washicosky, Eimer, Tucker, Ghofrani, Lefkowitz, McColl, Goldstein, Tanzi and Moir, 2016, Science Translational Medicine 8, 340ra72](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1126/scitranslmed.aaf1059?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its abstract). In 5XFAD mice, optogenetic driving of fast-spiking parvalbumin interneurons at 40 hertz in the hippocampus lowered amyloid-β 1-40 and 1-42, and a light flickering at 40 hertz lowered both in the visual cortex of pre-depositing mice and lessened plaque load in aged mice, with microglia changing form and colocalising more with amyloid-β; flicker left amyloid-β unchanged in hippocampus and barrel cortex, and the paper has no sound stimulation ([Iaccarino, Singer, Martorell and colleagues, 2016, Nature 540, 230-235](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature20587), read at its abstract and figure legends); a later paper is titled "Forty-hertz light stimulation does not entrain native gamma oscillations in Alzheimer's disease model mice", and readers of it report no reliable lowering of amyloid load across its two mouse models ([Soula, Martín-Ávila, Zhang, Dhingra, Nitzan, Sadowski, Gan and Buzsáki, 2023, Nature Neuroscience 26, 570-578](https://alzforum.org/papers/forty-hertz-light-stimulation-does-not-entrain-native-gamma-oscillations-alzheimers-disease), read at its title and a field site's comments).
- *Science explanation*: The 2016 infection paper's writers suggest that amyloid-β may play a protective part in innate immunity with a "dual protective/damaging role" like other antimicrobial peptides, and that infection or sterile inflammation may drive amyloidosis; the Nature paper's writers conclude that gamma rhythms recruit "both neuronal and glial responses" against the pathology.

### A shingles vaccine and fewer dementia diagnoses

- *Natural observation*: In Wales, eligibility for the live-attenuated shingles vaccine was set by date of birth, people born on or after 2 September 1933 being eligible, and uptake rose from 0.01% just before that date to 47.2% just after it; among 282,541 older adults compared across the date, receiving the vaccine lowered the probability of a new dementia diagnosis over seven years by 3.5 percentage points (0.6 to 7.1), a relative fall of 20.0% (6.5 to 33.4), with a stronger effect among women than men ([Eyting, Xie, Michalik, Heß, Chung and colleagues, last-named Geldsetzer, 2025, Nature 641, 438-446](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08800-x), read at its full text); a follow-up with the same Welsh design reports fewer diagnoses of mild cognitive impairment and fewer deaths due to dementia among people already living with dementia ([Xie, Eyting, Bommer, Ahmed and Geldsetzer, 2025, Cell 188, 7049-7064](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/182992), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The Nature paper's writers name the design a natural experiment and discuss two ways the vaccine could act, fewer reactivations of varicella zoster virus and an immunomodulatory effect independent of that virus, calling both plausible and their evidence suggestive only; the follow-up concludes that the vaccination "prevents or delays mild cognitive impairment and dementia and slows the disease course".

### Paracetamol joined to the body's own fatty acid

- *Natural observation*: Paracetamol was first tried on humans by von Mering in 1887, with clinical results published in 1893, was discarded for phenacetin, was readopted after 1948 work, and is "the most commonly used medication for pain and fever in both the United States and Europe" ([Paracetamol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracetamol), read at an encyclopedia's page). In the brain and spinal cord paracetamol is deacetylated to its primary amine, which is joined to arachidonic acid to make AM404, a potent TRPV1 agonist, and this conjugation is absent in mice lacking fatty acid amide hydrolase ([Högestätt, Jönsson, Ermund, Andersson, Björk, Alexander, Cravatt, Basbaum and Zygmunt, 2005, Journal of Biological Chemistry 280, 31405-31412](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1074/jbc.M501489200?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its abstract); oral paracetamol's pain relief in the formalin, tail immersion and von Frey tests was absent in mice lacking TRPV1 and in mice lacking the hydrolase, and a TRPV1 blocker given into the brain's ventricles abolished the relief in the formalin test ([Mallet, Barrière, Ermund, Jönsson, Eschalier, Zygmunt and Högestätt, 2010, PLoS ONE 5](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0012748?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,journal,tldr), read at its abstract); primary sensory neurons of rats made AM404 from 4-aminophenol, AM404 inhibited the sodium channels NaV1.7 and NaV1.8 at nanomolar concentrations through the local anaesthetic binding site and inhibited evoked firing independently of TRPV1, paracetamol itself and its other metabolites did not, and AM404 injected into the rat's paw raised withdrawal thresholds and lowered inflammatory pain ([Maatuf, Kushnir, Nemirovski, Ghantous, Iskimov, Binshtok and Priel, 2025, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 122(23), e2413811122](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12168006/), read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: The encyclopedia says paracetamol "appears to exert its effects through two mechanisms", inhibition of cyclooxygenase and the metabolite AM404 acting on cannabinoid receptors and TRPV1; the 2010 writers conclude that "TRPV1 in brain is involved in the antinociceptive action of acetaminophen"; the 2025 writers conclude that peripheral inhibition of sodium channels by AM404 likely mediates much of the pain relief and describe AM404 as a possible "selective local analgesic".

### Paracetamol at the back, and at felt empathy

- *Natural observation*: In 1,643 people in Australia with acute low back pain, regular paracetamol at 3,990 mg a day, paracetamol as needed up to 4,000 mg a day and placebo, for up to four weeks, gave median recovery at 17, 17 and 16 days, the hazard ratio for as-needed paracetamol against placebo 1.05 (0.92 to 1.19); 1,652 were randomised and pain, disability, function and sleep did not differ, as the field's account has it ([Williams, Maher, Latimer, McLachlan, Hancock, Day and Lin, 2014, Lancet 384, 1586-1596](https://www.hcplive.com/view/acetaminophen-no-more-effective-than-placebo-for-acute-back-pain), read at a news account, the citation at its OpenAlex record). A single dose of 1,000 mg lowered people's empathy for others' physical and social pain in two randomised double-blind placebo-controlled experiments with 80 and 114 undergraduates, a pooled analysis giving falls in perceived pain (Hedges' g 0.23), personal distress (0.47) and empathic concern (0.45), with mood unchanged ([Mischkowski, Crocker and Way, 2016, Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience 11, 1345-1353](https://academic.oup.com/scan/article/11/9/1345/2224135), read at its full text). In two experiments with paracetamol or placebo each day for three weeks, paracetamol lowered daily self-reported social pain, and during social rejection lowered responses of the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex and anterior insula on fMRI ([DeWall, MacDonald, Webster, Masten, Baumeister, Powell, Combs, Schurtz, Stillman, Tice and Eisenberger, 2010, Psychological Science 21, 931-937](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1177/0956797610374741), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The 2010 writers read their result as showing substantial overlap between social and physical pain; the 2016 writers say "acetaminophen reduces one's ability to feel another's pain" and that the mechanism is not established; in the noise-pain test alone, lower unpleasantness of one's own noise accounted for the lower empathy.

### Paracetamol in the liver, glutathione used up

- *Natural observation*: In the liver about 90% of a paracetamol dose passes to glucuronide and sulphate conjugation and about 10% is oxidized, mainly by cytochrome CYP 2E1, to the reactive NAPQI, which hepatic glutathione conjugates rapidly to non-toxic mercapturate and cysteine compounds excreted in urine; in overdose, usually above 150 mg/kg, glutathione is depleted, NAPQI binds the liver cells' own proteins, the mitochondrial ones above all, and the cells die by necrosis; a fall of about 70% in glutathione has been proposed as the limit past which the binding comes, and adducts found at one hour put that limit in dispute; N-acetylcysteine, ideally within the first 8 to 10 hours, prevents the injury and lowered mortality in overdose from 5% to 0.7%; an earlier paper is titled "Acetaminophen-induced hepatic necrosis. IV. Protective role of glutathione" (Mitchell, Jollow, Potter, Gillette and Brodie, 1973, volume 187, pages 211–217, of, as the field's account has it, the Journal of Pharmacology and Experimental Therapeutics, read at its title alone) ([Yoon, Babar, Choudhary, Kutner and Pyrsopoulos, 2016, Journal of Clinical and Translational Hepatology 4, 131–142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913076/), read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: Glutathione's conjugation of NAPQI removes it as it forms; N-acetylcysteine "replenishes and maintains hepatic GSH stores by providing cysteine", and the review adds that it may reduce NAPQI back to paracetamol.

### One drug in ten reaching approval

- *Natural observation*: Of drug programmes entering phase I from 2006 through 2015, 9.6% (n = 9,985) reached approval, and 5.1% (n = 3,163) in oncology, the lowest of 14 disease areas; the phase transition rates were 63.2% at phase I, 30.7% at phase II, 58.1% at phase III and 85.3% from filing to approval, and the report gives no split of failures into efficacy and safety ([Thomas, Burns, Audette, Carroll, Dow-Hygelund and Hay, June 2016, Clinical Development Success Rates 2006–2015, a report of BIO, Biomedtracker and Amplion](https://cdn.vanderbilt.edu/t2-main/medschool-prd/wp-content/uploads/sites/106/2025/05/BiomedtrackerPDF.pdf), read at its full text); of 218 reported failures between phase II and submission in 2013 to 2015, 174 of them with a stated reason, "lack of efficacy is the reason for approximately half of all phase II and phase III failures", and a share of a fifth to a quarter for safety in phase II is as the field's account has it ([Harrison, 2016, Nat. Rev. Drug Discov. 15, 817–818](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrd.2016.184), read at its opening text, its figures unread) (Hay and colleagues, 2014, not opened). Of 1,026 experimental treatments for acute stroke, the 114 used clinically were no more effective in experiments than the 912 tested in animal models alone; that none of them became a treatment, and that the acute stroke treatments that work reopen the blocked vessel, is as the field's account has it ([O'Collins, Macleod, Donnan, Horky, van der Worp and Howells, 2006, Annals of Neurology 59, 467–477](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16453316/), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: O'Collins and colleagues write that the finding raises doubt whether the most effective drugs are being chosen for stroke trials, which may in part explain the slow progress, and ask for greater rigour in the doing, reporting and analysis of animal studies; the report of Thomas and colleagues says its rates are "based on transition rates, not necessarily resulting from safety or efficacy data".

### Mouse and person, phenotypic screening, and falling yield

- *Natural observation*: In gene expression of blood samples from 167 trauma patients, 244 burn patients and four volunteers given endotoxin, the human conditions correlated with one another (trauma against burns R² = 0.91, endotoxaemia against the injuries R² = 0.47), and the orthologous genes of the matching mouse models correlated poorly with the human ones (R² ≤ 0.09, with 47 to 63% of genes changing in the same direction) and with one another (R² ≤ 0.13) ([Seok, Warren, Cuenca and colleagues, 2013, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 110, 3507–3512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3587220/), read at its full text); a re-evaluation of the same datasets, "focusing on genes whose expression levels were significantly changed in both humans and mice", found Spearman's rank correlations of 0.43 to 0.68 with 77 to 93% of genes changed in the same direction ([Takao and Miyakawa, 2015, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 112, 1167–1172, online in 2014](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1073/pnas.1401965111), read at its abstract). Of 75 first-in-class medicines with new molecular mechanisms among 259 agents approved by the US Food and Drug Administration from 1999 to 2008, 50 were small molecules, 28 of them from phenotypic screening and 17 from target-based approaches ([Swinney and Anthony, 2011, Nat. Rev. Drug Discov. 10, 507–519](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrd3480), read at its abstract). The number of new drugs approved per billion US dollars of research and development spending, inflation-adjusted, "has halved roughly every 9 years since 1950", a fall of about 80-fold ([Scannell, Blanckley, Boldon and Warrington, 2012, Nat. Rev. Drug Discov. 11, 191–200](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrd3681), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: Takao and Miyakawa compared the genes changed in both species alone; Swinney and Anthony suggest that a target-centric approach for first-in-class drugs, without regard to the best molecular mechanism of action, may add to attrition; Scannell and colleagues title the falling yield's figure "Eroom's Law in pharmaceutical R&D" and give four causes, the "better than the Beatles" problem, the "cautious regulator" problem, the "throw money at it" tendency and the "basic research–brute force" bias.

### Sham surgery improving alike with real surgery

- *Natural observation*: In 180 people with osteoarthritis of the knee, randomly assigned to arthroscopic débridement, arthroscopic lavage or placebo surgery of skin incisions and a simulated débridement with no arthroscope inserted, 165 completed 24 months, and at no point did either arthroscopy group report less pain or better function than the placebo group; at one year the mean knee-specific pain scores (0 to 100, higher being more pain) were 48.9 with placebo, 54.8 with lavage and 51.7 with débridement ([Moseley, O'Malley, Petersen, Menke, Brody, Kuykendall, Hollingsworth, Ashton and Wray, 2002, New England Journal of Medicine 347, 81–88](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:12110735&resultType=core&format=json), read at its abstract). Partial meniscectomy for degenerative tears and subacromial decompression of the shoulder doing no better than sham are as the field's account has it; of 53 randomised trials of surgery against a placebo procedure, the placebo arm improved in 39 (74%), the placebo's effect did not differ from surgery's in 27 (51%), and surgery was superior in 26 (49%), generally by a small margin; the review names ligation of the internal mammary artery for angina in its discussion, as an operation whose placebo-controlled trial changed practice ([Wartolowska, Judge, Hopewell, Collins, Dean, Rombach, Brindley, Savulescu, Beard and Carr, 2014, BMJ 348, g3253](https://www.bmj.com/content/348/bmj.g3253), read at its full text).
- *Science explanation*: The two publishings name the sham arm's improving a placebo effect; Moseley and colleagues conclude that the outcomes after lavage or débridement were "no better than those after a placebo procedure", and Wartolowska and colleagues that placebo-controlled trials are "a powerful, feasible way of showing the efficacy of surgical procedures".

### Stenting against a placebo procedure, two trials

- *Natural observation*: Of 230 people enrolled with angina and severe single-vessel coronary stenosis, after six weeks of medication optimisation 200 were randomised, 105 to stenting (PCI) and 95 to a placebo procedure; the gain in exercise time was 16.6 seconds greater with PCI (95% CI −8.9 to 42.0, p = 0.200), no significant difference; the patients being on about three anti-anginal drugs is as the field's account has it ([Al-Lamee, Thompson, Dehbi and colleagues, 2018, Lancet 391, 31–40](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29103656&resultType=core&format=json), read at its abstract). In 301 people with stable angina and objective evidence of ischaemia who had stopped all antianginal medication, 151 assigned to PCI and 150 to a placebo procedure, the mean angina symptom score at 12 weeks (scale 0 to 79, higher being worse) was 2.9 with PCI and 5.6 with placebo, odds ratio 2.21 (95% CI 1.41 to 3.47, P<0.001); three times as many becoming free of angina, and exercise time rising by about a minute more, are as the field's account has it ([Rajkumar, Foley, Ahmed-Jushuf and colleagues, 2023, New England Journal of Medicine 389, 2319–2330](https://keele-repository.worktribe.com/output/669498/a-placebo-controlled-trial-of-percutaneous-coronary-intervention-for-stable-angina), read at its abstract at a university repository).
- *Science explanation*: None is given beside the observing in the two abstracts; that the first therapy given seems to have the most effect is, as the field's account has it, the authors' note.

### Fever sought by lizards, a virus in a cool nose

- *Natural observation*: The desert iguana Dipsosaurus dorsalis develops a fever of about 2 °C after injection with the bacterium Aeromonas hydrophila; lizards infected with the live bacterium and placed at neutral, low or high ambient temperatures survived significantly more often at the fever-level temperature ([Kluger, Ringler and Anver, 1975, Science 188, 166–168](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1126/science.188.4184.166), read at its abstract); deaths rising under an antipyretic that prevented the fever are as the field's account has it (Bernheim and Kluger, 1976, Science, not opened). The lizard's fever after killed Aeromonas hydrophila is named a "behavioural fever" with survival value, and the paper's title has the same of teleost fishes ([Reynolds, Casterlin and Covert, 1976, Nature 259, 41–42](https://www.nature.com/articles/259041a0), read at its title and its opening paragraph). Most human rhinovirus isolates replicate better at the nasal cavity's 33–35 °C than at 37 °C; in mouse airway epithelial cells infected at 37 °C the antiviral response was stronger than at 33 °C, with more type I and type III interferon and interferon-stimulated gene expression; in cells lacking MAVS or the type I interferon receptor the virus replicated more, at 37 °C above all, and the temperature difference in its growth was partly removed ([Foxman, Storer, Fitzgerald, Wasik, Hou, Zhao, Turner, Pyle and Iwasaki, 2015, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 112, 827–832](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1073/pnas.1411030112), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: Kluger and colleagues conclude that a fever-level rise in body temperature after bacterial infection increases the host's survival; Foxman and colleagues conclude that the rhinovirus replicates better at nasal temperature in part through a weaker antiviral defence of the infected cells at the cool temperature, the interferon induction depending on MAVS, the adaptor of the RIG-I-like receptors.

### Fever brought down in the critically ill

- *Natural observation*: In 700 ICU patients with fever (38 °C or above) and known or suspected infection, 1 g of intravenous paracetamol each 6 hours against placebo gave a median of 23 and 22 ICU-free days to day 28 (P = 0.07), and deaths by day 90 in 55 of 345 (15.9%) and 57 of 344 (16.6%); 28-day mortality of 13.9% and 13.7% is as the field's account has it ([Young, Saxena, Bellomo and colleagues, 2015, New England Journal of Medicine 373, 2215–2224](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1508375), read at its abstract); the explanation line's estimate is of a model of published data ([Earn, Andrews and Bolker, 2014, Proceedings of the Royal Society B 281, 20132570](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1098/rspb.2013.2570), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: Young and colleagues conclude that early paracetamol for fever from probable infection did not change the number of ICU-free days. Earn, Andrews and Bolker, from published data they call incomplete and heterogeneous, estimate that fever suppression raises the expected number of influenza cases and deaths in the US, by 5% (95% CI 0.2–12.1%) for seasonal influenza and by 1% (95% CI 0.0–2.7%) for pandemic influenza, suppressed fever raising transmission; more virus shed and more going out while contagious are, as the field's account has it, the routes.

### Semaglutide at weight, alcohol and cigarettes

- *Natural observation*: In 1,961 adults without diabetes, with a body-mass index of 30 or more, or of 27 or more with a weight-related condition, semaglutide 2.4 mg once weekly for 68 weeks beside lifestyle intervention changed mean body weight by −14.9% against −2.4% on placebo ([Wilding, Batterham, Calanna, Davies and colleagues, 2021, New England Journal of Medicine 384, 989–1002](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2032183), read at its abstract); in 327 of them followed for a year after all treatment stopped at week 68, the semaglutide arm had lost 17.3%, regained 11.6 percentage points by week 120 and ended at a net loss of 5.6%, about two thirds of the loss regained, and the cardiometabolic improvements reverted towards baseline for most variables ([Wilding, Batterham, Davies, Van Gaal, Kandler and colleagues, 2022, Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism 24, 1553–1564](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1111/dom.14725), read at its abstract). In 48 non-treatment-seeking adults with alcohol use disorder, nine weeks of semaglutide (0.25 mg weekly for four weeks, 0.5 mg for four, 1.0 mg for one) against placebo lowered the alcohol drunk in a laboratory self-administration task (β = −0.48, 95% CI −0.85 to −0.11), the drinks per drinking day, the heavy drinking days and the weekly craving, and, in the subsample smoking cigarettes, the cigarettes per day; drinks per calendar day and the number of drinking days did not differ significantly ([Hendershot, Bremmer, Paladino, Kostantinis, Gilmore and colleagues, 2025, JAMA Psychiatry 82, issue 4, from page 395](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.4789), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: None is given beside the observing; the extension's writers conclude that the regain supports obesity as a chronic condition needing ongoing treatment for its gains to last.

### GLP-1 after a meal, minutes and a week

- *Natural observation*: GLP-1, a hormone of 30 amino acids, is made from proglucagon in the gut's endocrine L-cells and released at meals; it stimulates insulin secretion, inhibits glucagon secretion, slows gastrointestinal motility and regulates appetite and eating, and it is broken down rapidly by dipeptidyl peptidase IV ([Holst, 2007, Physiological Reviews 87, 1409–1439](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1152/physrev.00034.2006), read at its abstract); its release after a meal has "an early phase after 10–15 minutes" and "a longer second phase after 30–60 minutes", its active form lasts "approximately 2 minutes" in the circulation, and its insulin effect is glucose-dependent ([an encyclopedia's page on glucagon-like peptide-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucagon-like_peptide-1), read at an encyclopedia's page). Semaglutide, a GLP-1 analogue with two amino acid substitutions and a fatty acid moiety at lysine 26, was designed for once-weekly dosing by raising its albumin affinity and securing its stability against metabolic degradation; "The plasma half-life was 46.1 h in mini-pigs following i.v. administration", and a life of about a week in people's plasma is as the field's account has it ([Lau and colleagues, 2015, Journal of Medicinal Chemistry 58, 7370–7380](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1021/acs.jmedchem.5b00726), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The effects are laid to the GLP-1 receptor, on the pancreas's β cells and in the brainstem and hypothalamus, the encyclopedia's page not giving the number of receptor kinds; Holst adds that the rapid breakdown raises the possibility of part of the hormone's action passing by neural pathways.

### Siblings, farm dust and asthma

- *Natural observation*: Hay fever being less common in children with more older siblings is as the field's account has it ([Strachan, 1989, British Medical Journal 299, 1259–1260, titled "Hay fever, hygiene, and household size"](https://www.bmj.com/content/299/6710/1259), read at its title alone). Among 60 Amish and Hutterite children of similar genetic ancestry and lifestyle, the Amish on traditional farms and the Hutterites on industrialized ones, the prevalence of asthma was 4 times as low and of allergic sensitization 6 times as low in the Amish; median endotoxin in Amish house dust was 6.8 times as high, the dust's microbial composition differed, and the children's innate immune cells differed profoundly in proportions, phenotypes and functions; in a mouse model of allergic asthma, dust extracts from Amish homes given into the nose significantly inhibited airway hyperreactivity and eosinophilia, extracts from Hutterite homes did not, and the protection was abrogated in mice lacking MyD88 and Trif; asthma in about one in twenty Amish children and one in five Hutterite children is as the field's account has it ([Stein, Hrusch, Gozdz and colleagues, 2016, New England Journal of Medicine 375, 411–421](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1508749), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: Stein and colleagues conclude that "the Amish environment provides protection against asthma by engaging and shaping the innate immune response"; the field's hygiene hypothesis, as the field's account has it, comes from Strachan's paper, whose title has the word hygiene.

### Early peanut, and an infant gut's first bacteria

- *Natural observation*: 640 infants with severe eczema, egg allergy or both, aged at least 4 and under 11 months at entry, were randomly assigned to consume or to avoid peanuts to 60 months of age; among the 530 with a negative skin-prick test at entry, peanut allergy at 60 months was 13.7% with avoidance and 1.9% with consumption (P<0.001), and among the 98 with a wheal of 1 to 4 mm, 35.3% and 10.6% (P = 0.004) ([Du Toit, Roberts, Sayre, Bahnson and colleagues, 2015, New England Journal of Medicine 372, 803–813](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1414850), read at its abstract). As the field's account has it, in 222 infants of Finland, Estonia and Russian Karelia, Finnish and Estonian guts were dominated early by Bacteroides and Russian guts were otherwise, with autoimmunity and allergy more common in Finland than across the Russian border ([Vatanen, Kostic, d'Hennezel, Siljander, Franzosa and colleagues, 2016, Cell 165, 842–853](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.cell.2016.04.007), read at its title alone).
- *Science explanation*: Du Toit and colleagues conclude that the early introduction of peanuts lowered the frequency of peanut allergy and modulated the immune response to peanuts, peanut-specific IgG4 rising mainly in the consumption group. The second paper's title is "Variation in Microbiome LPS Immunogenicity Contributes to Autoimmunity in Humans"; as the field's account has it, Bacteroides lipopolysaccharide, structurally distinct from Escherichia coli's, brings less innate immune activation, and Escherichia coli's went with endotoxin tolerance and with mice free of autoimmune diabetes.

### Anaesthetic potency following oil solubility, and xenon

- *Natural observation*: Anaesthetic potency, measured in tadpoles, rises nearly linearly with the olive oil to water partition coefficient across alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, ethers and esters (Meyer, 1899, Archiv für experimentelle Pathologie und Pharmakologie 42, 109–118; Overton, 1901, Studien über die Narkose, Jena), and in homologous series potency ceases to rise past a chain length, about 13 carbons for the n-alcohols ([an encyclopedia's page on theories of general anaesthetic action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theories_of_general_anaesthetic_action), read at an encyclopedia's page). Xenon anaesthetizes animals and people, and its being a noble gas forming no ordinary bonds is as the field's account has it ([Cullen and Gross, 1951, Science 113, 580–582, titled "The Anesthetic Properties of Xenon in Animals and Human Beings, with Additional Observations on Krypton"](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1126/science.113.2942.580), read at its title alone). Firefly luciferase, "a pure soluble protein", is inhibited by 50% at anaesthetic concentrations "essentially identical to those which anaesthetize animals" over a 100,000-fold range of potencies, the anaesthetics competing with the substrate luciferin for binding ([Franks and Lieb, 1984, Nature 310, 599–601](https://www.nature.com/articles/310599a0), read at its abstract).
- *Science explanation*: The field names the Meyer–Overton correlation and its cutoff; its two accounts are action through the lipid about a cell and direct binding to a small number of protein targets; Franks and Lieb conclude that general anaesthetics, for all their chemical diversity, may act by competing with endogenous ligands for binding to specific receptors.

### Plants made still by anaesthetics, and a lag in waking

- *Natural observation*: Mimosa leaves, pea tendrils, Venus flytraps and sundew traps lost both their autonomous and their touch-induced movements under exposure to anaesthetics; in the Venus flytrap the loss came from the blocking of action potentials under diethyl ether, and the same ether concentration stilled pea tendrils; anaesthetics impeded cress seed germination and chlorophyll accumulation and affected endocytic vesicle recycling and the balance of reactive oxygen species in Arabidopsis root apex cells; the movements were followed before, under and after recovery from the anaesthetics, and the flytrap's recovery within about fifteen minutes is as the field's account has it ([Yokawa, Kagenishi, Pavlovič, Gall, Weiland, Mancuso and Baluška, 2018, Annals of Botany 122, 747–756, online in 2017](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1093/aob/mcx155), read at its abstract). In mice and fruit flies the induction of anaesthesia and the emergence from it follow different paths: for halothane the EC50 of induction is more than 2.5 times the EC50 of emergence in both species, with a smaller significant difference for isoflurane, and brain concentrations of both agents were higher at induction than at emergence ([Friedman, Sun, Moore and colleagues, 2010, PLoS ONE 5, e11903](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0011903), read at its full text). Tadpoles stilled by ethanol and moving again under high hydrostatic pressure are as the field's account has it (Johnson and Flagler, 1950, Science 112, 91–92, not opened). Of 31 pressure reversal papers reviewed, with the data of 8 re-plotted as dose-response curves, the studies differed in end-points, pressures, species and agents, and reversal appeared in some species alone and at certain pressures and anaesthetic concentrations ([a reappraisal, 2021, Clinical and Experimental Pharmacology and Physiology 48, 1454–1468, doi 10.1111/1440-1681.13554](https://www.ndcn.ox.ac.uk/publication/1187766), read at its abstract at a university's page).
- *Science explanation*: Friedman and colleagues name the hysteresis, "not fully explained by pharmacokinetics", "neural inertia", a resistance of the central nervous system to changing its behavioural state; mice lacking noradrenaline (Dbh knockout) emerged later, and Shaker mutant flies showed less of it. Yokawa and colleagues conclude that plants are sensitive to several anaesthetics of no structural similarity, action potentials, endocytic vesicle recycling and reactive oxygen species being the routes. The 2021 reappraisal concludes that "'pressure reversal' is not a universal phenomenon" and that pressure "cannot meaningfully be used to gain insight into anaesthetic mechanisms".
