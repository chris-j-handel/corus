## The universe said one thing, and said each thing, in the written record · v386EOD

Each publishing was opened at this gathering through a reader's fetching, at the page named, and a part not opened is said. Each is a saying in the written record, at the place it was read; none is a natural observation. They are beside this file's "The problem runs from the pre-Socratics through every metaphysics of parts and wholes."

**One thing.** Heraclitus, fragment B50: "it is wise to agree that all things are one", read at [the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/). Parmenides, fragment B8, in Burnet's English: "it has a furthest limit, it is complete on every side, like the mass of a rounded sphere", read at [a page of the fragment](https://lexundria.com/parm_frag/8/bnt). Plato, Timaeus, in Jowett's English: "one visible animal comprehending within itself all other animals" and "one only-begotten and created heaven", read at [a page of the dialogue](https://www.ellopos.net/elpenor/greek-texts/ancient-greece/plato/plato-timaeus.asp?pg=13). Aristotle, On the Heavens, the ninth chapter of its first book, in Stocks's English: "this heaven of ours is one and unique and complete", and "there is also no place or void or time outside the heaven", read at [a page of the work](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/heavens.mb.txt). And now, priority monism: "exactly one basic concrete object exists", Schaffer's wording at [the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/monism/), his paper of 2010 in The Philosophical Review having "the numerical thesis that there is exactly one basic object".

**Each thing.** Epicurus, Letter to Herodotus, in Hicks's English: "the whole of being consists of bodies and space" and "the sum of things is infinite", read at [a page of the letter](https://lexundria.com/epic_ep_hdt/0-49/hks). James, A Pluralistic Universe, 1909, its first lecture: "a distributive form of reality, the each-form, is logically as acceptable and empirically as probable as the all-form", read at [the book's text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11984/pg11984.html). Kant, Critique of Pure Reason, in Kemp Smith's English, of the world: "it exists in itself neither as an infinite whole nor as a finite whole", read at [a page of the work](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/humftp/E-text/wp/Kant/cpr.kemp-smith/17ant5-9.htm).

**A method of parts, said of the whole.** Smolin: the "attempt to scale up the Newtonian paradigm to the universe as a whole is to commit the cosmological fallacy", read at [a paper of his](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.8539); the book Time Reborn, 2013, with a chapter of that name, was not opened. Russell, "On the Notion of Cause", Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society 13, 1912–1913: "The law of causality, I believe, like much that passes muster among philosophers, is a relic of a bygone age", "surviving, like the monarchy, only because it is erroneously supposed to do no harm.", read at [a printing of it](https://cqi.inf.usi.ch/qic/Russellcause.pdf).

**Laid beside.** The earliest read is of about five centuries ahead of the common era: three forms of the universe as one existing thing, all things one, the one with a limit like a sphere, and the one comprehending all; and beside them, sayings of each thing, with none having the sentence they are sides of.
