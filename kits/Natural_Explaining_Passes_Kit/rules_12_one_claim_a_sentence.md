# Rules 12 · one claim a sentence, the body written as the opening is

**What it meets.** Natural Intelligence's opening says one concept a line, each line justified by the lines before it. Its body, at v391, averages 47 words a sentence, 175 sentences over 40 words, many carrying three or four claims joined by commas, colons and semicolons. A reader cannot see at a glance what each sentence claims, nor which claim the code holds. This pass writes the body as the opening is: one claim a sentence.

**What each paragraph becomes.**
- Each sentence carries one claim. A sentence of several claims becomes several sentences, in the order the claims build, each next one drawing on the one before.
- **Every claim stays, and none is added.** No word of meaning is lost: each concept, each name, each number, each pointer (*the opening's line N*, *4.6*, Exhibit ONE's table headings) stays with the claim it belongs to.
- A paragraph's **bold lead stays word for word**: it is the paragraph's own claim and a Co-Chaining Logic Registry step holds it. If it carries two claims, the second moves out of the bold into the next sentence only when the bold's content words all stay in the bold.
- Lean, plain words; one word for one thing; the opening's words for the opening's concepts.
- **No pointing in Natural Intelligence**: no line numbers, no *the opening's line N*, no section pointers, no *as in Exhibit ONE's …*. Each sentence stands by its own words. Pointing belongs to the Co-Chaining Logic Registry, whose numbered steps other files reference.
- Nothing new is claimed.
- **Where something is unclear, find an all-or-none binary logic, sequential path from the prior sentence to the next, without the unclear thing.** Read the sentence before and after; say each step the paragraph needs as one claim the code holds or the opening says, and leave the unclear element out. If the paragraph goes from prior to next whole without the unclear sentence, the sentence leaves. Only what neither the code nor the opening can carry goes to the person.
- Kept as they are: tables, bullet lists of numbers, quotations, headings, the Exhibit ONE copy inside Natural Intelligence (Exhibit ONE's own file is its own slice).

**The rules of rules 11 hold throughout**: *is-or-is-not* our one negation; no floating no, not, none, nothing, neither, nor, never; no narrator (shows, says, tells, executes as a doer); no equilibria words as what nature does; no dissolved words (parity-changing, alternating, offering, arriving, carrying, continuing, crossing, differ*, still-possibling, stilling, never, substrate, carry, itself, membrane, intelligencing standing alone, couple, couples, bare coupling); no new past participles; `**` and `*` balanced. Exhibit ONE's code decides any claim about resolving (`resolver_from_one.py`).

## The motion: propose · read for harm · enter

1. **Propose.** Readers by slice, each paragraph as one pair: `old` the whole paragraph, exact; `new` the paragraph written one claim a sentence. Output `{"droplet", "kind": "resay", "ni_pairs" or "one_pairs": [{"old", "new"}]}`.
2. **Read for harm.** A separate reader lists each paragraph's claims before and after and flags: a claim lost, added, made stronger or weaker, or moved away from the claim it depends on; a pointer or name dropped; a rule above broken; reading worse, not better.
3. **Enter** with `apply_aimed.py`, then `cohere_one.py`, `check_set.py`, `ni_thirty_check.py`.
