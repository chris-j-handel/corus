# Rules 11 · is-or-is-not, our one negation, and a reading for harm before entering

**What it meets.** *Is-or-is-not*, hyphenated, one thing, is the one negation natural intelligence and our naming need: a binary within a universal set, its complement taken there (Natural Intelligence's opening, line 6). Floating *no*, *not*, *none*, *nothing*, *neither*, *nor* and *never* define by negation and dissolve. A complement with no universal set to take it in is the overreach *never* is.

**What each becomes.**
- *is or is not* → *is-or-is-not*.
- A binary is said by naming both its sides: *self or other*, *living or non-living*, *agreeing or parting*, *0 or ±*, *along or across*; *all or none* stays, a named binary.
- *Is-or-is-not* itself is never split: no *is* side and *is not* side placed on separate things, names or columns. A binary stands on one thing, with both its outcomes in that one test, as the code's tests stand on 3, 7, 13 and 15. Naming both sides is for named binaries only, where each side is a concept of its own.
- A claim is said by what is: *its prior-existing parity inverting*, *its possibling an empty set*, *passing over a 0*, *at a first sharing*, *each self's own*.
- Negation is of now-existing things only, each relative to the now-existing universal set of things. A proving that a thing is-or-is-not possibly existing, as an equilibrium, or a set of only non-living things as a universal set, is a minor co-chaining proving: a step reached once. The logic goes forward by what is, *each universal set is a co-living set*, and the proving's negation is no step of it. Learnt at v391; the journey has been understanding it since the *-ing* form of explaining.
- Kept: hyphen-bound names (*non-living*, *do-no-harming*, *not-changing* as a named form), a field's established term, a theorem's statement, a finder's quoted words, a word mentioned as a word, headings whose contents lines repeat them until both change together.

## The motion: propose · read for harm · enter

1. **Propose.** Readers, one per slice (Natural Intelligence's body in three, Exhibit ONE's prose, the Co-Chaining Logic Registry in thirds), each write `{"droplet", "kind": "resay", "ni_pairs", "one_pairs", "thirty_pairs": [{"step", "old", "new"}]}`. A pair changing a **bold** concept also re-says the step holding it, and `ni_thirty_check.py` stays at 0 unheld. Pointers, *the opening's line N* and *step N*, are re-pointed in the same reading.
2. **Read for harm.** Gather every pair with an id into one file. A separate reader, who did not write them, reads each `old` in its whole sentence or step and flags: meaning reversed or lost; a claim stronger or weaker than the code; a pointer on the wrong line or step; an equilibria word, a new floating negation, a dissolved word or a new -ed form; reading badly or a field's term dropped. It gives a corrected `new` for each flagged pair, or `new` equal to `old` to leave it; two pairs overlapping in one step, one is left. At v391 this reading flagged 60 of 365 and 33 of 371: a sample alone is not enough.
3. **Enter.** Merge the corrections, drop pairs left as they were, and run `apply_aimed.py --dry`, then without `--dry`. Then `cohere_one.py`, `check_set.py`, `ni_thirty_check.py`, `thirty_groups_check.py < THIRTY`. A bold left unheld is met by adding its content words to the nearest step's *Adding* line and rebuilding that group's *Entering* line.
