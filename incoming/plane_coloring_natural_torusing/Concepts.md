# Plane coloring and natural torusing

The proposed connection between plane coloring and natural torusing concerns how prior unrelationing participates in discovering a next color, how seven colors can recur through an opening right spiral, and whether six can satisfy the same whole-plane requirements.

**The aim is to construct a complete seven-color surface through bi-folding, then determine whether the original conditions exclude six colors.** Uniqueness of the resolving method is a further claim.

Established relations, conditional obstructions, and unresolved correspondences are distinguished below. A complete coloring derived from the proposed spiral, a general exclusion of six, and uniqueness of method remain open.

## The original problem and the boundary of the question

Color every point of the infinite flat Euclidean plane using a finite set of colors. Every point receives exactly one color. Every two points exactly one unit apart must have different colors. Find the smallest palette for which this is possible.

Colors are labels. Their perceptual similarity, numerical names, ordering, and physical wavelengths impose no additional restrictions. A color can recur. The unit is straight-line Euclidean distance, not distance travelled along a spiral.

The statement does not require uniform regions, hexagons, circles, triangles, periodicity, fractality, a particular traversal, or a resolver. These may serve as constructions or investigating methods. An argument about all possible colorings must retain candidates outside a chosen construction.

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Must the whole plane be covered? | Yes, including gaps and boundaries. |
| Does using a color consume it? | No. Reuse is excluded at a location only by the applicable unit-distance relations. |
| Does a binary comparison leave one possible color? | Not necessarily. Several colors can each differ from the color compared. |
| Must six neighbors of a center have six different colors? | Not from center and consecutive-neighbor distances alone. |
| Must all valid colorings be periodic or fractal? | Neither follows from a finite palette. |
| Does a failed six-color construction exclude six altogether? | No. The exclusion must cover every six-color candidate. |

In solution-set terms, each color is a set of plane points; these sets are disjoint, cover the plane, and each contains no pair at distance one. Prior, now, and next are permitted ways of exploring those relations. They are not extra restrictions in the original statement.

The proposed forward construction retains each earlier placement and color while adding a next occurrence. Each completed candidate remains one consistent assignment across all its comparisons.

## The established constructions used as comparisons

**Nine-color squares.** A repeating 3-by-3 block, with nine different labels and square side 0.6, supplies a complete construction. Within a square the diagonal is less than one. Distinct squares with the same label are separated by at least 1.2 in one coordinate. Consistent half-open edges assign every boundary point. This is a construction with squares, not an assumption imposed on all solutions.

**Seven-color hexagons.** The reference construction uses hexagon circumradius 0.4, index-seven repetition, within-cell diameter at most 0.8, and between distinct same-color cells a lower distance bound of (√21 − 2) × 0.4, greater than one. Boundary points can be assigned to incident cells. These two distance cases account for the full plane.

A second sufficient construction uses centers 0.8 apart and a covering radius of 7/15. These parameters must remain separate from the 0.4-circumradius hexagon construction.

Seven available colors also make eight and nine sufficient allowances. Splitting an existing color class cannot introduce a same-color collision. A specific two-squares eight-color construction remains unspecified.

A restricted seven-region obstruction: for the particular chosen sizes, each pair among a central one-color cell and its six neighboring one-color cells contains some pair of points one unit apart. Under that one-color-per-cell condition, all seven cells need different labels. The witness points can differ for different cell pairs; this is not seven mutually unit-separated points.

Similarly, simply merging whole color classes of the examined seven-color construction introduces collisions. Neither fact excludes a differently arranged six-coloring. The original problem allows colors to vary within those proposed cells.

Moving a unit circle anywhere across a completed valid coloring preserves the test: the center’s color occurs nowhere on its unit circumference. Moving, reflecting, or rotating the entire valid arrangement preserves distances. Independently moving pieces requires further checks where their assignments overlap and across the joined pieces.
