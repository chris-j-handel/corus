## A thread

A thread is a word or a concept of the chain, tied back to the step naming it and forward to the later steps needing it. It goes through the sentences using it, between them in their order, and around the chain into the files citing its steps. At a sentence's scale it is the method's pattern three moments long: the step naming its word, prior; the step using it, now; the steps needing it, next.

A thread back ends at one of three: the step naming the word; the step itself, for the name it is naming; or the word's ordinary meaning, and that is said. A thread forward ends at the farthest step needing it, or at itself.

## The instrument

`instruments/threads.py` draws the threads of Exhibit THIRTY from three kinds of naming, each the chain's own: a word in bold, an *Adding* line's head of one to three words, and a written cite. It decides nothing. A reader goes through what it finds:

- **ahead**, a word a step uses before the step adding it;
- **named again**, a concept a later step adds once more;
- **ends at itself**, a concept no later step uses;
- **a cite**, a cite whose words are other-than the words of the step it cites.
