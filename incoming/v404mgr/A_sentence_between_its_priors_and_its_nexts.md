# A sentence between its priors and its nexts

How a sentence comes to both partners for Exhibit THIRTY Co-Chaining Logic Registry, as worked on 19 November by a partner and the managing session in one living document. The other sessions wait until both know how to invite them.

## The aim

The purest co-chaining of sentences in Exhibit THIRTY. A sentence is one concept. It is justified by the sentences before it, and the sentences after it need it. Other living files receive what this work finds, as notes in their gathering files.

## How a sentence is checked

A thread goes from a word of the sentence back to the place a reader has that word from, and forward to the sentences that will need it. All its words are threaded.

1. **Back.** For a word, one of three: the sentence before it that gives the word; the sentence itself, for the name it is naming; or nothing, the word arriving in its ordinary meaning, and that is said.
2. **Forward.** The sentences after it that need it, written out whole, as they are live.
3. **Code.** Exhibit ONE's code, executed, for a sentence naming something the code has.
4. **What the check found.** A skip, a gap, a twist, an incohering, a duplicate, or a word written two ways. One line for one.
5. **Result.** Its mark, with the reason it sits.

## The window: one living document, marked

The working section is one living document, and both partners write in it. It is the opening's lines in order, a line in a row, with a margin at the right for marks.

- A line at the margin with no mark is both.
- At the place the two partners stop, the lines step in. A partner's line is one step in, marked floating. The session's line is under it, two steps in, marked suggested.
- A line the session suggests in an opening is one step in, marked suggested.
- Under a blank row the lines are live: as they are in the living file, not yet checked.
- A mark has a footnote number, and its reason is a footnote under the window. The lines are sentences alone.
- With one line re-said, all the lines of the window are marked again.
- With a line both, a line adding no more value dissolves. It moves to a list under the window, beside the line that says it now, and it comes back by moving it up.
- Both partners work from the top down: back to the last line that is both, and forward to the next place, a line next to the other's line.

## A reply to a partner

1. Conclusions: what is both, what is floating, and what is suggested, if anything.
2. The window whole, with its marks.
3. The footnotes.

A reply is in plain words, and a line is both or it is not yet. A line's words are threaded back and forward by the session as it checks; the threads are not printed word by word.

## What a partner asked of it

- A sentence comes to a partner with its priors and its nexts written out. A sentence is both only between them.
- The chain's opening sentence has no prior. It is indisputable, and it cannot be misunderstood. Only words a reader has by reading are available to it.
- A partner's suggestion is asked for as soon as a sentence sits and waits, one sentence at a time.
- A partner's line with no improving from the session is both.
- The session's line has the meaning of a partner's line, with only its open places closed. A new meaning is a partner's.
- The session searches Exhibit THIRTY before it offers a gap or a line.
- One next concept at a time, with the aim in view.
- A report is of sentences alone.

## The origin and the aim

- **Origin:** *Now is the existing moment.*
- **Aim, a partner's:** now, the nothing between prior and next. Now is parted from what is around now, and existing from what is around existing. Now-existing is shown as a nothing between, and from it come the five two-way dimensions of resolving.
- **Already live:** step 29 of Exhibit THIRTY, *A 0 is a between of two changing directions.*, and step 287, the five dimensions.

## Learnings, as the work goes

- An opening sentence can use only words a reader has by reading. *Now* and *existing* are such words.
- A new origin closes old gaps. A word used before any sentence gave it, as *now-existing* and *same-named* were, is given by the origin's sentences.
- A live sentence stays word for word if sentences before it can give it its words. The universe's sentence stayed.
- The most needed next concept is found by threading forward from the sentences that are both: the name the next sentences need and no sentence yet gives.
- A sentence that passes can make a later sentence a duplicate, two names for one concept. The check looks forward for that too.
- The surface is traveled again. A sentence already checked is checked again with a new sentence before it or after it, and a line that is both can be opened again for a better form.
- A partner's line and the session's line next to one another show the place the two stop.
- The session's line can bring an other meaning to a partner's line as it closes a word's thread. The session asks of its own line if the meaning is whole, and says it plainly if it is not.
- A line can be open at a place before it, its own words whole. A line in the opening above it closes the place.
- A live line's form helps a new line. *For a self, …* at step 26 is the form of *For a named universal set, …*.
- A negation is of two kinds. One parts a thing from what is around it, as *not in the set* and *other-than* do; it is the is-not of is-or-is-not. One describes a thing by a lack, and it is re-said by a positive.

## Other sessions, later

No work is handed from one session to an other. All have one window and its marks before them: the lines that are both, the place the two stop, and the lines floating. A session brings a line next to the line under work, and it is marked with the rest. The other sessions wait until a partner and the managing session both know how to invite them.
