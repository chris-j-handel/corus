# A sentence between its priors and its nexts

How a sentence comes to both partners for Exhibit THIRTY Co-Chaining Logic Registry, as tested on 19 November with a partner and the managing session. It is a test. The other sessions wait until the test is further on.

## The aim

The purest co-chaining of sentences in Exhibit THIRTY. A sentence is one concept. It is justified by the sentences before it, and the sentences after it need it. Other living files receive what this work finds, as notes in their gathering files.

## The marks

| Mark | What it says of a sentence |
|---|---|
| 1 incoming | It has arrived. Nothing is checked. |
| 2 co-chaining in priors and nexts | Its words are given by the sentences before it, and the sentences after it need it. |
| 3 code cohering | For a sentence naming a number, a name of the seventeen or a stable form: Exhibit ONE's code, executed, gives the same. |
| 4 ready for both | It comes to a partner between its priors and its nexts. |
| 5 both | Both partners agree. It goes to the top of its gathering file, waiting to enter. |

A sentence arriving with its prior and next sentences can come in at 2.

## How a sentence is checked

A thread goes from a word of the sentence back to the place a reader has that word from, and forward to the sentences that will need it. All its words are threaded.

1. **Back.** For a word, one of three: the sentence before it that gives the word; the sentence itself, for the name it is naming; or nothing, the word arriving in its ordinary meaning, and that is said.
2. **Forward.** The sentences after it that need it, written out whole, as they are live.
3. **Code.** Exhibit ONE's code, executed, for a sentence naming something the code has.
4. **What the check found.** A skip, a gap, a twist, an incohering, a duplicate, or a word written two ways. One line for one.
5. **Result.** Its mark, with the reason it sits.

## The report, with a reply

A reply shows the working section whole: its ten to twenty lines, in order, a line with its mark and one short note.

- **Both:** both partners agree, up to and with this line.
- **Floating:** a line under improving. Its note says if it follows from the lines before it, and gives a suggested improving, or none.
- **Live:** a line as it is in the living file, not yet checked.

Under the section: its origin, its aiming, and the one place the improving is now. A line's words are threaded back and forward by the session as it checks; the threads are not printed word by word.

## What a partner asked of it

- A sentence comes to a partner with its priors and its nexts written out. A sentence is both only between them.
- The chain's opening sentence has no prior. It is indisputable, and it cannot be misunderstood. Only words a reader has by reading are available to it.
- A partner's suggestion is asked for as soon as a sentence sits and waits, one sentence at a time.
- A session checks a partner's sentence and adds nothing to it. A session drafts a sentence at a partner's asking.
- A report is of sentences alone.

## The test to now: the opening, both on 19 November

1. *Now is the existing moment.*
2. *Now-existing is existing in the now-moment.*
3. *A now-existing named-thing is a named-thing existing in the now moment.*
4. *A now-existing named-set is the set of all now-existing same-named things.*
5. *The universe is the set of all now-existing things.*

The fifth is the live opening sentence of Exhibit THIRTY and of Natural Intelligence, word for word. The four before it give it its words.

| Word | A reader has it from | Sentences that need it |
|---|---|---|
| *now* | By reading, with the opening sentence. | The second, the third. |
| *existing* | By reading, with the opening sentence. | The second, the third. |
| *moment* | Its ordinary meaning, with the opening sentence. | The second, the third. |
| *now-existing* | Named by the second. | The third, the fourth, the fifth. |
| *named-thing* | Its ordinary meaning, with the third. | The fourth. |
| *set*, *same*, *all* | Their ordinary meaning, with the fourth. | The fifth. |
| *now-existing named-set* | Named by the fourth. | None yet. |
| *the universe* | Named by the fifth. | The live sentences after it. |
| *things* | Alone in the fifth; the third and fourth have named-things. | The live sentences after it. |

The five are at the top of Exhibit THIRTY's gathering file in the next version, one note for one, and at the top of Natural Intelligence's.

## Learnings, as the test goes

- An opening sentence can use only words a reader has by reading. *Now* and *existing* are such words.
- A new origin closes old gaps. A word used before any sentence gave it, as *now-existing* and *same-named* were, is given by the origin's sentences.
- A live sentence stays word for word if sentences before it can give it its words. The universe's sentence stayed.
- The most needed next concept is found by threading forward from the sentences that are both: the name the next sentences need and no sentence yet gives.
- A sentence that passes can make a later sentence a duplicate, two names for one concept. The check looks forward for that too.
- The surface is traveled again. A sentence already checked is checked again with a new sentence before it or after it.
