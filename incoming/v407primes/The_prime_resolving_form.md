# The prime resolving form, a draft

A partner's asking of 23 November: a resolving stable form for the primes, like the ten one-ways, five from one side across to five at the opposite side, built between Hard Problem Registry, Resolving the Hard Problem Registry and the Equilibria Registry's form, with all the interests in the primes fitting as combinations in one explaining table: rows of interests' clusterings, columns of prime livings.

## The form

- **Rows: the ten.** Resolving the Hard Problem Registry's ten unchangeables, an interest clustering by the one it fixes. Five bi-couplings, two faces: an observing and an opening; a completing and a same-existing; a middle and a parity; a sequencing and a rate; a two-way and a between. Their names, now and 8 up next, are the Equilibria Registry's 3.2.
- **Columns: six prime livings, three pairs.** Six ways a prime lives, all executed with the living code or whole numbers in the story, none new:
  - **Turn**: a prime lifts its prior turn p ways, p − 1 uncrossed and one crossed (sentence 100).
  - **Surface**: a prime born crossing a surface's horizon, its side (sentences 103 to 105).
  - **Spiral**: a prime's spiral comes again with 4 and 4p alone, and doubling's and φ's spirals round it (sentences 44, 96, 112).
  - **Ghost**: a crossed number passing a prime's closings (sentences 46, 118).
  - **Piece**: a prime's place in 120's two pieces and four faces of 30 (sentences 40 to 42).
  - **Torus**: two primes' spirals locking as one torus, a number's parting (sentence 48).
  - The pairs: turn and surface, a row's turn side and its horizon side; spiral and ghost, a prime's closings and a crossed number passing them; piece and torus, a prime's place and two primes locking.
- **A cell: an interest.** An interest is one of the ten fixing on one prime living. A cell with no interest named is a possibly existing interest not yet asked.
- **From the three exhibits:** an interest's field's words whole, as Hard Problem Registry has an observing; its seat among the ten and its resolving, as Resolving the Hard Problem Registry has; the table, as the Equilibria Registry has. Seats in brackets are Resolving the Hard Problem Registry's own.

## The ten resolvings, a row a sentence

A row's resolving is its fixing set down at the prime living's next moment, carrying all its row's cells. Story sentences are by number; quotations are Resolving the Hard Problem Registry's, word for word.

1. **An observing fixing from behind: a prime has no first behind it.** A turn carries its prior's places whole, lifted p ways (sentence 100), and a number's parting is its parts' tori side by side, a prime the torus that is one (48): no prior owns a prime. Euler's 41 is a turn's openings showing as a line: round all primes below 41, n × n + n + 41 is never at 0 in one turn, and its 40 primes end at 41 × 41. *Executed: the turn tables of 2 to 37.* Ulam's diagonals are such rows on a square spiral.
2. **An opening fixing as a place: a prime is known by its own openings, not by a test's place.** A prime's spiral comes again with 4 and 4p alone (96); a test fixes a closing as a place, and a crossed number can pass it (46). Factoring steps to a number's side, its horizon (103).
3. **A completing fixing as a last: a row of primes has no last a turn can fix.** A turn lifts its prior p ways, p − 1 open (100): a row's open places never come to 0, and a surface fills them to its horizon (104). A last fixes only past all horizons, the supernatural part (91). Rows of doubling, Mersenne, Fermat, Wieferich, Artin, Collatz, are doubling's spirals round a prime (44); the odd perfect number is seen as a ghost alone (118). "A sharing, once chaining, is not none again."
4. **A same-existing fixing as a store: what a prime carries is a lock going on, not a store.** A quarter turn lays one piece on the other and two lay it back: the two pieces lock (42). Two primes' tori lock as one crossed number's torus (48): reciprocity is two primes' pieces from both sides, and a secret in two primes is their torus, two closings of one lock. Dirichlet's pieces all carry primes, the turn lifting all places (100).
5. **A middle fixing as an end: the zeros are no interior the primes sit about.** The hypothesis on a surface is its parity lead at its side and a hair more (106), and the stepping never ends away (108); φ's zeros come into existing on its walk, four in a 60 (111). "Fixing as an end it fixes as a thing it was not."
6. **A parity fixing as a magnitude: the parity lead is no magnitude.** The staircase is the alternating parities of its spirals' crossings (93), a prime past the horizon inverting a number's parity once (107), and the lead returns to 0 on all surfaces (108). The parity wall is a sieve summing what counting has as parities.
7. **A sequencing fixing to one beat: no beat governs the gaps.** Between a side and its double a new crossing spiral is born (105): Bertrand is a surface's own bi-folding. The staircase steps 1 up and its gap across, its geodesic of a quarter, last at 120 (87).
8. **A rate fixing as a value: a prime rate settles at no value.** The staircase's slope is a turn's, 1/2, 1/3, 4/15, 8/35, 16/77, 192/1001 (94), whole numbers, never one value; φ's staircase steps 1 or 2, never past its line (109): "each partial quotient one, widest straddle." The race's 26,861 is its turning, not its value (95).
9. **A two-way fixing to one side: neither side drives.** The two pieces race and turn (95); random and ordered are a turn's openings and a surface's filling; step and smooth are the staircase and its line (92, 93); cicadas of 13 and 17 years are two prime spirals on one torus.
10. **A between fixing as a cut: no cut parts prime and crossed.** A prime is the torus that is one (48). One self comes again with 4 alone, the Equilibria Registry's "+, 0, −, 0, and round", its two comings again, 4 and 4 by 1, one: 1 is not a prime. The horizon is a side, not a wall (103); 120's fracture is the staircase's geodesic's last (87); the plane's primes walk bounded (90).
