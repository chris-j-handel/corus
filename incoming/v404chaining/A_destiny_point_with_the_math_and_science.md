## 2 The math and science, each definition as its field says it

- **A geodesic.** A curve on a surface with no turning within the surface, its geodesic curvature zero at each point: the surface's straightest line. Locally it is the shortest. On a plane each geodesic is a straight line; on a sphere each is a great circle. Geodesy says a geodesic is the shortest path between two points on a curved surface, the analogue of a straight line on a plane, and its straightest-path definition is whole for geodesics going far enough to turn back toward their start.
- **The tangent plane and its 0.** At each point p of a surface the directions a self can go in form a flat plane, the tangent plane, its origin 0 at p itself.
- **The exponential map.** For a point p and a tangent vector v, the exponential map's value is the endpoint of the one geodesic leaving p with initial velocity v, after one unit of its parameter. Each straight line through the tangent plane's 0 goes to a geodesic through p, and the 0 goes to p. With a complete surface the map is defined for each v.
- **Geodesy's direct problem.** From a starting point, an initial azimuth (a bearing) and a distance along the geodesic, find the point reached: its *destination point*, or *terminus point*, with its azimuth at it. The inverse problem is from two points to their geodesic's distance and azimuths.
- **The horizon.** The geometric horizon of an observer above a sphere: each line of sight to it is tangent to the sphere and perpendicular to its radius, at a distance of about the square root of 2Rh for a radius R and a height h. The horizon divides each viewing direction into one meeting the surface and one meeting it nowhere. Astronomy also has the horizon as a great circle of the celestial sphere, and, in cosmology, as a boundary of what is observed or observable.
- **The cut point, and the antipode.** A geodesic from p is the shortest only to its cut point. On a sphere each point's cut locus is one point, its antipode, and each geodesic from p meets each other at it. With a positive curvature geodesics from one point come together; with a negative curvature they go apart.
- **An ideal point.** In hyperbolic space each geodesic ray goes to one endpoint at infinity, an ideal point, on a boundary at infinity; ideal points represent directions toward infinity. From each point, each unit direction is one ideal point, by the exponential map.
- **Physics.** In general relativity a particle acted on by no force but gravity follows a geodesic of spacetime. The particle horizon is the boundary of the observable universe; the event horizon is the boundary of each possible future observing, its outside never observed.
- **Two roots.** *Horizon*, from Greek *horizōn kyklos*, a bounding circle, of *horizein*, to bound, and *horos*, a boundary. *Destiny* and *destination*, each from Latin *destinare*, to make firm, to establish; *destination* has its sense of a journey's end by 1787 and 1813.
