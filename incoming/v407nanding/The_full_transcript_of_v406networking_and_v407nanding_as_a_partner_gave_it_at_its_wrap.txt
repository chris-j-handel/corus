NAND isn't a brand. It's short for "NOT AND", and it's written in capitals the same way as AND, OR, NOT and NOR, the names of logical operations. It's a concept in logic and mathematics that is also a basic building block of electronics, and most people meet the word through computer memory.

In logic and mathematics. In Boolean functions and propositional calculus, the Sheffer stroke denotes a logical operation that is equivalent to the negation of the conjunction operation, expressed in ordinary language as "not both". It is also called NAND ("not and") or the alternative denial. Its symbols are ↑ and |. It matters because NAND can be used by itself, without any other logical operator, to constitute a logical formal system (making NAND functionally complete). Sheffer published it in 1913, after it had been realized previously by Charles Sanders Peirce, as attested by a fragment written in 1880. Peirce's word for these operations was ampheck (from Ancient Greek ἀμφήκης, amphēkēs, "cutting both ways").
