# Natural Naming supplement: word journeys and fuzzyings

**No word here has authority. No living file and no gathering file is changed by this page.** A page of the session v404universe. Each row is a field's own word, set beside the living files for a reader to test. The living files were first read for it through summaries, so each section number and wording wants checking against the file.

---

Read back from Exhibit TWENTY, most English words for fixed things come from a few roots meaning stand, sit, cut and hold. Most words for togetherness come from roots meaning follow, gather and same. The exhibit has already dissolved three "stand" words, and still carries that root in existing, stable and establishing.

## Reading used

This page takes [Exhibit TWENTY](https://corus.me/Exhibit_TWENTY_Natural_Naming_v402mgr.md) at v402mgr as the reference line and reads back from it. For each naming and each dissolved word it gives the word's first recorded sense and the drift since.

The exhibit sets the standing of every row. Section 4.11 says a word's earlier meanings are a field's own linguistic account, and 2.4 says no wording has authority. So each row is linguistics' or history's account, offered for the set to weigh.

The record shows three kinds of drift, which this page calls fuzzying after the session's word:

- A going-on becomes a thing: a verb hardens into a noun.

- A relation among people becomes a quality of an object.

- One root splits into words that no longer look related, so the shared sense drops from view.

These session words are absent from the two published files searched for them, Natural Intelligence and Exhibit TWENTY: vault, horizon, backwardation, fuzzying, focal, super competency, super morality, natural property, god, faith, power, money. Exhibit TWENTY uses "family" only for families of words. They are treated as working words. Exhibit NINE (v391), read later, does have property, owner, currency and capital (3.3, 4.5, 4.6). Their nearest field words are gathered near the end.

The fixing roots name bodily acts of staying put: standing, sitting, cutting, holding. The together roots name acts between people: following, gathering, being as one. English keeps both sets in plain sight and reads past them.

## Universe, universal, university

One Latin word, "turned into one", has named a whole, a guild, a building, the cosmos, a field of discussion, a galaxy and a person's private world. Each new sense kept the old ones in circulation.

| When | Word | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| c\. 45 BCE | universum | Cicero's Latin for Greek to holon: the whole, "turned into one". |
| c\. 270 to 1350 | universale | The "universals": do kinds such as "human" exist apart from each human? Argued from Porphyry to Ockham. |
| 1088 on | [universitas](https://www.quword.com/etym/s/university) | Any guild or corporation, the whole body of its members. Then the guild of masters and scholars at Bologna and Paris. |
| c\. 1300 | university | The guild's name passes to the institution, and later to its buildings. |
| 1580s | universe | The physical cosmos, in English. |
| 1846, 1854 | [universe of discourse](https://oeis.org/wiki/Universe_of_discourse) | De Morgan and Boole: the field of all the things a given discussion is about. |
| 1895 | set (Menge) | Cantor's gathering of distinct things into a whole. The "universal set" and its paradox follow by 1902. |
| 1800s to 1920s | island universes | Each galaxy is called a universe, and the word takes a plural. |
| 1789, 1948 | universal rights | "Of every person", by declaration. |
| 1900s | my universe; a fictional universe; multiverse | A person's whole world; a story-world; many universes. |

Its neighbours drifted the same way. Catholic is Greek kata holou, "on the whole". Ecumenical is oikoumenē, "the inhabited". Cosmopolitan is "citizen of the order".

Boole's universe of discourse is the nearest field relative of the opening sentence: a universe that is nothing but the things under discussion. It differs in one way the set will care about. Boole's universe is chosen by the speakers and resized with each discussion.

University shows the fuzzying inside a single word. A set of persons became a place.

## Who has owned what

Every item on the session's list has been held as property by someone, and every such claim has a date at which it was publicly turned.

| What | Held as property by | Where the claim turned |
|---|---|---|
| Self | A god, a king, a master, a father. | Overton 1646 and Locke 1689: each owns their own person. Kant: a person is their own master and not their own property. |
| Family | The Roman paterfamilias, with power of life and death over wife, children and slaves alike. | Law narrowed the power over centuries. "Family" narrowed to kin by the 1660s. |
| Women | Father, then husband. Under English coverture a wife's "legal existence" was suspended in her husband's ([Blackstone, 1765](https://wisc.pb.unizin.org/ls261/chapter/ch-4-1-william-blackstone-on-coverture-1765/)). | Married Women's Property Acts: Mississippi 1839, England 1870 and 1882. |
| Slaves | [Aristotle](https://classicalwisdom.com/greek_books/politics-by-aristotle-book-i/2/), c. 340 BCE: a slave is a living possession, by nature another's. | The Roman jurist [Florentinus](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/54617/oulr063-171.pdf), c. 200 CE: slavery makes a person another's "against nature". Valladolid 1550: Las Casas against Sepúlveda. Abolition, 1807 to 1888. |
| Land | The city's god and temple; the king; the lord; the commons; the encloser. In Leviticus 25:23 the land is God's and its people are sojourners. | Enclosure by statute, 1235 to the 1850s. Terra nullius ended in Mabo, 1992. A river became a legal person in 2017. |
| Things in general | Roman dominium: one owner, one thing. | Hohfeld 1913 and Honoré 1961: ownership is a bundle of relations among persons about a thing. |
| Power | Gods, and kings by divine right. | Locke 1689: power is held in trust from the governed. |
| Money | Temples and palaces. About 85 to 90 percent of the [earliest writing](https://www.discovermagazine.com/what-the-earliest-texts-say-about-the-invention-of-writing-692), at Uruk c. 3300 BCE, is accounts. | Coin, c. 600 BCE; then credit. Searle 1995: money is money because people jointly treat it so. |
| Fame | Heroes: Greek kleos, "what is heard". | Reputation became a legal interest, then a count of followers. |
| Attention | No one, until it was named scarce. | Simon 1971: a wealth of information makes a poverty of attention. Since 2017 "attention" is also a computation inside language models. |

Two records stand out for the set. Writing itself began as a record of who held what. And on persons as property the word "nature" was used on both sides: Aristotle's "by nature another's" and Florentinus's "against nature".

The modern legal account is the nearest to the set's own terms. Since Hohfeld, lawyers describe property as relations among persons, with the thing in the between.

## Gods, faith and the all-powerful

For most of recorded history a god was how a people held something as no one's and everyone's at once. The same record shows gods named as owners, and used to fix the ownership of land and of persons.

| Word | First sense | Drift |
|---|---|---|
| god | Germanic \*guda-: "the invoked", or "the one poured to". | A calling-on became a being. |
| deity, divine, Zeus, Jupiter | \*dyeu-: to shine; sky; day. | Gods were the bright sky ones. |
| human | Latin humus, earth: "earthling". | The Indo-European pair is sky ones above and earth ones below. |
| lord, of a god | Hebrew ba'al: owner, husband, master, and a god's name. Latin dominus: master of the house. | The god was named as the household's owner. |
| almighty, omnipotent | Greek pantokratōr, all-ruler. Aquinas: able to do all that is possible. | All-powerful and all-possible share one root. |
| faith | Latin fides: trust, a guarantee, good faith in a contract. | Trust between parties became assent to a doctrine. |
| belief | Old English: to hold dear; kin to love. | Holding dear became holding an opinion, from the 1600s. |
| religion | Latin relegere, to go over again (Cicero), or religare, to bind (Lactantius). | Disputed since antiquity. |
| holy, whole, heal, health | One Germanic root: whole, unharmed. | Wholeness split into the sacred and the medical. |
| sacred, sanctuary, asylum | Latin sacer, set apart. Greek asylon, "not to be seized". | What no one may take. |
| worship | Old English weorth-scipe, worth-ship. | Acknowledging worth became a rite. |
| fate, weird | Fate: "the spoken". Weird: Old English wyrd, "what becomes", from the turn root. | A becoming became a decree. |

How gods held things:

- As owners. Sumerian city gods were named as owners of temple land, and the temples kept the accounts. In Roman law, things of divine right were no one's property. Sanctuary put places beyond seizure. The Jubilee returned land every fiftieth year. Islamic waqf endows property that can never be sold.

- As the society itself. Durkheim, 1912: a people's god is that people, figured to itself. Hume and Searle: property, money and promises hold by mutual expectation alone.

- As watchers. Norenzayan, 2013: moralizing gods who see everything helped strangers cooperate at scale. The timing of that is disputed.

The filling-in the session describes has field names. "God of the gaps" (Drummond, 1894) is assigning to an all-powerful agent whatever explaining has not reached. Theatre's older name is deus ex machina, the god lowered by crane to end the plot.

Faith and belief are the session's "mutual beliefs" in their first senses: trust given between parties, and holding dear.

## Natural property

Several fields have found something close to property without law: a holder's claim that others respect. Exhibit NINE (v391) 3.3 speaks of property as the residue of a capture, and of an owner as a self's parity treated as a thing. I found no naming there for this respected holding.

| Field | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Game theory | "Bourgeois": the owner defends and the intruder gives way. It can hold as a stable convention with no difference in strength. | Maynard Smith and Parker, 1976 |
| Field biology | Speckled wood butterflies: the male already on a sunspot nearly always kept it. Later studies found exceptions, and the reading is disputed. | [Davies, 1978](https://reflectionsonpaperspast.com/2018/01/15/revisiting-davies-1978/) |
| Primatology | Hamadryas baboons: rivals respect a male's prior bond with a female. | Kummer, 1971 |
| Sleep | Great apes build a fresh sleeping nest each night. In a Hadza camp someone was awake at almost every minute of the night. | Samson and others, 2017 |
| Distance | Animals keep an individual distance. People keep an intimate zone of about 45 cm around the body. | Hediger, 1950; Hall, 1966 |
| Home | Latin [focus](https://dictionary.dogonews.com/definitions/focus) is the hearth. Roman and English law made the house a refuge from seizure and summons. | Semayne's Case, 1604 |
| Child development | By two to three years children treat the first holder of a thing as its owner. | Friedman and Neary, 2008 |
| Psychology | People value a thing more once it is theirs. | Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler, 1990 |

In every row the "property" is a relation: a holder, an other who gives way, and a thing in the between. None is a quality of the thing.

The session's example, the space around a sleeping family member, sits where four rows meet: the nest, the watch others keep, the intimate zone and the hearth.

The record's caution is about the word "natural". It has fixed three ownership claims as fact: Aristotle's natural slave, coverture as the natural result of marriage, and "unimproved" land as naturally empty. Roman jurists used the same word to say the opposite. A naming of natural property will meet both uses and will need to say which it is.

Halliday's finding is the closest to the session's claim. He showed that scientific English, from Newton's Opticks on, restates each process as a noun so that it can be measured and argued from: "moves" becomes "motion". That is fuzzying described by a linguist as a method.

Backwardation as used here is what this page does. Every starred root above is a reading backward from words that exist now.

## Flags for the exhibit

Twelve places where the exhibit's own choices meet these records. By 4.11 none of them binds the set.

1.  **Stand.** The exhibit dissolves stand, cost and destiny. Existing, stable form, stable-forming and establishing carry the same root. The -ing in existing restores the stepping-forth; "stable" does not.

2.  **Turn.** Turn is dissolved at 2.1. Universe, uni-, inward, outward and forward carry the other "turn" root, and 5.43 already cites it.

3.  **Geodesic and time.** Both come from one root, "to divide". The exhibit's "surface dividing itself" is the Greek sense restored.

4.  **Set, sequence, social.** One root, "to follow". The opening sentence's set, the exhibit's sequencing and its social moral competency are one word in the record.

5.  **Thing.** An assembly, perhaps first an appointed time. The opening sentence's "things" were once gatherings.

6.  **Sharing.** The dissolved ordinary sense began as "a cutting", close to the exhibit's dividing.

7.  **Competition and co-competencing.** One Latin word, already "together".

8.  **Changing.** First sense "exchanging". Compare bi-tri-exchanging (5.51).

9.  **Interest (4.7).** "To be between."

10. **Improving (Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR).** Its first English sense is turning land to profit by enclosing it.

11. **Property (8.2).** The quality sense and the owning sense are one word.

12. **True.** First "faithful", a word for persons. The true-or-false of method began as a social and moral word.

These are the places where an ordinary reader's ear will pull a naming back toward its fixed sense. That is where a replacing definition does the most work.
