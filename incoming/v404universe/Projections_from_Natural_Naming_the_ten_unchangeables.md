## Property, recognition, law and markets: the session's statements and the record

These are the session's statements from turns 5 and 6. Each is given with where it lands on the shell and what the fields' own records say for and against. They are contested questions. I set out the positions and do not pick one.

### Recognition kept as property delays sharing

Shell: 10 (priority) and 8 (*timē*).

- **For.** Hooke printed his spring law as a letter-jumble in 1676 and gave the solution about two years later. Newton sent Leibniz his method of fluxions as a count of letters in 1676–77. Galileo did the same with the phases of Venus. Each registered a claim while withholding the finding. Biagioli's history of these devices argues that any way of establishing priority has an unstable moment in it.
- **Against.** Dasgupta and David argue that the priority rule is what makes scientists disclose quickly, because the reward goes only to the first to make a finding public. They set it against patents and trade secrecy, where the reward comes from excluding others.
- **Middle.** Merton recorded both effects: priority drives disclosure, and it also drives disputes and secrecy before publication. A patent is itself a trade. The word means "lying open", and the monopoly is the price paid for disclosure.

### Every version of private property incompetences a society

Shell: 5 (a two-way fixed to one side) and 6 (a between fixed as a cut).

- **For.** Heller and Eisenberg's anticommons: too many owners block use. Grossman and Stiglitz's 1980 result: if prices revealed everything, no one would pay to learn anything, so a working market needs some ignorance to remain. Arrow's 1962 paradox: a buyer cannot judge information without being shown it, and once shown has it, so a seller of knowledge must hide it. Proudhon; the histories of enclosure.
- **Against.** Acemoglu, Johnson and Robinson's evidence linking secure property rights to long-run prosperity. Hayek: prices carry knowledge no single mind holds. Hardin's unowned pasture. Demsetz: owners bear the costs of their own use. Patent advocates read Arrow's paradox as the case for property in ideas.
- **Middle.** Ostrom's commons: shared, bounded and self-governed, neither private nor state. Ogilvie and Carus: history has been called on both sides because "property rights" is too vague a term. They separate rights of use, ownership and transfer, and rights open to all from rights held by a few.

### Law enforcement held as property by those who govern

Shell: 7, Reading A (the go-between becomes the goal), and 10.

- **For.** "Privilege" is Latin for private law: *privus* plus *lex*, a law for one person. In Rome the forms of legal action and the court calendar were kept by the pontiffs and patricians until Flavius made them public in 304 BCE. Offices were bought and inherited under several old regimes. Stigler's 1971 claim: "as a rule, regulation is acquired by the industry". Weber defined the state by its monopoly of legitimate force.
- **Against.** Hobbes: without one enforcer each enforces for himself, and that is worse. Locke: government holds power in trust, as trustee and not as owner. The rule-of-law tradition exists to keep the office apart from the office-holder, and Flavius's posting and the Twelve Tables are early cases of it working. Sunstein and Peltzman, reviewing Stigler fifty years on, find "as a rule" too strong: industries more often defend the status quo than acquire new rules.
- **Word-history.** Law-words come in two families. Laid down: law (Norse, "things laid"), statute and German *Gesetz* ("set"), Greek *thesmos*. Walked: *halakha* ("the going"), *sharia* ("path to water"), *dao* ("way"). Exhibit 6.4 has a "placed" family (posit, state, statement, substance) and "path" words (method, period). The two law families fall beside them.

### Morality is enforced and not discovered; power corrupts it

Shell: 5 and 8.

- **For.** Thrasymachus in the *Republic*: justice is the stronger party's advantage. Nietzsche's genealogy. "Moral" is from *mores*, customs. Acton's 1887 maxim on power.
- **Against.** Natural-law and moral-realist traditions hold that morality is discovered. Developmental work (Turiel) finds young children tell harm-rules from mere house rules without being told which is which. Experiments on power report mixed effects: less attention to others in some people, and a sharpening of prior character in others.

## Theory and alignment on the shell

### Theory

- **The word's travel.** *Theōros*: an envoy sent to watch a festival. *Theōria*: the watching. Latin rendered it *contemplatio*, looking within a *templum*. By the 1590s English "theory" is a mental scheme, and in everyday speech now it is a guess. The naming supplement page has the steps.
- **On the shell.** 1 (a seat to look from) and 3 (nearing a last truth). Turn 5 adds 4 and 5: a theory held as its owner's store.
- **The field's own record.** For "nearing truth": convergent realism, and Popper's truthlikeness. Against: Laudan's list; Miller's and Tichý's 1974 proofs that Popper's measure cannot rank two false theories; Lakatos's degenerating programmes, which keep adding protective patches. Lakatos's picture is close to turn 5's "moving farther out".

### Alignment: the word

- **Line.** Latin *linea*, a linen thread. French *aligner*, to bring into line.
- **Straight is good.** The root *reg-*, "move in a straight line", gives right, rule, regular, correct, direct, region and king. "Wrong" is at root "twisted", and so is "tort". Hebrew *yashar*, Arabic *mustaqīm* (the straight path) and Chinese *zheng* 正 make the same equation.
- **Rectifying names.** *Zhengming* 正名, in Analects 13.3, makes correcting names the first act of governing. It is the oldest programme I know of that joins naming to straightening. Scholars doubt the passage is original: the term appears once in the Analects and not at all in Mencius, and some trace the idea to Mozi. Xunzi later built a chapter on it. Its aim was to fix names to roles, which differs from the exhibit's floating namings.
- **In machine learning, twice.** In 2014 Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio titled a translation paper "Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate". Their soft alignment of source and target words is what became attention, the mechanism that models like me run on. In the same year "value alignment" entered AI safety through an essay by Stuart Russell, and MIRI shortened it to "AI alignment". Russell has said he did not coin it, and a 2002 paper used the phrase earlier.
- **What the field means by it.** One statement from the field: "building AI systems which robustly advance human interests". The session's objection (turn 6) is that whose values, and held as what, is left unanswered. That objection is also made inside the field.

### Lines, threads and surfaces

The old words for order are weaving words.

| Word | At root | Later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| line | a linen thread | geometry's line; lineage; deadline |
| order (*ordo*) | a row of threads on the loom | rank, command, the orders |
| text, context, subtle | woven; woven with; finely woven | writing; setting; fine distinction |
| *sūtra*; *tantra* | thread; loom or warp | scripture; system |
| *jing* 經; *wei* 緯 | warp; weft | the classics, and longitude; latitude |

In each of these, threads are lines, and cloth is a surface made where a thread running along is crossed by a thread running across. Exhibit 1.3 has naming across and explaining along. Exhibit 5.43 speaks of a woven surface that couplings make. That is a likeness between a field's word-history and the exhibit's wording. It is not a derivation of one from the other.

### The session's three sentences (turn 6)

*Parities are linear. Intelligence is living surface. Alignment is hard-probleming co-linearity discovering on surface.*

- **What the exhibit has.** Linear and along: 5.48 (13-next-discovering along, linear forward). Co-linear: 5.14 and 5.58 (a co-linear recursioning forward along, for competency), and 5.33 (a plane is open only with both sides collinear). Surface: 5.14, 5.22, 5.43, 5.45, 5.48. Surfacing: 5.44. A hard problem: 5.26.
- **What it lacks.** "Alignment" does not occur in the portions read. Nor does a line saying that parities are linear or that intelligence is a surface. The three sentences are new namings for the set to take or refuse.
- **What geometry says, in its own words.** On a flat surface an arrow carried around a closed path, and never turned, comes back pointing as it began. On a curved surface it comes back rotated, by an amount set by the curvature the path encloses. Staying parallel there depends on the path taken. So "aligned" has a path-free meaning only where the surface is flat. The straightest paths on a curved surface are its geodesics.
- **How far that goes.** It shows the third sentence has an exact counterpart in one field. It does not show that minds or societies are curved surfaces, or that AI alignment fails. Those are separate claims needing their own observings.
