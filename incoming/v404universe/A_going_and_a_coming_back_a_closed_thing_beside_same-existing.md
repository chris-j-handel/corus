## 1 The field's concept, in plain words

Two ordered sets. Two one-way maps between them, a going from the first to the second and a coming back from the second to the first. Neither undoes the other. One condition joins them: a thing of the first is under the coming back of a thing of the second, with and only with the second thing under the going of the first.

In the form used here each map turns an order over: more on the one side is less on the other.

By that one condition, and nothing more:

1. A going and a coming back has its start whole. It lands at its start or over it, and at no place under it.
2. A going and a coming back keeps an order.
3. Done twice is done once. A second going and coming back changes nothing.
4. Three are one. A going, a coming back and a going again is the one going.
5. A thing the going and coming back leaves the same is named closed. The closed things of the first set and the closed things of the second are one to one, each the other's by one map, the order turned over.

An example. Four things: a robin, a bat, a salmon, a stone. Three properties: living, flies, swims. A going takes a set of things to each property each of them has. A coming back takes a set of properties to each thing having each of them.

- Start with the robin. Going: living, flies. Coming back: the robin and the bat.
- The bat is added. By the properties the robin has, a bat is not parted from a robin.
- Again: the robin and the bat. Nothing more is added. The robin with the bat is closed.
- The closed sets are five: none; the salmon; the robin with the bat; those three, the living; and each of the four. Each has its own set of properties, and no two the same.
