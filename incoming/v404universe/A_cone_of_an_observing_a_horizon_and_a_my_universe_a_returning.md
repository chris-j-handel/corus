## 2 A field's words, each as its field says it

- **A visual cone.** Euclid's Optics, about 300 BCE, begins from this: the rays of sight make a cone, its vertex at the eye and its base at the limits of the things seen. A thing a ray falls on is seen, and a thing no ray falls on is not seen. The rays have gaps between them, which is Euclid's reason a dropped needle is not found at once.
- **A light cone.** Relativity has two cones at each here-and-now, through one vertex. From inside the past cone a signal can have come. Into the future cone a signal can go. Exhibit EIGHTEEN Natural Physics 3.4 has this, with its break.
- **An observable universe.** Cosmology's observable universe is a ball centred on its observer, its edge the particle horizon. Each place has its own, and two of them may or may not overlap. Cosmology keeps one more word, *the universe*, for the whole, which is no one's observing.
- **A universe of discourse.** De Morgan, 1846, and Boole, 1854: in each discourse, of a mind with its own thoughts or of one person with others, there is a limit, assumed or said, and the field holding all the things the discourse is of is its universe. Logic's other word for it is the universal set.
- **An Umwelt.** Uexküll, 1909: each living thing has a surrounding world of its own, made of what it senses and what it acts on, one circle. He pictured it as a soap bubble around each creature. Many creatures share one surrounding and have different worlds.
- **A direction carried.** On a curved surface an arrow carried around a closed path, never turned, comes back turned. Whether two directions at two places are the same depends on the path between them.
- **Three roots.** *Horizon* is from Greek *horos*, a boundary stone, and the same root gives *horismos*, a definition. *Destiny* is from Latin *destinare*, to make firm, of the family of stand, with exist, stable, establish and cost. Geodesy's *terminus point* has the name of Rome's god of boundary stones.
