## Now

The record gives the 2,500-year recollection real ground, and four corrections. Greek thinkers of about 475 BCE did hold up "what is" as one unchanging whole. The particle, the void, place, measured time and bounded infinity followed as replies within 150 years.

The corrections: the parting was not all of humanity's; "exists" became a claim of its own about 1,500 years later; measuring is older than any universe; the numeral zero has a separate root.

Three more statements from the files guided the search.

So the history has four threads to follow: when "everything" became one named thing; when "exists" came to be said of it; when absolute location, measure and infinity attached to it; and who kept to the things.

## Before the word

For most of recorded history no people had one noun for "the universe". They named pairs, pluralities or goings-on.

| Language | Term | Literal sense | Kind of name |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sumerian | an-ki | heaven-earth | a pair |
| Biblical Hebrew | ha-shamayim ve-ha-aretz | the heavens and the earth | a pair |
| Egyptian | [n.tyt / iw.tyt](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/pc0vc-96y02) | what is, and what is not | a pair |
| Classical Chinese | tiandi 天地 | heaven-earth | a pair |
| Classical Chinese | wanwu 萬物 | the ten thousand things | a plurality |
| Early Greek | ta panta | all things | a plurality |
| Sanskrit | jagat | the moving, the going | a going-on |
| Old English | weorold | age of man (wer + ald) | a living span |
| Biblical Hebrew | olam | long duration; "world" only in later Hebrew | a span |
| Classical Chinese | [yuzhou 宇宙](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/%E5%AE%99) | yu: the four directions, above and below; zhou: past and present | space with sequence |

Three things stand out.

- The plural came first. Greek said "all things" before "the all" (to pan) and "the whole" (to holon). Chinese kept the plural.

- Several "world" words began as words for living time. English "world" and Hebrew olam named a span of living before they named a place.

- The words for nature were goings-on. Greek physis comes from phyein, to grow; Latin natura from nasci, to be born; Chinese ziran 自然 reads "self-so".

The single noun arrived late. Latin universum means "turned into one" (unus + versus), modelled on Greek to holon. English took "universe" in its present sense [in the 1580s](https://www.etymonline.com/word/Universe).

A whole science could run without the concept. Francesca Rochberg's [Before Nature](https://www.press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/B/bo24663151.html) argues that Assyrian and Babylonian scholars observed and predicted the sky with no word or notion for "nature".

## Around 2,500 years ago

The parting the recollection points to is visible in the Greek record between about 570 and 330 BCE. Each step answers the one before it.

| When (BCE) | Who | The step | What it set in place |
|---|---|---|---|
| c\. 570 | Anaximander | Things arise from the boundless (apeiron) and make amends to one another in time's order. Earth stays put by equipoise. | The first "infinite"; the first balance argument; nature still told in social and moral terms |
| c\. 530 | Pythagoreans | Later tradition says Pythagoras first called the whole a kosmos, an "order". Odd and even sit in their table of ten opposites. | One name for the whole; parity as a principle |
| c\. 500 | Heraclitus | This kosmos is the same for all and made by no one: an ever-living fire, kindling and going out in measures. | The whole as an ongoing changing, held by back-turning tension |
| c\. 475 | [Parmenides](https://gpullman.com/8170/parmenides.php) | Inquiry has two ways only: it is, or it is not. What-is is ungenerated, whole, unchanging, "now, all together, one". He likens it to a balanced sphere. | What-is as one fixed whole; a strict two-way test; change as seeming |
| c\. 450 | Zeno, Melissus | Defending Parmenides: motion, plurality and place fall into endless regress. Melissus makes what-is unlimited in extent. | Infinity and location as paradox |
| c\. 430 | Leucippus, Democritus | To save change: many small unchanging what-ises, the atoms, moving in "the nothing", the void. | The particle and the void |
| c\. 360 | Plato | The kosmos is one single living creature, made after a timeless model. Time is a moving image of eternity. | One universe as one being; timeless forms above changing things |
| c\. 340 | Aristotle | The universe is finite, single and eternal. Place is a body's containing boundary. Time is the number of motion. A statement and its denial cannot both hold. | Location, measured time, two-valued logic |

Read as a chain, this is the standard account: atomism, Plato's forms and Aristotle's physics are each a reply to Parmenides. It matches the session's sentence closely. Holding up what-is as one fixed whole was followed by the particle, the nothing, place, time as measure and infinity as a problem.

The same centuries elsewhere held both moves.

| Where | Holding up one "what is" | Keeping to things arising and passing |
|---|---|---|
| India | Uddālaka, Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.2: in the beginning this was being alone, one without a second. | The Buddha, [Kaccānagotta Sutta](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Kaccayanagotta_Sutta): "all exists" and "all does not exist" are two extremes; he teaches by the middle. |
| India | Jain teaching: the loka is uncreated, with a fixed shape and non-world space beyond it. | Jain many-sidedness, systematised later: a thing is said in seven ways, not two. |
| China | No close counterpart found by this session. | Daodejing and Zhuangzi: the ten thousand things transform. The Changes: generating and generating is called change. |
| Israel | Genesis 1, written near this time: creation by separating, into "the heavens and the earth". | Exodus 3:14 gives the divine name as an unfinished verb, "I will be". The Greek translation made it "the one who is". |

Karl Jaspers named this window, 800 to 200 BCE, the Axial Age. So the date holds, as a parting within humanity with both lines carried forward.

## The plus-one pattern

Each time the universe was fixed as one bounded thing, someone soon stood at its edge and found one more. This is the history behind "infinity is infinity plus one".

| When | Who | The edge, and the one more |
|---|---|---|
| c\. 400 BCE | [Archytas](https://cambridge.org/core/books/history-of-pythagoreanism/archytas/07B9D035C82DBA6C37CEC45B8B09C03F) | At the outermost heaven, can I stretch out my hand or staff? If so there is more outside, and the question returns without end. |
| c\. 340 BCE | Aristotle | Takes it as a definition: the infinite is what always has something further outside it. He allows it only as potential. |
| c\. 300 BCE | [Stoics](https://www.philpapers.org/rec/POWVAS) | Two words for two totals. "The whole" is the kosmos. "The all" is the kosmos plus the endless void around it. |
| c\. 55 BCE | [Lucretius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javelin_argument) | A javelin thrown from the last edge either flies on or is stopped. Either way there is a beyond. His name for the total is "the sum of things". |
| 529 CE | [Philoponus](https://philarchive.org/archive/COUPTA) | If past days were infinite, today would add one to an infinity. He holds that absurd, so the world began. |
| 1440 to 1584 | Cusa, Bruno | The universe has no centre and no edge; then, infinitely many worlds. |
| 1781 | [Kant](https://web.stanford.edu/class/history34q/readings/Kant/CritiqueAntinomies.html) | "The world has a beginning and a limit" and "the world is infinite" can each be argued from the other's failure. A second pair does the same for simple parts against endless division. |
| 1899 to 1902 | Cantor, Russell | A set of all sets leads to contradiction. Cantor calls such totals inconsistent multiplicities, not finished things. |
| 1920s to now | Cosmology | "Island universes" become galaxies. "The universe" gains a plural, then a "multiverse". |

Kant's reading is the closest historical match to the expedition's claim. He places the fault in the subject of the sentence: the world taken as one completed, given thing. On his account the contradictions about limit, age and smallest part stop once that is given up.

Natural Mathematics 6.3 already carries Cantor and Brouwer. Kant's antinomies and the Stoic pair of words would sit well beside them.

## "Exists" has its own history

"The universe exists" needs a detachable word "exists", and that word is younger than Parmenides by about 1,500 years.

| When | Word | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| Greek, to c. 300 BCE | einai, "to be" | [Charles Kahn](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=26838): the verb chiefly says "is so" or "is such". Existence is not a distinct concept in Greek philosophy. |
| Classical Chinese | you 有 / wu 無 | "There is" is said as having and lacking. No one verb joins "is such" with "is there" (A. C. Graham, 1959). |
| Pali, 5th c. BCE teaching | [atthitā / natthitā](https://leighb.com/sn12_15.htm) | Is-ness and is-not-ness are named as the two notions the world leans on, and both are set aside. |
| Latin | exsistere | First sense: to step out, emerge, appear. The noun existentia appears in the 4th century CE. |
| Arabic, 9th to 11th c. | [wujūd](https://ibnarabisociety.org/?p=5626) | The word for existence also means finding; the existent is "the found". [Avicenna](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arabic-islamic-essence/) (d. 1037) sets existence apart from what a thing is. |
| Latin Europe, 13th c. | esse / essentia | Aquinas and others take up Avicenna's distinction for a created world. |
| 1641 | existence | With Descartes it becomes something to prove: of oneself, of God, of the outer world. |
| 1781 | Sein | Kant: being is "not a real predicate". Saying a thing exists adds nothing to what it is. |
| 1884 | Existenz | Frege: existence is said of a concept (that something falls under it), not of a thing. |

Two points for the files.

- The Latin and Arabic roots already read as goings-on: stepping forth, and finding. Natural Philosophy 3.28 ties existing to discovering; wujūd says that in one word.

- The fixing has two dates, not one. What-is was fixed as one whole about 475 BCE. "Exists" was detached as a claim of its own between about 1000 and 1650 CE.

The files' origin sentence has a long line of earlier holders. None matches it whole; each carries a part.

| 4th to 2nd c. BCE | Zhuangzi | Space is real with no fixed place; duration has length with no start or end. Things transform. | No location; prior, now, next |
| c\. 200 CE | Nāgārjuna | Nothing stands by a nature of its own. Things arise in dependence. He reasons in four corners, not two. | Bi-coupling; a between |
| c\. 400 CE | Augustine | Three presents: of past things (memory), of present things (attention), of future things (expectation). | A prior observed now |
| 9th to 12th c. | Kalām atomists | The world is made again at every instant. Nothing carries itself over. | Moments |
| 1716 | [Leibniz](https://www.siue.edu/~evailat/molz-text.html) | Space is the order of co-existing things and time the order of successive things. Neither is a container. | Co-existing, co-sequencing |
| 1781 | Kant | The world-whole is never a given object. | Not one thing beside its things |
| 1909 | [William James](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11984/pg11984.html) | Reality may exist in the "each-form", distributively, and never be gathered into an "all-form". He called the rival picture a block universe. | The set of each thing |
| 1907 to 1929 | Bergson, Whitehead | Intellect cuts becoming into stills. Whitehead names two errors: simple location and misplaced concreteness. | Fixings; particle zero |
| 1922 | [Kurt Lewin](https://core.ac.uk/works/5139864) | Genidentity: the relation by which a thing now comes from the same thing a moment before. | Same-existing |
| 1980 | David Bohm | The rheomode: a trial of verb-first language, so that nouns stop freezing flow. | -ing naming |
| 1996 on | Carlo Rovelli | Properties exist only in interactions. The world is events, not things. | Bi-coupling |
| 2013 | [Markus Gabriel](https://www.deutschland.de/en/topic/knowledge/markus-gabriel-philosopher-new-realism) | The world, as a total holding everything, does not exist; it would have to hold itself. Everything else does. | The universe is no further thing |

Anthropology adds peoples, not only authors.

- Philippe Descola: "one nature, many cultures" is one of four ways peoples sort beings, and a recent, local one.

- Eduardo Viveiros de Castro and Eduardo Kohn: in Amazonian thought each kind of being is a self with its own view. Kohn calls a forest an ecology of selves.

- Robin Wall Kimmerer: Potawatomi is about 70% verbs. A bay is said as "to be a bay".

- W. E. H. Stanner: the Aboriginal Dreaming is neither a past nor a container. His word for it was "everywhen".

- [Rafael Núñez and Eve Sweetser](https://carta.anthropogeny.org/node/309712): Aymara speakers place the past in front, where it is seen, and the future behind.

The Aymara finding is a living case of a prior observed now.

## Medicine and health

Equilibrium entered medicine at the same date and place as the fixed whole entered philosophy. [Alcmaeon of Croton](https://hekint.org/alcmaeon-of-croton-philosopher-physician/), about 500 BCE, defined health as isonomia, equal standing among the body's powers, and disease as the "monarchy" of one. It is the earliest known definition of health, and its words are political.

| When | Who | Health is |
|---|---|---|
| c\. 500 BCE | Alcmaeon | Equal standing of opposed powers: hot and cold, wet and dry |
| c\. 400 BCE | Hippocratic writers | A blending of humours; the physician is to help, or at least to do no harm |
| c\. 100 BCE | Huangdi Neijing | Unblocked movement of qi between yin and yang, in step with the seasons |
| 1865 to 1878 | Claude Bernard | A steady inner environment that the body itself keeps |
| 1926 | Walter Cannon | Homeostasis: values held near set points |
| 1944 | Erwin Schrödinger | Living matter is what keeps away from equilibrium; equilibrium is death |
| 1977 | Ilya Prigogine | Dissipative structures: order kept only by flow, far from equilibrium |
| 1988 | Sterling and Eyer | Allostasis: stability through change, with no fixed set point |

The line bends toward where the files stand. Since Schrödinger, physiology has moved from balance at a still point toward stable form kept by continual changing. The Equilibria Registry and Natural Medicine could cite this turn as a field's own motion.

Two further priors sit ready. The Hippocratic tract On Ancient Medicine rejects doctors who reason from a fixed postulate instead of from the patient. And "do no harm", which the files use throughout, is the Hippocratic phrase.

## Method: true-or-false, and discovering

Aristotle wrote two-valued logic down about 340 BCE, and he left "next" outside it himself. In On Interpretation 9 he asks whether "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" is true or false today, and declines to fix it either way. Łukasiewicz built three-valued logic on that passage in 1920.

Other traditions never took two values as the whole of reasoning.

- Egypt: Erik Hornung held that two-valued logic, laid over Egyptian thought, yields contradictions at once, while the thought is coherent on its own terms.

- India: Jain sevenfold predication, and the Buddhist four corners (is, is not, both, neither).

- Mathematics: Brouwer, from 1907, accepts the excluded middle only for finite, decidable matters and admits no completed infinite. Natural Mathematics 6.3 already carries him.

On discovering, standard philosophy of science coheres with a narrow form of Natural Intelligence 5.4. Peirce (1903), Popper (1934) and Reichenbach (1938) each separate the finding of a hypothesis from its testing. They place only the testing inside the method's logic.

They do not conclude that science cannot discover. They conclude that its discovering is not done by its true-or-false step.

## What the history supports, and what it does not

Supported:

1.  A datable parting. Parmenides, about 475 BCE, fixed what-is as one unchanging whole. The particle, the void, place, measured time and bounded infinity followed as replies within 150 years.

2.  The single noun is late. "Universe" as one named thing follows pairs, plurals and goings-on in every language sampled here.

3.  The plus-one pattern. From Archytas to the multiverse, each bounded universe drew a beyond.

4.  The origin sentence has standing company: the Sabba Sutta, Leibniz, Kant, James, Whitehead, Gabriel.

Not supported, or needing a narrower claim:

1.  "Humanity diverted." The same centuries produced the strongest early statements of the files' own view. Both lines ran on, often in one person: Aristotle fixed place and kept possibility; Plato made the universe a living creature.

2.  One error at one date. "Exists" as a detachable claim is medieval and early modern. Parmenides' Greek could not quite say today's sentence.

3.  "Causes measuring." Babylonian scholars measured and predicted the sky for centuries with no concept of nature or universe. Measuring is older. What came later was reading measure as a property of a container, as in Newton's absolute space and time, 1687.

4.  "Causes 0." The atomists' void does come from the Eleatic argument. The numeral zero does not. It comes from place-value counting in Babylon, the Maya world and India, and Greek thought resisted it.

5.  Why then? [Havelock](https://journal.oraltradition.org/wp-content/uploads/files/articles/1i/6_havelock.pdf) argued that alphabetic writing produced the timeless "is". That would explain the date, and it is [contested](https://camws.org/meeting/2006/abstracts/flory.html). Boman's contrast of moving Hebrew with static Greek was criticised by Barr in 1961.

What the fields outside say back, in their own words:

- Spinoza, Schaffer's priority monism and working cosmology begin from the whole as one real thing, and cosmology's predictions are accurate. Natural intelligence does not admit that beginning, so the two share no first sentence. History records the parting. It does not choose.

- One universal now. Relativity's denial of absolute simultaneity is the first objection a physicist will raise. Natural Naming calls that reading of "one now" a misreading. The reply will need making in physics' own words.

- "Scientific method cannot discover" is stronger than its priors support. See Method.

## No universe with no one observing: a field's long quarrel, and its circle

Asked in the session: is there a long history of confusion here, and is it a chicken-and-egg circle? A field's record, each as its field says it. Lines marked (g) are from general knowledge and want checking.

- About 475 BCE, Parmenides: thinking and being are the same. (g)
- 9th to 16th century, Advaita Vedānta: two doctrines that differ only in their order. In one the world is created and then seen. In the other, set out whole by Prakāśānanda about 1550 to 1600, a thing is only while it is seen, and the seeing is the creating.
- 1710, Berkeley: to be is to be perceived. For a tree to go on with no one about, he needed one who is always looking, and that one was God.
- 1818, Schopenhauer: the world is my representation. He says the circle himself: the world waits on the first eye that opened, and that eye waits on a long world before it. (g, the second sentence)
- From 1927, quantum mechanics: observer and measurement are in a theory's first statements. (g)
- 1970s and 1980s, Wheeler: a self-excited circuit. Observers bring into being the universe that brings them into being. He took the circle as the answer.
- 1990, Bell: observer and measurement belong in no exact statement of physics. He asked whether the world waited for a single-celled creature to look, or for one with a doctorate. (g, the second sentence)
- 2006, Meillassoux: science dates things to before any life, and a saying that nothing is with no one it is given to cannot make sense of those dates.

Beside it, the living files:

- Natural Intelligence's opening has the universe as a set of now-existing things with no one observing in the sentence. Observing comes after, and with same-existing: *By observing, the universe is a same-existing set.*
- Exhibit TWENTY Natural Naming 2.4 has the word dissolved: *observings, and no observers; pattern matchers couple with each observing without filter or selection*. Natural Explaining 3.2 has *no observer that observes* among each doer a writing installs over an -ing.
- Exhibit THIRTY Co-Chaining Logic Registry: *An observing is a now-existing thing, a self observing: proof of existing.*
- Natural Intelligence's gathering file has no first one: *the code has no first me-parity, the first inherited from prior living*.

A reading, for a reader to test: the circle is made by two words, *observer*, a doer set over an observing, and *exists*, said of the universe as one thing. With neither word, a now-existing thing waits on no observing, and same-existing is by observing.
