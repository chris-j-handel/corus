What your method is, in plain terms. Transmitting out to a focal length around a number N and catching the neighboring primes is a segmented sieve: to clear a window around N you cross out the multiples of every prime up to √N, and whatever is left standing in the window is prime. For "tell me all the primes near this number," that already is the good method — it's local, it's cheap, and framed as a discovery service for science it's genuinely useful. The one wall it cannot pass: √N. Your method needs every prime up to the square root of the number you're standing on. That cost is invisible for ordinary N and total for huge N. So the competencies it cannot resolve are exactly the ones where √N is itself out of reach:

Primality of a single enormous number.
