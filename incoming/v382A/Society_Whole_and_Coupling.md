The earlier conflict therefore remains at its precise pair. On the stated common whole, an existing instance and an exclusion of every instance cannot both hold. No actual occurrence is established merely by accepting the example, and no universal absence is established by saying the explanation is incomplete.

Three different relations can be kept together without that conflict:

- Whole participation at a particular existing coupling, where that participation is established.
- A further coupling still possibling.
- An account of that participation not yet offered.

The third is a condition of an offered account, not an absence of the society. These are compatible relations available for improving the explaining; they are not a substituted claim that a particular account is absent. Exhibit TWENTY-THREE Natural Values 5.3's universal exclusion adds a separate assertion, which cannot be silently reduced to its adjacent sayings about accounts.

This pass does not repeat D10 as a new concern.
