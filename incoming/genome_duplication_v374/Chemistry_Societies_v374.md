## Societies opening: nucleation

C1. *(7 · 11)* In a supersaturated sodium chloride solution confined in a carbon nanotube cone, filmed at atomic resolution at 25 frames a second, cuboid crystals of some tens of NaCl units emerge from a disordered mixture of the ions; the times at which crystals emerged spread about their mean at a normal distribution (Nakamuro and colleagues, "Capturing the Moment of Emergence of Crystal Nucleus from Disorder", Journal of the American Chemical Society 143:1763, 2021, at a news account and its title).

C2. *(7 · 11)* Calcium carbonate solutions carry stable clusters of ions, about two nanometres across, before any crystal nucleates, even below saturation, which the field names prenucleation clusters (Gebauer, Völkel and Cölfen, "Stable Prenucleation Calcium Carbonate Clusters", Science 322:1819, 2008, at its title and the field's account); a 2024 paper names the pathway inconsistent with calcium carbonate's kinetics ("Prenucleation Cluster Pathway is Inconsistent with CaCO3 Kinetics", Crystal Growth and Design 2024, at its title).

C3. *(7 · 11 · built)* A supersaturated sodium acetate solution continues liquid until a seed crystal or a disturbance meets it, and then crystallizes through its whole volume from that place, warming as it does, the field's reusable hand warmer (the field's account, not fetched this session).

C4. *(7 · 11)* A supercooled melt or supercooled water, at nucleation, warms back toward its melting point as the crystal grows through it, the field naming the warming recalescence and the latent heat its cause (the field's account at an encyclopedia page's title and at its listed studies of supercooled water droplets, content not fetched this session).

## Societies parting: two ways of separating

C5. *(7 · 11)* A mixture made unstable parts into two phases everywhere at once, with no barrier, forming intertwined structures that coarsen, the field's spinodal decomposition; a mixture only metastable parts at a few discrete places, nuclei over a barrier, the field's nucleation and growth (the field's account, at an encyclopedia page).

## Societies merging and releasing at one scale

C6. *(7 · 11)* In a suspension of small crystals or an emulsion, the smaller particles dissolve and their material redeposits onto the larger, the mean size growing and the number falling, the field's Ostwald ripening, named at the surface energy and the Kelvin equation's higher solubility of the small; the grittiness of old ice cream, the coarsening of emulsions (the field's account, at an encyclopedia page).

C7. *(7 · 11)* Surfactant molecules above a critical concentration associate into micelles; in the field's relaxation account single molecules leave and rejoin a micelle within microseconds, while whole micelles form and dissolve over milliseconds to seconds (Aniansson and Wall, 1974, the field's account at a topic page and at listed studies, figures not fetched this session).

## One hand from many

C8. *(7 · 11)* Sodium chlorate, achiral as a molecule, crystallizes in left- and right-handed crystals; from an unstirred solution the two hands arise in about equal numbers, and from a stirred solution nearly all the crystals of one sample share one hand, the hand differing from sample to sample (Kondepudi, Kaufman and Singh, "Chiral Symmetry Breaking in Sodium Chlorate Crystallization", Science 250:975, 1990, at its title and the field's account).

C9. *(7 · 11)* Equal amounts of left- and right-handed sodium chlorate crystals, in their saturated solution ground continuously with glass beads, turn wholly to one hand, the excess of one hand rising slowly at first, then fast, then levelling at the whole, a sigmoidal course; the field names three mechanisms together, Ostwald ripening, attrition and fragments joining crystals of their own hand, *enantiospecific aggregation* (Viedma, Physical Review Letters 2005, at an encyclopedia's account).

## Societies locking: chemical oscillators

C10. *(7 · 11)* Droplets of the Belousov–Zhabotinsky oscillating reaction, held in a row in oil, oscillate coupled to their neighbours through species diffusing across the oil; the field reports anti-phase oscillation between neighbours, in-phase oscillation, and stationary patterns where some droplets stop oscillating, the pattern depending on droplet size, spacing and which species crosses (Toiya, Vanag and Epstein, "Diffusively Coupled Chemical Oscillators in a Microfluidic Assembly", Angewandte Chemie 2008, and Delgado and colleagues, "Coupled oscillations in a 1D emulsion of Belousov–Zhabotinsky droplets", Soft Matter 7:3155, 2011, at their titles and the field's account, contents not fetched this session).

C11. *(7 · 11 · 23)* About a hundred thousand catalytic particles, each an oscillator of the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction, suspended in a solution they exchange with, synchronize as their density rises in two ways: gradually, the oscillations becoming more coordinated as density rises, or suddenly, synchronized oscillation switching on at a density, which way set by the rate of exchange between the particles and the solution around them (Taylor, Tinsley, Wang, Huang and Showalter, "Dynamical Quorum Sensing and Synchronization in Large Populations of Chemical Oscillators", Science 323:614, 2009, at its abstract's account).

## Societies receding, then the snap

C12. *(7 · 11)* Two colourless solutions mixed show no change for a while, then turn dark blue at once: iodine forms throughout, and thiosulfate turns each iodine back to iodide as fast as it forms, until the thiosulfate is used up, when the iodine meets the starch and the blue appears, the field's iodine clock (Landolt, 1886; the field's account, at an encyclopedia page).

C13. *(below 5 · 7)* A mixture of hydrogen and oxygen reacts slowly or explodes, at sharp limits of pressure and temperature, the field's explosion limits: radicals open in pairs, pass the chain on at each reaction, open further radicals where a reaction branches, and end where two radicals meet or reach the vessel wall; explosion where branching outruns ending (the field's chain-branching account, not fetched this session).

## A pattern continuing only while fed

C14. *(7 · 11)* In the chlorite–iodide–malonic acid reaction, fed continuously in a gel reactor, a stationary pattern of spots or stripes at one wavelength of its own continues, set by no template, as long as the feed continues, the field's first sustained Turing pattern (Castets, Dulos, Boissonade and De Kepper, "Experimental evidence of a sustained standing Turing-type nonequilibrium chemical pattern", Physical Review Letters 64:2953, 1990, at its title and the field's account).
