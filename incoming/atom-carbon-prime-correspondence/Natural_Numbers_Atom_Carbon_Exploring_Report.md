## What nine times four can carry

Numbers §8.2 already carries 24 through 60 as nine rounds of the four-step parity map F(x,y) = (y,−x), also six passages of six. This is an exact step count. A return of the pair's form does not return its occurrence: a next can have the same form. The four joint forms and a side's four full exchanging momentaries remain different fours.

A further arithmetic construction connects the nine's pairings to 36 without treating four as an unexplained multiplier. On a ring of nine stations, choose each station c in turn as centre and pair:

$$
\{c-1,c+1\},\quad\{c-2,c+2\},\quad
\{c-3,c+3\},\quad\{c-4,c+4\}\pmod 9.
$$

At every centre, its other eight stations make four pairs. Across all nine centres, each unordered pair appears once: a pair {a,b} has the unique centre satisfying 2c = a + b modulo 9. Thus nine centres give four pairs each, exhausting all C(9,2) = 36 pairs.

This supplies **nine sets of four pairs**, not nine bi-folding four-cycles. Connecting the translated centres to an actual co-sequential resolving remains the next step; the pair enumeration alone has no timing or traversal order.

## Primes and the periods of the specified spiral

THIRTY steps 740–742 give a prime correspondence with an explicit operation. For the stated odd spiral receiving only its own selves' parities, let d be its least spatial repeating block. Then d divides its number n of selves and its pattern returns after 4d momentaries.

For odd prime n, d is either 1 or n: a uniform pattern returns after four momentaries and a nonuniform pattern after 4n. For nine selves, d can be 1, 3 or 9, allowing periods 4, 12 or 36. A composite can therefore carry the whole-span period too. The distinction is the availability of intermediate repeating blocks, not an inability of composites to go whole.

Likewise, stepping round nine by two visits all nine stations. Natural Numbers §1.6 already preserves this: prime and coprime are different relations. These periods belong to the specified coupling and initial pattern; they are not assigned durations for physical atoms.

## Phi and the stable form geometry

THIRTY steps 512–516 already state a definite geometric condition. Three mutually perpendicular rectangles with corners (0, ±1, ±r), (±1, ±r, 0) and (±r, 0, ±1), with r > 1, give twelve corners. A short edge within a rectangle has length 2; the relevant between-rectangle distance is √(2(1 + r² − r)). Equal edges require:

$$
r^2=r+1,\qquad r=\varphi.
$$

The 120-degree cycling of the three rectangles works for other r too. It is the equal-edge condition that requires φ. The static corner set also has mirror symmetry; a directed observing or traversal must carry the hand attributed to the living form. These distinctions preserve the exact value of the φ correspondence while keeping the directed resolving explicit.
