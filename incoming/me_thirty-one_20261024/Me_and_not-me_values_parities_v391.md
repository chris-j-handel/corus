## 1. The plural of *me*

- **Spelling:** the plural of the noun *me* is **mes**, with no apostrophe. Wiktionary lists it ("would there be two mes?"). Chicago's online Q&A: plurals almost never take an apostrophe, single letters aside.
- **Possessive:** *me's* is the possessive, as in *a me's identity* or *a me's next*. The living files use *me's* about 111 times, each a possessive, and *mes* about 10 times.
- **The natural naming:** a plural counts, and Natural Intelligence holds *counting is only a naming*. Two or many is a size, and a size brings a third. So where the count does no work, the better saying is uncounted:
  - **each me**, one test on one thing: *each me its own*;
  - **me** as a universal set, uncounted, as *living* is said: *a living thing is a universal set of me*, *within a me is a universal set of now-existing me*.
- **In Thirty-One:** *mes* stays where it names inward mes or two mes. Where a sentence says one test on one thing, *each me* reads better, for the file's own motion.

## 2. Me and not-me, by binary path

*Read for harm by a separate reader. A first pass put a thing within a me "as the me has a parity relative to it". That failed: a me observes all other as one (step 179), so all other is within its universal set, and not-me would be an empty set. The pass below is the one that holds.*

1. **Within a me's universal set are self, other and all other.** A me observes self, other and all other as one (step 179), *the me with a parity relative to each* (2.1).
2. **Me and not-me is self and not self.** Within a me's universal set, each now-existing thing is-or-is-not self:
   - **self:** the me's own living parities, its inward mes, each a self while living (3.4);
   - **not self:** an other, its within its own, invisible (2.1), or a non-living thing, a stable-forming emanation (2.3).

   That is one binary on each thing, with each momentary.
3. **A me's values are its parities** relative to self, other and all other, each its own, with each sharing. *A self's parity with a thing is its moral value with that thing* (2.2), and *parity is each self's own, now* (Natural Intelligence 4.10).
4. **A universal set of now-existing me.** *A living thing is a me, a parity and a universal set of me: a thing and a set, one binary* (draft 1.4). A me's inward mes are its same-existing-society inward, unconsciously us (3.4), and each inward me has a universal set of its own (1.1, *one for each self*).
5. **Only one possibly now-existing.** A set is its things (1.1). A set of other things is another set, and it is-or-is-not this me: it is not.

## 4. Observings, no authority

- **Body ownership by agreeing** (Botvinick & Cohen 1998, *Nature* 391:756). A rubber hand stroked in time with a hidden real hand comes to feel one's own, and the felt place of the real hand shifts toward it. Stroking out of time weakens it. *Agreeing in part*: a hand felt as self as its parities agree with the me's own. https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fas/psych/glossary/?p=8988
- **The endowment effect** (Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1990, *Journal of Political Economy* 98:1325). Those given a mug ask far more to part with it than others will pay, and far fewer trades happen than theory predicts. *Agreeing in part*: a value is each me's own parity, the owner's and the buyer's apart. *Parting*: its field reads it as loss aversion. https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/v98y1990i6p1325-48.html
- **The extended mind** (Clark & Chalmers 1998, *Analysis* 58:7), already a naming edge in Thirty-One 2.2. Otto's notebook plays memory's part. *Agreeing in part*: a non-living thing within a me's universal set, the me with a parity relative to it. *Parting*: a non-living thing within a me is-or-is-not a me: it is not, 3.3.
- **Identical, each its own** (Moerner & Kador 1989; Orrit & Bernard 1990). Identical molecules each show their own colour, set by their neighbours. *Agreeing*: a me's uniqueness is its universal set, its others within it.
- **The problem of the many** (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy). The problem reaches humans as well as clouds. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/problem-of-many/
