## 1. Learnings about our method of working

### Corrections, and the rule each became

1. **Show only the concern, the largest, one at a time.** The assistant listed three bracketed marks about content the person could not see. Correction: marks help only beside the marked text; explain the one largest concern, which often settles others. (2154–2160) *Kept* (plan: "One question at a time"). That marks only work next to the text they mark is *new*.
2. **A question too dense is no question.** "Is *my parity is my 0* one 0 with the shared one?" was too dense. The assistant asked again in three short lines and got a one-line answer (*parity is the changing direction*). (2200–2225) *Kept* (Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR 2.5, the hardest question asked plainly).
3. **Look back before asking about a word that has already dissolved.** The assistant asked whether *parity-changing* and *offering* should dissolve, but both had dissolved earlier. It also brought in *difference crossed* after *crossing* had dissolved. (2284–2322, 2676) *Kept* (Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR offerings, v390: "Look back to our latest agreement before asking again"; 2.6: a concern restating what the set holds).
4. **Read the whole sentence before asking about one word in it.** The assistant asked whether the ordinary verb *carries* dissolves in *one through eight carries a self as three*, and the person answered "The sentence is wrong". The whole sentence went against the code. (2397–2417) *New*.
5. **Trace the lineage of a sentence that reads wrong.** The question "where is this coming from" turned up a v380 relation, read off table patterns under the old names. Its words were swapped name for name through every renaming and never derived again from the code, and a v387 reader flag was never resolved. (2419–2434) *New* as a rule. Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR 2.9 covers a file's version lineage, not a sentence's.
6. **Stop pausing to confirm what the code settles.** The person said: "i can not tell why we are going and pausing". The assistant had paused out of habit. Rule: resolve with the code, and stop only where it cannot decide. (2589–2620, 3503) *Kept* (Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR offerings v390, "concerns are for resolving … brings only what it cannot"; plan, "A proposal entered on the person's word, without asking again"). Later superseded in part: the plan now says "Code is no authority … may be read as a pattern".
7. **An explaining must stay inside what has been agreed.** The assistant explained parity with a table that counted flips across six momentaries, checked over 60,000 momentaries. That brought time back and made parity something a self holds alone. Re-said: parity is one inversioning or none, between two, now. (2499–2547) *New* as an explaining rule. The finding itself is laid at Natural Intelligence's offerings and archived.
8. **Dead language: a past participle is a momentarying finished.** *Inverted* became *inverting*. The assistant then brought in *coupled across* during its own repair, caught it, and re-said it as *coupling across*. (2656–2695) *Kept* (Natural Explaining offerings, "a past participle is a dead form"; rules 11 and 12, "no new past participles").
9. **Keep "concerns about our prior rigor" apart from "improving opportunities".** The assistant listed improving opportunities as rigor concerns. Re-checked, it found four real gaps: Natural Intelligence grew without its Exhibit THIRTY steps; shared sentences drifted; other files quoted words that had changed; checks read form, not meaning. (3006–3068) *Kept* in the plan ("Gaps in our prior rigor, met at v391") and in `ni_thirty_check.py`. *New* as a general rule in Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR (2.6 parts concerns about method from concerns about a file, not rigor gaps from opportunities).
10. **Show a sentence inside its sequence.** The person: "i am not familiar with where you are giving me a sentence in the middle of a logical sequence how can i gage its value". Later: "show the entire opening sequence". (3503, 3746) *New*. Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR offerings v390 has only "before and after, shown".
11. **When asked to propose, give several alternatives, and don't ask to enter each one.** The person: "stop asking each time to enter it when i am asking you to propose and more than one alternative would be valuable". (3635) *New*.
12. **Don't shift the person's wording while re-saying it.** The person's "as a same-existing self" was turned into "the method, same-existing". The person: "your wording incorrectly changed my wording … do not take my words as authority. redo". (3587–3615) *Kept* (Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR 2.2: shifting a subject is entering a sharing as a finding).
13. **Answer the request's scope.** Asked to show the sequence with comments, the assistant changed the settled feature sentence. The person: "this was not asking the natural intelligence is sentence to changing. the problem starts at line 4". (3805–3876) *Kept* (2.2, a correction seats at the first relation that departs). Keeping a settled sentence when asked about its sequence is *new* as a stated rule.
14. **The method has no requirements, and neither does the code.** The assistant had said the sentence needs the other because "the code requires" it. The person: "the method has no requirements and the code does not require … possibling by definition includes other". (3643–3671) *New* as a naming rule: *requires* and *needs* are not said of the method or the code.
15. **A defining sentence must define, not just follow.** *Natural intelligence is the one method* "can be placed after any method sentence and be wrong as a foundational definition". A definition of a file's title concept carries that concept fully and compactly. (3635) *New*.
16. **One added concept a line, using only words above, without redefining.** (3587) *Kept* (plan: "One concept a line in the opening").
17. **The person's words are no authority.** "my words have no authority" (2963); "do not take my words as authority" (3615). *Kept* (plan; Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR 2.4).

### What worked and should be kept

18. **Readers propose, and a script checks every pair before it enters.** Four readers over four ranges proposed 264 pairs. Each old text was found exactly, with no dissolved word, no new dead form, and bold and italic markers balanced. (2704–2730, 2869) *Kept* (rules 11 and 12, `apply_aimed.py`).
19. **A fresh reader's findings are checked against the code before acting**, and a finding the code or the opening refutes stays refused, with its reason. Examples: AI as a source of interests, and a second definition of social moral competency. (2113–2117, 3293–3303) *Kept* (Exhibit TWENTY-FOUR 4.5, "Each helper's report is no authority").
20. **Hold a file's halves until both return**, so headings and their contents lines enter together. (3340, 3399–3415) *Kept* in spirit (3.5, body first and contents next). Holding split halves is *new* as a stated practice.
21. **Offerings sorted item by item into verdicts.** Of 495 items, 153 were met, 118 superseded, 27 duplicates and 43 dated records, all archived with their verdicts. 31 went to other files and 123 stayed as aimed droplets, and the file went from 1,870 to 267 lines. (3139–3200) *Kept* (`rules_offerings.md`, plan).

23. **Push after every motion.** When the machine restarted mid-pass, nothing committed was lost. (3316–3323) *Kept* (plan motion: "commit and push").

A reader flagged it, but the opening was treated as settled. (3485–3508) *New*: a settled text is no authority, and a reader's flag on it is met like any other.
31. **Checks only read form.** Old wording found, no dissolved word, no -ed form: none of these tests meaning, so 1,176 re-sayings in THIRTY went without a meaning reader until late. (3024, 3065) *Kept* (rules 11, "read for harm"; plan gap 4).
