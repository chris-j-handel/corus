### Records going with a proton number across bi-couplings

- **Inner X-ray lines.** Sulfur's Kα₁ line, about 2.3 keV, moves about 2 eV from a sulfide to a sulfate, about 0.09 percent, a probe calibrating it against standards to read an oxidation state from it. Iron's Kβ₁,₃, about 7,059 eV, moves 1.7 ± 0.5 eV between FeS and FeS₂, about 0.024 percent, and its Kβ₂,₅ is 7,108 ± 1 eV in both. Across Z from copper to uranium, a teaching laboratory's Kα energies fit a relativistic form of Moseley's law, χ² 13.4 for 10 degrees of freedom.
- **Lines in a quasar.** Iron, magnesium, zinc and chromium lines at redshift 1.15 toward HE 0515−4414, 16.1 hours with ESPRESSO, give Δα/α = 1.3 ± 1.3 (stat) ± 0.4 (sys) parts per million, and with 28 other absorbers −0.5 ± 0.5 ± 0.5, its authors calling their profile fitting somewhat subjective.
- **The Sun's lines.** Iron I lines in the Sun match a laboratory lamp's to a few m/s, a field setting aside a gravitational redshift of +635 m/s and convective shifts of a few hundred m/s, about 10⁻⁶.

### Records going with a nuclide or a particle

- **Nuclear magnetism.** A bare proton's gyromagnetic ratio is 42.577478461 MHz/T and a proton's in water at 25 °C 42.57638543 MHz/T, about 26 parts per million apart, a compound shifting it by parts per million; carbon-12 and carbon-13, one proton number, part in mass and in nuclear spin.

### Records going with bi-couplings

- **Allotropes.** Diamond, hardness about 10,000 kg/mm², band gap 5.45 eV, resistivity 10¹³ to 10¹⁶ Ω·cm; graphite of the same proton number soft and conducting.

## Claim 8 · Oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen, three views around carbon, no other elements in life

### Records against

- **Phosphorus in DNA.** Each link of DNA's and RNA's backbone is a phosphodiester, and ATP has three phosphates. GFAJ-1, grown with arsenate up to 40 mM and phosphate at 3.1 ± 0.3 µM from impurities, is read in 2010 as having arsenic in its DNA; in 2012 Erb and others find it "arsenate-resistant, phosphate-dependent", and Reaves and others find no detectable arsenate in its DNA; in July 2025 the journal retracts the 2010 paper on "flawed data", with no finding of misconduct.
- **Twenty-one elements.** "11 metals and 10 non-metals, i.e., 21 elements, are essential for humans" (Maret, 2016), chromium in dispute, among them sodium, potassium, magnesium, calcium, iron, zinc, manganese, copper, cobalt, molybdenum, selenium and iodine.

### Records going with it

### All or none

## The right spiral, one universal hand

**A caution first:** D and L name a configuration at one atom against glyceraldehyde, and are-not a spiral's hand; L-amino acids and D-sugars are two chiral building blocks, and are-not one left and one right spiral.

### Records against

- **Z-DNA.** A left-handed double helix of d(CGCGCG), 12 base pairs a turn (Wang, Rich and others, 1979), going with alternating purines and pyrimidines and with negative supercoiling; ADAR1's Zα domain binds it, a structure at 2.1 Å (1999); in mice ADAR1 restrains ZBP1, a sensor of left-handed Z-form nucleic acids, from switching on spontaneously, and with Zα's function lost cells die and skin inflames, ADAR1's lesser activity going with Aicardi–Goutières syndrome in its authors' account (2022).
- **A mirror enzyme.** HIV-1 protease of D-amino acids only folds into the mirror form, left-handed helices with it, and cuts only mirror substrates (1992).
- **Snails.** In *Lymnaea stagnalis*, handedness maps to 0.8 cM over more than 1,600 meioses, and sinistral snails carry a one-base deletion in the formin gene *Lsdia1*, its authors' strongest candidate (2016).
- **Twining plants.** Of 1,485 stems at 17 sites in 9 countries, 92 percent twine right-handed, the same across hemispheres and latitudes (2007).
- **A tendril.** A cucumber tendril with each end on a support forms a left helix and a right helix meeting at a perversion, adding turns to both under tension (2012).
- **Carbon tubes.** Chiral carbon tubes of one wall, many carbon atoms each, form as equal mixtures of both hands, one hand coming to the fore only with a chiral helper (2007).
- **Cellulose.** Cellulose nanocrystals assemble into a left-handed cholesteric phase, its pitch about 100 nm to several µm.
- **Protein helices.** Of 3,517 protein chains, 72 left-handed three-residue turns, 10 four-residue and 2 five-residue left-handed helices, under 1 percent of residues in such stretches.

### Records going with it

- B-DNA right-handed in cells, L-amino acids' alpha-helices right-handed, 92 percent of twining stems right-handed, most snails dextral, each hand going on within a kind and passing from one generation to the next.

### All or none

**One universal right hand breaks:** Z-form nucleic acid and its sensing in living mice, a mirror enzyme working, sinistral snails with one gene their strongest candidate, left-twining plants, a tendril's two hands, carbon tubes of both hands and cellulose's left cholesteric. **A cohering re-saying:** *a hand goes on within a lineage and passes from one generation to the next, a right bias strong in B-DNA and in L-amino acids' alpha-helices,* beside Natural Chemistry 2.2's "within one conformation going on, a hand reaches again rather than each same-existing-society choosing it on its own".
