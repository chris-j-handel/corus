## 1 Radiocarbon decay and the half-life

**Statement.** Carbon-14 decays to nitrogen-14 by beta emission with a half-life; the decay of any single nucleus is spontaneous and unpredictable; across a population a constant fraction decays per unit time; the decay constant is obtained from measured activity and measured abundance, not from observation across a half-life.
**Hardness.** The field states that individual decay events are random and that only ensemble rates are lawful; historical half-life determinations disagreed by about nine percent; a conventional half-life and a conventional "present" are fixed for reporting and calibration converts them.
**Accounts.** Quantum tunnelling through a nuclear potential barrier; weak-interaction transition probability per unit time.
**Conserving relation.** A rate constant independent of chemical and physical environment.
**Reach.** The constant is measured on populations; environment-dependence is reported for bound-state beta decay in fully ionised nuclei and for electron-capture rates with electron density.
**Persistence.** Open at the environment-dependence boundary; settled as a population law.

## 2 Resonance and the two drawings of a six-membered ring

**Statement.** The bonding of benzene and of aromatic bases cannot be represented by one valence-bond structure; two or more contributing structures are drawn and the molecule is described as a resonance hybrid that is none of them.
**Hardness.** The field states that contributors are not structures between which the molecule oscillates, that the hybrid is a single state, and that the representation is a limitation of the drawing convention.
**Accounts.** Valence-bond resonance; molecular-orbital delocalisation.
**Conserving relation.** One ground state.
**Reach.** Spectroscopic and diffraction data over molecules; drawings over structures.
**Persistence.** Settled as a limitation of representation; open as a question of what a bond is.

## 3 Epigenetic inheritance of methylation marks

**Statement.** Methyl groups on cytosine are copied to the daughter strand at replication by a maintenance methyltransferase; the pattern of marks is heritable through cell division though not encoded in sequence; marks are described as a memory of cell identity that is read.
**Hardness.** The field states that how patterns are established, maintained and erased across generations and tissues is incompletely understood, and that "reading" and "writing" are metaphors for binding and catalysis.
**Accounts.** Maintenance methylation; reader proteins; chromatin states.
**Conserving relation.** A pattern transmitted across division.
**Reach.** Measured as methylation fraction at sites across populations of cells.
**Persistence.** Open.

## 4 The immortal strand hypothesis

**Statement.** Stem cells were proposed to retain their original template strands through asymmetric divisions to protect against replication errors; tests in hematopoietic stem cells find random chromatid segregation; a chromosome-specific nonrandom segregation of sex chromosomes is reported in one germline stem cell, randomised under overproliferation.
**Hardness.** The field states that label-retention assays confound quiescence with strand retention and that mechanism, where nonrandom segregation exists, is unknown.
**Accounts.** Template-strand protection; epigenetic asymmetry between sister chromatids.
**Conserving relation.** A strand identity preserved across divisions.
**Reach.** Label retention over populations; chromosome-orientation hybridisation over single chromosomes.
**Persistence.** Refuted as general; open as chromosome-specific.

**Hardness.** As entry 12; and the field states that in-vitro replication is not life by its own definitions, which require metabolism and compartment.
**Accounts.** Genetic-first; metabolism-first; compartment-first.
**Conserving relation.** One continuous sequence from non-living to living.

## 6 DNA repair

**Statement.** Tens of thousands of lesions per cell per day are repaired by pathways that excise damaged bases or stretches and resynthesise them using the complementary strand as template; mismatch repair acts on the newly synthesised strand; double-strand breaks are repaired by end joining with loss, or by homologous recombination using the sister chromatid after replication; repair is faster in transcribed regions.
**Hardness.** The field states that pathway choice, strand discrimination in eukaryotes and the coordination of repair with replication and transcription are incompletely understood.
**Accounts.** Damage recognition proteins; strand-discrimination signals; pathway competition.
**Conserving relation.** Sequence fidelity across a lifetime.
**Reach.** Lesion and repair rates measured over cell populations; single-molecule imaging over short times.
**Persistence.** Open.

## 7 Polymerase fidelity and selection

**Statement.** A replicative polymerase selects the correct incoming nucleotide by induced-fit closing of its fingers domain on a Watson–Crick-geometry pair, independently of hydrogen bonding, as shown by efficient incorporation of a non-hydrogen-bonding isostere; mismatches are excised by a separate exonuclease site; pyrophosphate is released on incorporation.
**Hardness.** The field states that the relative contributions of geometric selection, hydrogen bonding, and conformational dynamics to fidelity are debated.
**Accounts.** Geometric selection; hydrogen-bond selection; kinetic proofreading.
**Conserving relation.** An error rate per base.
**Reach.** Rates over ensembles; structures over frozen states.
**Persistence.** Open on mechanism weighting; settled that geometry suffices for incorporation.

## 8 Thermal fluctuation and DNA breathing

**Statement.** Base pairs open transiently one at a time at physiological temperature, detected by imino proton exchange; opened lifetimes are orders of magnitude shorter than closed; AT pairs open more readily than GC; opening is termed thermal fluctuation or noise.
**Hardness.** The field states that the opened state is not directly observed, only inferred from exchange, and that the relation of breathing to recognition and to melting is unresolved.
**Accounts.** Nearest-neighbour thermodynamics; nonlinear lattice dynamics.
**Conserving relation.** An equilibrium constant per pair.
**Reach.** Exchange rates over ensembles.
**Persistence.** Open.

## 9 Strand discrimination in mismatch repair

**Statement.** Mismatch repair removes the mismatched base from the new strand; in some bacteria the new strand is identified by transient undermethylation; in eukaryotes the signal is proposed to be nicks or the replication machinery itself.
**Hardness.** The field states that the eukaryotic strand-discrimination signal is not established.
**Accounts.** Hemimethylation; strand nicks; PCNA orientation.
**Conserving relation.** The correct strand retained.
**Reach.** Bacterial system settled; eukaryotic open.
**Persistence.** Open.

## 10 Helical periodicity

**Statement.** B-DNA has about 10.4–10.5 base pairs per turn in solution, A-DNA about 11; the value varies with sequence, hydration and salt; the two forms differ by sugar pucker.
**Hardness.** None stated; the field treats periodicity as a measured parameter.
**Accounts.** Structural models from fibre and crystal diffraction and from solution assays.
**Conserving relation.** A pitch.
**Reach.** Over ensembles and crystals.
**Persistence.** Settled as a measured range.

## 11 Chromatin handedness across scales

## 13 Photorespiration

**Statement.** The carboxylating enzyme of photosynthesis also accepts oxygen, yielding a two-carbon product that must be recycled at energetic cost; the field terms this wasteful and an evolutionary relic.
**Hardness.** The field states that no known form of the enzyme fully discriminates, and that the reason is debated.
**Accounts.** Evolutionary constraint from an anoxic origin; inherent chemical similarity of the substrates; trade-off between speed and specificity.
**Conserving relation.** Carbon gain per energy spent.
**Reach.** Rates over leaves and enzymes.
**Persistence.** Open.

## 14 Radiocarbon calibration and the conventional present

**Statement.** Conventional radiocarbon ages use the original half-life value and "before present" with present fixed at 1950; calibration curves built from tree rings and other records convert conventional ages to calendar ages, accommodating a varying atmospheric ratio.
**Hardness.** The field states that the atmospheric ratio has varied, that reservoirs differ, and that calibrated ages carry probability distributions rather than values.
**Accounts.** Calibration curves; reservoir corrections.
**Conserving relation.** A calendar age.
**Reach.** Over samples with known-age records.
**Persistence.** Settled as a convention; open at each curve revision.

## 15 Spontaneous five-fold symmetry

## 16 Bomb-pulse dating of human cells

**Statement.** Carbon-14 in genomic DNA records the atmospheric level at the time of a cell's last division; cortical neurons are as old as the individual; cardiomyocytes renew at about one percent per year declining with age; adipocytes at about ten percent per year; hippocampal neurons at about 1.75 percent per year.
**Hardness.** The field states that the method reads populations and that DNA repair contributes a small, bounded replacement.
**Accounts.** Retrospective birth dating.
**Conserving relation.** DNA carbon fixed at last division.
**Reach.** Over sorted nuclei; one contrary study on hippocampal neurogenesis by immunohistochemistry.
**Persistence.** Settled for cortex and heart; disputed for hippocampus.

**Reach.** Over sequenced cells and clones.

## 18 Replication origins and promoters

**Hardness.** None stated.

## 24 Carbon fixation and the citric acid cycle

**Statement.** Ribulose bisphosphate, five carbons, is carboxylated at C2 after enolisation to a six-carbon intermediate that hydrolyses to two three-carbon molecules, the fixed carbon in one; the enzyme requires carbamylation of a lysine by a separate carbon dioxide with magnesium for activity; the cycle regenerates three five-carbon acceptors from five of six three-carbon products, exporting one. In the citric acid cycle a two-carbon unit joins a four-carbon acceptor; two carbons leave as carbon dioxide per turn, and in the first turn these derive from the acceptor, not from the entering unit.
**Hardness.** The field states that the carboxylating enzyme is slow and promiscuous and its evolutionary optimisation debated.
**Accounts.** Established pathways.
**Conserving relation.** Carbon balance per turn.
**Reach.** Over labelled substrates.
**Persistence.** Settled.

## 25 Mismatch dynamics and recognition

## 26 Palindromic sites, hairpins and cruciforms

**Hardness.** The field states that cruciform extrusion in vivo is hard to observe and that why recognition sequences are palindromic is explained by protein symmetry.

## 27 RNA priming of DNA synthesis

**Statement.** DNA polymerases cannot begin a strand; synthesis starts from a short RNA primer made by a primase, later removed and replaced with DNA, and the fragments joined; at chromosome ends the removal leaves a gap, shortening the end each division unless extended by telomerase.
**Hardness.** The field states that why DNA polymerases cannot initiate is explained by proofreading constraints, and that the end-replication problem is solved only in cells expressing telomerase.
**Accounts.** Proofreading incompatibility with initiation; RNA-world relic.
**Conserving relation.** Chromosome length.
**Reach.** Over cells and ends.
**Persistence.** Settled on mechanism; open on origin.

## 30 Helical phasing and sequence periodicity

## 31 The molecular clock

**Statement.** The number of sequence differences between lineages accumulates roughly in proportion to time since divergence for a given gene; the neutral theory accounts for this as a substitution rate equal to the mutation rate for changes without selective effect; rates vary by gene, lineage and generation time.
**Hardness.** The field states that the clock is sloppy, that rate constancy is approximate, and that separating neutral from selected change is a central unresolved problem.
**Accounts.** Neutral theory; nearly neutral theory; selection.
**Conserving relation.** A rate per site per time.
**Reach.** Over aligned sequences and fossil calibrations.
**Persistence.** Open on rate constancy; settled as a usable approximation.

## 36 The error threshold

**Statement.** For a given per-base error rate there is a maximum chain length beyond which the sequence cannot be maintained across generations; the information is lost in an error catastrophe; a long chain requires accurate replication and accurate replication requires enzymes encoded by a long chain — stated as a paradox of origin.
**Hardness.** The field states that the threshold is a limit derived from population dynamics of replicators and that early life must have been below it or circumvented it.
**Accounts.** Quasispecies theory; compartmentalisation; hypercycles; recombination.
**Conserving relation.** A master sequence maintained.
**Reach.** Over models and viral populations.
**Persistence.** Settled as a theorem of the model; open as a constraint on origin.

## 37 The end-replication problem and the division limit

**Statement.** Each round of replication leaves the extreme end of a linear chromosome unreplicated, shortening it; somatic cells with no telomerase divide a limited number of times and then cease, a limit on divisions; cells with telomerase extend ends and divide without that limit; the field terms the division limit senescence and relates it to ageing and to cancer suppression.
**Hardness.** The field states that the relation of the division limit to organismal ageing is contested and that the limit is a property of the culture conditions as much as of the cell.
**Accounts.** Telomere shortening as a mitotic clock; DNA damage response at short ends.
**Conserving relation.** A maximum number of divisions.
**Reach.** Over cultured cells and tissues.
**Persistence.** Settled as a culture observation; open as a cause.

## 38 Translation and the ribosome

**Statement.** A ribosome of two subunits, about two-thirds RNA by mass, assembles on a messenger RNA at a start codon that also specifies the first amino acid; transfer RNAs carrying one amino acid each pair their anticodon with the codon in the small subunit, passing through three sites; the peptide bond forms in the large subunit at an RNA catalytic centre; the chain advances three nucleotides at a time; at a stop codon no transfer RNA pairs and a release factor shaped like a transfer RNA ends synthesis; the protein folds as it forms, and pauses at rare codons shape its folding; a polypeptide's backbone repeats nitrogen, alpha-carbon, carbonyl-carbon.
**Hardness.** The field states that decoding accuracy, the coupling of translation rate to folding, and the origin of the ribosome's RNA core are open; it calls the process decoding and reading.
**Accounts.** Kinetic proofreading at decoding; induced fit of the decoding centre; co-translational folding.
**Conserving relation.** A reading frame.
**Reach.** Over ensembles, single ribosomes, and structures.
**Persistence.** Settled on mechanism; open on accuracy and origin.

## 41 The lipid bilayer and its closure

**Statement.** Membrane lipids are carbon chains with a phosphate-bearing head; in water they form a bilayer, heads outward on both faces and chains inward; a bilayer closes on itself into a vesicle because an open edge exposes chains to water; lipids move freely within a layer and rarely between layers; the two layers differ in composition, maintained by enzymes that move lipids across.
**Hardness.** The field states that how asymmetry is established and why specific compositions are maintained is incompletely understood; it describes the membrane as a fluid and as a barrier.
**Accounts.** Hydrophobic effect; flippases and scramblases; lipid rafts.
**Conserving relation.** A closed boundary between inside and outside.
**Reach.** Over vesicles, cells and models.
**Persistence.** Settled on closure; open on asymmetry.

## 42 Alternating access

**Statement.** Membrane transporters bind a substrate on one face and release it on the other by changing between an outward-open and an inward-open form; they are never open to both faces at once; channels, by contrast, are described as gated open or closed; pumps couple transport to energy release.
**Hardness.** The field states that the structural basis of alternating access is established for several families and that the coupling to energy is the open question; the never-both rule is stated as the defining property.
**Accounts.** Rocker-switch; rocking-bundle; elevator mechanisms.
**Conserving relation.** No open path across the membrane at any instant.
**Reach.** Over structures in each form and over transport rates.
**Persistence.** Settled as a rule; open on coupling.

## 43 Endocytosis, exocytosis and fusion

**Hardness.** The field states that the energetics of fusion intermediates and the specificity of recognition are incompletely understood.
**Accounts.** Coat proteins; fusion proteins; curvature-sensing.
**Conserving relation.** Membrane area and topology across events.
**Reach.** Over cells and reconstituted vesicles.
**Persistence.** Open.

## 44 Sugar chains: cellulose and starch

**Statement.** Polysaccharides are six-membered rings of five carbons and one oxygen joined ring to ring through an oxygen; in cellulose successive rings are rotated by half a turn relative to each other and the chain is straight, and straight chains hydrogen-bond side by side into crystalline fibres that form the plant cell wall; in starch (amylose) successive rings have the same orientation and the chain winds into a helix of about six residues per turn; chitin and glycogen are related forms; cellulose is the most abundant organic polymer on earth; cells carry sugar chains on their outer membrane face.
**Hardness.** None stated for structure; the field states that the biosynthesis of cellulose by membrane complexes and the control of fibre orientation are incompletely understood.
**Accounts.** Established structures; glycosidic linkage stereochemistry (beta versus alpha) as the determinant of straight versus helical.
**Conserving relation.** Linkage stereochemistry.
**Reach.** Over structures and walls.
**Persistence.** Settled on structure.
