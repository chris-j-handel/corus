## A. Ingested carbon reaching DNA

**A1.** A purine base, which is-not a carbon atom, is a double ring of five carbon atoms and four nitrogen atoms. Made anew in a cell, its carbon atoms come from three places: two carbon atoms and one nitrogen atom from one glycine; two carbon atoms, one at each ring, from formate carried on a folate; and one carbon atom, the one the field names C6, from bicarbonate — the dissolved form of carbon dioxide. The four nitrogen atoms come from aspartate, from glutamine twice, and from glycine. [S1]

**A2.** A pyrimidine base is a single ring of four carbon atoms and two nitrogen atoms. Made anew, one carbon atom and one nitrogen atom arrive together as carbamoyl phosphate, which forms from bicarbonate and the amide nitrogen of glutamine with two ATP; the other three carbon atoms and the other nitrogen atom arrive together as one aspartate. [S1]

**A3.** The sugar of a nucleotide arrives after the base, from a ribose-5-phosphate activated by ATP into PRPP; the ribose-5-phosphate forms from glucose along the pentose phosphate pathway. The deoxyribose of DNA is made from the ribose of a ribonucleotide by ribonucleotide reductase, one oxygen atom taken away. [S1]

**A4.** Bases freed when a cell's own RNA and DNA are broken down are taken back into new nucleotides, a base joining a fresh PRPP: adenine to AMP by one enzyme, hypoxanthine and guanine by another, pyrimidine bases by a third. A base can ride into a new strand a second time. [S1]

**A5.** When a person drinks water part-deuterated, deuterium enters the deoxyribose of new DNA in dividing cells and not in cells that are not dividing; the label goes in along the de novo route through the sugar, and DNA that was already made is not labelled. The method reads cell proliferation down to about 2,000 cells and is used in people. [S2, S3]

**A6.** With [1,2-¹³C₂]glucose fed to cells, the ribose of nucleic acids carries the label in patterns that show which carbon atoms arrived along the oxidative branch and which along the non-oxidative branch of the pentose phosphate pathway; the greater share of ribose carbon in tumour cells arrives along the non-oxidative branch. [S4]

*An explaining.* The exhaled form, carbon dioxide, is also an ingested form from within: a bicarbonate carbon enters each new purine ring at one position and each new pyrimidine ring at one position. A carbon atom at the bump connector's three orientings — hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen in the proposal — is met here by three arrival routes into one base ring, each route bringing a known count of carbon and nitrogen atoms. The proposal would gain a specific correspondence if the three routes (glycine, formate, bicarbonate) are followed as three connectings rather than three names.

## B. Inherited strands persisting through co-sequencing

**B1.** Root-tip cells of a bean grown briefly in tritiated thymidine and then in plain medium: at the first division after labelling both chromatids of each chromosome are labelled; at the second division one chromatid of each chromosome is labelled and the other is not. The inherited strand is seen at the scale of a whole chromatid, not only as a density band of pooled DNA. [S5]

**B2.** Blood-forming stem cells of mice given BrdU for days, then followed through divisions: label retention matches what random segregation of chromatids predicts, in every context examined, and no asymmetric segregation of chromosomes is found. The authors say the immortal-strand mechanism is not a general property of stem cells, and that BrdU retention is a poor marker of a stem cell. [S6]

**B3.** Male germline stem cells of the fruit fly, read by chromosome-orientation fluorescent hybridization at single-chromosome resolution: the sister chromatids of the X and the Y chromosomes go to the stem cell and its differentiating daughter non-randomly, in about 85 percent of divisions by a reader's count, while the autosomes' chromatids go randomly. The non-random going needs the centrosome, SUN–KASH proteins at the nuclear envelope, and Dnmt2. When the stem cells over-proliferate, or when a differentiating cell de-differentiates back into a stem cell, the going becomes random. The authors conclude the pattern is unlikely to protect an "immortal" strand from copying errors and may carry different information on the two sister chromatids into the two daughters. [S7]

**B4.** Heart muscle cells of people, dated by the carbon-14 of their DNA after nuclei were sorted by a heart-muscle protein at 96 percent purity: about 1 percent of heart muscle cells are renewed per year at age 20 and about 0.3 percent per year at age 75; at age 50 about 60 percent of a person's heart muscle cells are the ones present around birth; fewer than half are replaced in a lifetime. The non-muscle cells of the same hearts turn over at a median 18 percent per year. [S8]

**B5.** Fat cells of people, dated the same way: about 10 percent of fat cells are renewed each year at every adult age and body mass, a median 8.35 percent, half the cells replaced every 8.3 years; the average fat cell is about 9.9 years old in lean and about 9.7 years old in obese people; the number of fat cells is set in childhood and adolescence and holds in adults. [S9]

**B6.** Neurons of the dentate gyrus of the human hippocampus, dated the same way: about 700 new neurons are added per day, about 1.75 percent of these neurons renewed per year, about 35 percent of them exchanged across a life. A separate study counting an immature-neuron marker in 59 hippocampi finds such cells abundant in infants and children, fewer than a dozen per cubic millimetre in middle age and none reported after 50; over half its adult cases had significant neurological disease. [S10]

*An explaining.* The living files already carry copying as autorecursioning, each new molecule with one original strand and one fresh (Natural Biology 4.7). B1 shows that inheritance at the chromatid; B2 and B3 show that which daughter cell receives the inherited chromatid is random in blood-forming stem cells and non-random only for two chromosomes in one stem cell of one animal, and that the non-randomness dissolves under over-proliferation. The proposal's "inherited strands kept apart from a separate living spiral, connectors never entering the inherited strands" meets B3 as its nearest observing, and B2 as a possible breaking of any universal version of it. B4–B6 give the pace: inherited DNA carbon stays in place for years to a lifetime in heart muscle, neuron and fat cell, each tissue at its own rate, while the non-muscle cells beside them renew in years.

## C. Carbon exhausted from living

**C1.** A resting adult breathing about 16 times a minute, 500 cubic centimetres a breath, takes in air at about 0.04 percent carbon dioxide and gives it back at about 5 percent: about 900 grams of carbon dioxide a day, about 245 grams of carbon a day. [S11]

**C2.** Ten kilograms of fat, taken as the triglyceride C₅₅H₁₀₄O₆, oxidized whole, leaves the body as 8.4 kilograms of carbon dioxide and 1.6 kilograms of water, 84 percent of the mass by the lungs; every carbon atom of the fat leaves as carbon dioxide, and the oxygen atoms of that carbon dioxide are mostly the oxygen breathed in. [S12]

**C3.** A bat switched to a diet of a different carbon-13 share: the carbon-13 share of its breath moves to the new diet's with a half-time of 21.6 minutes, levelling within two hours; a sugar-feeding bat in 11.5 minutes, a blood-feeding bat in about 29.5 minutes, a mouse on corn in 20.4 minutes, a hay-eating alpaca in about 2.8 days. A fasting animal's breath is about 3.2 per mille lighter in carbon-13 than its diet; a fed animal with its reserves equilibrated to the diet breathes within about 0.5 per mille of it. Bone records a lifetime, muscle, fat and liver record shorter windows, breath records minutes. [S13]

**C4.** Water given with heavy oxygen and heavy hydrogen: the heavy hydrogen leaves only as water, the heavy oxygen leaves as water and as carbon dioxide, because the oxygen of body water and the oxygen of bicarbonate and carbon dioxide exchange through carbonic anhydrase before the carbon dioxide is breathed out. The difference between the two leaving rates gives the carbon dioxide made. [S14]

*An explaining.* The living files carry respiration giving back carbon dioxide and water, neither a waste (Natural Biology 4.6), and the through named by its carbon atoms (gathering-value, Natural Biology 1). C1 and C2 give the through's count: a quarter kilogram of carbon a day, and fat's carbon leaving entirely by the lungs. C3 gives the through's pace at breath — minutes — beside DNA's pace at B4–B6 — years to a life. C4 shows the exhausted carbon connector leaving with oxygen atoms it did not arrive with, exchanged with the body's water on the way out: the carbon atom goes out, the oxygen atoms around it are the body's.

## D. Possible breakings and open joinings for the bump-connector explaining

1. **An exhausted form re-entering.** A1 and A2: the carbon of carbon dioxide, as bicarbonate, is a required carbon of every base made anew. The proposal names ingested carbon bump-connecting and exhausted carbon breathed out. It must say whether a carbon atom arriving from the body's own carbon dioxide into a base ring is an ingested connector, an exhausted connector turned back, or a third thing. If exhausted carbon is by definition leaving, A1 is a possible breaking; if the proposal admits returning, it gains a specific place.

2. **Inherited strands are not kept together as a set.** B2: in blood-forming stem cells the inherited chromatids are dealt to daughters at random. A universal claim that living keeps its inherited strands apart as one inherited set meets this as a breaking. B3 keeps a chromosome-specific version open, with a named requirement (centrosome, nuclear-envelope proteins) and a named dissolving (over-proliferation).

3. **No connector enters an inherited strand, but bases from a broken strand enter a new one.** A4: a base freed from old nucleic acid rides into new DNA. The proposal's "connectors never entering or leaving the inherited strands" holds for an intact inherited strand (A5: already-made DNA takes no label) and must be said apart from a base's second ride after its strand is broken down.

4. **Two paces, one carbon.** C3 against B4–B6: breath carbon follows diet in minutes; DNA carbon in a heart muscle cell holds for decades. The proposal names one living co-sequencing. It must carry both paces, or name which of the two it is explaining.

5. **Three routes into one ring.** A1: glycine, formate and bicarbonate each bring a known count of atoms into set positions of the purine ring. The H/N/O three-orienting proposal and the 12/13/14 proposal are two triplets not yet joined (PR #157); this is a third triplet, with positions the field has observed by labelling. A correspondence derived here would be specific; a resemblance of three to three would not.
