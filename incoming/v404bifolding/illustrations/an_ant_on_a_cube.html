An ant on a cube

A path over an edge looks bent on the cube. Cut the cube open along other edges and lay it flat, and the same path is one straight line: the two purple squares are the same two faces.

Two ants walk side by side, each walking straight. One passes the corner on the top, one on the front, and on the side face their paths cross at 90°. Laid flat round the corner, its three squares meet at 270°: the corner is a cone's apex, 90 short of a whole circle, and that shortness is the one thing of the cube's whole an ant can find at a place.
