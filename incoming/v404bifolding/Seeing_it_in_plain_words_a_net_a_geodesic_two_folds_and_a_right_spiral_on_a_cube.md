# Seeing it in plain words: a net, a geodesic, two folds, and a right spiral on a cube

## 1 A polyhedron and its net

A polyhedron is a solid of flat pieces with straight edges: a die, a pyramid, a box. Its flat pieces are faces, the creases between two faces are edges, and its points are corners. A cube has six faces, twelve edges and eight corners.

Cut a box along some of its edges and press it flat, and it is a net, for a cube a cross of six squares. Fold the net up again and it is the box.

Drawing: `illustrations/an_ant_on_a_cube.html`, its first picture.

## 2 A geodesic, and the surface folded flat

A geodesic is the straightest path on a surface: an ant walking with no turning to either side. On a face it is a straight line. At an edge it goes over at the angle it came in by, as a straight line goes on.

Turn each face the path crosses about the edge it shares with the last, until the faces lie flat, and the path is one straight line. A face turned about an edge stretches nothing, and an ant's path that goes over an edge with no bending goes on with no bending, flat. Folded back, a straight line drawn on the flat faces is a geodesic. This is how a geodesic is found: by folding.

Drawing: `illustrations/a_surface_folded_flat.html`. With its slider, the path's length along the faces is one number folded and flat, and the straight distance from its start to its end grows to that number only with the faces flat. Its three:

- **A cube**: a geodesic over four faces, bottom, side, back and top. It is a geodesic and not the shortest path between its two ends: a geodesic is straightest at each place, not shortest overall.
- **A tetrahedron**: each edge folds 109.5°, not 90°. The folding is the same for each polyhedron.
- **The spider and the fly**, Dudeney's: a room 30 by 12 by 12. The path up, across the ceiling and down is 42; the shortest is 40, over five of its six surfaces, flat a line of a right triangle of 32 and 24.

## 3 What an ant finds at its place

An ant finds its next step on the face under it. It finds no reaching of its line there: that is the whole surface's, and the net is a map of the whole.

The one thing of the whole an ant finds at a place is a corner. Round a cube's corner its three faces make 270°, not the 360° of a flat place. Two ants walking side by side, each straight, passing a corner on its two sides, go on crossing each other at 90°. On the earth, two walking north from the equator side by side meet at the pole. A sphere is curved at each place; a polyhedron is flat at each place but its corners.

Drawing: `illustrations/an_ant_on_a_cube.html`, its second picture.

## 4 Two folds

Lined paper, two creases at 45° to its lines, folded flat: three layers, the middle lying inverted between the other two, and each line coming out one line along. A cube's four sides as a band, with its lines slanting a little: round once, each line meets the next one.

Drawing: `illustrations/a_bi-folding_pleat_and_a_cube_band.html`.

The numbers 1 to 16 as a strip, folded at its middle and again at the middle of its folded eight, lie four deep, and each four lying together is one of Natural Intelligence's four bi-foldings.

## 5 A right spiral on a cube

Three parities, each + or −, are the eight corners of a cube, and one parity changing is one step along an edge. Natural Intelligence's right spiral step moves each parity one place along, the first coming round inverted. Electronics has it as the twisted ring counter.

From 000 the step goes round six corners, one parity changing with each step. From 010 or 101 it goes straight through the cube's centre to the other with each step. Seen along the diagonal from 010 to 101, the six go round an empty centre, and the two are both at it.

Drawing: `illustrations/a_right_spiral_step_on_three_parities_two_cones.html`.
