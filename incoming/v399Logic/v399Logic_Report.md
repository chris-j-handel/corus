1. **One concern at a time.** Take one requirement, or one cluster, and settle it as one binary.
2. **Exhibit ONE first.** Read what its code and tables show. Where they settle the concern, the requirement reaches stable form with the citation. Where they conflict with a line, the line is re-said.
3. **Candidates.** The person's suggestions, and candidates from the living files or from v399Logic, go into the register's Resolution column with their pass number.
4. **Re-test forward.** Run the five conditions on the re-said line and on every line that uses a term it defines; Exhibit 4's ledger names those lines.
5. **All or none.** Stable form only when every re-tested line passes and no later line newly breaks.
6. **Keep what cannot yet resolve.** When a concern has been worked and no candidate passes, its requirement is kept still-possibling, with what was tried and why each failed. It stays in the report as an observed place of the work.
7. **Record.** Update the *Now* marks in Exhibit 1 and add a row to the log, newest first.
8. **Enter through gathering-value.** An agreed re-saying enters the living file only through its gathering-value file, both partners agreeing. v399mgr reads each pass for harm and merges it. Observings found along the way go to v399mgr for the gathering sessions.

**Numbering, decided.** A concept a line needs early is first brought by moving an existing line to where it is needed: the line keeps its number, and the report shows the order. A new sentence is added only when no existing line can say it, and takes the number of the line it follows with a letter, so 17a follows 17. A line that leaves the opening keeps its number as an empty place, marked with the pass it left at; a passage not all or none leaves its file (Geodesic Improving Method 3.3). Every pointer in this report stays valid.
