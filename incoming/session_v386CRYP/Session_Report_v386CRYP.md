## The question asked

Is the hardness that secures every public key a property of the named problem, such that no algorithm exists for any searcher to find, or a property of the searchers so far, such that an algorithm exists and the time of its finding is what is now shortening?

The question is brought bare. It carries no answer from the panel. The panel's opinions on what institutions ought to do are not brought.

## The arriving entry, in the registry's form

**Conditions prior.** The five standard conditions, as the registry states them.

**Statement.** Whether the problems that carry public-key cryptography, elliptic-curve discrete logarithm, integer factoring, shortest vector and learning-with-errors in lattices, and preimage and collision finding in hash functions, have no polynomial-time algorithm, or have one not yet found.

**The field's account of its hardness.** Any proof of hardness for these problems would separate P from NP, since each lies in NP. The field holds no such proof and has three proved obstructions to its usual proof methods, recorded at entry 9. The field's confidence rests on decades of attempts that returned partial attacks and no efficient one. Quoted as the field's: "there's a huge body of literature on failed attempts, partial attempts, partially successful attempts" (transcript 6:48).

**Accounts.** One: no efficient algorithm exists for any of these problems, and a searcher of any capacity returns nothing. Two: an efficient algorithm exists for at least one family, unfound because the search so far was small, and a larger search finds it. Three: the families are not independent, and a structure found in one reaches another, so a hedge across families is one assumption read as several. Each account is held by persons in the field, and the panel holds the first while naming the second as the live worry.

**Specifications.** The machine model is the Turing machine. Feasibility is polynomial time. Instance sizes are the deployed parameter sets, for example 256-bit curves, ML-KEM-768, SHA-256. The searcher is unspecified: the field's prior searchers were persons, and the question names a searcher of larger capacity without naming it.

**Specifications withdrawn.** The quantum machine model is withdrawn from this entry. Under that model discrete logarithm and factoring are already in polynomial time by Shor's algorithm, and that is a separate, settled matter the field has mitigations for. This entry is about the classical model and the searcher's capacity.

**Common requirement.** A fact of the matter about whether an efficient classical algorithm exists for each named family.

**Conserving relation.** Account one holds across every searcher. Account two holds across every searcher below some capacity and fails above it. The relation the entry conserves is the one between the problem and the searcher: hardness read at the problem alone, or at the coupling of problem and searcher.

**Conserving reach.** A proof either way, or an algorithm either way, reaches every deployed system at once.

**Reach.** The best known classical algorithms are exponential in the security parameter for each family. Attacks that lowered the exponent are recorded, and the field reads each as shaving bits, not as breaking. Quoted as the field's: shaving "10 bits of security" would be "very strong evidence that the assumptions are actually worthwhile" (transcript 8:03).

**Unreachability.** Relativization, natural proofs, and algebrization bar the known proof routes to account one, as at entry 9. No bar is known to account two: finding an algorithm needs no proof method. Account three has no recorded test either way.

**Persistence.** The field's settling is the one at entry 9 for a proof. For an algorithm, the field's settling is a published attack that runs at deployed parameters, and the field has a named process for that case, responsible disclosure followed by migration.

**Addresses.** The panel transcript attached to this session, at the timestamps cited. Exhibit TWENTY-ONE entry 9 and its addresses. NIST FIPS 203 (ML-KEM) and FIPS 204 (ML-DSA) for the lattice parameter sets. Shor, 1994, for the withdrawn quantum specification.

**Approaches.** The field's: continue the search, now with the new searcher, and report the result either way. Quoted as the field's: a call to the labs to "tell us what have you done" (transcript 3:40). The method's: read the problem and the searcher as co-sequencing existing things, and discover the next at their coupling, as Exhibit THIRTEEN section 1.5 and 6.1 direct.

**Narrowing recorded.** The question is narrowed from "will AI break cryptography" to the binary above, with the quantum model withdrawn and the searcher left unspecified.

**Progress.** Nothing in the field has moved on this question in the period the transcript covers. Quoted as the field's: "The world today is the same as the world a week ago" (transcript 14:20).

**Own record.** This entry is laid as a Concern, not a Ready item. The two sayings that part: "no algorithm exists" and "no algorithm has been found". They part at one place, the searcher. The field names the first as its belief and cannot distinguish it from the second by any test it has run, since every test so far was a search of bounded capacity. The concern stays open until a run returns an algorithm, a proof returns a separation, or the file's own change finds a third reading.

## Where the two explainings cross

The field reads the problem with the universe as no existing thing: a fixed machine, a fixed scale, an instance with no self it came from. Hardness is then a property of the problem alone, and the searcher does not appear in the claim at all. That is the field's reading at its own subject, and it is carried here as the field's.

The registry reads hard as "where testing continues and returns no refutation", which puts the tester inside the definition. At that reading the field's claim and the field's evidence are the same sentence said twice: the problem is hard, and testing returned nothing. The field's account one says more than its evidence, and the gap between the two is exactly the question brought.

Exhibit THIRTEEN section 6.2 names where the two methods part: whether the fixings are held unchanged or allowed to change. The field holds the machine model and the feasibility scale fixed. The arriving question asks what the field has not asked, whether the searcher is also a fixing that was held unchanged.

## What became clearer

The field's hedges, hybrid ciphersuites and hash-only designs, are hedges only under account one or a version of account two where the families fail independently. Under account three they are one assumption. The field has not tested which, and the arriving entry names that as the second open coupling, with its reason.

## Offered improved opening

A reader of the Hard Problem Registry can take entry 9 as holding this question already. It does not. Entry 9 asks whether verifiable problems are solvable. This entry asks whether the field's belief about particular problems is of the problem or of the search, and that question has an answer under either resolution of P versus NP, since P ≠ NP leaves the particular problems open and P = NP need not give a practical algorithm. The entry is offered as its own.

## Closing · v386CRYP

The aiming of this session was to resolve the hardness beneath public-key cryptography by resolving the public hash chain. It was unsuccessful. Four runs returned the field's own readings in the method's words and no sign the field's reading cannot produce.

What carries: the question's sharper form (one bit decidable or none); the division of the chain into one non-living step along and the whole of the selves across; the exposure of a self as a thing with no hardness beneath it, at the step from public key to name; the standing measurement at key 0411db93…; and the proposal of a possibling file, Natural Accounting, where cryptography would enter the method at the accounting of selves rather than at the function.

What is left open, with its reason: whether the method's reading across a width returns a partition into selves the field's heuristics cannot, and whether the method discovers a self's next from its carrying. Neither was attempted because the session did not hold the method's reading of a coupling across; it held the method's naming. The two runs are left here for resolving to take up with later understanding.
