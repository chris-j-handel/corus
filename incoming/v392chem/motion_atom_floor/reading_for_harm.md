## The opening test

**Does a molecule as the first self, with non-living atoms inward, contradict the opening?** As the pairs word it, yes. With one re-saying, no.

- *Each self is a same-existing-society inward and outward* and *A same-existing-society is many selves bi-coupling*: if the molecule is *the first self* and its atoms are non-living, it has no selves inward, so it is no same-existing-society inward. SIXTEEN-0 and SEVENTEEN-0 say exactly this.
- *A bi-coupling is a self*: the proposer's example, *a molecule's inward same-existing-society its bi-couplings, each a self, with atoms the nothings they wind round*, goes round in a circle. A bi-coupling is *two selves sharing possibling*. A bond whose two sides are non-living atoms has no two selves, so it is no bi-coupling, and it cannot supply the selves it needs.
- **The opening already names a self's inward selves:** *Each parity is a self while it is living. A living parity's me is within the self's me.* Natural Intelligence 6.5 says it outright: *Its selves inward are its living parities, a same-existing-society.* So the smallest re-saying that coheres is:

  > *A molecule is the first self of this ladder, a same-existing-society of its living parities; its atoms are non-living stable forms its sharing winds round.*

  Its inward selves are its parities; its outward society is the molecules it bi-couples with. A bi-coupling of two molecules is a self: the water pair, the *us self*. *First* has to mean *of this ladder*, as Societies 5.3's old already scoped it (*the first self of this ladder and not the first possibly existing*). Unscoped, *first self* contradicts the opening.
  - The weak point is that the opening does not say a self's parities bi-couple with each other. Only Natural Intelligence 6.5 calls them *a same-existing-society*.
- *Each non-living thing is a stable-forming emanation*: the atom has to be an emanation. Natural Intelligence 6.5 fits it well:
  - *A non-living thing's own parity with its scale is an empty set. It has its changing only from what comes to it*. This matches Living Learning 2's observings: rhenium's decay changes with its electrons; a lone atom repeats its same changing.
