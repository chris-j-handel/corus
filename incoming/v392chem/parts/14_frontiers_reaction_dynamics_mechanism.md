## Reading conventions

- **Labels:**
  - **OBS:** observing (an instrument reading on a sample under stated conditions).
  - **REC:** reconstruction from observings.
  - **CONV:** convention or definition.
  - **MOD:** model construct.
  - **SIM:** simulation.
- Within items, **Measured:** gives what was read and **Adds:** gives what interpretation contributes.
- **Tags:** [unverified] = not checked this session. [project corpus] = link and summary seen in project files this session.
- **Binary format:** Claim · Negation · For / Against · Why hard · Standing · Settle · Proponents · Concerns (observing or explaining).
- Disputes with more than two accounts are split into sub-binaries (a, b). Classification criteria appear as CONV items, so each class can be read as a set with a stated membership criterion.

- **Standing** reflects literature known to me up to about 2024–25. No 2025–26 item was verified this session apart from two 2025 items in the project corpus.
- **DOIs** marked [unverified] were given from memory. Check every one before use.
- **Membership criteria:** where a binary turns on a classification ("concerted", "intermediate", "statistical", "roaming", "rate-determining"), the criterion for belonging to it is stated as a CONV item. This lets each class be read as a set with an explicit criterion.

## 1. Statistical vs dynamic control

**Takeaway.** TST and RRKM remain the default. Nonstatistical outcomes are accepted as occurring for particular classes: surfaces that bifurcate after the transition state, roaming, and some very short-lived intermediates. The live disputes concern prediction and generality, so these are mostly explaining binaries built on product ratios and KIEs.

- **CONV · "statistical":** intramolecular vibrational energy redistribution (IVR) is faster than reaction. Rates then depend only on total energy or temperature, and product ratios follow the free energies of the product-determining transition states. A reaction is "nonstatistical" if its outcome depends on where energy was put in or on the trajectory's history. Reviews: Carpenter 2005, *Annu. Rev. Phys. Chem.* (doi 10.1146/annurev.physchem.56.092503.141240) [unverified]; Carpenter 2013, *Chem. Rev.* (doi 10.1021/cr300511u) [unverified].
- **CONV · "ambimodal transition state":** one saddle point whose steepest-descent paths lead to two or more products, through a valley–ridge inflection after the transition state. Review: Ess et al. 2008, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* (doi 10.1002/anie.200800918) [unverified]. An earlier "bispericyclic" case: Caramella et al. 2002, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/ja016622h) [unverified].
- **CONV · "roaming":** one fragment nearly dissociates, wanders in a flat long-range region, then reacts internally, bypassing the conventional tight transition state. Review: Bowman and Suits 2011, *Phys. Today* (doi 10.1063/PT.3.1330) [unverified].

### B1 · Can static descriptors predict the branching ratio after an ambimodal transition state?
- **Claim:** For thermal reactions that pass one ambimodal transition state, static features of the potential-energy surface predict the product ratio to experimental accuracy without trajectories. Such features include the forming-bond lengths at the transition state, the position of the valley–ridge inflection, and the downstream transition state linking the products.
- **Negation:** For the same reactions, static features do not predict the ratio to experimental accuracy. Trajectories, which carry momentum beyond the transition state, are required.
- **For the claim:**
  - SIM · Yang et al. 2018, *JACS* (Houk group; doi 10.1021/jacs.7b13562) [unverified]. Measured: no experiment; product ratios from trajectories on DFT surfaces for a set of ambimodal pericyclic reactions. Adds: a correlation between the transition-state ratio of forming-bond lengths and the trajectory ratio, used as a shortcut rule.
  - SIM · Lee and Goodman, VRAI-selectivity method, about 2020–21 [unverified; venue and DOI not reliably recalled]. A static valley–ridge analysis reproduced trajectory selectivities for many bifurcating reactions.
- **For the negation:**
  - REC · Ussing, Hang and Singleton 2006, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/ja0606024) [unverified]. Measured: periselectivity ([4+2] vs [2+2]) and natural-abundance ¹³C KIEs, ketene + cyclopentadiene, in solution. Adds: trajectories on a bifurcating surface account for the selectivity and isotope effects, whereas a two-transition-state statistical picture misassigns them.
  - SIM · Hong and Tantillo 2014, *Nat. Chem.* (doi 10.1038/nchem.1843) [unverified]. Terpene carbocation cascades with several sequential bifurcations, where single-transition-state reasoning breaks down.
  - REC · 2025 study, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/jacs.4c16760) [project corpus]. Bicyclobutane–triazolinedione cycloadditions. Calculations and dynamics propose branching after the transition state through an intersection of radical and charge-separated states. Experiments supported the predicted solvent dependence of selectivity. Adds: branching can involve a change of electronic state, which one ground-state transition-state geometry cannot encode.
- **Biological case (either side):** REC · the enzyme SpnF catalyses a cycloaddition in spinosyn biosynthesis (Kim et al. 2011, *Nature*, doi 10.1038/nature09981) [unverified]. Computation found an ambimodal [6+4]/[4+2] transition state, with product selection decided after it (Patel et al. 2016, *JACS*, doi 10.1021/jacs.6b00017) [unverified].
- **Why hard:**
  - Each condition yields one scalar ratio.
  - Both methods depend on the same computed surface, and a change of density functional can move the inflection.
  - Explicit-solvent friction changes trajectories.
  - Measured ratios are scarce exactly where the predictors disagree.
- **Standing:** open. It is narrowing towards the view that static rules suffice for simple pericyclic bifurcations but fail for multistate, solvent-coupled or cascading cases.
- **What would settle it:** a blind benchmark of bifurcating reactions with measured product ratios (including isotope-perturbed ratios) across solvents and temperatures. Static predictors and trajectories would be scored on one shared high-level surface.
- **Proponents:**
  - Static shortcuts: Houk group (which also runs trajectories); Goodman group.
  - Trajectories essential: Singleton, Tantillo, Carpenter.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B2a · Did roaming occur in formaldehyde photodissociation? (settled anchor)
- **Claim:** In H₂CO photodissociation near the radical threshold, a measurable fraction of H₂ + CO forms by a path that never comes near the tight molecular transition state.
- **Negation:** Under the same conditions, all H₂ + CO passes the tight transition state, and the low-rotation CO / hot-H₂ component has another origin.
- **Observings:**
  - REC · Townsend et al. 2004, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.1104386) [unverified]. Measured: CO rotational distributions after UV photolysis, by state-selected ion imaging, showing a low-J component correlated with highly vibrationally excited H₂. Adds: quasiclassical trajectories on a global surface (Bowman) assign that component to an H atom that strays to long range and then abstracts the other H.
  - OBS (prior) · The low-J shoulder had been seen earlier by van Zee, Foltz and Moore in 1993 [unverified].
  - OBS · Endo et al. 2020, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.abc2960) [unverified]. Coulomb-explosion imaging after excitation reported time-resolved signatures of roaming fragments.
  - REC · Roaming has been extended to acetaldehyde (Heazlewood et al. 2008, *PNAS*, doi 10.1073/pnas.0802769105) [unverified] and to NO₃ (Grubb et al. 2012, *Science*, doi 10.1126/science.1216911) [unverified].
- **Against:** I know of no current defence of the strict negation.
- **Why hard:** roaming is never seen as a geometry. It is reconstructed from correlated product-state data plus trajectories, and whether a path "avoids the transition state" depends on whether that is defined in configuration space or in phase space.
- **Standing:** settled as a reconstruction (2004–08). Real-time imaging in 2020 strengthened it.
- **What would settle it:** already met.
- **Proponents:** Suits, Bowman, Kable, Osborn, Harding, Klippenstein.
- **Concerns:** observing (reconstruction).

### B2b · Is roaming statistical or dynamical in kind?
- **Claim:** Roaming branching fractions can be predicted by statistical rate theory using a suitably placed roaming dividing surface (a "roaming transition state"), like any other unimolecular channel.
- **Negation:** Roaming branching fractions cannot be predicted statistically. They depend on phase-space structure or incomplete IVR, and trajectories are needed.
- **For the claim:** MOD · Klippenstein, Georgievskii and Harding 2011, *J. Phys. Chem. A* (doi 10.1021/jp208347j) [unverified]. A statistical theory with a roaming transition state reproduced measured branching for several systems.
- **For the negation:** MOD · Mauguière et al. 2017, *Annu. Rev. Phys. Chem.* (doi 10.1146/annurev-physchem-052516-050613) [unverified]. In this phase-space account, invariant manifolds steer roaming, and fractions depend on energy in ways a simple transition-state treatment misses.
- **Why hard:** "statistical" partly depends on the dividing surface chosen. A surface optimised variationally in phase space can absorb many dynamical effects, and both approaches can fit product-state data after adjustment.
- **Standing:** narrowing. Statistical roaming transition states are used in practice in combustion kinetics.
- **What would settle it:** blind, parameter-free predictions of energy-resolved roaming fractions for a new molecule, compared with state-resolved measurements.
- **Proponents:**
  - Statistical: Klippenstein, Harding, Georgievskii.
  - Phase-space/dynamical: Wiggins, Ezra, Carpenter, Mauguière.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B3 · Do nonstatistical dynamics decide outcomes of thermal reactions in solution?
- **Claim:** For some thermal organic reactions in solution near 300 K, measured product ratios or intramolecular KIEs depart from statistical predictions made on accurate surfaces. The departure comes from incomplete energy redistribution in short-lived intermediates, or from momentum carried across flat regions.
- **Negation:** Once surfaces, tunnelling and variational effects are treated accurately, statistical theory reproduces the same measured ratios and KIEs. The apparent nonstatistical effects are artefacts of the model.
- **For the claim:**
  - REC · Carpenter 1995, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/ja00128a025) [unverified]. Stereochemistry of the bicyclo[3.2.0]hept-2-ene → norbornene 1,3-shift, read as "dynamic matching": momentum on entering a diradical region carries into the exit.
  - REC · Kelly, Hirschi and Singleton 2009, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/ja9031083) [unverified]. Intramolecular ¹³C KIEs ("Newtonian" KIEs) were reproduced by trajectories but not by TST.
  - REC · Quijano and Singleton 2011, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/ja2043497) [unverified]. In vinyl-ether ozonolysis in solution, product distributions were read as fragmentation competing with IVR.
- **For the negation (partial):**
  - REC · Bailey and Singleton 2017, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/jacs.7b07175) [unverified]. For alkene hydroboration, both statistical and nonstatistical models first failed against the KIEs and selectivities, then were "redeemed" by refinement. This shows how far conclusions depend on the model.
  - MOD · The default assumption, discussed in Carpenter 2013 [unverified], is that solvent thermalises intermediates within picoseconds.
- **Why hard:**
  - Effects are small (a few percent in ratios; KIEs at the third decimal).
  - Predictions hinge on surface quality and on explicit solvent.
  - Trajectory counts are limited.
- **Standing:** narrowing. Accepted for bifurcations and very short-lived intermediates; generality disputed.
- **What would settle it:** generate one intermediate both thermally and by chemical activation, measure product ratios across solvents, and compare with explicit-solvent dynamics.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Carpenter, Singleton, Houk, Tantillo, Doubleday.
  - Negation: the default practice of physical organic chemistry. I identified no named advocate this session.
- **Concerns:** explaining, resting on observings of ratios and KIEs.

**Inferences (mine).** In this section the occurrence of dynamic effects is no longer disputed. What remains is (i) whether a cheaper static description can stand in for the dynamics, and (ii) how often the dynamics matter. Both are explaining binaries, and they resolve only against an independent observing such as product ratios, isotope-perturbed ratios or real-time imaging.

## 2. Concerted vs stepwise

**Takeaway.**
- "Concerted" is a classification whose membership depends on how long an intermediate lives compared with the observing timescale.
- Several famous disputes are settled for each reaction class (SN2 vs SET, 1,3-dipolar cycloadditions, E2 vs E1cB). The live ones are SNAr, PCET boundaries and asynchronous cycloadditions. There the observings (KIEs, LFER slopes, stereochemistry) fit a continuum, and the binary only works once a threshold has been fixed.

- **CONV · concerted:** two or more primitive bond changes occur within one elementary step, with no intermediate in between. IUPAC Gold Book "concerted" [unverified].
- **CONV · enforced mechanisms (Jencks 1980):** a stepwise path is impossible when the would-be intermediate lives less than about one bond vibration (~10⁻¹³ s). The mechanism is then "enforced" concerted. *Acc. Chem. Res.* (doi 10.1021/ar50153a001) [unverified].
- **CONV · species:** IUPAC counts interconverting forms as separate species when an experiment can tell them apart, and as one species when interconversion is fast on its timescale (doi 10.1351/goldbook.CT01038) [project corpus]. So "intermediate", and with it "stepwise", is relative to the observing.
- **CONV · synchronous vs asynchronous:** a concerted step is asynchronous when the bond changes progress unequally at the transition state. This is read from computed transition-state geometries, never measured directly.

### B4 · Are most SNAr reactions concerted?
- **Claim:** Most nucleophilic aromatic substitutions with common nucleophiles and leaving groups (Cl, Br, and F on weakly activated arenes) pass through a single transition state with no Meisenheimer intermediate.
- **Negation:** In the same reactions, a Meisenheimer σ-complex forms as a true intermediate (a local minimum on the free-energy surface in solution), so the reactions are stepwise.
- **For the claim:**
  - REC · Neumann, Hu and Ritter 2016, *Nature* (doi 10.1038/nature17667) [unverified]. Measured: rates and substituent effects for PhenoFluor deoxyfluorination of phenols. Adds: computation finds no Meisenheimer minimum, so the reaction is assigned as concerted.
  - REC · Kwan, Zeng, Besser and Jacobsen 2018, *Nat. Chem.* (doi 10.1038/s41557-018-0079-7) [unverified]. Measured: ¹²C/¹³C KIEs at the ipso carbon by a natural-abundance NMR method, for several SNAr reactions. Adds: computed KIEs for concerted versus stepwise models assign many reactions as concerted, with stepwise paths kept for strongly activated arenes carrying poor leaving groups (fluoride).
  - MOD · Review: Rohrbach, Smith and Murphy 2019, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* (doi 10.1002/anie.201902216) [unverified].
- **For the negation:**
  - OBS · Meisenheimer complexes have been isolated and characterised by NMR and X-ray for strongly activated arenes, such as 2,4,6-trinitroanisole with alkoxides (classical, pre-1950 onward) [unverified].
  - REC · The classical "element effect" (F > Cl ≈ Br > I) is read as rate-limiting addition, which implies an intermediate [unverified].
- **Why hard:**
  - Intermediates near the Jencks limit are invisible to product studies.
  - The KIE assignment needs computed models, and the solvent model can create or remove a shallow minimum.
  - Classical evidence comes from the activated extreme; modern evidence comes from the unactivated one.
- **Standing:** narrowing towards a continuum. Concerted is common for weakly activated arenes; stepwise holds for strongly activated ones; borderline cases remain.
- **What would settle it:** direct spectroscopic detection, or exclusion, of a Meisenheimer intermediate on a femto- to picosecond timescale in a reaction assigned as concerted. Alternatively, solvent-explicit free-energy surfaces that predict measured KIE trends blind.
- **Proponents:**
  - Concerted: Ritter, Jacobsen, Murphy.
  - Stepwise as default: classical physical organic tradition (Bunnett, Terrier) [unverified].
- **Concerns:** explaining, with the threshold set by convention.

### B5a · Is the parent and typical Diels–Alder reaction concerted (single transition state) rather than stepwise via a diradical?
- **Claim:** The thermal butadiene + ethylene and typical normal-demand Diels–Alder reactions are concerted. One transition state forms both σ-bonds, synchronously or asynchronously.
- **Negation:** The same reactions proceed stepwise through a diradical or zwitterionic intermediate that lives longer than a vibrational period.
- **For the claim:**
  - REC · Stereospecificity (retention of dienophile cis/trans geometry) and secondary KIEs, classical [unverified].
  - SIM/MOD · Houk, Li and Evanseck 1992, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* (doi 10.1002/anie.199206821) [unverified]: high-level transition structures are synchronous for the parent reaction.
- **For the negation (historical):**
  - MOD · Dewar 1984, *JACS* 106, 209 [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled] argued that multibond reactions cannot normally be synchronous.
  - REC · Femtosecond studies of retro-Diels–Alder reactions at high internal energy (Zewail group, mid-1990s) reported both concerted and stepwise channels [unverified]. These were contested because the excitation energies are far above thermal.
- **Why hard:** a very asynchronous concerted path and a stepwise path with a short-lived intermediate give the same stereochemistry and similar KIEs. The diradical's lifetime is model-dependent.
- **Standing:** settled for the parent and typical cases (concerted). Stepwise paths are accepted for specific polar or radical-stabilised partners. Open only at the borderline.
- **What would settle it:** already sufficient for typical cases. For borderline cases, real-time detection of an intermediate at thermal energies.
- **Proponents:**
  - Concerted: Houk, Woodward–Hoffmann tradition.
  - Stepwise (historical): Dewar.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B5b · Is "two-stage one-step" a distinct mechanistic class from "asynchronous concerted"?
- **Claim:** Some polar cycloadditions form the two bonds in two stages inside one elementary step. The second bond begins to form only after the first is largely complete, as identified by electron localisation function (ELF) bonding-evolution analysis. This is a kind of mechanism different from ordinary asynchronous concerted reactions.
- **Negation:** For the same reactions, "two-stage one-step" picks out no observable or energetic difference from a highly asynchronous concerted reaction. It is a partition of one continuous path chosen by the analyst.
- **For the claim:** MOD · Domingo and Sáez 2009, *Org. Biomol. Chem.* (doi 10.1039/b909611f) [unverified]; Domingo 2016, Molecular Electron Density Theory, *Molecules* (doi 10.3390/molecules21101319) [unverified].
- **For the negation:** CONV/MOD · No observing (KIE, stereochemistry, rate) has been proposed that separates the two classes. Activation-strain analyses treat asynchronicity as a continuous quantity. For example, Vermeeren, Hamlin, Fernández and Bickelhaupt 2020, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* (doi 10.1002/anie.201914582) [unverified], relate the greater asynchronicity under Lewis-acid catalysis to reduced Pauli repulsion, not to FMO LUMO-lowering. That paper opens a further explaining binary (orbital vs Pauli account of Lewis-acid catalysis) which rests on energy-decomposition terms, and those terms are not observables.
- **Why hard:** the stage boundary is placed by a topological analysis of a computed density, and the threshold is set by convention.
- **Standing:** open but terminological. It is not widely adopted outside the proponents' school.
- **What would settle it:** a measurable prediction that differs between the two classes, such as a KIE pattern or a field-dependence signature.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Domingo and co-workers.
  - Negation: implicit in the activation-strain and conventional transition-state practice of Bickelhaupt and Houk [unverified as explicit critiques].
- **Concerns:** a convention or definition presented as an explaining.

### B6 · Does a given PCET reaction occur by concerted proton–electron transfer (CPET) rather than by stepwise ET-PT or PT-ET?
- **Scope split:** the question is decidable for one system at a time. Across systems, the PCET reactions form a continuum (asynchronous CPET). So two binaries are kept apart: (i) for system S under conditions C, is there a kinetically competent intermediate (ET or PT product)? (ii) Is CPET versus stepwise a sharp kind-distinction? Answer to (ii): no. Darcy, Koronkiewicz, Parada and Mayer 2018, *Acc. Chem. Res.* (doi 10.1021/acs.accounts.8b00373) [unverified].
- **Claim (i):** Under C, S transfers e⁻ and H⁺ in one kinetic step, with no intermediate that lives long enough to be kinetically competent.
- **Negation (i):** Under C, S passes through an ET or PT intermediate that is kinetically competent.
- **For CPET:**
  - REC · Diagnostic logic: if the observed rate exceeds the maximum allowed by the thermodynamics of either first step, stepwise paths are excluded. Large H/D KIEs and characteristic driving-force and pH dependences support CPET. Mayer 2004, *Annu. Rev. Phys. Chem.* (doi 10.1146/annurev.physchem.55.091602.094446) [unverified]; Costentin, Robert and Savéant's electrochemical PCET reviews (*Chem. Rev.* 2008; *Acc. Chem. Res.* 2010) [unverified; DOIs not reliably recalled].
  - OBS/REC · Parada et al. 2019, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.aaw4675) [unverified]: intramolecular photo-PCET rates decreased at high driving force, a Marcus inverted region for CPET.
- **For stepwise:** REC · In many systems with an accessible ET or PT intermediate, rates follow pH and pKa as a pre-equilibrium followed by a rate-limiting step requires (Weinberg et al. 2012, *Chem. Rev.*, doi 10.1021/cr200177j) [unverified].
- **Why hard:**
  - Every diagnostic is indirect (rate, KIE, thermodynamic bounds).
  - Intermediates may exist but not be kinetically competent.
  - Proton vibronic theory (Hammes-Schiffer) blurs the boundary with asynchronous CPET.
- **Standing:** (i) settled for many well-studied model systems and open for many enzymes (photosystem II, ribonucleotide reductase); (ii) settled as a continuum.
- **What would settle it (per system):** time-resolved detection of the intermediate, or proof of its absence, at the required lifetime, combined with KIE and thermodynamic bounds.
- **Proponents:** Mayer, Savéant/Costentin, Hammes-Schiffer, Meyer, Knowles, Hammarström.
- **Concerns:** (i) observing (reconstruction); (ii) convention.

### Settled anchors in section 2 (compact form)
- **B7 · SN2 vs single-electron transfer (SET):**
  - Claim: ordinary SN2 reactions of alkyl halides with common nucleophiles go through a radical-pair (SET) intermediate. Negation: they go by a polar one-step displacement.
  - For SET: REC · Ashby's radical-clock and cyclisation-probe evidence for SET in hydride and organometallic reactions (Ashby 1988, *Acc. Chem. Res.*, doi 10.1021/ar00155a001) [unverified].
  - Against SET: REC · Newcomb and Curran 1988, *Acc. Chem. Res.* (doi 10.1021/ar00154a004) [unverified] showed that the probe results could come from halogen-atom-transfer chains.
  - MOD · Pross and Shaik's valence-bond "SN2 as electron shift" is a model of the polar transition state, not a two-step mechanism [unverified].
  - Standing: settled per class. Polar SN2 is the norm. SET and SRN1 are accepted for specific substrates (nitrobenzylic halides; photostimulated aryl halides). Savéant's concerted vs stepwise dissociative electron transfer gives the electrochemical analogue (Savéant 1987, *JACS*, doi 10.1021/ja00256a018) [unverified].
  - Concerns: explaining.
- **B8 · E2 vs E1cB:**
  - Claim: a given β-elimination is stepwise through a carbanion. Negation: it is concerted.
  - REC · Diagnostics: H/D exchange at the β-carbon during reaction (shows a reversible carbanion), leaving-group KIEs, and Brønsted slopes. On More O'Ferrall–Jencks diagrams the possibilities form a continuum.
  - Jencks's lifetime criterion decides membership: E1cB requires a carbanion longer-lived than a vibration (doi 10.1021/ar50153a001) [unverified].
  - Standing: settled per system; the boundary is set by convention.
  - Concerns: observing (reconstruction) for each system.
- **B9 · 1,3-dipolar cycloadditions:**
  - Claim: they are concerted (Huisgen). Negation: they go stepwise through a diradical (Firestone 1968, *J. Org. Chem.*, doi 10.1021/jo01270a005) [unverified].
  - REC · Stereospecificity and solvent-insensitive rates favoured the concerted view (Huisgen 1976 reply, *J. Org. Chem.*, doi 10.1021/jo00866a001) [unverified].
  - Stepwise zwitterionic paths were later shown for sterically hindered, strongly polarised partners, for example thiocarbonyl ylides with tetracyanoethylene (Huisgen, mid-1980s) [unverified].
  - Standing: settled per class. Concerted is the norm; stepwise is accepted in documented exceptions.
  - Concerns: explaining.

**Inferences (mine).**
- Each concerted/stepwise binary becomes decidable only after a lifetime threshold is fixed (about one vibration, or the experiment's resolution). That fixing is a convention.
- The observings (stereochemistry, KIEs, rate–driving-force relations) constrain the continuum, but they do not by themselves place reactions into two kinds.

**Gaps (section 2).**
- I could not verify 2023–26 SNAr work (for example ultrafast spectroscopy of Meisenheimer species, or new ¹³C/¹⁸F KIE sets).
- I could not check whether any explicit, peer-reviewed critique of "two-stage one-step" exists.

## 3. Tunnelling, quantum effects and enzyme catalysis

**Takeaway.**
- Three things are settled. Hydrogen tunnelling in enzymes is settled as a reconstruction from KIEs. Carbon tunnelling and tunnelling control are settled in cryogenic matrices.
- The enzyme disputes are mostly explaining binaries in which "dynamics" carries two definitions.
- Once the definitions are separated, two observing conflicts remain: heavy-enzyme effects, and the adenylate-kinase lid rate.

- **CONV · KIE diagnostics of tunnelling (Bell criteria):** the semiclassical H/D primary KIE is at most about 7 at 298 K; A_H/A_D lies within roughly 0.7–1.4; and ΔEa(D−H) is at most about 1.2 kcal mol⁻¹. Values outside these limits are read as tunnelling. Source: Bell 1980, *The Tunnel Effect in Chemistry* [unverified]. The project's own chemistry text uses the same "near seven" ceiling. That is a project statement, not an external source.
- **CONV · "dynamical effect"** has two definitions.
  - (i) Warshel/Truhlar: anything not captured by equilibrium TST, that is, a transmission coefficient κ < 1 or nonequilibrium energy flow.
  - (ii) Klinman/Kohen/Schwartz: protein motions on fs–ms timescales, including equilibrium conformational sampling, that modulate the barrier or the donor–acceptor distance (DAD).
  - Kohen 2015 sorts the disputes into substantive and semantic ones: *Acc. Chem. Res.* (doi 10.1021/ar500322s) [unverified]. Much of B12–B14 is decided by which definition is in use.

### B10 · Does carbon tunnelling contribute substantially to thermal reactions near room temperature?
- **Claim:** For some thermal organic rearrangements at 250–350 K, carbon tunnelling contributes a measurable share of the rate. It shows as ¹²C/¹³C KIEs above TST predictions, or as curvature in Arrhenius plots.
- **Negation:** For the same reactions at 250–350 K, heavy-atom tunnelling is negligible. KIEs and Arrhenius behaviour match TST without tunnelling, within error.
- **For the claim:**
  - OBS/REC · Zuev et al. 2003, *Science* (Sheridan, Truhlar, Borden; doi 10.1126/science.1079294) [unverified]. Measured: ring-expansion rate of 1-methylcyclobutylfluorocarbene in a nitrogen matrix at about 8–16 K; the rate was nearly independent of temperature. Adds: tunnelling from the vibrational ground state, matched by computed rates.
  - REC · Gonzalez-James et al. 2010, *JACS* (Singleton, Borden; doi 10.1021/ja1055593) [unverified]. Measured: intramolecular ¹²C/¹³C KIEs for the ring opening of the cyclopropylcarbinyl radical, down to well below 0 °C. Adds: the KIEs exceed semiclassical predictions; tunnelling-corrected computation fits them.
  - SIM · Doubleday et al. 2017, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled]. Computed tunnelling contributions were substantial in 13 reactions at 250 K and above.
- **For the negation:** MOD · Common practice treats heavy-atom tunnelling as negligible above about 200 K. Most computed contributions have no individual experimental confirmation (my inference).
- **Why hard:** the effect is a correction of tens of percent in rate and a few percent in ¹³C KIEs, and it cannot be separated from errors in barrier shape. The tunnelling share is a simulation output, not a measured quantity.
- **Standing:** settled at cryogenic temperatures (2003 onward). Near room temperature it is narrowing towards "non-negligible", mostly on computational grounds and a thin experimental base.
- **What would settle it:** temperature-dependent intramolecular ¹³C KIEs and Arrhenius curvature over 150–350 K for reactions predicted to differ in barrier width, with blind predictions made with and without tunnelling.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Borden, Singleton, Sheridan, Doubleday, Kozuch.
  - Negation: the default assumption; no named advocate identified.
- **Concerns:** observing (magnitude) and explaining (share).

### B11a · Tunnelling control in cryogenic matrices (settled anchor)
- **Claim:** In solid argon at 11 K, methylhydroxycarbene converts to acetaldehyde, reached over a higher but narrower barrier, rather than to vinyl alcohol, reached over a lower but wider one. The product is selected by tunnelling probability, not barrier height.
- **Negation:** Under the same conditions, the product follows the lower barrier.
- **Observings:**
  - OBS · Schreiner et al. 2011, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.1203761) [unverified]. Measured: matrix-isolation IR of the carbene, made by flash pyrolysis of pyruvic acid; at 11 K it decayed to acetaldehyde with a half-life of about an hour, and the O–D isotopologue was far more persistent [details unverified]. Adds: computed barriers and tunnelling rates rank acetaldehyde first. The authors named tunnelling control as a third paradigm, beside kinetic and thermodynamic control.
  - OBS (prior) · Schreiner et al. 2008, *Nature*: hydroxymethylene tunnels to formaldehyde at 11 K (doi 10.1038/nature07010) [unverified].
  - Perspective: Schreiner 2017, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/jacs.7b06035) [unverified].
- **Standing:** settled (an observing plus a computed reconstruction).
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B11b · Does tunnelling control decide selectivity in solution near room temperature?
- **Claim:** For some solution reactions near 300 K, tunnelling makes the major product the one reached by the narrower barrier, not the lower one.
- **Negation:** For the same reactions near 300 K, product ratios follow the free-energy barriers. Tunnelling is a correction that never inverts selectivity.
- **Observings:**
  - I recall no widely accepted experimental case at room temperature. The claims are mainly computational, for H-shifts (discussed in the 2017 perspective) [unverified].
  - Enzymatic H-transfers with KIEs of 50–100 or more show that tunnelling can dominate a rate. They do not show a selectivity inversion (my inference).
- **Why hard:** a test needs two competing channels with inverted height and width ordering, both measurable near 300 K.
- **Standing:** open (thin evidence).
- **What would settle it:** a product ratio near 300 K that switches with H/D substitution, where TST predicts the opposite major product.
- **Proponents:** Schreiner, Kozuch, Borden.
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B12 · Does the temperature dependence of enzymatic KIEs report protein sampling of donor–acceptor distance?
- **Claim:** Wild-type soybean lipoxygenase-1 (SLO-1), thermophilic alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) and DHFR show intrinsic KIEs that hardly change with temperature, and mutants show more temperature dependence. This is explained by protein motions sampling a narrow DAD distribution (wild type) or a broad one (mutants). The KIE's temperature dependence then measures how well the protein preorganises tunnelling-ready configurations. These are "Marcus-like" or environmentally coupled tunnelling models.
- **Negation:** Models without explicit DAD-gating dynamics reproduce the same temperature dependences. Examples are ensemble-averaged variational TST with multidimensional tunnelling, and empirical valence bond (EVB) models with quantised nuclei. On this view the KIE pattern does not specifically measure DAD distribution width.
- **For the claim:**
  - OBS/REC · Kohen et al. 1999, *Nature*: thermophilic ADH (doi 10.1038/20981) [unverified]. Measured: KIEs and Arrhenius parameters over about 5–65 °C, with a change in behaviour below about 30 °C. Adds: the protein is more rigid at low temperature, which alters tunnelling.
  - REC · Knapp, Rickert and Klinman 2002, *JACS*: SLO-1 (doi 10.1021/ja012205t) [unverified]. Measured: a KIE near 80 at room temperature with small ΔEa, in wild type and mutants.
  - OBS · Hu et al. 2014, *JACS*: an SLO double mutant had a room-temperature KIE in the hundreds (doi 10.1021/ja502726s) [unverified].
  - Review: Klinman and Kohen 2013, *Annu. Rev. Biochem.* (doi 10.1146/annurev-biochem-051710-133623) [unverified].
- **For the negation:**
  - SIM · Pu, Gao and Truhlar 2006, *Chem. Rev.* (doi 10.1021/cr050308e) [unverified]. Variational TST with multidimensional tunnelling reproduces enzymatic KIEs with transmission coefficients near 1.
  - SIM · Liu and Warshel 2007, *J. Phys. Chem. B*: DHFR (doi 10.1021/jp070938f) [unverified]. The KIE temperature dependence was reproduced from equilibrium DAD changes.
  - MOD · Kamerlin and Warshel 2010, *Proteins* (doi 10.1002/prot.22654) [unverified].
- **Why hard:**
  - A few KIE numbers are fitted by several models with adjustable parameters.
  - DAD distributions are never measured directly; X-ray, hydrogen–deuterium exchange (HDX) and computation give proxies.
  - The two definitions of "dynamics" collide here.
- **Standing:** open. Both sides accept tunnelling and that DAD matters. The dispute is whether this counts as "dynamics", and whether the temperature dependence of the KIE is a specific DAD reporter. It is narrowing on the observing side through HDX studies linking distal SLO-1 motions to the activation barrier (Offenbacher et al. 2017, *ACS Cent. Sci.*, doi 10.1021/acscentsci.7b00142) [unverified].
- **What would settle it:** direct, temperature-resolved DAD distributions (from crystallography, or from DAD-sensitive vibrational probes) in wild type and mutants, correlated with the KIE temperature dependence and scored against blind predictions from both families of models.
- **Proponents (as I recall) [unverified]:**
  - Claim: Klinman, Kohen, Scrutton, Hay.
  - Negation: Warshel, Kamerlin. Truhlar and Gao accept tunnelling but not the dynamical reading.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B13 · Do femtosecond "promoting vibrations" of the protein speed up the chemical step?
- **Claim:** Particular protein motions on fs–ps timescales couple to the reaction coordinate and raise the rate of the chemical step above what the equilibrium free-energy barrier predicts.
- **Negation:** In the same enzymes, such motions belong to the thermal ensemble and do not raise the chemical-step rate above equilibrium TST (κ ≈ 1). Changing protein mass acts through equilibrium electrostatics or recrossing, not through promotion.
- **For the claim:**
  - SIM · Antoniou and Schwartz 2001, *J. Phys. Chem. B* (doi 10.1021/jp004413m) [unverified].
  - SIM · Basner and Schwartz 2005, *JACS*: transition-path sampling of lactate dehydrogenase (doi 10.1021/ja051936q) [unverified].
  - OBS/REC · Silva, Murkin and Schramm 2011, *PNAS*: "heavy" (¹³C, ¹⁵N, ²H) human purine nucleoside phosphorylase (doi 10.1073/pnas.1111099108) [unverified]. Measured: the single-turnover chemical step was slower in the heavy enzyme, while steady-state kcat was less affected. Adds: femtosecond protein vibrations couple to barrier crossing.
- **For the negation:**
  - OBS/REC · Luk et al. 2013, *PNAS*: heavy *E. coli* DHFR (doi 10.1073/pnas.1312437110) [unverified]. Measured: hydride transfer slowed modestly, with temperature dependence. Adds: QM/MM attributes this to altered coupling with the environment and recrossing, not to promoting vibrations.
  - Neutral or mixed · Wang, Singh, Czekster, Kohen and Schramm 2014, *JACS*: heavy-DHFR effects read as going "beyond promoting vibrations" (doi 10.1021/ja501936d) [unverified]. The evidence sits between the two sides.
- **Why hard:**
  - Heavy labelling shifts every protein frequency and can also change stability and electrostatics.
  - The effects are small.
  - Single-turnover rates can mix several steps.
- **Standing:** open. Heavy-enzyme effects on the chemical step have been observed in several enzymes; their interpretation is split.
- **What would settle it:** site-specific heavy labelling of the proposed promoting residues, measured by pre-steady-state rates and ultrafast spectroscopy, and compared with equilibrium-only simulations of the same mass effect.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Schwartz, Schramm.
  - Negation: Warshel; Allemann, Moliner and Tuñón (coupling with the environment, but no promotion).
- **Concerns:** explaining, built on an observing of mass effects.

### B14 · Does electrostatic preorganization account for most enzymatic rate enhancement?
- **Claim:** For enzymes in general, most of the rate enhancement over the uncatalysed reaction in water comes from a preorganised electrostatic environment that stabilises the transition state. Conformational dynamics add little to the chemical step.
- **Negation:** For a substantial class of enzymes, conformational dynamics contribute to the chemical step at least as much as static preorganization does.
- **For the claim:**
  - MOD/SIM · Warshel 1978, *PNAS* (doi 10.1073/pnas.75.11.5250) [unverified]; Warshel et al. 2006, *Chem. Rev.* (doi 10.1021/cr0503106) [unverified].
  - OBS/REC · Fried, Bagchi and Boxer 2014, *Science*: ketosteroid isomerase (KSI) (doi 10.1126/science.1259802) [unverified]. Measured: vibrational Stark shifts of a carbonyl probe bound in the active site of KSI and its mutants, giving fields of order 10⁸ V cm⁻¹ that correlate with log(rate). Adds: the field accounts for a large share of the rate enhancement.
  - OBS · Zheng, Ji, Mathews and Boxer 2023, *Nat. Chem.* (doi 10.1038/s41557-023-01287-x) [unverified]. An engineered increase in the active-site field speeded up KSI.
- **For the negation:**
  - REC · Henzler-Wildman et al. 2007, *Nature* (doi 10.1038/nature06407) [unverified].
  - REC · Otten et al. 2020, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.abd3623) [unverified]: directed evolution reshapes the conformational landscape to raise activity.
  - SIM/REC · Jara et al., *eLife* 13:RP93099, final version 2025, https://elifesciences.org/articles/93099 [project corpus]. A wide transition-state ensemble is treated as a key part of enzyme catalysis. The specific enzyme and methods were not checked.
- **Why hard:**
  - The "share" is not measured. It depends on model decompositions and on the reference reaction chosen.
  - Motions change fields, so the two factors are not independent.
  - KSI is an unusually favourable case for Stark probes.
- **Standing:** open. My reading, not a verified consensus: preorganization dominates the chemical step in many enzymes, while dynamics matter for binding, product release and evolvability.
- **What would settle it:** a panel of enzymes with Stark-probe fields and pre-steady-state rates, under perturbations that change dynamics but not the mean field, and the reverse.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Warshel, Kamerlin, Boxer (in its electric-field form).
  - Negation: Kern, Klinman, Schwartz, and Hammes-Schiffer/Benkovic (networks of coupled promoting motions in DHFR; Agarwal et al. 2002, *PNAS*, doi 10.1073/pnas.042698699) [unverified].
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B15a · Does lid opening limit adenylate-kinase turnover? (observing conflict)
- **Claim:** Under turnover, adenylate-kinase (Adk) lid opening proceeds at a rate close to kcat and limits turnover.
- **Negation:** Under turnover, lid opening and closing are much faster than kcat, so the lid does not limit turnover.
- **For the claim:** OBS/REC · Wolf-Watz et al. 2004, *Nat. Struct. Mol. Biol.* (doi 10.1038/nsmb821) [unverified]. Measured: NMR relaxation-dispersion exchange rates in mesophilic and thermophilic Adk during turnover, matching kcat. Adds: a two-state fit identifies the exchange as lid opening, which is then taken as rate-limiting.
- **For the negation:** OBS · Aviram et al. 2018, *PNAS* (doi 10.1073/pnas.1720448115) [project corpus]. Measured: photon-by-photon single-molecule FRET showed substrate-dependent domain motions much faster than turnover (microsecond scale, as I recall [unverified]). Adds: some other step limits the rate.
- **Why hard:** the two methods use different probes (nuclear spins vs dye pairs), temperatures and constructs, and "opening" may mean a local or a global motion.
- **Standing:** open; I could verify no resolution from 2024–26.
- **What would settle it:** NMR dispersion and smFRET on the same construct under the same conditions, with controls for dye effects.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Kern.
  - Negation: Haran.
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B15b · Did a "dynamic knockout" show that millisecond motions affect DHFR's chemical step?
- **Claim:** In the *E. coli* DHFR mutant N23PP/S148A, the loss of millisecond conformational fluctuations is what lowers the hydride-transfer rate.
- **Negation:** In the same mutant, changed electrostatic preorganization accounts for the lower rate.
- **For the claim:** OBS/REC · Bhabha et al. 2011, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.1198542) [unverified]. Measured: NMR showed loss of millisecond exchange, and pre-steady-state hydride transfer was slower.
- **For the negation:**
  - OBS/REC · Loveridge et al. 2012, *Nat. Chem.* (doi 10.1038/nchem.1378) [unverified]: KIE and temperature analyses found the chemical step essentially unaffected by the loss of dynamics.
  - SIM · Adamczyk et al. 2011, *PNAS* (doi 10.1073/pnas.1111252108) [unverified].
- **Standing:** open.
- **What would settle it:** independent replication of the rates, together with Stark-probe fields in the mutant.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Wright, Dyson.
  - Negation: Allemann, Warshel.
- **Concerns:** explaining, with a partial observing dispute about the rates.

**Inferences (mine).**
- Under definition (i), most simulations give κ ≈ 1, so the nonequilibrium-dynamics claim is weak.
- Under definition (ii), conformational sampling clearly matters, but it is equilibrium sampling and compatible with TST and preorganization.
- Much of the "dynamics vs electrostatics" binary therefore collapses into a question of definition. The part that does not collapse is the heavy-enzyme effects (B13) and the lid-rate conflict (B15a).

**Gaps (section 3).**
- I could not verify 2023–26 heavy-enzyme studies, SLO-1 network or temperature-resolved structural work, or the enzyme and methods in Jara et al.
- I could not verify whether a room-temperature tunnelling-control case has been reported.

## 4. Field and environment effects

**Takeaway.** This section holds the field's live observing binaries: cavity-modified thermal rates, CISS at the measured magnitude, and the biological magnetic compass. Here the dispute is whether the effect happens at all, not only how to explain it. Field catalysis in single-molecule junctions and the occurrence of force-selected products are better established. Their mechanism and how far they generalise remain open.

- **CONV · vibrational strong coupling (VSC):** a cavity mode and a molecular vibration exchange energy faster than either decays. The sign is a resolved Rabi splitting into upper and lower polariton bands in the IR transmission spectrum. "Cavity effect on rate" is judged by the rate constant on resonance against detuned cavities and cavity-free controls.

### B16a · Does VSC change thermal reaction rates? (observing)
- **Claim:** For specific thermal reactions in Fabry–Pérot microcavities, tuning a cavity mode into resonance with a reactant or solvent vibration (with Rabi splitting observed) changes the rate constant compared with detuned cavities and cavity-free controls at the same temperature.
- **Negation:** For the same reactions, cavities and temperatures, resonant and detuned rate constants are equal within error.
- **For the claim:**
  - OBS · Thomas et al. 2016, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* (doi 10.1002/anie.201605504) [unverified]. Measured: rate of fluoride desilylation of 1-phenyl-2-trimethylsilylacetylene, followed spectroscopically inside the cavity. The rate fell (about 5-fold, figure unverified) when the Si–C stretch band was coupled.
  - OBS · Thomas et al. 2019, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.aau7742) [unverified]. The branching between Si–C and Si–O cleavage in a bis-silyl substrate shifted under VSC.
  - OBS · Lather et al. 2019, *Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.* (doi 10.1002/anie.201905407) [unverified]. Rate enhancement in ester solvolysis when the C=O bands of both reactant and solvent were coupled ("cooperative VSC").
  - OBS · Ahn et al. 2023, *Science* (Simpkins group; doi 10.1126/science.ade7147) [unverified]. An isocyanate + alcohol urethane-forming reaction slowed under VSC of the NCO stretch. The change tracked Rabi splitting and detuning.
  - Adds: the authors attribute the effects to hybrid light–matter states changing the ground-state reaction landscape. No accepted mechanism has been given.
- **For the negation:**
  - OBS · Imperatore, Asbury and Giebink 2021, *J. Chem. Phys.* (doi 10.1063/5.0046307) [unverified]. They repeated the cooperative-VSC ester solvolysis and found no rate change beyond error.
  - OBS · Wiesehan and Xiong 2021, *J. Chem. Phys.* (doi 10.1063/5.0077549) [unverified]. An independent repeat of the same reaction found negligible enhancement.
  - REC · Later analyses, as I recall including work from the Simpkins group, report that nonuniform cavity length can bias rates extracted from in-cavity spectroscopy [unverified; citation not reliably recalled].
  - MOD · Galego et al. 2019, *Phys. Rev. X* (doi 10.1103/PhysRevX.9.021057) [unverified] and Li, Nitzan and Subotnik 2020, *J. Chem. Phys.* (doi 10.1063/5.0006472) [unverified]. Within equilibrium TST, collective VSC with about 10⁶–10¹⁰ molecules per mode should leave thermal rates essentially unchanged.
- **Why hard:**
  - Cavity volumes are tiny.
  - Rates are read through cavity-filtered spectra.
  - Temperature, mirror surfaces, concentration and cavity-length uniformity are hard to control.
  - Effects are a few-fold at most.
  - There is no theory that predicts magnitude, so no number sets the size a null result must reach.
- **Standing:** open and contested. Independent positive reports exist (Ebbesen/George, Simpkins), and independent failures exist for one specific claim. Reviews state the conflict, for example Simpkins, Dunkelberger and Vurgaftman 2023, *Chem. Rev.* (doi 10.1021/acs.chemrev.2c00774) [unverified]. I could not check 2025–26 replication efforts.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A multi-laboratory round robin with identical cavities and in-situ temperature measurement.
  - Blinded tuning, with mirror chemistry the same on and off resonance.
  - Rate constants measured outside the cavity after quenching.
  - Pre-registered k-versus-detuning curves.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Ebbesen, George, Simpkins.
  - Negation (on replication): Giebink, Asbury, Xiong.
  - Theory spans both: Yuen-Zhou, Feist, Nitzan, Subotnik, Huo, Rubio, Foley.
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B16b · If VSC rate effects exist, are they collective-polaritonic?
- **Claim:** Rate changes arise from collective coupling of many molecules to the cavity mode. They scale with collective Rabi splitting, which goes as √(concentration).
- **Negation:** Rate changes arise from local or single-molecule effects. Candidates are dark-state or disorder-mediated channels, hot spots, and cavity-modified dielectric or thermal environments. None needs the collective polariton.
- **Observings and models:**
  - REC · Concentration and detuning dependences are reported (2019, 2023 papers above) [unverified].
  - MOD · Campos-Gonzalez-Angulo, Ribeiro and Yuen-Zhou 2019, *Nat. Commun.* (doi 10.1038/s41467-019-12636-1) [unverified] predict resonant effects only under restrictive conditions.
  - OBS · Chen et al. 2022, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.add0276) [unverified]. 2D-IR showed polaritons speeding vibrational energy redistribution in Fe(CO)₅. This is a real dynamics effect, but on vibrationally excited polaritons, not thermal rates.
- **Standing:** open. It depends on how B16a is resolved.
- **What would settle it:** rate change as a function of molecule number at fixed per-molecule coupling, compared with the theory's scaling.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

- **CONV · CISS:** chirality-induced spin selectivity. Electrons transmitted through, or transferred across, chiral molecules acquire a spin polarisation whose sign follows the molecule's handedness. The observing criteria are platform-specific: Mott polarimetry of photoelectrons, magnetoresistance in a junction with one ferromagnetic contact, enantiospecific electrochemistry or adsorption on magnetised electrodes, and EPR of radical pairs.

### B18a · Does CISS occur at the reported magnitudes? (observing)
- **Claim:** Under the reported conditions at room temperature, electrons passing through or across chiral organic molecules acquire handedness-dependent spin polarisation of order 10–60%, not attributable to instrumental or contact artefacts.
- **Negation:** Under the same conditions and observing criteria, no handedness-dependent polarisation remains above artefact levels. Artefacts include ferromagnetic-contact magnetoresistance and asymmetric junctions.
- **For the claim:**
  - OBS · Ray et al. 1999, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.283.5403.814) [unverified]: asymmetric scattering of polarised photoelectrons by chiral films.
  - OBS · Göhler et al. 2011, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.1199339) [unverified]. Mott polarimetry of photoelectrons from gold through self-assembled monolayers of dsDNA gave large polarisation (about 60%, figure unverified).
  - OBS · Banerjee-Ghosh et al. 2018, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.aar4265) [unverified]. Enantiomers adsorbed at different rates on achiral ferromagnetic substrates magnetised up or down.
  - OBS · Mtangi et al. 2017, *JACS* (doi 10.1021/jacs.6b12971) [unverified]. Chiral-coated anodes cut H₂O₂ by-product in water oxidation.
  - OBS · Eckvahl et al. 2023, *Science* (Wasielewski group; doi 10.1126/science.adj5328) [unverified]. Time-resolved EPR on covalent donor–chiral bridge–acceptor molecules showed spin polarisation that depends on handedness after photoinduced electron transfer, with no metal electrode involved.
- **For the negation, or against the magnitudes:**
  - MOD · Bardarson's theorem: in two-terminal, single-channel, linear-response transport with time-reversal symmetry, no spin polarisation can appear. Two-terminal magnetoresistance therefore needs extra ingredients (Evers et al. 2022, *Adv. Mater.*, doi 10.1002/adma.202106629) [unverified].
  - REC · Evers et al. 2022 [unverified] also survey contact- and ferromagnet-related artefacts in conductive-probe AFM and two-terminal data.
- **Why hard:**
  - Each platform has its own artefacts.
  - The effect is reported in many different quantities that are not mutually calibrated.
  - Theory cannot give a target magnitude.
- **Standing:** narrowing towards "occurs". Reviews: Bloom, Paltiel, Naaman and Waldeck 2024, *Chem. Rev.* (doi 10.1021/acs.chemrev.3c00661) [unverified]; Naaman, Paltiel and Waldeck 2019, *Nat. Rev. Chem.* (doi 10.1038/s41570-019-0087-1) [unverified]. The 2023 EPR result removed electrode artefacts in one platform. Transport magnitudes are still contested.
- **What would settle it:** cross-platform calibration on one molecule set (photoemission, junction, EPR), with racemic and achiral controls, run in independent laboratories.
- **Proponents:**
  - Claim: Naaman, Waldeck, Paltiel, Wasielewski.
  - Doubts on magnitudes: Evers, Yan and other theorists.
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B18b · Is CISS explained by single-particle spin–orbit coupling in the molecule?
- **Claim:** Spin–orbit coupling of the chiral molecule's own electrons, treated in a one-electron picture, accounts for the measured polarisations.
- **Negation:** For the same systems, one-electron molecular spin–orbit coupling (meV-scale in light elements) falls short by orders of magnitude. Further ingredients are needed: electron correlation or exchange, electron–vibration coupling, electrode spin–orbit coupling with orbital polarisation, or interfacial spin-dependent charge reorganisation.
- **Models:**
  - MOD · Evers et al. 2022 [unverified] concluded that simple models fail on magnitude.
  - MOD · Liu, Xiao, Koo and Yan 2021, *Nat. Mater.* (doi 10.1038/s41563-021-00924-5) [unverified] proposed chirality-induced orbital polarisation converted to spin at the metal contact.
  - MOD · Fay and Limmer 2021, *Nano Lett.* (doi 10.1021/acs.nanolett.1c01707) [unverified] tied polarisation in photoinduced ET to spin–orbit coupling interfering with exchange.
- **Standing:** open. No account is accepted.
- **What would settle it:** a model predicting the magnitude and its dependence on length, temperature and element substitution (for example heavy-atom bridges), tested blind.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B17a · Did an oriented electric field catalyse a Diels–Alder reaction in a single-molecule junction?
- **Claim:** In an STM break-junction setup, the Diels–Alder reaction between a surface-bound dienophile and a tip-bound furan diene speeds up when the applied field points along the direction that stabilises a charge-transfer resonance of the transition state.
- **Negation:** Under the same setup, the field-orientation dependence of the bond-formation signal does not come from transition-state stabilisation. Alternatives are tip–surface geometry, molecular alignment or approach frequency, or electrochemistry under bias.
- **Observings:**
  - OBS · Aragonès et al. 2016, *Nature* (doi 10.1038/nature16989) [unverified]. Measured: how often current "blinks" appeared, read as C–C bond formation making a molecular junction. The frequency rose about 5-fold for one bias polarity and not the other. Adds: computed field effects on the transition state.
  - MOD · Shaik, Mandal and Ramanan 2016, *Nat. Chem.* (doi 10.1038/nchem.2651) [unverified]. Oriented fields act as "smart reagents".
- **Standing:** narrowing. I know of no retraction. The result is broadly accepted as field catalysis, but data come from few systems.
- **What would settle it:** repeats in other laboratories and other reactions, with junction geometry fixed independently, for example by simultaneous force measurement.
- **Proponents:** Coote, Diez-Perez, Ciampi, Shaik.
- **Concerns:** observing and explaining.

### B17b · Do externally applied fields catalyse reactions in bulk isotropic solution?
- **Claim:** Macroscopic external fields measurably catalyse reactions in bulk isotropic solution through the oriented-field mechanism.
- **Negation:** In bulk isotropic solution, random orientation and screening cancel the effect. Observed field effects need interfaces (electrode double layers, droplets), pre-oriented molecules or built-in charged groups.
- **Observings:**
  - OBS · Gorin et al. 2013, *JACS* (Kanan group) [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled]. The double-layer field at an electrode changed the selectivity of a Rh-porphyrin carbene reaction. An earlier 2012 paper from the group reported a field effect on an epoxide rearrangement [unverified].
  - OBS/REC · Fried et al. 2014 (B14) and Coote's work on charged functional groups [unverified] show fields built into enzymes or molecules.
  - Review: Shaik and co-workers 2020, *JACS* perspective on electric-field-mediated chemistry [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled].
- **Standing:** open. Interfacial and internal fields are accepted; true bulk catalysis by an applied field is not established, as far as I recall.
- **What would settle it:** reproducible bulk rate changes with applied field strength and orientation, with electrode-interface contributions excluded.
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B19a · Do Earth-strength fields change radical-pair yields in cryptochrome enough to serve as a compass?
- **Prior, settled:** OBS · millitesla magnetic-field effects on radical-pair recombination yields in solution, together with CIDNP. Steiner and Ulrich 1989, *Chem. Rev.* (doi 10.1021/cr00091a003) [unverified].
- **Claim:** At about 50 μT, cryptochrome radical pairs in bird retinas change their product yields enough to underlie magnetic compass orientation.
- **Negation:** At about 50 μT, cryptochrome yield changes are too small or too short-lived to be biologically detected. The compass rests on another mechanism.
- **For the claim:**
  - OBS · Maeda et al. 2008, *Nature* (doi 10.1038/nature06834) [unverified]. A carotenoid–porphyrin–fullerene triad responded to fields of order 50 μT at low temperature.
  - OBS · Xu et al. 2021, *Nature* (doi 10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9) [unverified]. Purified songbird cryptochrome 4 showed magnetic sensitivity in vitro, at fields above Earth strength as I recall.
  - REC · Behavioural disruption by radiofrequency noise (Engels et al. 2014, *Nature*, doi 10.1038/nature13290) [unverified].
  - Review: Hore and Mouritsen 2016, *Annu. Rev. Biophys.* (doi 10.1146/annurev-biophys-032116-094545) [unverified].
- **Against:** no in-vivo demonstration at Earth strength. The magnitude required at physiological temperature is debated (my characterisation).
- **Standing:** open.
- **What would settle it:** in-vivo or in-retina measurement of radical-pair yield changes at 50 μT, linked to behaviour.
- **Related hypothesis:** lithium-isotope effects explained by a flavin–superoxide radical pair are a stated hypothesis open to test (Zadeh-Haghighi and Simon 2021, *Sci. Rep.*, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-91388-9) [project corpus].
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B19b · Magnetic isotope effect in enzymatic ATP synthesis (notable claim, not reproduced)
- **Claim:** ATP synthesis by kinases runs faster with magnetic ²⁵Mg than with non-magnetic ²⁴Mg or ²⁶Mg, by a radical-pair, spin-dependent step (Buchachenko et al. 2005, *PNAS* 102, 10793) [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled].
- **Negation:** There is no isotope-dependent rate difference.
- **Observings:** a re-examination with creatine kinase found no magnetic isotope or field effect (Crotty et al. 2012, *PNAS* 109, 1437) [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled].
- **Standing:** not reproduced. It is labelled here as historically notable only, because "spin catalysis" in enzymes is cited from it.
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B20a · Does mechanical force give products that are not formed thermally? (settled anchor)
- **Observing:** OBS · Hickenboth et al. 2007, *Nature* (doi 10.1038/nature05681) [unverified]. Benzocyclobutene units built into polymer chains were ring-opened by ultrasound and trapped. NMR showed stereochemistry matching formally symmetry-forbidden pathways, for both cis and trans isomers.
- **Further observing:** OBS · Lenhardt et al. 2010, *Science* (doi 10.1126/science.1193412) [unverified]. Force trapped a diradical-like transition region in gem-dihalocyclopropanes.
- **Standing:** settled. Force can select products that thermal activation does not deliver.
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B20b · Are force-driven reactions statistical on a force-modified surface?
- **Claim:** Force-driven reactions in stretched polymers are statistical. Equilibrium TST on a force-modified potential-energy surface, with force as a parameter, predicts their rates and products. This is the "tilted surface" (Bell) picture.
- **Negation:** For the same reactions, products are set by nonequilibrium dynamics. Trajectories cross regions faster than energy redistributes and can skip intermediates ("flyby").
- **For the claim:**
  - OBS · Wiita et al. 2006, *PNAS* 103, 7222 [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled]. Single-molecule AFM showed disulfide reduction rates rising exponentially with force, as the Bell-type model predicts.
  - MOD · Ribas-Arino and Marx 2012, *Chem. Rev.* 112, 5412 [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled].
- **For the negation:** SIM · Ong et al. 2009, *JACS* 131, 6377 (Martínez group) [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled]. First-principles dynamics of mechanochemical cyclobutene ring opening gave trajectories departing from minimum-energy paths.
- **Methodological critique:** Akbulatov and Boulatov 2017, *ChemPhysChem* [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled]. Sonication data are hard to interpret because the force is unknown and there may be local heating.
- **Standing:** open.
- **What would settle it:** single-molecule experiments at known force giving product ratios, compared with both models.
- **Proponents:**
  - Statistical: Boulatov, Marx.
  - Dynamical: Martínez group.
  - Experiment: Moore, Craig, Sijbesma.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B25 · Microdroplet acceleration (brief; covered elsewhere)

- **Negation:** Apparent accelerations come from evaporation and concentration, or from gas-phase chemistry inside the mass spectrometer.

## 5. Rate-theory foundations

**Takeaway.**
- The intramolecular inverted region is settled.
- Adiabatic vs nonadiabatic electron transfer and Kramers friction are settled for each system, but stay open as general rules.
- "Rate-determining step" is a convention that depends on conditions. It is replaced by a continuous measure, the degree of rate control.

- **CONV/MOD · Marcus relation:** ΔG‡ = (λ + ΔG°)²/4λ. The rate peaks where −ΔG° = λ and falls beyond it, in the inverted region. Marcus predicted the inverted region in his 1960 paper. Project corpus reading of Marcus's 1992 Nobel Lecture: https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/marcus-lecture.pdf [project corpus]. That reading attributes the first paper to 1956, the prediction to 1960, and the best experimental evidence to Miller, Calcaterra and Closs in 1984.

### B21a · Did the intramolecular inverted region occur? (settled anchor)
- **Observing:** OBS · Miller, Calcaterra and Closs 1984, *JACS* 106, 3047 [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled].
  - Measured: intramolecular ET rates from biphenyl radical anions to acceptors joined by a rigid steroid spacer, generated by pulse radiolysis in solution (methyltetrahydrofuran, as I recall [unverified]).
  - Result: rates passed through a maximum as the driving force increased.
  - Adds: Marcus theory with quantised high-frequency modes fits the curve.
- **Standing:** settled since 1984 [project corpus reading of the Nobel Lecture]. Further cases include CPET inverted behaviour (Parada et al. 2019, B6) [unverified].
- **Concerns:** observing.

### B21b · Why is the inverted region missing in bimolecular reactions?
- **Accounts:** the observing is the Rehm–Weller plateau. Bimolecular fluorescence quenching rates level off at the diffusion limit and do not fall at high driving force (Rehm and Weller 1970, *Isr. J. Chem.*) [unverified]. Several explanations compete, so they are split into pairwise binaries with one scope:
  - (i) Excited-state or product channels open at high driving force (electronically excited products, exciplexes).
  - (ii) Distance dependence: at high driving force, ET happens at longer distances where the reaction is nearly activationless. Tachiya and Murata 1994, *JACS* [unverified; DOI not reliably recalled].
  - (iii) Diffusion masks the intrinsic rate decrease.
- **Binary (ii) vs not-(ii):**
  - Claim: for typical donor–acceptor quenching pairs in polar solvents, the plateau comes mainly from ET at a driving-force-dependent distance.
  - Negation: for the same pairs, distance variation is not the main cause, and (i) or (iii) dominates.
- **Observings:**
  - OBS · Ultrafast studies by Vauthey and others report an inverted region for charge recombination of ion pairs [unverified].
  - Rosspeintner, Angulo and Vauthey 2014, *JACS*: a femtosecond-resolution re-examination of the Rehm–Weller experiment beyond the diffusion limit (doi 10.1021/ja410895j) [unverified].
- **Standing:** narrowing. Vauthey's work supports distance and solvent effects without a hidden inverted region for forward ET, as I recall. Not verified.
- **What would settle it:** femtosecond quenching kinetics with distance distributions reconstructed from the time-dependent rate, across solvents.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B22 · Adiabatic vs nonadiabatic electron transfer (settled per system)
- **Claim:** For a given donor–acceptor system, the ET rate is set by nuclear motion on one adiabatic surface, so the prefactor does not depend on electronic coupling.
- **Negation:** For the same system, the rate is set by the probability of hopping between diabatic states, so the prefactor scales with the square of the coupling (|H_DA|²).
- **Observings:**
  - REC · Distance-decay constants β, from rigid donor–bridge–acceptor series and from Ru-modified proteins (Gray and Winkler 2005, *PNAS*, doi 10.1073/pnas.0501669102) [unverified]. Exponential decay with distance shows nonadiabatic behaviour.
  - REC · Solvent-relaxation-limited rates for strongly coupled systems show adiabatic behaviour.
  - MOD · The interpolation is the Landau–Zener adiabaticity parameter (Zusman; Rips and Jortner) [unverified].
- **Standing:** settled per system as a regime classification. Membership in each class follows from coupling compared with solvent relaxation (a criterion set by convention).
- **Concerns:** explaining (regime assignment).

### B23 · Does Kramers solvent-friction theory explain rate changes with viscosity in liquids?
- **Claim:** For isomerisations in liquids, rate changes with solvent viscosity follow Kramers theory, with a transmission coefficient reduced by friction. At high viscosity the rate goes as 1/η.
- **Negation:** For the same reactions, the viscosity dependence departs from Kramers in a systematic way and needs frequency-dependent friction (Grote–Hynes) or a barrier that changes with the solvent.
- **Observings:**
  - OBS · trans-stilbene photoisomerisation rates in alkanes, from gas to liquid at high pressure, showed the Kramers turnover from rising to falling rates (Schroeder and Troe, mid-1980s) [unverified]. Fractional power laws η^(−α) with α < 1 were also found.
  - MOD · Grote and Hynes 1980, *J. Chem. Phys.* (doi 10.1063/1.440017) [unverified]. Frequency-dependent friction for sharp barriers.
  - Review: Hänggi, Talkner and Borkovec 1990, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* (doi 10.1103/RevModPhys.62.251) [unverified].
- **Standing:** narrowing. Kramers with frequency-dependent friction is accepted. Separating friction from solvent-dependent barriers remains hard.
- **Concerns:** explaining.

### B24 · Is the "rate-determining step" well defined?
- **Claim:** For a multistep mechanism under stated conditions, one step can be named whose rate constant alone sets the overall rate.
- **Negation:** For general mechanisms under realistic conditions, no single step has this property. Control is spread across steps and changes with conditions, so a continuous sensitivity measure is needed.
- **Observings and conventions:**
  - CONV · Campbell's degree of rate control: X_RC,i = (∂ ln r / ∂ ln k_i), holding the equilibrium constant K_i fixed. The values sum to 1 over transition states (Stegelmann, Andreasen and Campbell 2009, *JACS*, doi 10.1021/ja9000097) [unverified].
  - CONV · Kozuch and Shaik's energetic span model for catalytic cycles: "TOF-determining" intermediate and transition state rather than a step (Kozuch and Shaik 2011, *Acc. Chem. Res.*, doi 10.1021/ar1000956) [unverified].
  - MOD · Metabolic control analysis (Kacser and Burns; Heinrich and Rapoport, 1973–74) has the same structure [unverified].
  - REC · Measured apparent activation energies and reaction orders that change with conditions show control shifting between steps.
- **Standing:** settled. "Rate-determining step" is a limiting case (one X_RC ≈ 1) that holds only in some regimes. The binary was resolved by a change of convention, not by an observing.
- **Concerns:** convention or definition.
