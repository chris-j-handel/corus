## How entries are written

- **Labels for each observing item:**
  - **OBS**: an observing, meaning a measured quantity with its instrument, sample and conditions.
  - **REC**: a reconstruction from observings, such as an inverted spectrum or a fitted structure.
  - **CONV**: a convention or definition, such as a cutoff, a class boundary or a choice of sign.
  - **MOD**: a model construct, such as a two-state equation of state or a critical point.
  - **SIM**: a simulation.
- **Entry order:** Claim · Negation · Observings (side A / side B) · Why hard · Standing 2026 · What would settle it · Proponents · Type (observing or explaining).
- **'Standing 2026'** means standing as of prior knowledge (to about mid-2026). It was **not** re-checked in-session, so it is marked as a prior state wherever a later development could exist.

## A. Supercooled and glassy water

### Beyond the LLCP entry the project already holds (context, not a new binary)

- **2026 result (checked this session from secondary pages; the paper itself was not read).**
  - You et al., 'Experimental evidence of a liquid-liquid critical point in supercooled water', Science 391(6792):1387–1391, published 26 March 2026, doi:10.1126/science.aec0018 ([Sci.News, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.sci.news/physics/critical-point-supercooled-water-14655.html); [Analytik News press release, 1 Apr 2026](https://analytik.news/en/press/2026/64.html)).
  - **OBS:** HDA and LDA were heated by IR fs pulses and probed by PAL-XFEL X-ray scattering before ice formed. They reported a steep rise in Cp, enhanced density fluctuations and slowing dynamics.
  - **REC/MOD:** these were read as critical divergence, placing an LLCP at about 210 K and about 1000 atm (about 0.1 GPa), where the liquid–liquid transition line ends.
  - Team: Nilsson, Perakis, Tyburski, Karina, Andronis (Stockholm), with POSTECH, Mainz and St. Francis Xavier.
  - The press material quotes only the authors; no critics are cited.
  - Companion Perspective by Paesani, 'Catching water's hidden transition', Science 391:1318, doi:10.1126/science.aef3474 ([EBSCO record](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/science/3898e672-1201-5162-9fb8-f778a4dd1310/catching-water-s-hidden-transition)). Its abstract frames the work as narrowing where a hidden liquid–liquid transition lies.
  - **Standing:** a first claimed direct experimental location of the LLCP. Independent replication or published critiques by October 2026 were **not found or checked**.
- **2026 simulation on the structural origin of Cp (SIM, preprint).**
  - Chan, Folkner, Berrens, Stuchebrukhov, Wang & Donadio, arXiv:2607.08957, 9 July 2026 ([papers.cool](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2607.08957)).
  - Classical and path-integral MD with machine-learned potentials found that nuclear quantum effects mainly damp high-frequency modes.
  - They attribute the temperature dependence of Cp to structural fluctuations, tracked by a second-shell 'intruder' order parameter.
  - A two-state mapping gives about 4 kJ/mol between low- and high-density-like local structures. This bears on binary 2.
- **Accounts in play: four, not two.**
  - **LLCP:** a liquid–liquid critical point at positive pressure, with a Widom line emanating from it.
  - **Singularity-free:** finite maxima with no transition.
  - **Stability limit:** a re-entrant spinodal.
  - **Critical-point-free:** the liquid–liquid transition line runs into the liquid–gas spinodal.
  - A fifth, deflationary account says the apparent transition is an ice-coarsening artefact.
  - Proper binaries must therefore pit one account against its negation within a fixed (P, T) window. The LLCP framing is reviewed in Gallo et al. 2016, Chem. Rev., doi:10.1021/acs.chemrev.5b00750 [unverified]. **MOD**.
- **Kim et al. 2020 (OBS + REC).** HDA was laser-heated under pressure, and X-ray free-electron-laser (XFEL) scattering followed the transient liquid. They reported a first-order-like high-density-to-low-density liquid change at about 205 K, at pressures around 0.1–0.3 GPa. Science, doi:10.1126/science.abb9385 [unverified].
- **Amann-Winkel et al. 2023 (OBS + REC).** Ultrafast IR heating of LDA, followed by XFEL scattering, was interpreted as liquid–liquid phase separation in the 'no-man's land'. Nat. Commun. 14, 442, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-36091-1 [unverified].
- **Simulation side (SIM):**
  - An LLCP was found in the ST2 model (Palmer et al. 2014, Nature, doi:10.1038/nature13405 [unverified]).
  - LLCPs were found in TIP4P/2005 and TIP4P/Ice (Debenedetti, Sciortino & Zerze 2020, Science, doi:10.1126/science.abb9796 [unverified]).
  - An LLCP was found in a SCAN-trained deep-learning potential (Gartner et al. 2022, PRL 129, 255702 [unverified]).
  - Constraints on the LLCP location from the MB-pol potential: Sciortino, Zhai, Bore & Paesani, Nat. Phys. 2025 [unverified; DOI not recalled].
  - **Deflationary side:** Limmer & Chandler 2011, J. Chem. Phys., doi:10.1063/1.3643333 [unverified] argued that the ST2 'transition' was ice coarsening. Later free-energy work (Palmer 2014) rebutted this for ST2.
- **Two-state equations of state (MOD).** These fit the measured anomalies with an LLCP placed at positive pressure (Holten & Anisimov 2012, Sci. Rep., doi:10.1038/srep00713 [unverified]). Sound-velocity data on stretched water in quartz inclusions show anomalies at negative pressure that are compatible with, but do not prove, an LLCP (Pallares et al. 2014, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1323366111 [unverified]).
- **Thin films (OBS).** Pulsed heating of amorphous-ice films with IR readout gave reversible structural spectra from 135 to 245 K. These decompose into two motifs whose fractions shift steeply near about 210 K (Kringle, Thornley, Kay & Kimmel 2020, Science, doi:10.1126/science.abb7542 [unverified]). This is a possible point of contact with the 2026 report of a heat-capacity divergence near 210 K, but that link is not checked.

### 1. Do the 0.1 MPa response functions pass through finite maxima near 229 K?

- **Claim:** At 0.1 MPa, the isothermal compressibility κT, the isobaric heat capacity Cp and the correlation length ξ of bulk supercooled H2O pass through finite maxima near 228–230 K. On this view, water crosses a Widom line rather than meeting a divergence.
- **Negation:** At 0.1 MPa, these quantities show no maximum near 229 K. They either keep rising monotonically down to the lowest temperature reached, or they diverge at a lower temperature (a spinodal or critical point at ambient pressure).
- **Side A observings:**
  - Evaporatively cooled micron-sized droplets in vacuum were probed by XFEL wide- and small-angle scattering. κT and ξ peaked at about 229 K (Kim et al. 2017, Science, doi:10.1126/science.aap8269 [unverified]). **OBS** of scattering; **REC** of κT and ξ from structure factors; temperature from an **evaporation MOD**.
  - Ultrafast calorimetry, with IR heating of droplets and X-ray thermometry, found an enhanced Cp with a maximum near 228 K (Pathak et al. 2021, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.2018379118 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
- **Side B observings:**
  - Older bulk and emulsion measurements fitted Speedy–Angell power laws diverging near 228 K. These are **REC**, by extrapolation.
  - Caupin, Holten, Qiu and colleagues 2018 commented that the droplet temperature scale and the κT extraction carry uncertainties large enough to question the maximum (Science comment, doi:10.1126/science.aat1634 [unverified]). Kim and colleagues replied (doi:10.1126/science.aat1729 [unverified]).
- **Placement of the 2026 Cp divergence (checked):** You et al. 2026 (doi:10.1126/science.aec0018) put it at about 210 K and **about 1000 atm**, not at 0.1 MPa. It therefore does not contradict finite maxima at about 229 K and 0.1 MPa. Within the LLCP account (MOD), it is what side A predicts: finite maxima along the Widom line at low pressure, and divergence only at the critical point.
- **Why hard:**
  - Ice nucleates within microseconds below about 232 K.
  - Droplet temperatures are inferred from models rather than measured.
  - The quantities are not measured directly but reconstructed from scattering.
  - Finite maxima are predicted by both the LLCP and the singularity-free accounts, so even settling the observing does not settle the explaining.
- **Standing 2026:** Narrowing towards finite maxima near 229 K (prior state, 2017–2021). Calibration objections were answered but not withdrawn. The 2026 Science report sits at about 1000 atm, so it supports the LLCP framing rather than reopening the 0.1 MPa question. Independent thermometry is still lacking.
- **What would settle it:** An independent, direct thermometry of droplets (for example Raman or fluorescence thermometry checked against the evaporation model), plus κT and Cp measured on the same droplets. A mapping of how the position of the maximum moves with pressure, since the Widom line should bend towards the LLCP.
- **Proponents:** A: Nilsson, Kim, Perakis, Pathak, Amann-Winkel. B / critics: Caupin, Holten (and Speedy–Angell for power-law divergence, prior).
- **Type:** observing (did a finite maximum occur?), with explaining consequences.

### 2. Is ambient liquid water structurally bimodal or a continuum?

- **Claim:** At 298 K and 0.1 MPa, water's local structure falls into two distinguishable classes that interconvert on picosecond timescales: tetrahedral, low-density-like and disordered, high-density-like. On this view, the measured X-ray spectra and small-angle scattering show the two-class mixture directly.
- **Negation:** Under the same conditions, the distribution of local structure is a single continuous, unimodal one of distorted tetrahedra. The same spectra and scattering are reproduced without two classes.
- **Side A observings:**
  - X-ray absorption and X-ray Raman spectra show a pre-edge and main-edge shape that was read as most molecules having one strong and one broken donor H-bond (Wernet et al. 2004, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1096205 [unverified]). **OBS** of spectra; **REC** of coordination through spectrum calculations.
  - X-ray emission spectroscopy (XES) shows a split lone-pair peak. Small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) shows a rise at low Q, which was read as density fluctuations on the ~1 nm scale (Huang et al. 2009, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.0904743106 [unverified]).
  - Synthesis: Nilsson & Pettersson 2015, Nat. Commun., doi:10.1038/ncomms9998 [unverified].
  - Simulations using inherent structures give a bimodal order parameter ζ (Russo & Tanaka 2014, Nat. Commun., doi:10.1038/ncomms4556 [unverified]). **SIM + CONV**, since the outcome depends on the choice of order parameter.
- **Side B observings:**
  - The temperature dependence of XAS gave a rearrangement enthalpy consistent with a continuum, mostly tetrahedral network (Smith et al. 2004, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1102560 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
  - SAXS at low Q was reproduced as ordinary compressibility-driven fluctuations of a homogeneous liquid (Clark et al. 2010, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1006599107 [unverified]).
  - Quantitative XES/XAS were shown to be compatible with continuous-distribution simulations (Niskanen et al. 2019, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1815701116 [unverified]). **SIM + REC**.
- **Why hard:**
  - The spectra are indirect, and reading them requires excited-state theory, where core-hole and final-state effects are themselves debated.
  - Instantaneous configurations are always blurred by thermal motion, while bimodality appears only in coarse-grained or inherent-structure coordinates.
  - What counts as 'two states' is a **CONV**: which order parameter, which timescale, and whether a dip between peaks is required.
- **Checked 2026 preprint (SIM):** Chan et al., arXiv:2607.08957 ([papers.cool](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2607.08957)). ML-potential path-integral MD ties Cp(T) to the population of second-shell 'intruder' structures, with a two-state enthalpy scale of about 4 kJ/mol. This is a two-state description in a chosen coordinate (**CONV**), not proof of bimodality.
- **Standing 2026:** Open on whether the structure is 'two-state'. There is broad agreement that two-state thermodynamic models (MOD) fit the anomalies well, and that ambient structural bimodality is not directly observable. Prior state to about 2024; not re-checked.
- **What would settle it:**
  - An agreed, observable order parameter whose distribution can be measured, for example through joint X-ray and neutron reverse Monte Carlo constrained with spectroscopy.
  - Agreement on what bimodality requires.
  - Prediction tests: whether the two-state parameters fitted at ambient conditions predict independent quantities at deep supercooling.
- **Proponents:** A: Nilsson, Pettersson, Tanaka, Russo. B: Saykally, Head-Gordon, Galli/Prendergast (on XAS), Soper (on the uniqueness of structure from diffraction), Skinner (spectroscopic modelling; prior).
- **Type:** explaining (which structural account explains the spectra), with a **CONV** core.

### 3. Is the ~136 K calorimetric step a glass-to-liquid transition?

- **Claim:** The heat-capacity step near 136 K in hyperquenched glassy water and low-density amorphous ice (LDA) marks a glass-to-liquid transition. Above it, the material is an ultraviscous liquid with translational diffusion, before it crystallizes near 150–160 K.
- **Negation:** The same step marks only the unfreezing of molecular reorientation. It is an orientational glass transition without liquid-like translational diffusion, so the material above 136 K is not a liquid. (A variant places the true glass transition temperature near 165 K.)
- **Side A observings:**
  - Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) shows a small endothermic step at about 136 K in hyperquenched water (Johari, Hallbrucker & Mayer 1987, Nature, doi:10.1038/330552a0 [unverified]). **OBS**.
  - Isotope intermixing in nanoscale layered amorphous films above about 150 K was read as translational diffusion of a supercooled liquid (Smith & Kay 1999, Nature, doi:10.1038/19725 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
  - Dielectric and calorimetric evidence was reported for two glass transitions: LDA near 136 K and HDA near 116 K (Amann-Winkel et al. 2013, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1311718110 [unverified]).
  - X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy (XPCS) shows diffusive dynamics in HDA near 110 K (Perakis et al. 2017, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1705303114 [unverified]).
- **Side B observings:**
  - Annealing and enthalpy-relaxation analysis placed the glass transition near 165 K (Velikov, Borick & Angell 2001, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1061757 [unverified]). **OBS + MOD**.
  - A large H/D isotope shift of the calorimetric glass transition was read as reorientation-governed, not translational (Shephard & Salzmann 2016, J. Phys. Chem. Lett., doi:10.1021/acs.jpclett.6b00881 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
  - The intermixing in films has been suggested to accompany crystallization rather than liquid diffusion [unverified; specific paper not recalled].
- **Why hard:**
  - The window between the glass transition and crystallization is narrow, about 10–20 K.
  - The calorimetric step is tiny.
  - 'Glass transition' is partly a **CONV**: the timescale-based definition (relaxation time of about 100 s) does not say which degree of freedom must unfreeze.
- **Standing 2026:** Open. The 136 K assignment is widely used, but the orientational reading has had sustained support since 2016. Prior state; not re-checked.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A direct measure of translational diffusion, for example XPCS or quasi-elastic neutron scattering on bulk LDA between 136 and 150 K, together with an independent rotational probe on the same sample and heating rate.
  - A demonstration that the viscous flow follows the calorimetric relaxation time.
- **Proponents:** A: Johari, Mayer, Loerting, Amann-Winkel, Nilsson, Kay & Kimmel. B: Angell (Tg near 165 K, prior), Salzmann, Shephard.
- **Type:** explaining (what the step is), resting on an open observing (whether translational diffusion is present at 136–150 K).

### 4. Is HDA the glass of a distinct liquid, or a collapsed crystal? (with an MDA sub-binary)

- **Claim (4a):** High-density amorphous ice (HDA, about 1.15–1.17 g cm⁻³ at 77 K and 1 bar) and low-density amorphous ice (LDA, about 0.94 g cm⁻³) are distinct amorphous phases joined by a first-order-like transition. Each is the glassy counterpart of a distinct liquid (HDL and LDL).
- **Negation (4a):** HDA, as made by compressing ice Ih at 77 K, is a mechanically collapsed or 'derailed' crystalline state (strained, possibly nanocrystalline). It is not the glass of any liquid, and the LDA↔HDA change is not a transition between glasses of two liquids.
- **Side A observings:**
  - Compressing ice Ih at 77 K to about 1 GPa gave an amorphous solid, identified by diffraction (Mishima, Calvert & Whalley 1984, Nature, doi:10.1038/310393a0 [unverified]). **OBS**.
  - A sharp, hysteretic volume jump between LDA and HDA under pressure near 135 K (Mishima et al. 1985, Nature, doi:10.1038/314076a0 [unverified]). **OBS**. Its reading as 'first-order' is **REC**.
  - Very-high-density amorphous ice (VHDA, about 1.26 g cm⁻³) obtained by annealing HDA under pressure (Loerting et al. 2001, PCCP, doi:10.1039/b108676f [unverified]).
  - Two calorimetric and dielectric glass transitions, and diffusive XPCS dynamics in HDA (Amann-Winkel 2013; Perakis 2017; see binary 3).
- **Side B observings:**
  - Lattice-dynamics calculations suggested pressure amorphization is a mechanical instability of the crystal (Tse et al. 1999, Nature [unverified; DOI not recalled]). **SIM**.
  - Annealing gives a continuum of HDA-like structures rather than two discrete states (Tulk et al. 2002, Science [unverified; DOI not recalled]). **OBS**.
  - Slow, hydrostatic compression of ice at about 100 K gave crystalline phases, not HDA (Tulk et al. 2019, Nature, 'Absence of amorphous forms when ice is compressed at low temperature' [unverified; DOI not recalled]). **OBS**.
  - Structural similarity to the ice I→IV pathway: Shephard et al. 2017, J. Phys. Chem. Lett., asked whether HDA is a 'derailed' state on that pathway [unverified; DOI not recalled].
- **Sub-binary 4b (MDA, observing):**
  - **Claim:** Ball-milling ice Ih at 77 K yields a distinct amorphous ice, MDA, with density about 1.06 g cm⁻³. MDA is separated from LDA and HDA by density gaps and has structure close to that of liquid water at ambient pressure.
  - **Negation:** The milled product is not a distinct amorphous state but a sheared or nanocrystalline mixture, or a point on a continuum.
  - **Evidence:** Rosu-Finsen, Davies, …, Salzmann 2023, Science, doi:10.1126/science.abq2105 [unverified; abstract fetch failed this session].
    - **OBS:** diffraction and density measurements; heat released when compressed MDA recrystallizes.
    - **REC/MOD:** its proposed relation to liquid water, and the authors' suggestion that the amorphous ices may not all be glasses of liquids.
  - **Checked follow-up (SIM, not peer-reviewed):** de Almeida Ribeiro, Dhabal, Kumar, Banik, Sankaranarayanan & Molinero, ChemRxiv, version 2, 26 Sep 2024 ([ChemRxiv](https://www.cambridge.org/engage/coe/article-details/66f47e90cec5d6c142348040)).
    - Simulated shear of ice Ih, LDA and HDA at 77 K gives steady-state 'shear-driven amorphous ices' whose density varies with shear rate, from LDA-like to HDA-like.
    - On this account MDA is a non-equilibrium, shear-rate-dependent state, and shear rate is proposed as an extra variable in the phase diagram.
    - The fastest-sheared states match water quenched from room temperature within 10 ps.
    - This supports the negation of 4b (no distinct equilibrium amorph). It does not support the 'nanocrystalline mixture' variant.
  - An APS March Meeting 2024 talk, 'What is medium-density amorphous ice?', shows that the question was live in 2024 ([APS abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/mar/2024/s25/10); content not read).
- **Why hard:**
  - Amorphous ices are out of equilibrium and depend on their history.
  - 'Glass of a liquid' requires showing a reversible glass transition into that liquid, which ties this to binary 3.
  - The class 'amorphous ice polymorph' has no agreed membership criterion (**CONV**): density bands, structure-factor peak position, or a thermodynamic transition.
- **Standing 2026:** Open. MDA (2023) reopened whether the amorphous ices map one-to-one onto two liquids. The two-liquid reading is strengthened by XFEL studies of liquid–liquid change (Kim 2020; Amann-Winkel 2023), but these probe heated liquids, not the solids directly. Prior state.
- **What would settle it:**
  - Showing that each amorph converts reversibly, at the same pressure, into a liquid of matching structure, via glass transitions measured on the same sample.
  - Routes that are independent of preparation history reaching the same amorphous state.
  - For MDA: whether it shows a glass transition into a liquid, or recrystallizes without one.
- **Proponents:** A: Mishima, Stanley (prior), Loerting, Amann-Winkel, Nilsson, Debenedetti. B: Tse, Klug, Tulk, Salzmann, Shephard.
- **Type:** 4a is explaining; 4b is observing.

### 5. The Mpemba effect in water (split by criterion)

- **Scope problem:** The phrase covers at least three criteria:
  - **5i:** time for the whole sample to cool to 0 °C;
  - **5ii:** time to the onset of freezing (stochastic nucleation from a supercooled state);
  - **5iii:** time to complete freezing.
  - Results for colloidal, spin-glass and quantum systems belong to different binaries with different participants. They share the name (a **CONV**), not the scope.
- **Claim (5i):** In identical containers and surroundings, with fixed mass, fixed dissolved-gas content and evaporation accounted for, water starting hotter reaches 0 °C throughout sooner than water starting cooler.
- **Negation (5i):** Under the same controls and the same criterion, the hotter sample never reaches 0 °C sooner.
- **Side A observings:**
  - Mpemba & Osborne 1969, Phys. Educ., doi:10.1088/0031-9120/4/3/312 [unverified]: in uncontrolled freezer tests, initially hot samples froze first. **OBS** (no controls).
  - The supercooling version (5ii): hot samples sometimes supercool less and so nucleate earlier (Auerbach 1995, Am. J. Phys., doi:10.1119/1.17917 [unverified]). **OBS**, with a stochastic outcome.
  - Proposed mechanisms (**MOD**) include evaporation, convection, dissolved gases and 'hydrogen-bond memory'. The last is a non-mainstream proposal [unverified].
- **Side B observings:**
  - Burridge & Linden 2016, Sci. Rep., doi:10.1038/srep37665 [unverified; fetch failed]: carefully placed thermocouples, time to 0 °C. **OBS**.
    - No genuine effect was found.
    - Moving the sensor by about 1 cm could create an apparent effect.
  - A follow-up with minimal bias found nucleation times stochastic, with no robust effect (Burridge & Hallstadius 2020, Proc. R. Soc. A, doi:10.1098/rspa.2019.0829 [unverified]).
  - Review: Bechhoefer, Kumar & Chétrite 2021, Nat. Rev. Phys., doi:10.1038/s42254-021-00349-8 [unverified]. The water case is unsettled, while cleaner systems show the effect.
- **Checked 2026 water study (OBS):** Janni, Botero Ampudia & Dahlberg (Univ. Minnesota), 'An experimental investigation of the Mpemba effect', RSC journal, first published 16 Jan 2026 ([RSC HTML](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/lf/d5lf00296f); DOI not shown on the page).
  - **Setup:** 176 samples of 35 mL (deionized or tap water) in 25 trials; chest freezer at −14 to −17 °C; a central thermocouple read every 2 s.
  - **Results:** an apparent effect in 26 % of comparisons by the 0 °C criterion and 33 % by the freezing-onset criterion.
  - Lids made no difference, so evaporation was not the cause. Tap and deionized water did not differ (dissolved gas was not measured directly).
  - **REC by the authors:** apparent effects track the scatter in fitted Newton cooling constants, which comes from uneven freezer convection, and from stochastic supercooling (50 % of samples).
  - **Bearing on 5i:** this points to a setup-driven, not intrinsic, effect. The authors nonetheless say the effect 'occurs' in their setup, so 5i turns on whether 'identical surroundings' can be achieved.
  - Coverage of a separate experiment exists ([Science News](https://sciencenews.org/?p=3090276); not read).
- **Different-scope positives** (separate binaries, all **OBS** within their own scope):
  - A colloidal particle in an engineered optical double-well potential relaxed exponentially faster from a hotter start, the 'strong' effect (Kumar & Bechhoefer 2020, Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-020-2560-x [unverified; fetch failed]). **OBS**, with a Markovian theory (Lu & Raz 2017, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1701264114 [unverified]) as **MOD**.
  - Spin glasses (Baity-Jesi et al. 2019, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1819803116 [unverified]). **SIM** on special hardware.
  - The quantum Mpemba effect, through 'entanglement asymmetry' (Ares, Murciano & Calabrese 2023, Nat. Commun., doi:10.1038/s41467-023-37747-8 [unverified]). **MOD**.
  - Trapped-ion observations:
    - Joshi et al. 2024, PRL 133, 010402 [unverified].
    - Shapira et al. 2024, single-ion qubit ([ar5iv 2401.05830](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2401.05830); not read).
    - A quantum **strong** Mpemba effect in a single ⁴⁰Ca⁺ three-level ion, where a state with no overlap on the slowest Liouvillian mode relaxed exponentially faster. The strong/weak crossover sits at a Liouvillian exceptional point (Nat. Commun. 2025, Hui Jing co-lead; [Physics World, 10 Apr 2025](https://physicsworld.com/a/quantum-mpemba-effect-appears-in-a-real-experimental-system/)). The DOI implied by the link is 10.1038/s41467-024-54303-0, not confirmed.
  - Review: 'The quantum Mpemba effects', arXiv:2502.08087 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08087); not read).
- **Why hard:**
  - Water carries many uncontrolled variables: mass lost to evaporation, convection, dissolved gas, frost contact, sensor position and stochastic supercooling.
  - The criterion is ambiguous.
  - Effect sizes are small, and positive results are more likely to be published.
- **Standing 2026:**
  - 5i: open, leaning negative under the best controls.
  - 5ii: an effect can appear but is stochastic and prone to bias.
  - Colloidal and quantum versions: established within their own scopes.
  - Prior state; 2025–26 water studies not checked.
- **What would settle it:** Preregistered, blinded, large-sample experiments with whole-field temperature (multiple sensors or IR thermography). Sealed versus open arms, degassed versus gas-saturated arms, each criterion reported separately, and replication in several labs.
- **Proponents:** A: Mpemba and Osborne (historical); Auerbach (5ii). B: Burridge, Linden, Hallstadius. Bechhoefer is cautious on water but positive on colloids.
- **Type:** observing.

## B. Proton and electron in water

### 6. Is the hydrated excess proton Eigen-like or Zundel-like? (three accounts, split)

- **Accounts:**
  - **Eigen:** H3O⁺ with three waters (H9O4⁺).
  - **Zundel:** a proton shared between two waters (H5O2⁺).
  - **Fluctuating or asymmetric:** a continuum of configurations, including the 'special-pair dance' (Markovitch & Agmon 2007–08 [unverified]) and larger delocalized forms such as H13O6⁺ (Stoyanov, Stoyanova & Reed 2010, JACS, doi:10.1021/ja9101826 [unverified]).
  - Ideal Eigen and ideal Zundel are limiting **MOD**s. The classes are defined by **CONV**: the proton-sharing coordinate δ = r(O1–H) − r(O2–H) and the O–O distance.
- **Proper binary:**
  - **Claim:** In bulk aqueous acid (about 1–4 M HCl, 298 K), most excess-proton configurations, weighted over time, have |δ| above a stated threshold (about 0.2 Å, Eigen-like; hydronium-centred).
  - **Negation:** Most have |δ| below that threshold (Zundel-like; shared).
- **Zundel-side observings:**
  - 2D IR of acids showed cross-peaks linking the ~1760 cm⁻¹ bend and the ~3200 cm⁻¹ stretch, assigned to Zundel-like species with lifetimes over 480 fs (Thämer et al. 2015, Science, doi:10.1126/science.aab3908 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
  - 2D IR read as direct evidence of a double-minimum proton potential, with Zundel cations predominant. The proton samples between the two waters in under 100 fs and loses memory of its position, and H-bond rearrangement converts the geometry in a few ps (Dahms, Fingerhut, Nibbering, Pines & Elsaesser 2017, Science, doi:10.1126/science.aan5144; summary checked via [Lab Manager](https://www.labmanager.com/water-m-7031)). **OBS + REC**.
  - Femtosecond nonlinear IR on H5O2⁺: continuum excitations live for less than 60 fs, and the extreme breadth was traced to fluctuating solvent fields acting on the proton-transfer coordinate (Dahms et al. 2016, Angew. Chem., doi:10.1002/anie.201602523; checked via [MBI press release on adlershof.de](https://adlershof.de/news/extrem-breite-infrarotspektren-durch-strukturelle-fluktuationen-von-fluessigkeiten)).
  - Broadband 2D IR read as dominant asymmetric H5O2⁺-like structures (Fournier et al. 2018, Nat. Chem., doi:10.1038/s41557-018-0091-y [unverified; fetch failed]).
- **Eigen-side observings:**
  - Multistate empirical valence bond and AIMD simulations find a distorted Eigen-like structure with a hydronium core. These simulations reproduce the 'Zundel-like' spectral features from Eigen configurations with a short special pair (Calio, Li & Voth 2021, JACS, doi:10.1021/jacs.1c08552 [unverified; fetch failed]). **SIM**.
  - THz and FTIR read as 'Eigen structure, Zundel dynamics' (Decka, Schwaab & Havenith 2015, PCCP [unverified]).
  - IR computed with a clusters-in-liquid method (Kulig & Agmon 2013, Nat. Chem., doi:10.1038/nchem.1503 [unverified]).
  - Path-integral analysis of time-scales (Napoli, Marsalek & Markland 2018, J. Chem. Phys., doi:10.1063/1.5023704 [unverified]).
- **Why hard:**
  - Spectra are broad continua, strongly anharmonic and coupled. Assigning them needs simulations that include nuclear quantum effects.
  - The populations depend on the threshold chosen for δ, on concentration and on the counterion.
  - Structures interconvert in about 100 fs to 1 ps.
- **Checked 2026 simulation (SIM):** Mendive-Tapia, Schran, Das, Gatti, Schröder, Marx & Vendrell, 'Deciphering the infrared spectrum of the hydrated proton using full-dimensional quantum dynamics', Nat. Chem. 2026, doi:10.1038/s41557-026-02209-3 ([RUB press release, 27 Jul 2026](https://news.rub.de/english/press-releases/2026-07-27-complex-simulations-new-findings-proton-transport-water)).
  - Full quantum dynamics of 51 coupled modes on a neural-network potential, stepping from a symmetric six-water Zundel cluster towards an asymmetric Eigen form.
  - The computed IR spectrum reproduces experiment across the full range.
  - Ideal Eigen and Zundel are treated as limiting idealizations, with spectra and hopping governed mainly by local asymmetry of the surrounding waters.
- **Standing 2026:** Narrowing. Both camps now describe fluctuating, asymmetric configurations, and the 2026 quantum-dynamics result supports this. The remaining dispute is largely over whether to call the dominant motif 'distorted Eigen' or 'asymmetric Zundel' (**CONV**).
- **What would settle it:**
  - Agreement on the coordinate and the threshold.
  - Full 2D IR lineshapes across concentrations reproduced by path-integral simulations trained on high-level electronic structure.
  - An independent structural probe, such as neutron diffraction with H/D substitution or O K-edge X-ray absorption spectroscopy of hydronium, read with the same coordinate.
- **Proponents:** Eigen-like: Agmon, Voth, Markland (partly). Zundel-like: Tokmakoff, Elsaesser, Fingerhut, Nibbering, Pines, Bakker (Thämer). Delocalized H13O6⁺: Reed.
- **Type:** explaining, with a **CONV** core.

### 7. Does excess-proton transfer proceed by sequential single hops or by concerted multi-proton moves?

- **Claim:** In bulk liquid water at 298 K, the net migration of the excess proton (structural diffusion) proceeds mainly by temporally separated single-proton transfers between neighbouring water pairs. This is sequential Grotthuss hopping, gated by second-shell H-bond rearrangement.
- **Negation:** Under the same conditions, a substantial part of the migration (stated as a fraction above a set threshold, for example >30 %) occurs through correlated transfers of two or more protons along H-bond wires within a single transfer window, about 100 fs.
- **Sequential-side observings:**
  - The NMR-derived proton-hopping time is about 1.5 ps. The Arrhenius activation energy of about 2.5 kcal mol⁻¹ is read as H-bond cleavage in the second shell (Agmon 1995, Chem. Phys. Lett., doi:10.1016/0009-2614(95)00905-J [unverified]). **OBS** of conductivity and NMR; mechanism as **MOD**.
  - Early AIMD shows presolvation-gated single transfers (Marx, Tuckerman, Hutter & Parrinello 1999, Nature, doi:10.1038/17579 [unverified]). **SIM**.
- **Concerted-side observings:**
  - AIMD analyses show bursts of correlated transfers and long proton 'wires', alternating with resting periods (Hassanali, Giberti, Cuny, Kühne & Parrinello 2013, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1306642110 [unverified]). **SIM**.
  - Experiments related to correlated transfer in hydroxide and confined systems [unverified].
- **Why hard:**
  - The relevant events take tens to hundreds of femtoseconds, and no experiment yet resolves single transfer events in the bulk liquid.
  - The fraction counted as 'concerted' depends on time-window and threshold conventions (**CONV**).
  - Results are sensitive to the density functional and to nuclear quantum effects (**SIM**).
- **Standing 2026:** Open, with the explaining resting on simulation. Ultrafast X-ray and THz-pump probes have begun to look at proton dynamics in water [unverified for 2023–26 specifics].
- **What would settle it:**
  - An observable that separates the two pictures, for example the H/D isotope effect on mobility in H/D mixtures. Concerted multi-proton transfer predicts a nonlinear dependence on the D fraction (in the spirit of a proton inventory) that differs from sequential hopping.
  - Simulations with converged nuclear quantum effects, reported with a shared definition of 'concerted'.
- **Proponents:** Sequential: Agmon, Marx, Tuckerman, Voth. Concerted or bursty: Hassanali, Parrinello, Kühne.
- **Type:** explaining.

### 8. Is hydroxide transport the mirror image of hydronium transport?

- **Claim:** OH⁻(aq) migrates by the mirror image of H3O⁺ structural diffusion. It is threefold coordinated as an acceptor, and transfer occurs through a hydrated-hydroxide analogue of the Zundel form. The mobility difference comes only from hydrogen-bond geometry, not from a different solvation motif.
- **Negation:** OH⁻ is mainly hypercoordinated: it accepts four H-bonds in a square-planar arrangement and weakly donates one. Transfer requires converting this to a tetrahedral, threefold-coordinated presolvated state first. So OH⁻ transport is not the mirror image of H3O⁺ transport.
- **Mirror-side observings:**
  - The 'proton hole' picture (Bernal–Fowler; Agmon 2000, Chem. Phys. Lett. [unverified]). **MOD**.
  - Some AIMD with particular functionals finds the mirror image [unverified].
- **Hypercoordination-side observings:**
  - Path-integral AIMD found the hypercoordinated OH⁻(H2O)4 and 'presolvation' via a shift in coordination (Tuckerman, Marx & Parrinello 2002, Nature, doi:10.1038/nature00797 [unverified]). **SIM**.
  - Neutron diffraction with isotopic substitution on concentrated NaOD and KOD supports fourfold acceptor coordination (Botti, Bruni, Imberti, Ricci & Soper 2004, J. Chem. Phys. [unverified]). **OBS + REC** (EPSR).
  - Simulations with the SCAN functional and nuclear quantum effects reproduce the H3O⁺/OH⁻ diffusion ratio. They attribute OH⁻'s slower diffusion to hypercoordination and to the cost of the transition to the presolvated state (Chen, Ko, Remsing, Calegari Andrade, Santra, Sun, Selloni, Car, Klein, Perdew & Wu 2018, Nat. Chem., doi:10.1038/s41557-018-0010-2 [unverified; fetch failed]). **SIM**.
- **Why hard:**
  - Concentrated-base experiments (several molar) need not reflect dilute OH⁻.
  - Spectroscopic signatures of the OH⁻ lone-pair donor are weak.
  - Older generalized-gradient functionals overstructure water and bias the result.
- **Standing 2026:** Narrowing towards hypercoordination plus presolvation, prior state. A dissenting reading persists in some cluster-based or spectroscopic work [unverified].
- **What would settle it:** Direct structural probes of dilute OH⁻, such as O K-edge XAS, neutron diffraction across concentrations extrapolated to dilution, and 2D IR of the OH⁻ stretch, compared with simulations with converged nuclear quantum effects.
- **Proponents:** Hypercoordination: Tuckerman, Marx, Parrinello, Car/Wu, Imberti/Soper. Mirror or hole picture: Agmon (prior).
- **Type:** explaining.

### 9. Is the bulk hydrated electron a cavity electron or a non-cavity electron?

- **Claim:** In bulk liquid water at 298 K, the hydrated electron's spin density mostly (>50 %) occupies a solvent-excluded cavity of about 2.5 Å radius. The cavity is lined by about four to six OH groups pointing inward, with only small spin density on water molecules.
- **Negation:** Under the same conditions, the electron occupies a region of enhanced water density. Its spin density largely overlaps several water molecules, with no well-defined excluded cavity.
- **Cavity-side observings:**
  - EPR and electron spin-echo envelope modulation (ESEEM) in alkaline glasses at 77 K show about six equivalent water molecules with OH bonds oriented towards the centre (Kevan 1981, Acc. Chem. Res. [unverified]). **OBS** in a glass; extrapolation to the liquid is **REC**.
  - Resonance Raman shows a moderate downshift of the OH stretch and a modest enhancement of the bend, read as cavity-lining OH groups (Tauber & Mathies 2003, JACS, doi:10.1021/ja021069d [unverified]). **OBS**.
  - Simulations with second-order Møller–Plesset (MP2) theory, combined with path-integral simulations including nuclear quantum effects, find a stable cavity and reproduce the absorption spectrum, the radius of gyration and the Raman spectra (Wilhelm, VandeVondele & Rybkin 2019, Angew. Chem.; Lan, Kapil, Gasparotto, Ceriotti, Iannuzzi & Rybkin 2021, Nat. Commun., doi:10.1038/s41467-021-20914-0 [unverified; fetch failed]). **SIM**.
- **Non-cavity-side observings:**
  - Mixed quantum-classical simulations with a revised pseudopotential produce a non-cavity electron. They are said to reproduce the positive partial molar volume trend and the temperature dependence of the absorption peak (Larsen, Glover & Schwartz 2010, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1187435 [unverified]). **SIM**.
  - Comments disputed this: Jacobson & Herbert 2011; Turi & Madarász 2011, Science comments [unverified].
  - The measured partial molar volume and the temperature-dependent spectrum are claimed to favour non-cavity [unverified; specific measurement papers not recalled].
- **Why hard:**
  - The pseudopotential or electronic-structure method controls the outcome.
  - Experiments are indirect: spectra, moments and Raman shifts.
  - Glass EPR may not represent the liquid.
- **Checked 2024–25 items (cited within a checked preprint):** The preprint is Sajeev, 'Is the electron hydrated through covalent sharing?', arXiv:2508.03318, 23 Sep 2025 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03318v1)). It cites:
  - Time-resolved X-ray absorption capturing cavity formation (Sopena Moros et al., JACS 146, 3262 (2024)). **OBS + REC**.
  - A benchmark treating the electron as a simple quantum solute (Jordan et al., Nat. Commun. 15, 182 (2024)).
  - Sajeev's own CCSD calculations on clusters of one to four waters (**SIM**, small clusters only) argue a third account: a cavity forms as a consequence of covalent sharing through associative attachment to the a₁ orbitals of water, not electrostatic trapping. This reframes the binary rather than siding with non-cavity.
- **Live in 2025:** A Tel Aviv chemical-physics seminar, 'The hydrated electron dilemma: to be or not to be in a cavity?', shows that the question remained open ([TAU listing](https://en.cs.tau.ac.il/node/6052); content not read).
- **Standing 2026:** Narrowing towards the cavity. Time-resolved X-ray absorption (2024) adds experimental support. A 'covalent-sharing' variant and the Schwartz non-cavity view persist [the latter unverified for 2024–26].
- **What would settle it:**
  - Structural observables that discriminate directly, such as a pump-probe or XFEL measurement of the O–O pair distribution around the electron, time-resolved X-ray absorption, and liquid-phase ESEEM analogues.
  - The partial molar volume measured and computed with converged methods.
- **Proponents:** Cavity: Kevan (prior), Mathies, Herbert, Turi, Rybkin, VandeVondele, Ceriotti. Non-cavity: Schwartz, Larsen.
- **Type:** explaining.

### 10. Does a long-lived surface-bound hydrated electron exist at the water–air interface?

- **Claim:** At the liquid water–vapour interface at 298 K, an equilibrated hydrated electron exists whose binding energy (about 1.6 eV) is distinctly lower than the bulk value (about 3.3–3.7 eV) and which persists for more than 100 ps.
- **Negation:** No such long-lived, distinctly bound surface state exists. Electrons generated at the surface either bind with bulk-like energies, internalize within picoseconds, or appear only transiently.
- **Side A observings:**
  - Liquid-microjet photoelectron spectroscopy gave a peak at about 1.6 eV, assigned to a surface-bound electron living longer than 100 ps (Siefermann et al. 2010, Nat. Chem., doi:10.1038/nchem.580 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
- **Side B observings:**
  - Later microjet experiments found binding energies of about 3.3–3.7 eV and no 1.6 eV state (Buchner, Lübcke, Heine & Schultz 2010, Rev. Sci. Instrum. [unverified]; Shreve, Yen & Neumark 2010, Chem. Phys. Lett. [unverified]). **OBS**.
  - Surface-specific second-harmonic generation found a transient surface electron that internalizes within about 100 ps, depending on the measure (Matsuzaki et al. 2016, J. Phys. Chem. Lett. [unverified]). **OBS**.
  - Corrected photoelectron spectroscopy accounting for inelastic scattering gives a single bulk-like value of about 3.7 eV (Luckhaus, Yamamoto, Suzuki 2017, Sci. Adv. [unverified]).
- **Why hard:**
  - Photoelectron energies are distorted by inelastic scattering, streaming potentials at the jet and the escape depth.
  - What 'surface' means differs between techniques (**CONV**).
- **Standing 2026:** Narrowing towards negation for the 1.6 eV long-lived state, prior state. A transient surface electron on the 10–100 ps timescale is accepted in some form.
- **What would settle it:** Photoelectron spectroscopy with independently calibrated jet potentials and multiple photon energies (to vary probing depth), combined with surface-specific nonlinear spectroscopy on the same jets.
- **Proponents:** A: Abel, Faubel, Winter (Siefermann). B: Neumark, Schultz, Suzuki, Tahara.
- **Type:** observing.

## C. Interfaces

### 11. The charge of the neat air–water interface: is the outermost molecular layer enriched in OH⁻ or in H3O⁺? (split by scope)

- **Scope split:** Electrokinetics (bubble and droplet ζ-potential) observes a net negative charge at the hydrodynamic slip plane, a few nanometres from the surface. Spectroscopy and simulation probe the outermost one or two molecular layers. These are two binaries:
  - **11a:** Within the outermost molecular layer (top about 0.5 nm) of neat water at pH 7 and 298 K, is the surface excess of OH⁻ larger than that of H3O⁺?
  - **11b:** Does the negative ζ-potential of gas bubbles in neat water come from OH⁻ adsorption, from interfacial charge transfer, or from trace surface-active impurities? See binary 12.
- **Claim (11a):** In the outermost molecular layer of neat water, the surface excess of OH⁻ exceeds that of H3O⁺. On this view, this makes the surface layer 'basic'.
- **Negation (11a):** In the same layer, H3O⁺ is enriched relative to OH⁻ (or OH⁻ is depleted). On this view, this makes the surface layer 'acidic', or at least not OH⁻-enriched.
- **Side A observings:**
  - Bubbles and oil droplets show negative ζ-potentials (about −30 to −65 mV). The isoelectric point is near pH 2–4, which was read as OH⁻ adsorption with an adsorption free energy of about −20 kT (Beattie & Djerdjev 2004, Angew. Chem.; Takahashi 2005, J. Phys. Chem. B [unverified]). **OBS** of electrophoresis; **REC** of OH⁻ adsorption.
  - Titrations of oil emulsions (Beattie et al. 2009, Faraday Discuss. [unverified]).
  - A review favouring OH⁻: Beattie, Djerdjev & Warr 2009, Faraday Discuss. 141 [unverified].
- **Side B observings:**
  - Vibrational sum-frequency (SFG) and second-harmonic generation spectroscopies of acidic and basic solutions show H3O⁺ but not OH⁻ at the surface (Petersen & Saykally 2005, Chem. Phys. Lett. [unverified]; Tarbuck, Ota & Richmond 2006, JACS [unverified]). **OBS**.
  - **Checked:** SFG of the D2O–air interface (Das, Bonn & Backus 2019, Angew. Chem. 58:15636, doi:10.1002/anie.201908420; [UvA DARE record](https://dare.uva.nl/id/9b7af8cb-b169-4824-89cf-611bea346b92)). **OBS**.
    - Surface spectra change from about 2.7 mM bulk D⁺(aq), while OD⁻ needs several hundred mM.
    - On this reading, the hydrated proton is orders of magnitude more surface-active than hydroxide.
  - **Checked (SIM):** Enhanced-sampling deep-potential MD at about M06-2X accuracy on neat water slabs (Zhang & Xu, arXiv:2404.07027v3, Nov 2024; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.07027v3)).
    - Autoionization free energies are reproduced (20.8 kcal/mol computed vs 21.4 experimental).
    - The model gives an ionic double layer: H3O⁺ in a well of about −1.6 kcal/mol around 3 Å below the surface, OH⁻ in a well of about −1.3 kcal/mol around 8 Å below.
    - So both ions are mildly attracted, with hydronium outermost, which favours the 'acidic top layer' within 11a.
  - **Related checked SIM (different interface):** ML potentials put protons in the first contact layer at graphene–water, with hydroxide bimodal (Advincula, Fong, Michaelides & Schran, arXiv:2408.04487, Aug 2024; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04487v1)).
  - Simulations by MD and polarizable force fields find H3O⁺ enriched at the surface and OH⁻ neutral or depleted (Jungwirth & Tobias 2006, Chem. Rev., doi:10.1021/cr040370w [unverified]; Buch, Milet, Vácha, Jungwirth & Devlin 2007, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.0701329104 [unverified]). **SIM**.
  - Surface tension: acids decrease it slightly relative to bases [unverified].
  - Liquid-jet X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy of hydroxide solutions finds OH⁻ not enriched [unverified].
- **Why hard:**
  - SFG and SHG need high concentrations (molar), while electrokinetics probes trace levels at neutral pH.
  - The two observing criteria differ in location.
  - 'Surface' is defined by method (**CONV**).
- **Standing 2026:** Open but narrowing. For 11a, spectroscopy (Das et al. 2019, checked) and first-principles-quality simulation (Zhang & Xu 2024, checked) both put H3O⁺ outermost. A direct pH-7 measurement of the top layer is still lacking. The electrokinetic negative charge itself is an established observing; its origin is binary 12.
- **What would settle it:** A technique sensitive to the outermost layer at pH 6–8 that detects OH⁻ or H3O⁺ at sub-micromolar bulk concentrations. Examples: heterodyne-detected SFG with an isotope-diluted HOD signature, or ambient-pressure X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy with depth profiling on rigorously clean surfaces.
- **Proponents:** OH⁻ enriched: Beattie, Djerdjev, Warr, Takahashi, Zimmermann, Mishra/Colussi (in part). H3O⁺ or no OH⁻ enrichment: Saykally, Jungwirth, Tobias, Richmond, Buch, Vácha.
- **Type:** observing (11a: what is present in the top layer).

### 12. Origin of the negative charge on hydrophobic and air–water interfaces (three accounts, split)

- **Accounts:**
  - **(i)** Specific adsorption of OH⁻ (binary 11a).
  - **(ii)** Charge transfer between water molecules in the asymmetric interfacial H-bond network leaves the interfacial layer with net negative partial charge (Vácha et al. 2011, JACS, doi:10.1021/ja201027k [unverified]; Vácha, Marsalek, Willard, Bonthuis, Netz & Jungwirth 2012, J. Phys. Chem. Lett. [unverified]; Poli, Jong & Hassanali 2020, Nat. Commun., doi:10.1038/s41467-020-14659-5 [unverified; fetch failed]). **SIM + OBS**: second-harmonic scattering by the Roke group.
  - **(iii)** Trace anionic surface-active impurities, such as fatty acids in oils or contaminants in water (Roger & Cabane 2012, Angew. Chem., doi:10.1002/anie.201108038 [unverified]; Uematsu, Bonthuis & Netz, impurity analyses 2019–20 [unverified]).
- **Proper observing binary:**
  - **Claim:** The negative electrophoretic mobility of hexadecane droplets in about 1 mM NaCl at pH 6–7 keeps its magnitude when oil and water are purified until carboxylic-acid impurities fall below a stated level (for example, about 1 nM equivalent).
  - **Negation:** Under the same conditions, the mobility tends towards zero as the impurity content falls.
- **Side A observings:** Negative mobility persists with purified oils. Isoelectric points near pH 2–4 were read as OH⁻ adsorption (Beattie, Djerdjev, Warr 2009, Faraday Discuss. [unverified]; Zimmermann, Dukhin & Werner 2001, J. Phys. Chem. B [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
- **Side B observings:** Charge titrated like carboxylic acids (pKa about 4–5). Trace fatty-acid levels were enough to account for the measured charge (Roger & Cabane 2012 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
- **Why hard:**
  - The charge involved corresponds to tiny surface coverages, about 1 charge per 10–100 nm².
  - Purity at the nanomolar level is hard to prove.
  - Simulations give partial charges in the interfacial layer, while experiments give charge at the slip plane (**CONV** gap).
- **Standing 2026:** Open; all three accounts persist (prior state).
- **What would settle it:** Mobility measured as a function of deliberate impurity doping, extrapolated to zero, with impurity levels quantified by mass spectrometry. Matched D2O/H2O comparisons. Simulations that compute slip-plane charge rather than layer partial charges.
- **Proponents:** (i) Beattie, Djerdjev, Zimmermann, Takahashi; (ii) Jungwirth, Vácha, Roke, Hassanali; (iii) Roger, Cabane, Uematsu, Netz.
- **Type:** explaining (via an observing test).

### 13. Do pure-water microdroplets spontaneously form H2O2 at the air–water interface?

- **Claim:** Microdroplets of pure water (about 1–20 µm), whether sprayed or condensed in air, produce H2O2 at micromolar levels (about 1–30 µM) from water alone. On this view, interfacial processes such as strong fields or OH⁻ oxidation do this without ozone, solid contact or dissolved-O2 chemistry.
- **Negation:** When ozone is excluded, solid–liquid contact is controlled and dissolved O2 is removed, H2O2 in such droplets is at or below detection (sub-micromolar). On this view, reported amounts come from these extrinsic sources.
- **Side A observings:**
  - Sprayed and ultrasonicated droplets were analysed by a peroxide test strip, fluorescent probes, NMR and potassium titanium oxalate. The H2O2 found decreased with droplet size (Lee, Walker, Han, Kang, Prinz, Waymouth, Nam & Zare 2019, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1911883116 [unverified; fetch failed]). **OBS**.
  - Condensation of water vapour onto cooled surfaces produced H2O2 (Lee et al. 2020, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.2020158117 [unverified]).
  - Re-confirmation after the controversy, with a different assay (Mehrgardi, Mofidfar & Zare 2022, JACS, doi:10.1021/jacs.2c02002 [unverified]).
  - Stimulated Raman spectroscopy was read as showing strong interfacial fields (Xiong, Lee, Zare & Min 2020, J. Phys. Chem. Lett. [unverified]).
- **Side B observings:**
  - No H2O2 above detection was found for condensation or ultrasonication under controlled atmospheres (Musskopf et al. 2021, J. Phys. Chem. Lett., doi:10.1021/acs.jpclett.1c02953 [unverified]).
  - H2O2 tracked the ozone in the ambient gas and vanished without it (Gallo et al. 2022, Chem. Sci., doi:10.1039/D1SC06465G [unverified]).
  - Contributions from liquid–solid contact and dissolved O2 were identified. When these were removed, H2O2 fell to background (Eatoo & Mishra 2024, Chem. Sci., doi:10.1039/D3SC06534J [unverified; fetch failed]).
  - Water–solid contact electrification as the generator of OH radicals that recombine to H2O2 (Zare-group co-authored PNAS 2022 [unverified; DOI not recalled]). This concedes a non-interfacial driver.
  - Simulations of interfacial fields: Hao, Leven & Head-Gordon 2022, Nat. Commun., doi:10.1038/s41467-021-27941-x [unverified; fetch failed]. **SIM**; what it concludes about H2O2 is not checked.
- **Checked 2024–25 positives (scope: water-in-oil droplets, not air–water):**
  - Krushinski & Dick 2024, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.2321064121 ([SUFE newsletter summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/263109)). **OBS**.
    - Stochastic electrochemistry on single subfemtolitre water droplets in 1,2-dichloroethane, collected on an ultramicroelectrode of about 5 µm.
    - A luminol control reduced the signal.
    - They estimate hundreds of µM H2O2 and read this as spontaneous formation.
  - Zhou, Du, Zhang & Zheng 2025, Chem. Sci., doi:10.1039/d4sc08098j ([RSC HTML](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/sc/d4sc08098j)). **OBS + REC**.
    - Ultrasonicated water in hexadecane (droplets of about 0.5 µm) produced about 14 mM H2O2 in 1 h.
    - Partial N2 purging cut the yield to about 23 %; ¹⁸O2 labelling put up to about 70 % of the product oxygen as coming from O2.
    - A surfactant blocking the interface suppressed formation.
    - The authors limit their claim to ultrasound-driven formation, not spontaneous formation. Ozone and cavitation were not isolated.
- **What this does to the binary:** It splits. 13a covers air–water droplets with ozone and solids controlled (narrowing towards negation). 13b covers oil–water droplets (positive under ultrasound, with the oxygen traced to dissolved O2; the spontaneous electrochemical evidence is contested in method).
- **Why hard:**
  - The quantities are tiny.
  - Ozone at ppb levels and nebulizer or electrode materials both contribute.
  - Fluorescent probes and test strips have interferences.
  - Groups use different generation methods, so the results are not like-for-like.
- **Standing 2026:**
  - For air–water droplets, narrowing towards extrinsic origins (prior state to 2024).
  - For oil–water droplets, active, with 2024 electrochemical support for spontaneous formation and 2025 ultrasound work tracing the product to dissolved O2 (both checked).
  - No retraction known [unverified].
- **What would settle it:**
  - A blinded, multi-lab protocol with ozone-free gas, polymer-free and metal-free generators, degassed water, and isotope labelling (H2¹⁸O plus ¹⁸O2) to locate the oxygen source.
  - Two independent assays per sample.
- **Proponents:** A: Zare, Nam, Head-Gordon (fields). B: Mishra, Gallo, Musskopf, Eatoo, Wilson (kinetic critiques).
- **Type:** observing.

### 14. Are bimolecular rate constants intrinsically larger at microdroplet interfaces?

- **Claim:** Solution-phase bimolecular reactions run at the air–water interface of microdroplets with intrinsic rate constants 10³–10⁶ times larger than in bulk, after correcting for evaporation.
- **Negation:** Once evaporation-driven concentration, gas-phase or ionization chemistry in the mass spectrometer, and surface partitioning are accounted for, intrinsic interfacial rate constants are within about tenfold of bulk.
- **Side A observings:** Mass-spectrometric product-to-reactant ratios in spray and electrospray, which are **OBS** of ion ratios; rates are **REC** (Yan, Bain & Cooks 2016, Angew. Chem. [unverified]; Wei, Li, Cooks & Yan 2020, Annu. Rev. Phys. Chem., doi:10.1146/annurev-physchem-121319-110654 [unverified]).
- **Side B observings:** Single-droplet levitation kinetics and analysis of electrospray methods showed that much of the acceleration comes from evaporation and the ionization step (Rovelli et al. 2020, Chem. Sci., doi:10.1039/D0SC04611F [unverified]; Wilson et al. 2020, Chem. Sci., kinetic model of interfacial enhancement [unverified]).
- **Why hard:** Mass spectrometry measures ions after desolvation, and droplet size changes during flight.
- **Standing 2026:** Narrowing. Modest enhancements are accepted for some reactions, with partition-plus-interface models (**MOD**) explaining them. The claims of 10⁶-fold acceleration are contested (prior state).
- **What would settle it:** Optical in-droplet kinetics (Raman or fluorescence) on levitated droplets of known size, compared with bulk.
- **Proponents:** A: Cooks, Zare, Yan. B: Wilson, Rovelli, Reid.
- **Type:** observing.

### 15. Is the out-of-plane dielectric response of water in slits of 2 nm or less strongly suppressed?

- **Claim:** In slit channels of hBN or graphite of height ≤ 2 nm at 298 K, water's out-of-plane dielectric constant is ε⊥ ≈ 2–4. On this view, orientational polarization perpendicular to the walls is mostly suppressed.
- **Negation:** In the same channels, ε⊥ of the water is within a factor of about 2 of bulk (≥ 40). On this view, the low measured capacitance arises from something other than the water's polarizability, such as wall or electrode contributions or the tip-model inversion.
- **Side A observings:**
  - Scanning dielectric microscopy measured local capacitance over channels ~1–300 nm high. A series-capacitor fit gave an interfacial layer of about 2–3 molecules with ε ≈ 2, and ε⊥ ≈ 2 for the thinnest channels (Fumagalli et al. 2018, Science, doi:10.1126/science.aat4191 [unverified]). **OBS** of capacitance; **REC** of ε through electrostatic modelling.
  - Simulations reproduce a strongly reduced ε⊥ arising from anticorrelated interfacial dipoles (Schlaich, Knapp & Netz 2016, PRL, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.048001 [unverified]; Bonthuis, Gekle & Netz 2011, PRL, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.107.166102 [unverified]). **SIM**.
- **Side B observings:** No direct experimental contradiction is known to me. The objections are conceptual: a local ε is ill-defined at sub-nanometre scale and nonlocal response is needed (**CONV/MOD**). No independent replication with a different method was confirmed this session [unverified].
- **Checked 2025:** 'In-plane dielectric constant and conductivity of confined water', Nature 2025, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09558-y (Fumagalli lead, with Geim; Manchester) ([Nanowerk, 15 Oct 2025](https://www.nanowerk.com/nanotechnology-news3/newsid=67894.php)). **OBS + REC**.
  - Scanning dielectric microscopy from kHz to GHz.
  - In channels of 1–2 nm, the in-plane ε reaches values close to 1000, against about 80 in bulk.
  - In-plane conductivity approaches that of superionic liquids, with a sharp 'superionic-like' transition below some size.
  - The 'ferroelectric-like' and 'superionic-like' labels are analogies (**MOD**).
  - Confined water is therefore strongly anisotropic, and the single 'dielectric constant of confined water' is a **CONV** that needs a stated direction. The out-of-plane binary above stands unchanged.
- **Why hard:** Inverting from capacitance to ε needs geometry and model assumptions, and the walls contribute.
- **Standing 2026:** The observing of low out-of-plane capacitance is broadly accepted, though replication is limited. The explaining (dead layer, interfacial anticorrelation, nonlocal dielectric) is open.
- **What would settle it:** Independent methods on the same channels, such as impedance spectroscopy, THz or IR measurements of confined water, or electrochemical capacitance with varied wall materials. A nonlocal theory that predicts both the in-plane and out-of-plane data.
- **Proponents:** Fumagalli, Geim (A, observation); Netz, Schlaich, Bocquet, Kornyshev (theory).
- **Type:** observing (the explaining sits on top).

## D. Ions and solutes

### 16. Hofmeister effects: local or long-range? (split into an observing and an explaining)

- **16a, Claim:** In aqueous salt solutions (about 0.5–6 m, 298 K), ions change water's orientational dynamics only within the first hydration shell. Water beyond it is bulk-like within error.
- **16a, Negation:** For at least some salts, for example MgSO4 (where both ions are strongly hydrated), the slowdown reaches measurably beyond the first shell under the same conditions.
- **Side A observings:**
  - Femtosecond mid-IR pump-probe of the OH stretch of HDO in D2O, in Mg(ClO4)2, NaClO4 and Na2SO4 solutions. The reorientation time of water outside the first shells matched pure water (Omta, Kropman, Woutersen & Bakker 2003, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1084801 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
  - X-ray absorption spectroscopy found that perturbations by ions are local (Cappa, Smith, Messer, Cohen & Saykally 2006, J. Phys. Chem. B [unverified]).
  - Neutron diffraction with structural refinement found ion effects on water resembling applied pressure (Mancinelli, Botti, Bruni, Ricci & Soper 2007, PCCP [unverified]).
- **Side B observings:**
  - GHz dielectric relaxation combined with fs-IR found that the number of slowed waters per MgSO4 exceeds the first-shell count, with cooperative effects between cation and anion that are not additive (Tielrooij, Garcia-Araez, Bonn & Bakker 2010, Science, doi:10.1126/science.1195723 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
  - Femtosecond elastic second-harmonic scattering found long-range orientational correlations even at micromolar ionic strength (Chen, …, Roke 2016, Sci. Adv., doi:10.1126/sciadv.1501891 [unverified]). This is a different observable: orientational order, not dynamics.
- **Why hard:**
  - The methods weigh single-molecule rotation, collective dipole relaxation and pair orientational correlation differently.
  - The dielectric kinetic-depolarization correction is model-based.
  - Counting 'shells' is a **CONV**.
- **Standing 2026:** Open. A reconciling reading says effects are local for most salts and cooperative for strongly hydrated ion pairs (prior state).
- **What would settle it:** The same samples measured by all three methods, with one simulation computing every observable from the same trajectories.
- **16b, Claim:** The Hofmeister ordering of salt effects on protein solubility, polymer cloud points and colloid stability arises mainly from direct ion–solute and ion–surface interactions.
- **16b, Negation:** It arises mainly from ions making or breaking the structure of bulk water.
- **16b evidence:**
  - PNIPAM cloud-point shifts separated into surface-tension and direct-binding terms (Zhang, Furyk, Bergbreiter & Cremer 2005, JACS [unverified]).
  - Reviews: Zhang & Cremer 2006, Curr. Opin. Chem. Biol., doi:10.1016/j.cbpa.2006.09.020 [unverified]; Jungwirth & Cremer 2014, Nat. Chem., doi:10.1038/nchem.1852 [unverified].
  - Series reversals with surface charge and polarity (Schwierz, Horinek & Netz 2010, Langmuir [unverified]).
  - The rule that ions pair preferentially with partners of matching hydration strength (Collins 1997, Biophys. J. [unverified]). **MOD**.
- **16b standing:** Largely settled towards direct interactions by about 2014 (prior state). The water-structure vocabulary survives in teaching.
- **Proponents:** Local or direct: Bakker (2003), Saykally, Cremer, Jungwirth, Netz, Collins. Long-range or cooperative: Bakker/Bonn (2010), Roke, Havenith (THz) [unverified].
- **Type:** 16a observing; 16b explaining.

### 17. How much do ions pair in simple aqueous salts?

- **Claim:** In aqueous NaCl at 1–5 m and 298 K, at least 10 % of Na⁺ ions are in contact pairs with Cl⁻. A contact pair is defined, by **CONV**, as an Na–Cl distance within the first minimum of the pair distribution, about 0.35 nm.
- **Negation:** Under the same definition and conditions, fewer than 10 % are.
- **Observings:**
  - Dielectric relaxation spectroscopy detects solvent-shared and double solvent-separated pairs in 2:2 salts such as MgSO4, but little or no pairing in alkali halides at moderate concentration (Buchner, Chen & Hefter 2004, J. Phys. Chem. B [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
  - Review: Marcus & Hefter 2006, Chem. Rev., doi:10.1021/cr040087x [unverified].
  - Simulated contact-pair fractions vary several-fold with the force field. Some parameter sets cluster or crystallize below the solubility limit (Auffinger, Cheatham & Vaiana 2007, J. Chem. Theory Comput.; Joung & Cheatham 2008, J. Phys. Chem. B [unverified]). **SIM**.
  - Diffraction coordination numbers are averages that need modelling (**REC**).
- **Why hard:**
  - The species are defined by distance cutoffs and lifetime criteria (**CONV**).
  - Each method sees a different subset: dielectric spectroscopy sees dipolar species living longer than about 1 ps, Raman sees contact pairs of polyatomic anions, and diffraction sees averages.
- **Standing 2026:** Qualitatively agreed: weak pairing for 1:1 salts, strong for 2:2. Quantitatively open (prior state). Nanoscale heterogeneity in 'water-in-salt' electrolytes is a related open question [unverified].
- **What would settle it:** An agreed definition. Joint refinement of neutron data with isotopic substitution and X-ray data, constrained by dielectric spectroscopy. Machine-learned potentials trained on high-level electronic structure.
- **Type:** observing, with a **CONV** core.

### 18. Small-solute hydrophobic hydration: water ordering ('iceberg') or cavity cost? (with a dynamics sub-binary)

- **18a, Claim:** The large negative hydration entropy of small apolar solutes at 298 K (methane, noble gases) arises mainly from enhanced H-bond ordering of first-shell water ('iceberg': Frank & Evans 1945, J. Chem. Phys., doi:10.1063/1.1724033 [unverified]).
- **18a, Negation:** It arises mainly from the cost of opening a cavity in a liquid of small, densely packed molecules, with first-shell water structure not enhanced relative to bulk.
- **Supporting the negation:**
  - Neutron diffraction with isotopic substitution on methane in water found no enhanced H-bond structure (Buchanan, Aldiwan, Soper, Creek & Koh 2005, Chem. Phys. Lett. [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
  - Cavity-statistics theories: Pratt & Chandler 1977; Lee 1985; Hummer et al. 1996 information theory [unverified]. **MOD**.
- **Mixed evidence:**
  - Raman spectroscopy with multivariate curve resolution found the hydration shells of small alcohols slightly more tetrahedral than bulk at low temperature. Shells of chains longer than about 1 nm gain dangling OH groups on heating (Davis, Gierszal, Wang & Ben-Amotz 2012, Nature, doi:10.1038/nature11570 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
- **Length-scale crossover:**
  - On this view, solvation free energy scales with volume below about 1 nm (entropy-dominated) and with area above it (enthalpy-dominated at room temperature) (Lum, Chandler & Weeks 1999, J. Phys. Chem. B, doi:10.1021/jp984837g [unverified]; Chandler 2005, Nature, doi:10.1038/nature04162 [unverified]). **MOD/SIM**.
  - Consistent single-polymer force spectroscopy: Li & Walker 2011, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1105450108 [unverified]. **OBS**.
- **18b, Claim (observing):** Reorientation of water OH groups next to methyl groups (for example in TMAO or tert-butanol, 298 K) is slowed more than fivefold for a sub-population of about four waters per methyl.
- **18b, Negation:** All first-shell waters are slowed at most about twofold.
- **18b, Side A:** fs-IR anisotropy found an immobilized fraction (Rezus & Bakker 2007, PRL, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.99.148301 [unverified]).
- **18b, Side B:**
  - NMR relaxation found modest retardation (Qvist & Halle 2008, JACS [unverified]).
  - Simulations using an extended jump model attribute the slowdown to excluded volume, about 1.4-fold (Laage, Stirnemann & Hynes 2009, J. Phys. Chem. B [unverified]).
- **Why hard:**
  - 'Structure' has no agreed meaning (**CONV**: tetrahedrality, H-bond count or dynamics).
  - Entropy–enthalpy compensation hides changes in structure.
  - Solute aggregation at high concentration biases IR measurements.
- **Standing 2026:**
  - 18a: the strong iceberg view is largely abandoned, with only subtle changes in shell structure accepted (prior state).
  - 18b: narrowing towards a modest slowdown at infinite dilution.
- **What would settle it:** Concentration series extrapolated to dilution, using IR, NMR and THz on the same solutes, plus simulations that compute every observable.
- **Proponents:** Ordering: Frank (historical), Bakker (18b, side A), Ben-Amotz (subtle ordering). Cavity or excluded volume: Chandler, Pratt, Lee, Graziano, Soper, Halle, Laage, Hynes.
- **Type:** 18a explaining; 18b observing.

### 19. Cold denaturation: how unfolded is the cold-denatured state, and what drives it?

- **Background (MOD, accepted):** A large positive ΔCp of unfolding curves the stability function (Gibbs–Helmholtz), which predicts unfolding on cooling as well as heating (Privalov 1990, Crit. Rev. Biochem. Mol. Biol. [unverified]).
- **Claim (observing):** The cold-denatured ensemble of a protein with an accessible cold transition (yeast frataxin Yfh1 near 280 K at low salt, or ubiquitin in supercooled water near 250 K) is as expanded and unstructured as its heat-denatured ensemble, judged by radius of gyration and residual secondary structure.
- **Negation:** It is measurably more compact, with more residual structure.
- **Observings:** Yfh1 unfolds near 0 °C without denaturants, studied by CD and NMR (Pastore, Martin, Politou, Kondapalli, Stemmler & Temussi 2007, JACS [unverified]). Hot and cold denatured states differ in residual structure in several NMR and SAXS studies [unverified; specific papers not recalled].
- **Explaining sub-binary:** Does cooling mainly weaken the hydrophobic effect through apolar hydration thermodynamics, or does water penetrate the core through a density- or pressure-like mechanism (Dias et al. 2008, PRL [unverified])? The two may be compatible descriptions (**CONV** note).
- **Why hard:** Most proteins cold-denature only below 0 °C, which needs supercooling, emulsions, pressure or destabilizing mutations, and each of these perturbs the protein.
- **Standing 2026:** Thermodynamics settled; structure and mechanism open (prior state).
- **What would settle it:** Matched SAXS, NMR and single-molecule FRET on the hot and cold states of the same construct, with no added perturbant.
- **Proponents:** Privalov, Makhatadze (thermodynamics); Pastore, Temussi (Yfh1); Dill (models).
- **Type:** observing (the state), explaining (the cause).

### 20. Is anomalous underscreening a bulk property of concentrated electrolytes?

- **Claim:** In concentrated aqueous electrolytes (for example NaCl above about 1 M) and in ionic liquids at about 295 K, the bulk electrostatic screening length grows with concentration. It reaches several nanometres, and the ratio of screening length to Debye length scales with the cube of the ratio of ion diameter to Debye length. The long-range forces measured between surfaces report this bulk length.
- **Negation:** Under the same conditions, the bulk screening length of these electrolytes does not show this anomalous growth: it stays within a few ion diameters, with oscillatory decay beyond the Kirkwood crossover. On this view, the long-range forces between surfaces come from surface, confinement or instrumental effects.
- **Side A observings:**
  - Surface force apparatus (SFA) with mica in NaCl, LiCl and ionic liquids found exponential tails with decay lengths rising with concentration (Smith, Lee & Perkin 2016, J. Phys. Chem. Lett., doi:10.1021/acs.jpclett.6b00867 [unverified]).
  - The scaling relation (Lee, Perez-Martinez, Smith & Perkin 2017, PRL 119, 026002, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.119.026002 [unverified; fetch failed]). **OBS** of force against distance; **REC** of the decay length.
  - Ionic liquids described as behaving like dilute electrolytes (Gebbie et al. 2013, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1307871110 [unverified]).
  - Supporting data from fluorescence and other methods: Gaddam & Ducker 2019, Langmuir [unverified].
- **Side B observings:**
  - Force measurements between smooth silica surfaces found no anomalous long-range screening in concentrated aqueous electrolytes (Kumar et al. 2022, J. Colloid Interface Sci., doi:10.1016/j.jcis.2022.04.111 [unverified; fetch failed]). **OBS**.
  - Very large-scale bulk simulations found screening lengths far shorter than the SFA values (Zeman, Kondrat & Holm 2020, Chem. Commun.; Coles et al. 2020, J. Phys. Chem. B; Krucker-Velasquez & Swan 2021, J. Chem. Phys. [unverified]). **SIM**.
  - Theory: Kjellander's dressed-ion theory gives underscreening but not of the measured magnitude [unverified].
  - A 2023–24 critical overview, 'known unknowns' (Elliott et al. 2024 [unverified]).
- **Checked 2024–26:**
  - **Interfacial-origin result:** Tilger, Ohnesorge, Tsintsaris, Robertson, von Klitzing & Kurihara, 'Colloidal probe AFM reveals anomalous underscreening: a matter of experimental conditions', arXiv:2603.08326 (Mar 2026) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08326v1)). **OBS + REC**.
    - Silica probe against quartz in LaCl3 at 0.001–1 M.
    - Long decay lengths consistent with p = 3 appeared only with SFA-like conditions together: an 8.5 µm probe, 48 h equilibration and slow approach.
    - The authors ascribe them to ion adsorption and slow interfacial ordering, not bulk screening.
    - The fits are model-dependent (DLVO plus an ad hoc attraction).
  - **Bulk-scattering route:** Dinpajooh, Biasin, …, Benmore, Schenter, Fulton, J. Chem. Phys. 161, 151102 (2024), doi:10.1063/5.0234518 ([PNNL highlight](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/detecting-underscreening-generalized-kirkwood-transitions-aqueous-electrolytes)). **OBS + MOD**.
    - Links the SAXS prepeak to charge–charge correlations and generalized Kirkwood transitions, giving bulk access to screening lengths.
    - The highlight does not say whether the bulk lengths are anomalous.
  - **Heuristic review:** Shvydka & Karpov, arXiv:2606.15492 (2026) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15492)). **MOD**.
    - A Coulomb-binding barrier model fitted to the Smith–Lee–Perkin data.
    - It lists the competing explanations (clustering, steric or hydrodynamic, glassy relaxation) without adjudicating, and cites Gebbie et al. 2015, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1508366112; Lee et al. 2017, Faraday Discuss., doi:10.1039/c6fd00250a; and Hartel et al. 2023, PRL, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.130.108202 as reporting long or anomalous lengths.
- **Why hard:**
  - The forces at 5–10 nm are small, and the surface charge, regulation and roughness of mica differ from silica.
  - Simulations need very large boxes.
  - The 'screening length' of a force law and of a bulk correlation function may not be the same quantity (**CONV**).
- **Standing 2026:** Open and contested. As an observing, long decay lengths are reproduced by a second method (CP-AFM, 2026) only under SFA-like conditions, which that study reads as interfacial in origin. As a bulk property, it remains unshown: bulk SAXS methods (2024) now exist but have not been reported as confirming anomalous lengths.
- **What would settle it:**
  - A bulk-only measurement, such as small-angle scattering of charge–charge correlations or a determination of activity coefficients sensitive to long-range decay.
  - SFA and colloidal-probe measurements on identical solutions with several surface chemistries in one blinded comparison.
- **Proponents:** A: Perkin, Lee, Smith, Gebbie, Israelachvili (prior), Ducker. B: Holm, Kondrat, Espinosa-Marzal, Swan, Kjellander (in part), Craig.
- **Type:** observing (a bulk property, or not?).

## E. Other contested water claims

### 21. Nuclear quantum effects on hydrogen bonding: net weakening or net strengthening in liquid water?

- **Claim:** At 298 K, quantizing the hydrogen nuclei leaves liquid water's H-bond network on balance less structured than it would be with heavier, more classical nuclei. The measurable proxy (**CONV**) is that H2O is less structured than D2O, and D2O less than T2O, in the same direction for structure, diffusion and the temperature of maximum density.
- **Negation:** Quantizing the nuclei leaves the network on balance more structured, or the isotope ordering differs between these observables.
- **Observings:**
  - The temperature of maximum density is about 4 °C for H2O and about 11 °C for D2O. D2O has slower self-diffusion and higher viscosity. **OBS** (standard data).
  - Neutron and X-ray diffraction resolved small structural differences between H2O and D2O, with H2O less structured (Soper & Benmore 2008, PRL 101, 065502, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.101.065502 [unverified]). **OBS + REC**.
- **Models and simulations:**
  - The 'competing quantum effects' account: zero-point motion of the stretch strengthens H-bonds, while librational delocalization weakens them, so the net effect is small and depends on the model (Habershon, Markland & Manolopoulos 2009, J. Chem. Phys., doi:10.1063/1.3167790 [unverified]). **SIM/MOD**.
  - The sign depends on H-bond strength: weak bonds are weakened and strong bonds strengthened (Li, Walker & Michaelides 2011, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1016653108 [unverified]).
  - Transient proton excursions along H-bonds (Ceriotti, Cuny, Parrinello & Manolopoulos 2013, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1308560110 [unverified]).
  - Review: Ceriotti et al. 2016, Chem. Rev., doi:10.1021/acs.chemrev.5b00674 [unverified].
- **Why hard:**
  - 'Classical water' cannot be observed, so isotope substitution serves as the proxy.
  - The net sign is a small difference between two large effects, and it depends on the potential.
- **Standing 2026:** Narrowing. The observing of a small net destructuring in H2O relative to D2O is accepted. Whether this cancellation is a general rule for H-bonded systems is open (prior state).
- **What would settle it:** Isotope series including T2O. Path-integral simulations with high-level potentials (for example MB-pol, or machine-learned potentials trained on coupled-cluster data) that reproduce every isotope observable without fitting.
- **Proponents:** Manolopoulos, Markland, Michaelides, Ceriotti, Soper, Paesani.
- **Type:** explaining (the observing side is settled).

### 22. Heavy versus light water in biology

- **22a, Claim (observing):** Pure D2O tastes distinguishably different to humans (sweet) from purified H2O, in blinded discrimination tests.
- **22a, Negation:** In such tests, D2O cannot be told apart from H2O.
- **Negation side:** An early self-test reported no distinct taste (Urey & Failla 1935, Science [unverified; DOI not recalled]). **OBS**, unblinded.
- **Claim side:**
  - Human triangle and sensory tests found D2O distinguishable and sweet.
  - Cells expressing the sweet receptor TAS1R2/TAS1R3 responded to D2O, and the sweet-taste inhibitor lactisole suppressed both the perception and the cellular response.
  - Mice showed no preference.
  - Source: Ben Abu, Mason, …, Niv 2021, Commun. Biol., doi:10.1038/s42003-021-01964-y [unverified; fetch failed]. **OBS**; receptor-mediation is **REC** from the inhibitor results.
- **Standing 2026:** Positive in one modern study. Independent replication was not checked [unverified].
- **What would settle it:** Multi-site blinded replication with matched purity (minerals, organics) and controls for isotope exchange in saliva.
- **22b, explaining:** Are D2O's whole-organism effects (mammalian toxicity at high body-water replacement, lengthening of circadian periods, arrest of mitosis) mainly kinetic isotope effects on specific reactions, or mainly solvent isotope effects on the stability and dynamics of proteins and assemblies (D2O tends to stabilize proteins and microtubules)?
- **22b evidence:** Solvent isotope effects on protein stability (Makhatadze, Clore & Gronenborn 1995, Nat. Struct. Biol. [unverified]). Classic circadian results in D2O (Bruce & Pittendrigh 1960 [unverified]).
- **22b standing:** Open and probably not a strict binary, since both mechanisms act.
- **Fringe:** Health claims for 'deuterium-depleted water' at natural-abundance levels are fringe. Natural D/H (about 150 ppm) is far below the levels at which isotope effects on organisms are seen [unverified for clinical-trial status].
- **Type:** 22a observing; 22b explaining.

### 23. Do long-lived gas-filled bulk nanobubbles exist in water?

- **Claim:** Water treated by hydrodynamic cavitation, electrolysis or pressure cycling contains gas-filled bubbles of about 50–200 nm at more than 10⁷ per mL that persist for days.
- **Negation:** These particle populations are not gas-filled bubbles but solid or oil contaminants, or nanodroplets. Gas bubbles of that size dissolve within milliseconds or less because of their Laplace pressure (**MOD**: Epstein–Plesset).
- **Claim-side observings:**
  - Nanoparticle tracking and dynamic light scattering detect particles. Resonant mass measurement separates positively and negatively buoyant particles, and particle counts fall after freeze–thaw cycles (Nirmalkar, Pacek & Barigou 2018, Soft Matter [unverified]). **OBS**.
- **Negation-side observings:**
  - Particle 'nanobubbles' appear without gas, or come from shed contaminants (Alheshibri, Qian, Jehannin & Craig 2016, Langmuir, doi:10.1021/acs.langmuir.6b01644 [unverified]).
  - Measurements of how the populations respond to degassing [unverified].
- **Scope note:** Surface nanobubbles are a different binary and are broadly settled as existing. They are stabilized by contact-line pinning and gas oversaturation (Lohse & Zhang 2015, Rev. Mod. Phys., doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.87.981 [unverified]).
- **Why hard:** Every counting method detects scatterers, not gas. Proving gas content needs density, refractive-index or compressibility contrast for each particle.
- **Standing 2026:** Open for bulk nanobubbles (prior state).
- **What would settle it:** Single-particle density or compressibility (for example acoustic response or resonant mass) measured jointly with gas-isotope labelling and degassing tests, by labs with opposing positions.
- **Proponents:** A: Ohgaki, Ushikubo, Nirmalkar, Barigou. B: Craig, Alheshibri.
- **Type:** observing.

### Fringe or rejected claims (historically notable; labelled)

- **'Exclusion-zone' water as a 'fourth phase' (fringe).**
  - **OBS:** Microspheres and solutes are excluded from zones about 100–200 µm wide next to Nafion and other hydrophilic surfaces (Zheng, Chin, Zhao, Wexler & Pollack 2006, Adv. Colloid Interface Sci., doi:10.1016/j.cis.2006.10.001 [unverified]). The exclusion itself reproduces.
  - **MOD (fringe):** Pollack reads it as a liquid-crystalline 'fourth phase' of water.
  - **Reason labelled fringe:**
    - No phase boundary, diffraction or NMR evidence for a distinct phase.
    - The exclusion is accounted for by diffusiophoresis and diffusioosmosis driven by ion-exchange gradients from the Nafion (Florea, Musa, Huyghe & Wyss 2014, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1322857111 [unverified]; Schurr 2013, J. Phys. Chem. B [unverified]).
- **Water 'memory' (rejected).**
  - **OBS reported:** Basophil degranulation by antiserum diluted far beyond Avogadro limits (Davenas et al. 1988, Nature, doi:10.1038/333816a0 [unverified]).
  - Supervised blinded repeats were negative (Maddox, Randi & Stewart 1988, Nature, doi:10.1038/334287a0 [unverified]).
  - **Reason rejected:**
    - Failure under blinding.
    - No mechanism: H-bond-network correlations are lost within about 1 ps, as seen in OH-stretch spectral memory decaying in tens to hundreds of femtoseconds (Cowan et al. 2005, Nature, doi:10.1038/nature03383 [unverified]).
- **Polywater (rejected; historical, 1962–1973).** Anomalous 'water' condensed in quartz capillaries (Fedyakin, Deryagin) was traced to dissolved silica and organic contaminants. The proponents withdrew the claim in 1973 [unverified; DOIs not recalled]. It is a reference case of a contamination artefact, useful as an analogue for the microdroplet debates.

## Further candidate binaries (one line each; not developed, all [unverified])

- **Ice premelting:** the onset temperature and thickness of the quasi-liquid layer on ice. Sum-frequency spectroscopy, ellipsometry and AFM disagree by tens of kelvin.
- **Homogeneous ice nucleation rates near 235 K:** do classical nucleation theory, simulation and experiment agree within one order of magnitude?
- **Cubic ice:** does pure ice Ic form from supercooled water, or only stacking-disordered ice Isd? Pure Ic was made from hydrate routes in 2020 (del Rosso et al.; Komatsu et al.).
- **Depletion layer at extended hydrophobic surfaces:** is its width measurable and sub-ångström, or about 1 nm? X-ray reflectivity results (Mezger 2006; Poynor 2006) diverge.
- **'On-water' reaction acceleration:** do dangling OH groups at the oil–water interface catalyse it, or hydrophobic packing?
- **Halide surface propensity:** are I⁻ and Br⁻ enriched at the air–water surface? Simulations by Jungwirth and Tobias versus some photoelectron and second-harmonic data; narrowing towards yes for I⁻.
- **Proton transport through pristine graphene or hBN:** intrinsic, or defect-mediated?
- **Fast water flow in carbon nanotubes:** how large is the flow enhancement, and is it intrinsic? Slip depends on radius (Secchi et al. 2016).
- **Glycerol–water polyamorphism:** is the liquid–liquid transition in aqueous glycerol real, or ice nanocrystallization?
- **Fragile-to-strong crossover in supercooled water:** does it occur near 220–228 K, or is it an artefact of confinement in nanopores?
- **Long-range 'hydration dance':** do terahertz-detected protein hydration shells extend beyond 1 nm?
- **Water-in-salt electrolytes:** nanoheterogeneous domains, or homogeneous?
- **Isotope fractionation at the air–water interface:** does the surface enrich in D?
