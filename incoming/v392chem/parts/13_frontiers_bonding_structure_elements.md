# Frontiers of chemical bonding, molecular structure, quantum-chemical foundations and the elements: frontier binaries (status as of 23 October 2026)

## Key gathered points

- Most of these disputes are about explaining, not observing. The measured quantities (bond energies, Compton profiles, NMR couplings, photoelectron spectra, adsorption enthalpies) are accepted; what they stand for is disputed. New data alone rarely close such binaries.
- Observing binaries still open in 2026:
  - Metallic solid hydrogen under static compression. The Dias–Silvera 2017 claim is not accepted, and the 2020 infrared evidence was called "probable" by its own authors [unverified].
  - A measured parity-violation energy difference between enantiomers. None had been demonstrated as of the latest review seen.
  - The bulk character of Cn, Fl and Og. There are few-atom data for Cn and Fl and none for Og.
- Narrowing in 2023–2026: superheavy p-block chemistry. GSI in 2024 gave SiO2 adsorption enthalpies of about 58 kJ/mol for Nh and 54 kJ/mol for Mc. Both bind more weakly than Tl and Bi and more strongly than closed-shell Cn and Fl, which is read as relativistic 7p1/2 stabilisation.
- Settled or nearly settled:
  - There is no substantial d-orbital role in main-group hypervalency (consensus since about 1990).
  - He2 exists as a van der Waals dimer about 52 Å across, bound by about 1 mK.
  - The 2-norbornyl cation is non-classical (2013 crystal structure).
  - NaCl3 and Na3Cl were made at high pressure.
- Settled by convention, not data: group 3. IUPAC's 1988 report and 2021 provisional report favour Sc–Y–Lu–Lr, and the 2021 report calls the choice a convention. I saw no final decision.
- Foundations:
  - Woolley's argument remains an open interpretive problem. It holds that an isolated molecule's Coulomb eigenstates carry no shape. Responses invoke the Born–Oppenheimer approximation, decoherence, or the measurement problem.
  - Hund's paradox: collisional decoherence (2009) is cited as resolving it for small molecules.
- Aromaticity indices disagree in documented cases. For Herges' Möbius hydrocarbon, HOMA is 0.35, or −0.02 on reanalysis. The multidimensional view prevails. Global ring currents in porphyrin nanorings and Al4^2− aromaticity remain contested readings of accepted spectra.
- Classification binaries (hydrogen bond, halogen bond, charge-shift bond, aromatic, group 3) turn on a stated membership criterion. IUPAC's definitions (hydrogen bond 2011, halogen bond 2013) list types of evidence rather than one test.
- Source caveat: the search budget was spent before any search ran, and most journal pages were blocked. These notes rest on about 45 Wikipedia pages opened this session and the DOIs they print. Prior knowledge is marked [unverified].

## A. The nature of the bond

### B1. Origin of the covalent bond: kinetic- vs potential-energy lowering

- **Claim.** In H2+ and H2, and by extension other covalent bonds, a drop in electronic kinetic energy drives the bonding energy lowering. The drop comes from constructive interference (delocalisation) of atomic orbitals. This is the Hellmann 1933 / Ruedenberg 1962 line [bibliography unverified].
- **Negation.** In the same molecules, a drop in potential energy drives the lowering, through attraction between nuclei and charge gathered between them. Kinetic lowering then plays no driving role. This is the Slater-virial / Feynman-electrostatic textbook line [unverified].
- **Scope.**
  - Both sides accept the virial theorem: at equilibrium, total kinetic energy rises and potential energy falls relative to the free atoms [unverified].
  - The dispute is about which term drives the energy lowering during approach under a chosen partition: interference kinetic energy, quasi-classical electrostatics, or orbital contraction.
  - A third account holds that the causal question depends on the partition. See Frenking & Krapp 2007, "Unicorns in the world of chemical bonding models", doi:10.1002/jcc.20543, via [WP: Chemical bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_bond); content [unverified].
  - The clean binary therefore exists only inside a Ruedenberg-type partition.
- **Observings.**
  - Measured: dissociation energies and bond lengths of H2+ and H2 {observing}. Every account reproduces them.
  - Added by the models: decompositions of near-exact wavefunctions into kinetic, potential, interference and contraction terms {model construct}{simulation}. No instrument measures these terms.
- **Claim side.**
  - WP: Chemical bond now gives the kinetic account as standard. It credits stability to electrons spreading over a larger region (longer de Broglie wavelength), not to potential-energy lowering.
  - It cites Levine & Head-Gordon 2020, Nature Communications, doi:10.1038/s41467-020-18670-8 ([PMC7524788](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7524788)). From memory, the paper separates interference kinetic lowering from contraction-driven potential lowering [unverified; fetch blocked].
  - Also on this side: Rioux 2001 (doi:10.1007/s00897010509a, via WP); Bacskay & Nordholm [unverified].
- **Negation side.** Electrostatic-theorem (Hellmann–Feynman) accounts. Critics note that kinetic energy at equilibrium exceeds the free-atom value, so "kinetic lowering" concerns one sub-term, not the total [unverified].
- **Why hard.** Energy components are not observables. Results depend on the reference state (free vs contracted atoms) and on the partition. The virial theorem forces kinetic and potential energy to change in opposite directions.
- **Standing 2026.**
  - Narrowing toward the kinetic/interference view among those who accept the partition. It is now textbook-level on Wikipedia.
  - The objection that partitions are arbitrary stands.
  - I found no decisive 2023–2026 paper [gap].
- **Settle.** A partition tied to an observable or a limit theorem, or proof that all reasonable partitions agree. Empirical settlement is unlikely.
- **Proponents.**
  - Kinetic: Hellmann, Ruedenberg, Bacskay & Nordholm, Levine & Head-Gordon [partly unverified].
  - Potential: Slater–Feynman tradition [unverified].
  - Partition-sceptic: Frenking.
- **Type.** Explaining.

### B2. Is the hydrogen bond partly covalent? (split by evidence)

- **Criterion (IUPAC 2011).** A hydrogen bond is an attraction between an X–H group (X more electronegative than H) and an atom or group, with evidence of bond formation. The definition lists electrostatics, charge transfer and dispersion as contributors (Arunan et al., doi:10.1351/PAC-REC-10-01-02, via [WP: Hydrogen bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bond)) {convention}.

#### B2a. Does ice's Compton anisotropy measure covalency?

- **Claim.** The anisotropy of ice's Compton profile directly measures covalent (interference) character of O–H···O.
- **Negation.** The same anisotropy is reproduced by antisymmetrised (Pauli-exchange) monomer wavefunctions without charge transfer, so it does not measure covalency.
- **Observings.**
  - Measured: directional Compton profiles of ice with synchrotron X-rays (Isaacs et al. 1999, Phys. Rev. Lett. 82:600, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.82.600, via WP) {observing}. Sample and beam details [unverified].
  - The Fourier-transformed difference oscillates at hydrogen-bond distances {reconstruction}. The authors read the oscillations as interference across the hydrogen bond {model construct}.
  - Ghanty et al. 2000, JACS 122:1210 (doi:10.1021/ja9937019, via WP) challenged this reading. From memory, they reproduced the anisotropy with negligible charge transfer [unverified].
  - Barbiellini & Shukla 2002 (doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.66.235101; arXiv cond-mat/0210316) is described on WP as clarifying the reading; content [unverified].
- **Why hard.** Covalency has no definition independent of a model. Exchange and charge transfer both make fringes. Ice is a proton-disordered solid.
- **Standing 2026.** The data stand. The covalency reading is contested and largely terminological, and the field moved to the mixed description in IUPAC 2011. I found no 2023–2026 change [gap].
- **Settle.** An operational definition of covalency mapped onto the profile. Then exchange and charge transfer separated in calculation (e.g. charge-transfer-blocked wavefunctions) and compared with the data.
- **Proponents.** Covalent: Isaacs, Shukla, Barbiellini [partly unverified]. Sceptical: Ghanty, Davidson and co-workers.
- **Type.** Explaining an observing.

#### B2b. Do NMR couplings across hydrogen bonds show covalency?

- **Claim.** Spin–spin couplings transmitted across hydrogen bonds show covalent electron sharing.
- **Negation.** The couplings show only wavefunction overlap. Overlap also couples non-bonded nuclei through space, so the couplings do not establish covalency.
- **Observings.**
  - Couplings across hydrogen bonds were first reported in 1992 (Blake et al., doi:10.1007/BF02192814; doi:10.1021/ja00038a084, via [WP: J-coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-coupling)) {observing}.
  - An amide-H to carbonyl-C coupling across a hydrogen bond was seen in a perdeuterated protein (Cordier, Rogowski, Grzesiek & Bax 1999, doi:10.1006/jmre.1999.1899) {observing: splittings in Hz}.
  - The canonical cases are [unverified]: base-pair N–N couplings of about 6–7 Hz (Dingley & Grzesiek 1998) and sub-hertz N–C′ couplings in ubiquitin (Cornilescu et al. 1999).
- **Claim side.**
  - These couplings follow the electron-mediated polarisation mechanism of couplings through covalent bonds (Dingley, Cordier & Grzesiek 2001, doi:10.1002/1099-0534(2001)13:2<103::AID-CMR1001>3.0.CO;2-M) {model construct}.
  - WP: Hydrogen bond takes this to require some covalency.
  - The NBO account calls this "resonance covalency" (Weinhold & Klein 2014, doi:10.1039/C4RP00030G).
- **Negation side.** Through-space couplings between non-bonded nuclei (e.g. F···F) show that a J coupling needs overlap, not a bond [unverified].
- **Why hard.** J is an observable; covalency is not.
- **Standing 2026.** The observing is settled, and these couplings are routinely used to detect hydrogen bonds in biomolecules. The covalency reading is widely adopted but definitional.
- **Settle.** A definition of covalency that holds across schemes, plus a correlation between J and charge transfer that survives several decompositions.
- **Proponents.** Covalent: Grzesiek, Bax, Dingley, Weinhold. Sceptical: electrostatic tradition [unverified].
- **Type.** Explaining.

### B3. σ-hole interactions (halogen, chalcogen bonds): electrostatic vs charge transfer

- **Criterion (IUPAC 2013).** Evidence of net attraction between an electrophilic region on a halogen and a nucleophilic region (Desiraju et al., doi:10.1351/pac-rec-12-05-10, via [WP: Halogen bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halogen_bond)) {convention}. I reached no chalcogen-bond sources [gap].
- **Claim.** Halogen bonds are predominantly electrostatic: a positive σ-hole meets a nucleophile, plus polarisation and dispersion. Charge transfer is not a separate contributor.
- **Negation.** In the same complexes, distinct n→σ* charge transfer contributes substantially to binding and directionality.
- **Scope.** Three proper binaries, each scoped by strength (weak complexes vs strong ones such as I2···amine):
  - Is directionality explained by electrostatics alone?
  - Is binding dominated by electrostatics?
  - Can polarisation and charge transfer be separated at all?
- **Observings.**
  - Measured: X-ray structure of Br2·1,4-dioxane (Hassel & Hvoslef 1954, [Acta Chem. Scand.](http://actachemscand.org/pdf/acta_vol_08_p0873.pdf)). The O···Br distance is 2.71 Å against a 3.35 Å van der Waals sum, in near-linear geometry {observing}. Hassel read it as charge transfer (doi:10.1126/science.170.3957.497) {model construct}.
  - Legon's gas-phase rotational spectra of B···XY adducts serve both sides {observing}.
- **Electrostatic side.**
  - The σ-hole comes from computed electrostatic potentials (Clark et al. 2007, doi:10.1007/s00894-006-0130-2; Politzer, Murray & Clark 2010, doi:10.1039/c004189k) {model construct}{simulation}.
  - Kelvin-probe force microscopy imaged a σ-hole (Mallada et al. 2021, Science, doi:10.1126/science.abk1479) and a π-hole (2023, doi:10.1038/s41467-023-40593-3) {observing: contact-potential map}.
  - My inference: the images show the anisotropy exists, not its share of the binding energy.
- **Charge-transfer side.**
  - Kohn–Sham MO theory with energy decomposition compared halogen and hydrogen bonds (Wolters & Bickelhaupt 2012, doi:10.1002/open.201100015) {simulation}; findings [unverified].
  - Spectroscopy showed π-covalency comparable to coordination bonds (Kellett, Kennepohl & Berlinguette 2020, doi:10.1038/s41467-020-17122-7) {observing → reconstruction}; method [unverified].
- **Why hard.** Energy-decomposition terms are not observables. The line between charge transfer and polarisation shifts with scheme and basis. Experiments probe charge or covalency signatures, never energy shares.
- **Standing 2026.**
  - Open, and partly reframed as dependence on the scheme.
  - Common working view [unverified]: electrostatics governs directionality in weak complexes, and charge transfer grows in strong ones.
  - I found no 2023–2026 closure [gap].
- **Settle.** Observables specific to charge transfer (XAS pre-edge, NMR/NQR), tracked along a graded series and matched by several decomposition schemes that agree. Alternatively, a consensus that the question is ill-posed.
- **Proponents.** Electrostatic: Politzer, Murray, Clark. Charge transfer: Hassel (historic), Wolters & Bickelhaupt, Kennepohl, Weinhold.
- **Type.** Explaining.

### B4. Is C2 quadruply bonded?

- **Claim.** Ground-state dicarbon (X¹Σg⁺) has four bonding interactions: σ, two π, and a weak fourth "inverted" bond.
- **Negation.** The same molecule has at most three bonding interactions. The MO count gives 2; other analyses give between 2 and 3.
- **Scope.**
  - Bond order has several definitions: MO occupation, Wiberg/Mayer indices, VB bond count, force constant. [WP: Bond order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_order) notes that a standard quantum definition "has been debated for a long time".
  - The clean binary is narrower: does a VB/full-CI analysis show a fourth interaction with positive bond energy, and does an independent observable track it?
- **Observings.**
  - Spectroscopic constants: bond length about 1.24 Å, vibrational frequency, dissociation energy {observing} [values unverified].
  - A 2021 PNAS photodissociation study measured the dissociation energy by velocity-map imaging: about 602 kJ/mol, doi:10.1073/pnas.2113315118 [unverified; fetch blocked].
- **Claim side.**
  - Shaik, Danovich, Wu, Su, Rzepa & Hiberty 2012, Nature Chemistry 4:195 (doi:10.1038/nchem.1263, via [WP: Quadruple bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadruple_bond)).
  - Their VB and full-CI calculations {simulation}{model construct} gave a fourth bond of about 12–17 kcal/mol, extended to CN⁺, BN and NO⁺ [figures unverified].
- **Negation side.**
  - Grunenberg's commentary in the same issue (doi:10.1038/nchem.1274) flagged the dispute.
  - Frenking & Hermann (2013 onward) used MO/EDA and force constants to argue for a double bond with partial triple character [unverified].
  - Standard MO theory gives 2 (WP: Quadruple bond).
- **Why hard.** Multiplicity is a model construct. The fourth interaction is weak and has no exclusive observable. Force-constant comparisons depend on reference molecules.
- **Standing 2026.**
  - Open, as a definitional standoff.
  - The 2021 dissociation energy constrains all accounts but does not count bonds [unverified].
  - I found no 2023–2026 resolution [gap].
- **Settle.** One operational bond-order definition tied to an observable, or a spectroscopic signature unique to the fourth bond. None is known to me [unverified].
- **Proponents.** For: Shaik, Danovich, Hiberty, Wu, Rzepa. Against: Frenking, Hermann; Grunenberg sceptical [partly unverified].
- **Type.** Explaining, with a supporting observing (the dissociation energy).

### B5. Do QTAIM bond paths indicate bonds? (H–H in phenanthrene and biphenyl)

- **Claim.** A bond path between two nuclei indicates that they are bonded. A bond path is a line of maximum electron density through a density saddle point. On this view the bay H···H paths in planar biphenyl and phenanthrene are stabilising H–H bonds.
- **Negation.** In the same molecules, H···H bond paths do not indicate stabilisation. The bay contact is a net steric (Pauli) repulsion, and phenanthrene's extra stability over anthracene has other causes.
- **Observings.**
  - Phenanthrene is thermochemically more stable than anthracene {observing} [values unverified].
  - Bond paths come from computed or X-ray-refined densities. [WP: Atoms in molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoms_in_molecules) defines them as paired gradient trajectories from the saddle point to the nuclei {model construct on simulation or reconstruction}.
- **Claim side.**
  - Matta, Hernández-Trujillo, Tang & Bader 2003 (doi:10.1002/chem.200204626) assigned the phenanthrene H–H path a stabilisation of 12.1 kcal/mol (51 kJ/mol) {simulation}. An ortho H–H bond was credited with stabilising planar biphenyl.
  - Bader's general programme: Chem. Rev. 1991 (doi:10.1021/cr00005a013); 2005 (doi:10.1007/s00706-005-0307-x).
- **Negation side.**
  - Poater, Visser, Solà & Bickelhaupt 2007 (doi:10.1021/jo061637p) credit phenanthrene's stability to better π overlap in the central double bond. Per WP, they do not dispute that the paths exist; that the H···H contact is repulsive is from memory [unverified].
  - Dunitz & Gavezzotti 2005 (doi:10.1002/anie.200460157) explain it by resonance.
  - Other critics (Pendás, Shahbazian) [unverified].
- **Why hard.** Bond paths are well defined topologically; "bond" is not. Assigning an energy to one contact needs a partition (IQA, EDA), and partitions disagree.
- **Standing 2026.**
  - Open, as a dispute over the membership criterion for "bond".
  - Many no longer treat a path as sufficient for bonding. Some QTAIM authors now call it a "privileged exchange channel" [unverified].
  - I found no 2023–2026 decisive work [gap].
- **Settle.** A bonding criterion stated in observables, or a demonstration that bond paths track measurable force constants or energies in every case, including bay H···H.
- **Proponents.** Paths as bonds: Bader, Matta, Hernández-Trujillo. Critics: Bickelhaupt, Solà, Poater, Dunitz, Gavezzotti.
- **Type.** Explaining; classification convention.

### B6. Hypervalency: d-orbital participation vs 3c–4e and ionic bonding

- **Claim.** In SF6, PF5 and XeF2, valence d orbitals on the central atom form an expanded octet (sp³d, sp³d²) and contribute substantially to bonding.
- **Negation.** In the same molecules, d functions only polarise. Bonding is 3c–4e plus large ionic contributions within an octet.
- **Observings.**
  - Structures, vibrational spectra and stabilities of SF6, PF5, XeF2 and I3⁻ {observing}.
  - PF5 exists but PH5 is unknown ([WP: Hypervalent molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypervalent_molecule)) {observing}. Some read this as pointing to ligand electronegativity.
  - All bonding descriptions are {model construct}.
- **Negation side (dominant).**
  - The 3c–4e model: Rundle 1947/1949; Pimentel 1951 (doi:10.1063/1.1748245; [WP: Three-center four-electron bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-center_four-electron_bond)).
  - d functions reflect basis-set needs, not bonding: Reed & Weinhold 1986 (doi:10.1021/ja00273a006); Magnusson 1990 (doi:10.1021/ja00178a014).
  - WP says modern theory supports Langmuir's octet-plus-ionic view.
  - Gillespie 2002 found no ELF difference between hypervalent and octet bonds (doi:10.1016/S0010-8545(02)00102-9).
  - Braïda & Hiberty 2013 (doi:10.1038/nchem.1619), ab initio VB on XeF2: per WP, ionic structures make up about 81% of the wavefunction; an expanded-octet-type structure at about 11% still adds about 30 kJ/mol {simulation}.
- **Residual claim side.**
  - The model persists in teaching.
  - IUPAC recommends expanded-octet drawings for sulfones and phosphoranes for clarity despite "lack of chemical realism" (WP) {convention}.
- **Definitional binary.**
  - Musher 1969 defines hypervalency by valence state (doi:10.1002/anie.196900541).
  - Durrant 2015 instead uses a computed valence electron equivalent γ > 8 (doi:10.1039/C5SC02076J). Under it, PCl5, SO4²⁻ and XeF4 are hypercoordinate but not hypervalent, while O3, N2O and Me3NO are hypervalent.
  - Schleyer 1984 prefers the term "hypercoordinate".
- **Why hard.** Orbital participation depends on basis and method. VB weights are not observables. The terms mix geometry with bonding.
- **Standing 2026.** Physics settled: no substantial d participation (since about 1986–90). Vocabulary open: which criterion, if any, defines a hypervalent class.
- **Settle.** For the vocabulary, an IUPAC criterion.
- **Proponents.** d orbitals: Pauling-era VB (historic). Octet/3c–4e/ionic: Rundle, Pimentel, Musher, Reed, Weinhold, Magnusson, Gillespie, Hiberty, Braïda, Durrant.
- **Type.** Explaining (settled), plus convention.

### B7. Is charge-shift bonding a third class of electron-pair bond?

- **Claim.** Some electron-pair bonds owe their stability mainly to covalent–ionic resonance energy. Examples: F–F, bonds to F and O, the inverted bond in [1.1.1]propellane, hypervalent bonds. This "charge-shift resonance energy" makes them a third class beside covalent and ionic.
- **Negation.** These bonds are ordinary covalent bonds, fully described in MO/EDA terms without a separate class. "Charge-shift" is an artefact of the VB partition.
- **Observings.**
  - Bond energies and lengths of F2 and related bonds {observing}.
  - The classification itself is VB computation {simulation}{model construct}. Sources:
    - Shaik, Danovich, Wu & Hiberty 2009, Nature Chemistry, doi:10.1038/nchem.327 [fetch blocked; content unverified].
    - Review by Shaik, Danovich & Hiberty 2021, Molecules 26:1624, doi:10.3390/molecules26061624, listed on [WP: Valence bond theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_bond_theory) but not opened.
  - Proposed signatures [unverified]: low density with positive Laplacian at the bond midpoint; ELF features.
- **Negation side.** I found no explicit critique text in this session [gap]. My inference: the negation is the default MO/EDA stance.
- **Why hard.** VB resonance energy is not observable, and VB structure sets are not unique. The proposed signatures are themselves model constructs.
- **Standing 2026.** Open. It is used within the VB community (cf. XeF2, B6). Acceptance beyond it [unverified].
- **Settle.** A non-VB observable or reactivity pattern predicted only by the class.
- **Proponents.** For: Shaik, Hiberty, Danovich, Wu, Braïda. Against or indifferent: MO/EDA tradition [unverified].
- **Type.** Explaining; classification (membership criterion defined by a model).

### B8. He2, and helium bonded to metals under pressure (two binaries)

#### B8a. Is ground-state He2 bound?

- **Claim.** Ground-state He2 has a bound state.
- **Negation.** It has none.
- **Observings.**
  - Detected in cold beams (Schöllkopf & Toennies 1994, doi:10.1126/science.266.5189.1345) {observing}.
  - Grating diffraction gave a mean separation of about 52 Å and binding of about 1.3 mK (Grisenti et al. 2000, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.85.2284; [WP: Helium dimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium_dimer)) {observing → reconstruction}.
  - The halo wavefunction was imaged with a free-electron laser (Zeller et al. 2016, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1610688113) {reconstruction}.
- **Standing.** Settled since 1994–2000: bound.
- **Remaining question.** Is the binding chemical? WP: it is van der Waals, and MO theory predicts no bond. By contrast, He2⁺ and excimers have covalent character.
- **Type.** Observing (settled); the "chemical?" question is a convention.

#### B8b. Does helium bond in Na2He?

- **Claim.** In Na2He above about 113 GPa, He takes part in chemical bonding through orbital interaction and charge exchange.
- **Negation.** He is an inert space-filler. The Na electride lattice is stabilised without He bonding.
- **Observings.**
  - Dong, Oganov et al. 2017, Nature Chemistry (doi:10.1038/nchem.2716; fetch blocked) predicted the compound by structure search {simulation}.
  - They then made it in a laser-heated diamond anvil cell and identified it by X-ray diffraction [synthesis details unverified].
  - [WP: Helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium) describes it as an insulating fluorite-type electride, stable above 113 GPa {observing: XRD; reconstruction: structure}.
- **Negation side.** The authors' own reading, from memory [unverified]: He carries almost no charge and lowers the Madelung repulsion between electride electron pairs.
- **Claim side.** Small He charges reported in later work [unverified].
- **Why hard.** Charges depend on the partition. At about 100 GPa overlap is everywhere, and ambient bond criteria strain.
- **Standing 2026.** Observing: one synthesis, with no retraction seen. Explaining: leans toward "no conventional He bond" [unverified].
- **Settle.** In-situ He-edge inelastic X-ray scattering under pressure, compared with partition predictions.
- **Proponents.** Oganov, Dong, Prakapenka [unverified]. Critics [unverified].
- **Type.** Explaining.

## B. Molecular structure and quantum theory

### B9. Does molecular shape follow from the full Coulomb Hamiltonian, or does it need the Born–Oppenheimer separation?

- **Claim.** The eigenstates of an isolated molecule's full non-relativistic Coulomb Hamiltonian yield molecular structure without added assumptions. Structure here means a definite nuclear framework: bond lengths, angles and handedness.
- **Negation.** Those eigenstates are isotropic and contain no fixed framework. Shape appears only after clamping the nuclei (the Born–Oppenheimer step) or after adding an environment that breaks the symmetry.
- **Scope.** Three accounts are in play:
  - (i) BO is a controlled approximation, and shape follows from it. This is mainstream quantum chemistry.
  - (ii) Shape is emergent and needs an environment, through decoherence or superselection (Woolley, Primas).
  - (iii) The question is the quantum measurement problem and is not specific to chemistry.
  - The clean binary is between "derivable from the Coulomb Hamiltonian alone" and "not derivable without an added ingredient".
- **Observings.**
  - Structures are measured by microwave spectroscopy (rotational constants), electron diffraction, X-ray and neutron diffraction, and NMR ([WP: Molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_geometry)) {observing}.
  - Each method yields a differently defined geometry (equilibrium, vibrationally averaged, or thermally averaged) {reconstruction}{convention}.
  - BO clamps the nuclei and defines a potential-energy surface. It fails near conical intersections ([WP: Born–Oppenheimer approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born%E2%80%93Oppenheimer_approximation); Born & Oppenheimer 1927, doi:10.1002/andp.19273892002) {model construct}.
- **Negation side.**
  - Woolley (1976–78 onward, "Must a molecule have a shape?") argued that eigenstates of the isolated molecule carry no shape.
  - Primas argued that classical properties need superselection or an environment.
  - Sutcliffe & Woolley (2005–12) argued that potential-energy surfaces are not derivable without clamping.
  - All three are [unverified; not opened].
- **Claim side / responses.**
  - BO is controlled asymptotically by the electron/nuclear mass ratio.
  - Structure appears in conditional distributions (exact factorisation, conditional amplitudes).
  - Experiments prepare wavepackets, not eigenstates.
  - All [unverified].
- **Why hard.** The problem is conceptual: what counts as "derived", the role of symmetry breaking, and measurement. The numbers are not in dispute.
- **Standing 2026.** Open as a foundations problem. Practising chemists treat BO as justified. I found no 2023–26 decisive work [gap].
- **Settle.** A rigorous derivation of shape-bearing observables (e.g. rotational spectra of a rigid framework) from the Coulomb Hamiltonian under accepted approximations. Alternatively, an agreed account of shape as an environment-indexed emergent property.
- **Proponents.** Not derivable: Woolley, Primas, Sutcliffe, Hendry. Derivable with BO: the mainstream; responses by Matta, Cassam-Chenaï, Fortin and Lombardi [unverified].
- **Type.** Explaining (foundations).

### B10. Hund's paradox: chiral molecules vs parity eigenstates (two binaries)

- **Background.**
  - Hund 1927 (doi:10.1007/BF01397249) posed the puzzle. If the Hamiltonian is parity-invariant, its stationary states superpose the left- and right-handed forms. Yet chiral molecules are found as one hand or the other.
  - Tunnelling between the forms should occur but is extremely slow for large molecules ([WP: Friedrich Hund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hund)).
- **B10a (explaining).**
  - Claim: for small chiral molecules with non-negligible tunnelling, environmental decoherence (collisions, quantum-Zeno-like) explains why handed states are stable.
  - Negation: the parity-violating weak interaction explains it instead, by lifting the degeneracy and suppressing tunnelling.
- **B10b (observing).**
  - Claim: a parity-violating energy difference between enantiomers has been measured.
  - Negation: none has been measured.
- **Observings.**
  - 10a: Trost & Hornberger 2009, Phys. Rev. Lett. 103:023202 (doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.023202; arXiv 0811.2140) calculated collisional stabilisation of chiral states {simulation}. Physics Today reported it as a month-long calculation resolving an 82-year-old paradox (doi:10.1063/1.3226702). Molecules and pressures are [unverified].
  - 10b: [WP: Homochirality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homochirality) says a link between chirality and parity-violating energy differences has not been experimentally demonstrated (Sallembien et al. 2022, doi:10.1039/D1CS01179K; Blackmond 2019, doi:10.1101/cshperspect.a032540).
  - Predicted differences are tiny, about 10⁻¹³–10⁻¹⁰ cm⁻¹ for heavy-atom molecules. They are sought by ultra-high-resolution vibrational spectroscopy (e.g. CHFClBr, Re complexes), Mössbauer and NMR [unverified].
  - Parity violation decides stability only where it exceeds the tunnelling splitting (Quack, e.g. ClSSCl) [unverified].
- **Why hard.**
  - The energy difference is below current resolution.
  - Decoherence accounts depend on the environment.
  - Both mechanisms can act at once, so the binary is properly molecule-specific: which scale dominates, the tunnelling splitting, the parity-violating energy, or the decoherence rate?
- **Standing 2026.**
  - 10a: narrowing. Decoherence is widely cited for small molecules in gas (2009).
  - 10b: open. No measurement in sources up to 2022. I could not check 2023–26 [gap].
- **Settle.**
  - 10b: a reproducible splitting between enantiomer lines at the predicted size.
  - 10a: coherence loss versus collision rate measured in a molecule with resolvable tunnelling.
- **Proponents.** Decoherence: Joos, Zeh, Trost, Hornberger. Parity violation: Quack, Berger, Schwerdtfeger, Darquié/Daussy [partly unverified].
- **Type.** 10a explaining; 10b observing.

### B11. Are orbitals observed (imaged) or only model constructs?

- **Claim.** Experiments have directly observed or imaged orbitals (atomic, molecular or Dyson).
- **Negation.** No experiment observes orbitals. Experiments measure densities, cross-sections or momentum distributions, from which an orbital is reconstructed only within a one-electron model.
- **Observings.**
  - Zuo, Kim, O'Keeffe & Spence 1999, Nature (doi:10.1038/43403), claimed to observe d-orbital holes in Cu2O from convergent-beam electron diffraction plus X-ray structure factors, i.e. a difference density {reconstruction} [unverified; fetch blocked].
  - Scerri's 2000 critique held that orbitals are not observable in many-electron systems [unverified].
  - [WP: Atomic orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_orbital) mentions STEM-EDX imaging of 1s/2p orbitals in SrTiO3 (Jeong et al. 2016, doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.93.165140), complicated by the beam–orbital interaction. It also says orbital shapes are not unique, since any linear combination is valid.
  - [WP: Molecular orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital) says photoelectron spectra measure ionisation energies, which relate to orbital energies only via Koopmans' approximation (doi:10.1351/goldbook.k03411). Accurate wavefunctions need not contain orbitals.
  - [Unverified]: photoemission orbital tomography (Puschnig et al. 2009); high-harmonic tomography of N2 (Itatani et al. 2004); STM orbital-density images (Repp et al. 2005).
- **Why hard.** Orbitals are defined within one-electron theories. Exact many-electron observables correspond to Dyson or natural orbitals. Canonical orbitals are not unique.
- **Standing 2026.** Largely settled in principle for the negation: experiments reconstruct densities, Dyson orbitals or momentum maps. "Orbital imaging" persists as usage {convention}.
- **Settle.** Terminology that names the quantity actually reconstructed.
- **Proponents.** Imaging claims: Spence, Zuo, Puschnig, Corkum, Repp [unverified]. Critique: Scerri; Ogilvie [unverified].
- **Type.** Explaining (what an observing shows); mainly terminology.

## C. Aromaticity

### B12. Is aromaticity one phenomenon or several?

- **Claim.** Aromaticity is one phenomenon: cyclic electron delocalisation. Energetic, magnetic, structural and reactivity criteria are manifestations of it and should agree for any ring.
- **Negation.** The criteria measure different properties that can diverge for the same ring. "Aromatic" then names overlapping classes, i.e. it is multidimensional.
- **Observings.**
  - Measured:
    - Heats of hydrogenation and aromatic stabilisation energies (ASE) {observing → reconstruction against a reference compound, itself a convention}.
    - ¹H NMR ring-proton shifts and magnetic susceptibility exaltation {observing}.
    - X-ray bond lengths {observing}.
  - Indices:
    - NICS: a computed, sign-reversed shielding at the ring centre {simulation}. Schleyer 1996 (doi:10.1021/ja960582d); Chen et al. 2005 (doi:10.1021/cr030088+).
    - HOMA: a normalised bond-length deviation from an optimal C–C distance of 1.388 Å {reconstruction + convention}. Kruszewski 1972 (doi:10.1016/S0040-4039(01)94175-9), via [WP: HOMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_oscillator_model_of_aromaticity).
- **Divergence cases seen.**
  - Herges' Möbius compound: HOMA 0.35 vs −0.02 on reanalysis (B13).
  - Cyclobutadiene: its destabilisation is reassigned mainly to angle strain, torsional strain and Pauli repulsion (Wu et al. 2012, doi:10.1039/c2cc33521b, via [WP: Antiaromaticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiaromaticity)). The same page notes that reactivity alone is not diagnostic.
  - [WP: Aromaticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromaticity) separates global from local measures. Phenanthrene's central ring is less aromatic than its outer rings (Krygowski & Szatyłowicz 2015, doi:10.1007/s40828-015-0012-2).
- **Why hard.** Every index needs a reference (a non-aromatic model, an optimal bond length, a probe point). Ring currents are computed unless inferred from proton shifts.
- **Standing 2026.**
  - The multidimensional view is the working consensus. Unification remains a goal (a Chemistry World "grand unification" feature is cited on WP; not opened).
  - Hoffmann's 2015 critique of overuse and the 2023 "Aromaticity: Quo Vadis" forum are [unverified; blocked].
- **Settle.** For unification: one quantity that predicts all the others across a test set. Otherwise, adopt criterion-indexed terms such as "magnetically aromatic".
- **Proponents.** Unifiers: the Schleyer school, Solà, Feixas. Pluralists and critics: Krygowski, Cyrański, Katritzky, Hoffmann. All positions [unverified].
- **Type.** Convention (classification), with an explaining component.

### B13. Was the 2003 Möbius hydrocarbon aromatic?

- **Claim.** The singly twisted 4n π annulene derivative of Ajami, Oeckler, Simon & Herges 2003 (Nature, doi:10.1038/nature02224) is Möbius-aromatic.
- **Negation.** The same compound is twisted but non-aromatic, because strain spoils π overlap.
- **Observings.**
  - Measured: the X-ray structure {observing}.
  - Two readings of the same data {reconstruction + simulation}:
    - Herges: HOMA 0.35, read as moderately aromatic.
    - Castro, Schleyer et al. 2005 (doi:10.1021/ja0458165): strong bond alternation, HOMA −0.02, computed NICS −3.4 ppm, overlap blocked by strain.
    - Both via [WP: Möbius aromaticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6bius_aromaticity).
  - Theory: Heilbronner 1964 (doi:10.1016/S0040-4039(01)89474-0) {model construct}.
- **Wider field.**
  - Möbius porphyrinoids have stronger support, including a Hückel–Möbius switch in a 28π system (Stępień, Latos-Grażyński et al. 2007, doi:10.1002/anie.200700555; review by Yoon, Osuka & Kim 2009, doi:10.1038/nchem.172). NMR specifics [unverified].
  - WP: Aromaticity says that as of 2012 no Möbius aromatic molecule had been proven synthesised.
- **Why hard.** Strain, conformer interconversion, and conflicting indices.
- **Standing 2026.**
  - 2003 hydrocarbon: doubtful or open.
  - Porphyrinoids: largely accepted, on combined index evidence.
  - 2023–26 not checked [gap].
- **Settle.** Energetic, measured diatropic NMR and structural criteria that agree, using a Hückel conformer of the same molecule as control.
- **Proponents.** For: Herges; Osuka, Kim, Latos-Grażyński (porphyrinoids). Sceptical: Schleyer, Castro.
- **Type.** Observing (did aromatic delocalisation occur?), read through index conventions.

### B14. Baird (excited-state) aromaticity

- **Claim.** In the lowest ππ* triplet (and some excited singlet) states, Hückel's rule reverses: 4n π rings are aromatic and 4n+2 rings antiaromatic (Baird 1972 [unverified]).
- **Negation.** No such reversal occurs. Computed indices for excited states do not correspond to stabilising cyclic delocalisation visible in measurable energies or structures.
- **Observings.**
  - The cyclopentadienyl cation has a triplet ground state, consistent with Baird, from rovibronic spectroscopy (Wörner & Merkt 2007, doi:10.1063/1.2748049, via WP: Antiaromaticity) {observing → reconstruction}.
  - Time-resolved spectroscopy of expanded porphyrins (Kim, Osuka) and photoreactivity studies (Ottosson) [unverified].
- **Why hard.** The states are short-lived. Excited-state ring currents are almost always computed, and the proxies are indirect.
- **Standing 2026.** Narrowing toward use as a design concept in photochemistry. Direct observation of excited-state ring currents is rare [unverified].
- **Settle.** Time-resolved magnetic-response or diffraction measurements on matched 4n/4n+2 pairs.
- **Proponents.** Baird, Ottosson, Kim, Osuka [unverified].
- **Type.** Observing, with an explaining component.

### B15. Global ring currents in porphyrin nanorings

- **Claim.** Oxidised butadiyne-linked porphyrin nanorings sustain global ring currents around the whole macrocycle, following 4n+2/4n counting up to rings of many dozens of π electrons and nanometre diameter (Anderson group, about 2017–2020).
- **Negation.** The observed shifts do not reflect a global current. They arise from local per-porphyrin currents or from template geometry.
- **Observings.**
  - ¹H NMR of the template protons inside the ring: their shift changes sign with oxidation state, following 4n+2/4n {observing}.
  - Sources [unverified; blocked]: Peeks, Claridge & Anderson 2017 (Nature); Rickhaus et al. 2020 (Nature Chemistry, doi:10.1038/s41557-019-0397-1).
  - DFT ring-current and NICS calculations support the global reading and estimate a size limit {simulation} [unverified].
- **Negation side.** Later calculations attributed the shifts to local currents (Sundholm, Valiev et al.?) [unverified].
- **Why hard.** The current is inferred from template-proton shifts. Separating global from local contributions requires computation, which is sensitive to the functional chosen (delocalisation error).
- **Standing 2026.** The shifts are accepted; their attribution is contested. 2023–26 [unverified].
- **Settle.** Independent magnetic probes (susceptibility exaltation) plus current-density calculations free of delocalisation error. Also, a ring-size series with local contributions held fixed.
- **Proponents.** Global: Anderson, Peeks, Rickhaus, Jirasek. Local: Sundholm, Valiev, Fliegl. All [unverified].
- **Type.** Explaining an observing.

### B16. All-metal aromaticity (Al4^2−)

- **Claim.** The square Al4^2− unit in MAl4⁻ clusters is aromatic (π and σ) by the same criteria as organic aromatics.
- **Negation.** The unit is stable and delocalised but not aromatic in that sense. Its signatures do not cohere, or the "aromaticity" is an analogy based on orbital counting.
- **Observings.**
  - Li, Kuznetsov, Zhang, Boldyrev & Wang 2001, Science (doi:10.1126/science.291.5505.859): MAl4⁻ (M = Li, Na, Cu) made by laser vaporisation, mass-selected, and measured by photoelectron spectroscopy {observing: binding energies}.
  - Computed structure: a planar Al4^2− with the counterion at the apex. The HOMO is a delocalised π orbital obeying 4n+2, and computed and measured energies match ([WP: Metal aromaticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_aromaticity)) {simulation + model construct}.
- **Negation side.** Disputes over the σ vs π accounting, over Al4^4− antiaromaticity, and over mixed current-density results are [unverified]. WP: Aromaticity flags metal aromaticity as uncited. Review: Feixas, Matito, Poater & Solà 2012 (doi:10.1002/wcms.1115; not opened).
- **Why hard.** Gas-phase clusters give only photoelectron spectra. Every criterion is computed. Metal σ, π and δ orbitals give separate electron counts.
- **Standing 2026.** The interpretation is contested; the photoelectron spectra are settled.
- **Settle.** Agreeing indices on isolable analogues characterised by NMR and X-ray.
- **Proponents.** For: Boldyrev, Wang. Critics: Schleyer, Fowler, Havenith [unverified].
- **Type.** Explaining.

## D. The elements

### B17. Group 3: Sc–Y–Lu–Lr or Sc–Y–La–Ac?

- **Claim.** Lu and Lr sit under Sc and Y in group 3. The f-block is then La–Yb and Ac–No, 14 wide.
- **Negation.** La and Ac sit under Sc and Y. The f-block is then Ce–Lu and Th–Lr.
- **Scope.**
  - There is a third format: blanks under Y with a 15-wide f-block. [WP: Group 3 element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_3_element) says it conflicts with the 14-electron f limit.
  - The question has an observing core: do La/Ac use valence f orbitals while Lu/Lr do not? It also has a convention layer: which layout to adopt.
- **Observings.**
  - La's low-temperature superconductivity is linked to 4f activity (Kondō 1963, doi:10.1143/PTP.29.1; Hamilton 1965, doi:10.1119/1.1972042) {observing + model}.
  - Sc–Y–Lu trends parallel Ti–Zr–Hf, and Lu is the least basic lanthanide {observing}. Cotton et al. 2022 (doi:10.1016/j.ccr.2021.214366) say the structural differences track ionic size and do not decide the question.
  - Lr's first ionisation energy is 4.96 (+0.08/−0.07) eV, measured by surface ionisation of ²⁵⁶Lr (Sato et al. 2015, Nature, doi:10.1038/nature14342; predicted 4.963(15) eV). Its second ionisation energy is >13.3 eV (2021) ([WP: Lawrencium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrencium)) {observing}.
  - Lr's ground state is computed as 7s²7p¹ but is unmeasured {simulation}. Lr behaves as a trivalent homologue of Lu.
  - Calculations find no valence f orbitals in Lu or Lr (Xu & Pyykkö 2016, doi:10.1039/c6cp02706g) {simulation}.
- **Convention record.**
  - Jensen argued for Lu–Lr in 1982 (doi:10.1021/ed059p634) and 2015 (doi:10.1007/s10698-015-9216-1).
  - IUPAC's 1988 report favoured Lu–Lr (doi:10.1351/pac198860030431).
  - The 2021 provisional IUPAC project report called the choice a convention and suggested Lu–Lr (Scerri, doi:10.1515/ci-2021-0115). [WP: Periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table) says both reports endorse Lu–Lr.
  - Lavelle 2008 defended La–Ac (doi:10.1021/ed085p1482).
  - A 2026 Foundations of Chemistry paper continues the debate (doi:10.1007/s10698-026-09567-3; content unseen).
- **Why hard.** Gas-phase electron configurations, condensed-phase chemistry and block symmetry point different ways for different properties. Placement is a classification that needs a stated criterion.
- **Standing 2026.** Specialists are converging on Lu–Lr by convention; textbooks remain mixed. I saw no final IUPAC recommendation after 2021 [gap].
- **Settle.** An IUPAC recommendation that names its criterion. Measured Lr chemistry, such as its configuration and compound stoichiometries, would address the observing core.
- **Proponents.** Lu–Lr: Jensen, Scerri, Xu & Pyykkö, Smits et al. 2023 (doi:10.1038/s42254-023-00668-y). La–Ac: Lavelle; many textbooks.
- **Type.** Convention, with an observing core.

### B18. Do Nh and Mc bind surfaces more weakly than Tl and Bi?

- **Claim.** Elemental Nh and Mc bind surfaces more weakly than Tl and Bi, as relativistic 7p1/2 stabilisation predicts.
- **Negation.** Nh and Mc bind at least as strongly as Tl and Bi, as plain extrapolation of the group trend would give.
- **Observings.**
  - Dubna 2010–12: about 10–20 Nh atoms were made and none detected after PTFE transport.
  - Dubna 2017: strong retention on PTFE (−ΔH > 45 kJ/mol), probably as the hydroxide (Aksenov et al. 2017, doi:10.1140/epja/i2017-12348-8; [WP: Nihonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihonium)) {observing → reconstruction}.
  - GSI 2024: ²⁸⁸Mc and its daughter ²⁸⁴Nh (from ²⁴³Am + ⁴⁸Ca) were studied by gas-phase adsorption chromatography on SiO2 and gold.
  - Results ([WP: Moscovium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscovium); WP: Nihonium):
    - SiO2 adsorption enthalpy: Nh 58 (+8/−3) kJ/mol; Mc 54 (+11/−5) kJ/mol, at 68% confidence.
    - Both bind less than Tl and Bi and more than Cn and Fl.
    - This is single-atom deposition data, converted to enthalpies with a transport model {observing → reconstruction}.
  - The attribution to 7p1/2 stabilisation is {model construct}.
- **Why hard.** Atoms are studied one at a time. Enthalpies are model fits. The chemical form (element vs hydroxide) is uncertain.
- **Standing 2026.** Narrowing, on one 2024 study whose primary paper was not opened. Reproduction [gap].
- **Settle.** More atoms, more surfaces, controlled chemical form.
- **Proponents.** GSI/TASCA (Yakushev, Düllmann) and JINR (Aksenov) [partly unverified].
- **Type.** Observing.

### B19. Flerovium on gold: volatile metal or noble-gas-like?

- **Scope.** Only the single-atom question has data. Bulk Fl is untested.
- **Claim.** An Fl atom chemisorbs on gold through metal–metal bonding, i.e. it is a volatile metal.
- **Negation.** An Fl atom only physisorbs, i.e. it behaves noble-gas-like.
- **Observings.**
  - [Unverified]: PSI/FLNR 2008–10, about three atoms, suggested weak, noble-like adsorption. GSI 2014 (Inorg. Chem.) and 2022 (Front. Chem.) reported Fl as the most volatile group-14 metal, forming weaker metal bonds on gold than Pb.
  - Seen: GSI 2024 found Fl less reactive with SiO2 than Nh and Mc {observing}.
  - [WP: Carbon group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_group): possibly noble-gas-like but most likely a post-transition metal. Predicted boiling point −60 °C {simulation}. No Fl compounds are known.
  - A 2021 study weakened the case for a strong Z = 114 shell closure ([WP: Island of stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_of_stability); [Science news](https://www.science.org/content/article/hopes-evaporate-superheavy-element-flerovium-having-long-life)).
- **Why hard.** Few atoms, half-lives of seconds, and adsorption enthalpies obtained from models. The predicted energy gap between "weak metal" and "closed shell" is small.
- **Standing 2026.** Narrowing toward a volatile, weakly metallic element [unverified]. Bulk behaviour unmeasured.
- **Settle.** Higher-statistics runs over several surfaces and temperatures.
- **Proponents.** Metal: Yakushev, Düllmann. Noble-like (early data): Eichler [unverified].
- **Type.** Observing.

### B20. Copernicium and oganesson: bulk character

- **Cn binary.**
  - Claim: condensed Cn is a noble-liquid-like, weakly bound, possibly insulating element.
  - Negation: condensed Cn is a Hg-like metal.
  - Observings [unverified; page blocked]:
    - Eichler et al. 2007, Nature: two atoms adsorbed on gold, −ΔH about 52 kJ/mol, read as volatile and Hg-like {observing → reconstruction}.

### B21. Does the periodic law break down for the heaviest elements?

- **Claim.** For Z ≳ 112–120, relativistic effects make group membership a poor predictor of chemistry.
- **Negation.** For the same elements, group membership still predicts the kind of chemistry. Relativity shifts only magnitudes.
- **Observings.**
  - Elements 103–108 behave as homologues of Lu–Os ([WP: Superheavy element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superheavy_element); Schädel 2015, doi:10.1098/rsta.2014.0191) {observing}.
  - Nh and Mc are group-like in kind but weakened (B18).
  - Configurations of elements 109–118 are unmeasured (WP: Periodic table).
  - Predictions:
    - Strong late-period-7 relativistic effects may collapse periodicity, and the unknown region may not follow known patterns (WP: Periodic table) {simulation}.
    - Element 120 would reverse the group-2 trend after Ba and resemble Sr/Ca ([WP: Unbinilium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbinilium)) {simulation}.
  - Calibration cases (relativistic effects in familiar elements; [WP: Relativistic quantum chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativistic_quantum_chemistry)) {simulation explaining observings}:
    - About 10 of the 12 V of a lead–acid battery is attributed to relativity (Ahuja et al. 2011, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.106.018301).
    - Gold's colour is attributed to its 5d→6s absorption.
    - Mercury's liquidity is attributed to 6s contraction (Calvo et al. 2013, doi:10.1002/anie.201302742).
- **Production.**
  - Berkeley made two atoms of ²⁹⁰Lv via ²⁴⁴Pu + ⁵⁰Ti in 2024 ([WP: Livermorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livermorium)).
  - A ²⁴⁹Cf + ⁵⁰Ti search for element 120 had begun by September 2025, with no result reported. Any 2026 outcome [gap].
- **Why hard.** "Breakdown" needs a criterion: which properties, and what tolerance. Data exist for single atoms only.
- **Standing 2026.** Open. Measured chemistry up to Z = 115 shows modified group behaviour. The breakdown claim rests on simulation.
- **Settle.** Chemistry of Og, element 119 and element 120 compared with predictions under a criterion stated in advance.
- **Proponents.** Strong departures: Pyykkö (doi:10.1146/annurev-physchem-032511-143755), Schwerdtfeger, Pershina. Continuity: emphasised by experimentalists [unverified characterisation].
- **Type.** Explaining, with observing tests pending.

### B22. Has metallic solid hydrogen been observed under static compression?

- **Scope.** Three questions:
  - (a) the Dias–Silvera 2017 claim;
  - (b) any static observation;
  - (c) liquid metallic hydrogen under dynamic compression, which is broadly accepted [unverified].
- **Claim (a).** Dias & Silvera (Science, 2017) observed atomic metallic hydrogen at about 495 GPa at low temperature, by optical reflectance in a diamond anvil cell [unverified].
- **Negation (a).** That experiment did not demonstrate metallic hydrogen.
- **Observings.** All [unverified] except the WP summary.
  - The claim rested on reflectance from one sample with extrapolated pressure. Critics (Eremets, Loubeyre, Goncharov, Struzhkin; arXiv comments in 2017) questioned the pressure scale and the alumina coating, and noted there was no replication. The sample was reported lost.
  - Eremets et al. 2019 reported semimetallic hydrogen near 350–360 GPa from electrical measurements.
  - Loubeyre, Occelli & Dumas 2020, Nature: synchrotron infrared showed a reversible band-gap closure near 425 GPa and 80 K. The authors called it probable metallic hydrogen.
  - [WP: Hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) says only that the metallic phase lies above 400 GPa and probably exists inside giant planets.
  - Context: Dias's later superconductivity papers were retracted (2022–23), and Rochester found misconduct in 2024. This bears on credibility only. The 2017 paper's status [gap].
- **Why hard.** Above 400 GPa is the limit for diamond anvils. Pressure scales are extrapolated, samples are micron-sized, and optical and electrical probes are hard to combine.
- **Standing 2026.**
  - (a) Not accepted and unreplicated [unverified].
  - (b) Open; the best evidence is "probable" [unverified].
  - (c) Accepted [unverified].
  - No 2023–26 confirmation found [gap].
- **Settle.** Multi-lab conductivity with metallic temperature dependence plus optical evidence, at calibrated pressure.
- **Proponents.** (a): Dias, Silvera. Probable metal: Loubeyre. Semimetal: Eremets. Critics of (a): Eremets, Loubeyre, Goncharov [unverified].
- **Type.** Observing.

### B23. High-pressure "forbidden" stoichiometries: do valence rules fail?

- **Observing binary.**
  - Claim: Na–Cl compounds other than NaCl (e.g. NaCl3, Na3Cl) are thermodynamically stable at high pressure.
  - Negation: only NaCl is stable there.
- **Observings.**
  - Zhang et al. 2013, Science 342:1502 (doi:10.1126/science.1244989; arXiv 1310.7674) predicted Na3Cl, Na2Cl, Na3Cl2, NaCl3 and NaCl7 by structure search {simulation}.
  - Cubic and orthorhombic NaCl3 and 2D-metallic tetragonal Na3Cl were then confirmed at high pressure ([WP: Sodium chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_chloride)) {observing: laser-heated diamond anvil cell + XRD [method unverified]}.
  - 2D hexagonal NaCl was reported later (Tikhomirova et al. 2020, doi:10.1021/acs.jpclett.0c00874).
  - WP calls these compounds that violate chemical intuition.
- **Standing.** Settled as an observing (2013). Independent reproduction [unverified].
- **Explaining binary.**
  - Claim: these compounds show that ambient valence rules fail under pressure.
  - Negation: the rules hold once the set of motifs is widened (polyhalide anions, metallic Na layers, electride electrons).
  - Open and partly terminological. It depends on whether a rule means its ambient formulation or its underlying energetics [my inference].
- **Settle.** State the scope of the rule (pressure range, bond types), then test whether the rule predicts the observed stoichiometries.
- **Proponents.** Oganov, Zhang, Goncharov, Prakapenka. Critics of the "failure" framing: Hoffmann, Grochala [unverified].
- **Type.** Observing (settled); explaining (open).

## G. Inferences and gaps

- **Inference.** Observing-type binaries (B8a, B10b, B13, B18–B20, B22, B23 observing) close with better instruments or more atoms.
