## Key gathered points

Post-mid-2026 developments are not covered. Labels: [observing], [reconstruction], [convention], [model], [simulation].
- **Ranking, most consequential and cleanest first:** 1.1 hydride superconductivity occurs; 4.2 A-Lab new compounds; 1.7 ambient-pressure nickelate films; 3.4 BET reproducibility; 3.1 AFM "bond" lines; 4.1 hull vs synthesizability; 1.2 hydride pairing; 2.3 hot carriers vs heat; 2.1 entropy decisive?; 1.6 bulk vs filamentary La3Ni2O7; 1.5 hydrogen in nickelates; 4.3 GNoME novelty; 3.3 Cu in [Cu(CF3)4]−; 3.2 partial charges; 2.4 negative gas adsorption; 3.5 XPS errors; 2.5 ZrTe5; 2.7 TMD photoluminescence; 2.6 VSe2 magnetism; 2.8 metallic-glass order; 2.2 magic-size clusters; 3.6 synthesis reproducibility. Settled cases kept for calibration: 1.3 Dias (two retractions) and 1.4 LK-99.
- **Observing settled ahead of explaining.** Most of the field accepts hydride superconductivity as observed, from resistance, isotope shifts, flux trapping, NV-centre magnetometry and a 2025 tunnelling gap. A small published dissent (Hirsch, Marsiglio) contests both the observing and the phonon explanation.
- **Retractions turned on data, not theory.** CSH (retracted 2022), Lu–N–H (retracted 2023) and LK-99 (refuted 2023) fell to scrutiny of raw data and to independently made samples. Flat-band calculations amplified LK-99; they did not test it.
- **Membership criteria carry many of the disputes:**
  - "superconductor": zero resistance plus bulk flux expulsion;
  - "high-entropy": element count, entropy value, or shown entropy stabilization;
  - "new material": novelty, credibility, utility;
  - "topological": DFT symmetry indicator, or a measured surface state;
  - "oxidation state": IUPAC formal count, or spectroscopic d-count.

  Once the criterion is fixed, the binary often splits or dissolves.
- **Measured numbers carry models.** BET areas, XPS chemical states, atomic charges, bond orders and AFM lines each build a model into a reported measurement. The 2022 BET round robin and a 2020 audit of XPS in the literature put numbers on the spread.
- **Computation vs synthesis.** Being on the 0 K DFT convex hull is not necessary for a compound to exist (about half of known compounds lie above it) and has not been shown to be sufficient. In 2024 critics challenged GNoME's novelty and the A-Lab's phase identification.

## 1. Superconductivity claims in chemistry: hydrides, retracted room-temperature claims, LK-99, nickelates

### Takeaway
As known to mid-2026, superconductivity in megabar hydrides is the field's accepted observing and its phonon mechanism the accepted explaining, with a persistent minority dissent; both Dias claims are retracted and LK-99 is refuted; nickelates are an established family whose open binaries concern hydrogen content, bulk versus filamentary volume, and strain-stabilized ambient-pressure films. Cuprate mechanism and strange metal stay with the project's registry entries 157 and 173 (Keimer et al. review, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14165 [project-cited]).

### Cited findings: frontier binaries

#### 1.1 Do compressed H3S and LaH10 superconduct?
- **Claim.** H3S near 150 GPa and LaH10 near 170–190 GPa, synthesized in diamond-anvil cells, become superconducting (zero resistance with flux expulsion or trapping) below about 200 K and 250 K respectively.
- **Negation.** In the same samples, pressures and temperature ranges, the resistance drops and magnetic signals are not superconductivity.
- **Observings, claim side.**
  - [observing] Four-probe resistance falling to near zero at 203 K near 150 GPa in compressed H2S, a lower transition in the deuterated analogue, and magnetization in a miniature cell (Drozdov et al., Nature 2015, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14964 [unverified]). *Field adds:* the phase is H3S, assigned from diffraction plus DFT [model]; the isotope shift is read as phonon involvement.
  - [observing] Transitions near 250 K at ~170 GPa (Drozdov et al., Nature 2019, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1201-8 [unverified]) and above 260 K near 180–200 GPa (Somayazulu et al., PRL 2019, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.122.027001 [unverified]) from separate groups, both suppressed by applied field.
  - [observing] Further clathrate hydrides from other groups, e.g. CaH6 near 215 K at 172 GPa (Ma et al., PRL 2022, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.167001 [unverified]).
  - [observing] Trapped flux by SQUID in H3S and LaH10 (Minkov et al., Nature Physics 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-023-02089-1 [unverified]); local flux expulsion imaged by nitrogen-vacancy centres implanted in the anvil, in CeH9 (Bhattacharyya et al., Nature 2024, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07026-7 [unverified]); a gap of roughly 60 meV in H3S, smaller in D3S, by tunnelling spectroscopy (Du et al., Nature 2025 [unverified; DOI not seen]).
- **Observings, negation side.**
  - [reconstruction] Hirsch and Marsiglio re-analyse published curves: resistive transitions they judge too sharp and too little broadened by field; moments that depend on subtracted backgrounds; trapped-flux field dependence they find non-standard (PRB 2021, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.103.134505 [unverified]; Physica C 2021, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physc.2021.1353866 [unverified]). *Their account adds:* hydrides are nonstandard superconductors or not superconductors at all.
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Samples are micrometre-sized, multiphase after laser heating and enclosed in metal gaskets; their moments are tiny beside cell backgrounds; they do not survive decompression; few groups can make them; raw-data release lagged.
- **Standing in 2026.** Narrowing toward the claim (mainstream acceptance); the dissent persists in print.
- **What would settle it.** On one sample, public raw data showing zero resistance, a bulk shielding fraction, a thermodynamic anomaly and a gap all vanishing at one Tc, repeated by several groups.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: M. Eremets group (Mainz), R. Hemley and M. Somayazulu, C. Jin (Beijing), Y. Ma (Jilin). Negation: J. E. Hirsch, F. Marsiglio.
- **Kind.** Observing.

#### 1.2 Granting 1.1, is hydride pairing electron–phonon?
- **Claim.** In H3S and LaH10, pairing is mediated by hydrogen-dominated phonons as described by Migdal–Eliashberg theory.
- **Negation.** In the same phases, pairing is not predominantly phonon-mediated.
- **Observings, claim side.** [simulation] DFT–Eliashberg calculations predicted high Tc for compressed H2S-derived phases before the measurement (Duan et al., Sci. Rep. 2014, https://doi.org/10.1038/srep06968 [unverified]); [observing] isotope shifts (sources at 1.1); [observing] gap size and its isotope dependence (Du et al. 2025 [unverified]). *Field adds:* anharmonic and quantum-nuclear corrections to fit structures and Tc [simulation]; a fitted Coulomb pseudopotential [model].
- **Observings, negation side.** [model] Hirsch's hole-superconductivity theory, under which conventional hydride superconductivity should not occur [unverified]; no independent contrary measurement found.
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Eliashberg inputs are partly adjustable; hydrogen is nearly invisible to X-rays, so structures lean on DFT; phonon spectra at megabar pressure are scarcely measured.
- **Standing in 2026.** Settled in the mainstream, conditional on 1.1.
- **What would settle it.** Phonon dispersions measured at pressure (inelastic X-ray scattering) matching the spectral function inverted from tunnelling data.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: N. Ashcroft (early proposals), L. Boeri, I. Errea, C. Pickard, E. Zurek, Y. Ma. Negation: J. E. Hirsch.
- **Kind.** Explaining.

#### 1.3 The Dias group's room-temperature claims (retracted; historically notable)
- **Claim.** (a) A carbonaceous sulfur hydride superconducts up to 287.7 K at 267 GPa (Snider et al., Nature 2020, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2801-z [unverified]). (b) Nitrogen-doped lutetium hydride superconducts near 294 K at about 1 GPa (Dasenbrock-Gammon et al., Nature 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05742-0 [unverified]).
- **Negation.** For each, the same material under the same conditions does not superconduct, the published data do not show it.
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing, as reported] resistance drops and ac susceptibility (a); resistance, susceptibility, heat capacity and a pressure-driven colour change from blue to pink to red (b).
- **Observings, negation side.** [reconstruction] analyses of the published susceptibility of (a) pointing to non-standard background subtraction (D. van der Marel and J. E. Hirsch, 2022–23 [unverified]); [observing] independently made LuH2±xNy reproduced the colour changes but showed no superconductivity (Ming et al., Nature 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06162-w [unverified]); retractions of (a) in September 2022 and of (b) in November 2023, the latter at co-authors' request [unverified]; Nature news reported in 2024 that a University of Rochester investigation found research misconduct [unverified].
- **Why the field finds it hard.** As 1.1, plus processed or withheld raw data; the near-ambient claim (b) drew fast replications.
- **Standing in 2026.** Retracted; settled negative as published claims.
- **What would settle it (to reopen).** Independent samples showing zero resistance and flux expulsion; none known.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: R. Dias, A. Salamat. Negation: J. E. Hirsch, D. van der Marel, the H.-H. Wen group and other replication groups.
- **Kind.** Observing. Rejected claims, kept for historical notability (retraction and failed replication).

#### 1.4 LK-99 (refuted; historically notable)
- **Claim.** Copper-substituted lead apatite Pb10−xCux(PO4)6O ("LK-99") superconducts at ambient pressure up to about 400 K (arXiv:2307.12008 and arXiv:2307.12037, July 2023 [unverified]).
- **Negation.** Phase-pure Pb10−xCux(PO4)6O does not superconduct at ambient pressure at any measured temperature.
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] a resistivity drop near 104 °C in polycrystalline samples, and partial levitation on a magnet [unverified]; [simulation] DFT flat bands near the Fermi level (Griffin, arXiv:2307.16892 [unverified]). This shows only that the model has a flat band, not that pairing occurs.
- **Observings, negation side.** [observing] the drop coincides with a structural transition of a Cu2S impurity near 385 K (Zhu et al., arXiv:2308.04353 [unverified]; Jain, J. Phys. Chem. C 2023, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcc.3c05684 [unverified]); [observing] single crystals are transparent insulators with weak magnetism and no superconductivity (Puphal et al., APL Materials 2023, https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0172755 [unverified]); partial levitation attributed to the magnetic response of impurities [unverified]; a Korean Society of Superconductivity and Cryogenics verification committee reported no evidence in December 2023 [unverified]; news summary (Nature 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/d41586-023-02585-7 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** It was hard only briefly. The samples were multiphase, an impurity has a resistive transition in the claimed range, and public attention amplified the claim.
- **Standing in 2026.** Settled negative within weeks; formalized December 2023.
- **What would settle it.** Already settled; later sulfur-containing variants announced by original authors (2024) lack independent confirmation [unverified].
- **Main proponents.** Claim: Lee Sukbae, Kim Ji-Hoon, Kwon Young-Wan, Kim Hyun-Tak. Negation: IOP-CAS (Beijing), P. Jain, P. Puphal and B. Keimer (Stuttgart), the KSSC committee.
- **Kind.** Observing. Rejected claim, kept because historically notable.

#### 1.5 Infinite-layer nickelates: does superconductivity need hydrogen?
- **Claim.** Superconductivity in infinite-layer (Nd,Sr)NiO2-type films requires hydrogen taken up during topotactic reduction (the superconducting phase is NiO2Hx-like).
- **Negation.** Hydrogen-free infinite-layer films of the same composition and strain superconduct.
- **Observings, shared background.** [observing] Superconductivity at roughly 9–15 K in Nd0.8Sr0.2NiO2 films on SrTiO3 made by CaH2 reduction of perovskite films (Li et al., Nature 2019, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1496-5 [unverified]); bulk powders not superconducting (prior state: Li Q. et al., Commun. Mater. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1038/s43246-020-0018-1 [unverified]).
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] hydrogen content correlating with superconductivity in a narrow window, lost when hydrogen is removed (Ding et al., Nature 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05657-2 [unverified]); [simulation] DFT favours topotactic hydrogen uptake (Si et al., PRL 2020, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.166402 [unverified]).
- **Observings, negation side.** [observing] superconducting films made by in-situ aluminium reduction with no hydride reagent (Wei et al., Sci. Adv. 2023, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adh3327 [unverified]); reports of no detectable hydrogen in superconducting films [unverified; source not identified].
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Hydrogen is hard to quantify in thin films; reduction is kinetic and leaves variable oxygen and hydrogen; films degrade in air.
- **Standing in 2026.** Open, leaning toward "not required" [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** Calibrated hydrogen depth profiles (nuclear reaction analysis) on the very films measured, across reduction routes and laboratories.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: Ding et al. (L. Qiao group); L. Si and K. Held (theory). Negation: C. Ahn group (Yale) [unverified].
- **Kind.** Observing.

#### 1.6 Pressurized La3Ni2O7: bulk or filamentary superconductivity?
- **Claim.** Near 14–40 GPa, La3Ni2O7-type samples superconduct near 80 K in bulk (shielding of the order of the sample volume).
- **Negation.** In those samples the superconductivity is filamentary (a small volume fraction from a minority phase or interfaces).
- **Observings, shared background.** [observing] a resistance drop near 80 K at 14–43.5 GPa with a weak diamagnetic signal (Sun et al., Nature 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06408-7 [unverified]); zero resistance in a hydrostatic medium (Zhang Y. et al., Nature Physics 2024, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-024-02515-y [unverified]).
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] ac susceptibility indicating near-complete shielding in Pr-substituted La2PrNi2O7 (Wang N. et al., Nature 2024, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07996-8 [unverified]).
- **Observings, negation side.** [observing] small shielding fractions in early crystals (L. Sun group, 2023–24 [unverified]); [observing] stacking polymorphs ("1313" beside bilayer "2222") and intergrowths (Chen X. et al., JACS 2024, https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.3c14052 [unverified]). *Field adds:* interlayer Ni d(z²) bonding in the bilayer as the pairing-relevant orbital [model].
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Polymorphism, oxygen non-stoichiometry, non-hydrostatic pressure, and susceptibility inside anvil cells.
- **Standing in 2026.** Narrowing: bulk in phase-pure (notably Pr-substituted) samples; early crystals filamentary (prior state).
- **What would settle it.** Shielding fraction plus specific heat on phase-pure single crystals, with diffraction on the same sample at pressure.
- **Main proponents.** Bulk: M. Wang (Sun Yat-sen University), J.-G. Cheng (IOP Beijing). Filamentary caution: L. Sun group (IOP) [unverified].
- **Kind.** Observing.

#### 1.7 Do strained La3Ni2O7 films superconduct at ambient pressure?
- **Claim.** Compressively strained (La,Pr)3Ni2O7 films on SrLaAlO4 superconduct at ambient pressure with onset near or above 40 K.
- **Negation.** Those films at ambient pressure are not superconducting; the resistance drops come from other transitions or from minor inhomogeneous paths.
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] resistive onset in roughly the 26–42 K range, zero resistance at low temperature and a diamagnetic response in La3Ni2O7 films (Ko et al., Nature 2025, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08525-3 [unverified]); onset above 40 K in (La,Pr)3Ni2O7 films (Zhou G. et al., Nature 2025, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08755-z [unverified]). *Field adds:* compressive strain stands in for pressure [model]; oxygen stoichiometry set by ozone annealing.
- **Observings, negation side.** [observing] broad transitions with zero resistance well below onset, and sample-to-sample variation [unverified]; no published refutation found.
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Oxygen control, film inhomogeneity, substrate interdiffusion; shielding fractions in thin films are hard to quantify.
- **Standing in 2026.** Narrowing toward the claim (two independent groups); higher onsets reported later [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** Sharp transitions with full mutual-inductance screening in several laboratories, plus spectroscopic gaps closing at Tc.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: H. Hwang (Stanford); Z. Chen and Q.-K. Xue (SUSTech). Negation: no named opponents; caution about filamentary paths.
- **Kind.** Observing.

### Inferences
- The decisive moves in 1.3 and 1.4 were independent samples (single crystals, fresh synthesis) and raw data. For superconductivity claims, the class-membership test in practice is zero resistance plus bulk flux expulsion on the same sample.
- Binaries 1.6 and 1.7 are partly disputes over criteria: which of zero resistance, shielding fraction and a thermodynamic anomaly has been shown on which sample.
- Pressure, strain and composition work as interchangeable control variables in 1.6–1.7. Superconductivity appears on compression and goes on release, an observation of reversibility in chemistry's own terms.
- Computation predicted (H3S) and amplified (LK-99 flat bands). The prediction's success in 1.2 did not replace the observings of 1.1.

### Gaps
- No source opened. Statuses for 2025–26 are not checked: H3S critiques and replies, hydrogen in nickelates, and record film Tc values.
- A reported 2025 ternary La–Sc–H superhydride with Tc near room temperature above 200 GPa [unverified] was not checked; it is listed under further candidates.
- Retraction-notice DOIs and the Rochester investigation documents were not seen.

## 2. Solid-state and nanoscale binaries

### Takeaway
Configurational entropy is not decisive as a general rule for high-entropy alloys, while entropy stabilization remains the accepted reading of the original rock-salt oxide. Negative gas adsorption is metastable by thermodynamic argument. Hot carriers versus heating remains case-by-case. The topological, 2D and glass binaries turn on dependence on the sample and on the scope of the claim. For plasmonics, cross-reference the catalysis researcher's notes.

### Cited findings: frontier binaries

#### 2.1 Is configurational entropy decisive for single-phase "high-entropy" materials? (split)
- **Claim.** (a) In equiatomic alloys of five or more principal elements, high configurational entropy (ideal R ln 5 ≈ 1.61 R per mole) decides single-phase solid-solution formation. (b) In (Mg,Co,Ni,Cu,Zn)O, the single-phase rock salt formed on heating is entropy-stabilized (configurational entropy overcomes a positive mixing enthalpy).
- **Negation.** (a) In the same alloys, enthalpy, atomic-size mismatch and kinetics decide, not configurational entropy. (b) That oxide's single-phase state is not entropy-stabilized (enthalpy-favoured or kinetically trapped).
- **Observings, claim side.** (a) [observing] single-phase fcc CrMnFeCoNi from melts (Cantor et al. 2004, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msea.2003.10.257 [unverified]); [model] the entropy hypothesis (Yeh et al. 2004, https://doi.org/10.1002/adem.200300567 [unverified]). (b) [observing] a reversible, endothermic transformation between multiphase and single-phase states on heating and cooling; removing any one cation prevents the single phase under the same conditions (Rost et al., Nat. Commun. 2015, https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms9485 [unverified]). *Field adds:* ideal-mixing entropy as the stabilizing term [model].
- **Observings, negation side.** (a) [observing] replacing one element of CrMnFeCoNi by a chemically similar one gives multiphase alloys at unchanged ideal entropy (Otto et al., Acta Mater. 2013, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actamat.2013.01.042 [unverified]); [observing] CrMnFeCoNi decomposes after long anneals at intermediate temperatures, so its room-temperature single phase is kinetically retained (Otto et al., Acta Mater. 2016, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actamat.2016.04.005 [unverified]); review (Miracle and Senkov, Acta Mater. 2017, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actamat.2016.08.081 [unverified]). (b) [convention] the argument that most materials labelled high-entropy oxides have not been shown to be entropy-stabilized, so the labels need definitions (Brahlek et al., APL Materials 2022 [unverified; DOI not seen]); [observing] local distortions and short-range order that lower the actual entropy [unverified].
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Configurational entropy is computed from an ideal formula, not measured. Short-range order, vibrational entropy and magnetic entropy shift the balance, and mixing enthalpies are small and comparable.
- **Standing in 2026.** (a) Settled negative as a general rule. (b) Narrowing for this compound; the broad label remains contested.
- **What would settle it.** Calorimetric enthalpies and measured short-range order (diffuse scattering, EXAFS) on equilibrated samples across temperature.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: J.-W. Yeh (a); J.-P. Maria, S. Curtarolo, C. Rost (b). Negation: E. P. George, F. Otto, D. Miracle, O. Senkov (a); definitional caution from M. Brahlek and co-authors (b).
- **Kind.** Explaining.

#### 2.2 Magic-size clusters: steps on the path or side products?
- **Claim.** In colloidal InP and CdSe syntheses, quantum dots form through discrete magic-size-cluster intermediates (stepwise, non-classical).
- **Negation.** In the same syntheses, dots form by continuous monomer addition, and magic-size clusters are off-path products. Evidence differs by system (InP, CdSe, CdS) and temperature regime, so proper binaries are per system.
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] InP: isolated In37P20 carboxylate clusters, characterized by crystallography and pair distribution function, convert into dots, and in-situ absorption shows clusters preceding dot growth (Gary et al., Chem. Mater. 2015, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.5b00286 [unverified]); [observing] CdSe clusters whose absorption peaks jump between fixed positions rather than drift (Mule et al., JACS 2021 [unverified; DOI not seen]). *Field adds:* closed-shell "magic" stability and two-step nucleation [model].
- **Observings, negation side.** [observing] continuous exciton-peak shifts and size focusing in standard hot-injection growth; [model] LaMer burst nucleation (LaMer and Dinegar, JACS 1950, https://doi.org/10.1021/ja01167a001 [unverified]) and Ostwald ripening.
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Intermediates are transient and dilute; sizes come from absorption via a calibration [model]; quenching aliquots perturbs them.
- **Standing in 2026.** Open and system-specific; InP narrowing toward cluster intermediates [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** In-situ X-ray scattering with absorption and mass spectrometry, ideally isotope labelling that follows atoms from clusters into dots.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: B. Cossairt, K. Yu, D. Norris groups. Negation: classical-growth accounts.
- **Kind.** Explaining. Related non-classical nucleation in the project's files: liquid-like CaCO3 polymer clusters (Demichelis et al. 2011, https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1604 [project-cited]); protein crystals growing from differently organized early assemblies (Van Driessche et al. 2018, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature25971 [project-cited]).

#### 2.3 Plasmonic photocatalysis: hot carriers or heat?
- **Claim.** For Cu–Ru antenna–reactor particles decomposing NH3 under visible light, much of the rate enhancement is non-thermal (hot carriers), beyond what the true catalyst temperature would give.
- **Negation.** For the same catalysts and conditions, photothermal heating, including gradients the thermometry missed, accounts for the enhancement.
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] rates versus intensity and wavelength at nominally fixed temperature, with an apparent barrier falling as intensity rises (Zhou et al., Science 2018, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat6967 [unverified]); earlier, light-driven H2 dissociation on gold (Mukherjee et al., Nano Lett. 2013, https://doi.org/10.1021/nl303940z [unverified]). *Field adds:* hot electrons transiently filling adsorbate antibonding states [model].
- **Observings, negation side.** [reconstruction] the thermocouple sat below the illuminated surface, and heating models reproduce the light-dependent barrier (Sivan et al., Science 2019 comment, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw9367 [unverified]; reply, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw9545 [unverified]; Dubi, Un and Sivan, Chem. Sci. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1039/C9SC06480J [unverified]); [convention] proposed discriminating procedures (Baffou et al., Light Sci. Appl. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41377-020-00345-0 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** The temperature of the reacting surface is not measured directly; both effects grow with intensity; Arrhenius analysis assumes uniform temperature.
- **Standing in 2026.** Open, case-by-case; broad agreement that thermal contributions were often underestimated [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** Operando surface thermometry (e.g. anti-Stokes Raman) on the same catalyst with isothermal dark controls, plus wavelength-dependent selectivity that heating cannot reproduce.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: N. Halas, P. Nordlander, S. Linic. Negation: Y. Sivan, Y. Dubi. Methods: G. Baffou, A. Baldi, R. Quidant.
- **Kind.** Explaining.

#### 2.4 MOF breathing and negative gas adsorption
- **Claim.** Negative gas adsorption (NGA) in DUT-49 is a transition out of a metastable, overloaded open-pore state into a contracted-pore state. In NGA, the adsorbed amount falls and gas is released as pressure rises.
- **Negation.** NGA in DUT-49 is an equilibrium adsorption branch of the framework–gas system.
- **Observings, claim side.**
  - [observing] With methane at 111 K and n-butane at 298 K, uptake falls in a step as pressure rises, the framework contracts at the same time in in-situ diffraction, and the cycle shows hysteresis (Krause et al., Nature 2016, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17430 [project-cited]).
  - [simulation] Free-energy profiles in the same work [project-cited].
  - [model] Thermodynamic stability requires equilibrium uptake not to fall with pressure, so NGA must involve metastability (Evans, Bocquet and Coudert, Chem 2016, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chempr.2016.11.004 [unverified]).
  - [observing] NGA vanishes in small crystallites (Krause et al., Nat. Commun. 2018, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03979-2 [unverified]).
- **Observings, negation side.** I found no equilibrium account. The negation is excluded by the thermodynamic definition.
- **Why the field finds it hard.** The hard part is the sequel: which variables set where the metastable state collapses (crystal size, defects, temperature, heat transfer).
- **Standing in 2026.** Settled on metastability. A sub-binary remains open: NGA is intrinsic to defect-free DUT-49 at any crystal size, or it needs crystallites above a threshold size.
- **What would settle it (sub-binary).** In-situ adsorption with diffraction on single crystals across sizes and measured defect densities.
- **Main proponents.** S. Kaskel, S. Krause, F.-X. Coudert. Breathing precedent: MIL-53 (Serre et al., JACS 2002, https://doi.org/10.1021/ja0276974 [unverified]).
- **Kind.** Explaining; the sub-binary is an observing.

#### 2.5 Topological quantum chemistry versus measurement: ZrTe5 (split)
- **Claim.** Three binaries, because there are more than two accounts. ZrTe5 crystals at low temperature are (i) a strong topological insulator, (ii) a weak topological insulator, (iii) a Dirac semimetal.
- **Negation.** For each, the same crystals at the same temperatures are not in that state.
- **Observings.**
  - [simulation] Topological quantum chemistry (Bradlyn et al., Nature 2017, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23268 [unverified]) and DFT symmetry-indicator catalogues listed thousands of candidates. Sources, all [unverified]:
    - Vergniory et al., Nature 2019, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0954-4
    - Zhang T. et al., Nature 2019, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0944-6
    - Tang et al., Nature 2019, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0937-5
    - Vergniory et al., Science 2022, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abg9094

    Each classification depends on the input lattice parameters and the exchange–correlation functional. Chemical-rule heuristics: Schoop, Pielnhofer and Lotsch, Chem. Mater. 2018, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.7b05133 [unverified].
  - [simulation] ZrTe5 predicted near the strong/weak boundary (Weng, Dai and Fang, PRX 2014, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.4.011002 [unverified]).
  - [observing] ARPES read as a strong TI (Manzoni et al., PRL 2016, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.237601 [unverified]).
  - [observing] Negative magnetoresistance read as a Dirac-semimetal chiral anomaly (Li Q. et al., Nature Physics 2016, https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys3648 [unverified]).
  - [observing] Step-edge states seen by STM, read as a weak TI (Wu R. et al., PRX 2016 [unverified]).
  - [observing] Strain tunes the gap through a transition (Mutch et al., Sci. Adv. 2019, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aav9771 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** The gap is tens of meV and sits near a boundary. Growth method shifts Te vacancies and lattice constants. A Lifshitz shift moves with temperature, and the probes are surface-sensitive.
- **Standing in 2026.** Open; converging on "near a boundary, sample-dependent" [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** On one crystal: kz-resolved ARPES, STM, quantum oscillations and strain-controlled transport, with lattice parameters measured.
- **Main proponents.** Theory: Z. Fang, X. Dai, H. Weng. Strong TI: Manzoni and co-authors. Dirac semimetal: Q. Li and co-authors. Strain: J.-H. Chu group [unverified].
- **Kind.** Observing (which state occurs), judged against simulations.

#### 2.6 Monolayer VSe2: intrinsic room-temperature ferromagnetism?
- **Claim.** Defect-free monolayer 1T-VSe2 is intrinsically ferromagnetic at room temperature.
- **Negation.** Defect-free monolayer 1T-VSe2 has no room-temperature ferromagnetic order. The reported magnetism is extrinsic: defects, interfaces, adsorbates or impurities.
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] SQUID and magneto-optical hysteresis at room temperature in MBE monolayers on graphite and MoS2 (Bonilla et al., Nat. Nanotechnol. 2018, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-018-0063-9 [unverified]); [simulation] DFT predicted monolayer ferromagnetism.
- **Observings, negation side.**
  - [observing] ARPES showing no exchange splitting and an enhanced charge-density wave (Feng et al., Nano Lett. 2018, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.nanolett.8b01649 [unverified]).
  - [observing] Element-specific XMCD finding no ferromagnetic order, read as spin frustration (Wong et al., Adv. Mater. 2019 [unverified]).
  - [model] The charge-density wave suppresses ferromagnetism (Coelho et al., J. Phys. Chem. C 2019 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Monolayer moments are tiny against substrate and contaminant backgrounds, and vacancy density is rarely measured on the same sample.
- **Standing in 2026.** Narrowing toward the negation for pristine monolayers (the 2018 claim is a prior state); magnetism induced by defects remains plausible [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** XMCD and spin-polarized STM on monolayers whose Se vacancies have been counted.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: M. Batzill group (2018). Negation: Feng and co-authors; Wong and co-authors.
- **Kind.** Observing.

#### 2.7 Lost photoluminescence in monolayer TMDs: defects or free carriers?
- **Claim.** The low photoluminescence quantum yield of as-exfoliated monolayer MoS2 and WS2 comes mainly from defect-mediated non-radiative recombination.
- **Negation.** It comes mainly from free carriers (non-radiative trion recombination) and disappears when carriers are removed without changing defects.
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] Superacid treatment raised the MoS2 yield to near unity (Amani et al., Science 2015, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aad2114 [unverified]). *The initial reading (prior) adds:* defect passivation [model].
- **Observings, negation side.** [observing] Electrostatic gating alone raised yields to near unity in untreated MoS2 and WS2 (Lien et al., Science 2019, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw8053 [unverified]). *This reading adds:* defects matter mainly as dopants.
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Vacancies dope the layer, and the yield depends on excitation density (exciton–exciton annihilation).
- **Standing in 2026.** Narrowing toward the free-carrier account at low excitation; defect trapping varies by sample [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** Defect counts on the same flake (STM/TEM) set against yield as a function of gate voltage.
- **Main proponents.** Both readings came from the A. Javey group (2015, revised 2019).
- **Kind.** Explaining.

#### 2.8 Medium-range order in metallic glasses: icosahedral or crystal-like? (split by alloy)
- **Claim.** In metallic glasses, medium-range order is built mainly from distorted icosahedral, non-crystallographic clusters.
- **Negation.** In the same glass, medium-range order is mainly crystal-like (fcc-, hcp- and bcc-like networks).
- **Scope note.** Alloys and methods differ: a binary Zr–Ni glass studied by local diffraction, and a multicomponent nanoparticle studied by tomography. Proper binaries are per alloy.
- **Observings, claim side.**
  - [reconstruction + simulation] Reverse Monte Carlo with ab initio MD: solute-centred clusters packed icosahedrally (Sheng et al., Nature 2006, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04421 [unverified]).
  - [observing] Angstrom-beam electron diffraction from single clusters, matched to distorted icosahedra in a Zr–Ni glass (Hirata et al., Nature Materials 2011, https://doi.org/10.1038/nmat2897 [unverified]; the project's Chemistry offerings cite it at its abstract).
  - *Field adds:* Frank's frustrated polytetrahedral packing [model].
- **Observings, negation side.** [observing/reconstruction] Atomic electron tomography of a multicomponent metallic-glass nanoparticle: disordered short-range clusters joined into crystal-like superclusters (Yang et al., Nature 2021, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03354-0 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Three-dimensional atomic positions are accessible only in nanoparticles. Reverse Monte Carlo fits are non-unique, and simulated cooling rates far exceed experimental ones.
- **Standing in 2026.** Open.
- **What would settle it.** Tomography on bulk-relevant compositions (e.g. Cu–Zr), cross-checked with ensemble diffraction and fluctuation electron microscopy.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: E. Ma, M. Chen, A. Hirata. Negation: J. Miao group.
- **Kind.** Observing, at the level of reconstruction. Project cross-references: the glass transition (registry entry 35); rings in 2D silica glass (Huang et al., Nano Letters 2012, project-cited at its title).

### Inferences
- Several apparent contradictions dissolve once scope is stated: different alloys (2.8), different syntheses (2.2), different samples of a nominally single compound (2.5, 2.6). The proper binaries are per sample class, with the preparation stated.
- Membership criteria differ for each class:
  - "high-entropy": element count, entropy value, or shown entropy stabilization;
  - "magic-size": a fixed absorption peak and a discrete composition;
  - "topological": a DFT indicator, or a measured surface state.

  Each binary depends on which criterion is used.
- Reversible transformations with hysteresis (2.1b, 2.4) are where chemistry's own terms do the work: reversibility, metastability, path dependence.
- Questions of intrinsic versus defect-driven behaviour (2.6, 2.7) are settled by counting defects on the same sample that is measured, not by better averages.

### Gaps
- No source found on what fraction of catalogued topological materials has been confirmed by experiment.
- No 2024–26 primary source checked on hot carriers versus heating; see the catalysis notes.
- Recent work on magic-size clusters and on metallic-glass tomography was not checked.

## 3. Measurement foundations

### Takeaway
AFM line features alone do not show bonds; this is settled for lines between molecules. Partial charges and oxidation states depend on definitions, and spectroscopy constrains d-counts once a criterion is fixed. The BET round robin and the XPS literature audit show that measurements carrying a model diverge across analysts. Evidence on chemistry's overall reproducibility is thin and rests mostly on surveys.

### Cited findings: frontier binaries

#### 3.1 Do CO-tip AFM line features show chemical bonds? (split)
- **Claim.** (a) Line-like contrast between molecules in CO-tip AFM images reports intermolecular bonds such as hydrogen bonds. (b) Differences in apparent length and contrast among C–C bonds within a molecule measure their bond order.
- **Negation.** (a) Those lines come from CO-tip relaxation and appear equally where no bond exists. (b) The differences within a molecule are tip artefacts uncorrelated with bond order.
- **Observings, claim side.**
  - [observing] Submolecular resolution of pentacene with a CO tip at about 5 K (Gross et al., Science 2009, via https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/the-chemical-structure-of-a-molecule-resolved-by-atomic-force-microscopy [project-cited]).
  - [observing] Bond orders distinguished in C60 and polycyclic aromatics (Gross et al., Science 2012, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225621 [project-cited]).
  - [observing] Lines between 8-hydroxyquinoline molecules on Cu(111), read as hydrogen bonds (prior reading; Zhang J. et al., Science 2013, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1242603 [unverified]).
  - [observing] Alternating contrast in cyclo[18]carbon used to support a polyynic structure (Kaiser et al., Science 2019, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aay1914 [unverified]).
- **Observings, negation side.**
  - [observing] Lines between atoms that have no bond between them (Hämäläinen et al., PRL 2014, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.186102 [project-cited]).
  - [simulation] A probe-particle model reproduces sharp lines from lateral CO bending along ridges of the interaction potential (Hapala et al., PRB 2014, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.90.085421 [unverified]).
  - *Field adds (both sides):* the contrast comes from Pauli repulsion, which tracks electron density [model], and images are interpreted by matching probe-particle simulations [simulation].
- **Why the field finds it hard.** The instrument measures a frequency shift of a compliant probe, so the image mixes the sample's electronic structure with the mechanics of the tip.
- **Standing in 2026.** (a) Settled: lines alone do not show bonds, and interaction strengths need force-field mapping (Sweetman et al., Nat. Commun. 2014, https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms4931 [unverified]). (b) Settled with caveats: the correlation is real but distorted by tip tilt.
- **What would settle it (residual b).** Stiffer tips, such as oxygen-terminated copper, and full three-dimensional force spectroscopy compared with computed densities.
- **Main proponents.** (a) Bond reading: X. Qiu group. Artefact reading: I. Swart, P. Liljeroth, P. Jelínek. (b) L. Gross, G. Meyer.
- **Kind.** Explaining: what the observed contrast is. Consistent with the project's earlier note in the chemistry exploring report (incoming folder), "Binding and instruments".

#### 3.2 Are atomic partial charges measurable quantities?
- **Claim.** An atom in a compound has a unique partial charge that experiment measures without depending on any partitioning model.
- **Negation.** It has no unique partial charge; every value depends on a chosen partition.
- **Observings, claim side.**
  - [observing] Measurable quantities related to charge: Born effective-charge tensors from infrared LO–TO splitting, core-level XPS shifts, and X-ray charge densities by multipole refinement [unverified].
  - [observing] A 2025 electron-diffraction study reporting refined partial charges in crystals [unverified; citation not seen]. *It adds:* an ionic scattering-factor model.
- **Observings, negation side.**
  - [model] Mulliken, Hirshfeld, Bader and electrostatic-potential charges differ for one and the same density [unverified].
  - [simulation] The local charge on a transition metal stays nearly constant across formal oxidation states, called charge self-regulation (Raebiger, Lany and Zunger, Nature 2008, https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07009 [unverified]).
  - [convention] Oxidation states treated as formal descriptors, distinct from charge (Walsh et al., Nat. Mater. 2018, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41563-018-0165-7 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Electron density is continuous, so assigning it to atoms requires a rule for where one atom ends.
- **Standing in 2026.** The mainstream view is that charges depend on convention. The 2025 diffraction claim reopens part of the question [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** A partition defined from an observable (Born charges, scattering factors) that independent methods reproduce to a stated precision.
- **Main proponents.** Convention: A. Walsh, C. R. A. Catlow, A. Zunger. Measurement: the authors of the 2025 diffraction study [unverified].
- **Kind.** Convention or definition.

#### 3.3 Copper in [Cu(CF3)4]−: d10 or d8?
- **Claim.** Under a spectroscopic criterion (3d occupancy), copper in [Cu(CF3)4]− is close to d10. This is an inverted ligand field: physically Cu(I)-like, with holes on the ligands.
- **Negation.** Under the same criterion, it is close to d8, physically Cu(III).
- **Observings, claim side.**
  - [observing] Cu K- and L-edge X-ray absorption, interpreted with DFT and multiplet calculations, read as d10-like (Walroth et al., JACS 2016, https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.5b10819 [unverified]).
  - [reconstruction] A review supporting inverted ligand fields (Hoffmann et al., Chem. Rev. 2016, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.6b00251 [unverified]).
  - [observing + model] A 2019 study arguing that many formally d8 Cu(III) complexes are physically nearer d10 (DiMucci et al., JACS 2019, https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.9b09016 [unverified]).
- **Observings, negation side.** [convention] The IUPAC 2016 rules assign +3 by formal ionic counting (Karen, McArdle and Takats, Pure Appl. Chem. 2016, https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2015-1204 [unverified]). Some computational analyses retain substantial d8 character [unverified].
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Bonding lies on a continuum of covalency, assigning spectra needs theory, and the d-count depends on the orbital basis.
- **Standing in 2026.** Narrowing toward d10-like with strong covalency. The dispute over the label persists because the formal and spectroscopic criteria answer different questions.
- **What would settle it.** Agreement on the 3d population, under one stated partition, from XAS, RIXS, XPS satellites and several wavefunction methods.
- **Main proponents.** d10: K. Lancaster, R. Hoffmann, S. Alvarez, and J. P. Snyder's 1995 proposal [unverified]. Formal +3: the IUPAC convention and those who use it.
- **Kind.** Convention for the formal label; an observing once the criterion is fixed.

#### 3.4 Does one isotherm give one BET area?
- **Claim.** Given identical N2 isotherms, independent laboratories applying the BET method obtain the same surface area within a few percent.
- **Negation.** They obtain widely differing areas, mainly because they choose different pressure ranges for the fit.
- **Observings, claim side.** [convention] The IUPAC (Rouquerol) consistency criteria are meant to fix the range. For micropores, IUPAC calls the result an apparent area (Thommes et al., Pure Appl. Chem. 2015, https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2014-1117 [unverified]).
- **Observings, negation side.** [observing] 61 research groups analysed the same 18 isotherms, mainly of MOFs and other porous solids, and reported widely spread areas. The authors released BETSI, software that applies the criteria algorithmically (Osterrieth et al., Adv. Mater. 2022, https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202201502 [unverified]). *Field adds:* a monolayer–multilayer model whose assumptions fail in micropores [model].
- **Why the field finds it hard.** The analyst chooses the range, and micropore filling is not BET multilayer adsorption.
- **Standing in 2026.** Settled for the negation (2022). A remedy has been proposed; how widely it is used is unknown.
- **What would settle it (going forward).** Algorithmic standards across journals, and deposition of raw isotherms.
- **Main proponents.** Negation: D. Fairen-Jimenez and more than 60 co-author groups. Claim: implicit in literature practice.
- **Kind.** Observing.

#### 3.5 Are published XPS analyses mostly sound?
- **Claim.** In materials publications, XPS peak fits and chemical-state assignments mostly follow accepted practice.
- **Negation.** A large fraction, of the order of a third or more, contain significant errors.
- **Observings, claim side.** No systematic evidence found.
- **Observings, negation side.**
  - [observing] A literature survey found significant errors in roughly 40% of papers that fitted peaks (Major et al., J. Vac. Sci. Technol. A 2020, https://doi.org/10.1116/6.0000685 [unverified]), with a companion guide to fitting (https://doi.org/10.1116/6.0000377 [unverified]).
  - [observing] Referencing to the C 1s peak of adventitious carbon shifts with work function, giving errors of the order of an eV (Greczynski and Hultman, Prog. Mater. Sci. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pmatsci.2019.100591 [unverified]).
  - An editorial on faulty analysis (Linford et al., Microsc. Microanal. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1017/S1431927619015332 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Fits need constraints on line shapes, spin–orbit pairs and widths, and a defensible energy reference, and reviewers rarely check raw spectra.
- **Standing in 2026.** Narrowing toward the negation: one audit, with no independent replication found.
- **What would settle it.** Blind re-analysis of deposited raw spectra by several groups.
- **Main proponents.** Negation: M. Linford, G. Major, D. Baer, N. Fairley; G. Greczynski and L. Hultman.
- **Kind.** Observing.

#### 3.6 Do published syntheses reproduce across laboratories?
- **Claim.** Published procedures for Zr–porphyrin MOFs give the claimed phase-pure products in independent laboratories.
- **Negation.** In most independent attempts they do not.
- **Observings, claim side.** No systematic evidence found.
- **Observings, negation side.**
  - [observing] An interlaboratory study in which participants following the same procedures obtained phase-pure products in only a minority of attempts (Boström et al., Adv. Mater. 2024, https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202304832 [unverified]).
  - [observing] A survey in which chemists reported the highest rates of failing to reproduce both others' results and their own (Baker, Nature 2016, https://doi.org/10.1038/533452a [unverified]).
  - An editorial (Bergman and Danheiser, Angew. Chem. 2016, https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201606591 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Polymorphs compete, products are sensitive to modulators and water, and variables go unreported. Surveys measure what people report, not replication rates.
- **Standing in 2026.** Narrowing toward the negation for this family. No replication project covering chemistry as a whole was found.
- **What would settle it.** Replications in several laboratories across representative families of syntheses, with every variable reported.
- **Main proponents.** Negation: B. Lotsch group and co-authors.
- **Kind.** Observing. Cross-reference: project registry entry 32 (the replication crisis).

### Inferences
- Each measurement item has three layers: the instrument's response (frequency shift, isotherm, photoelectron counts), a model (probe-particle mechanics, BET monolayer, peak shapes, partition rule), and the quantity reported (bond, area, chemical state, charge).
- Where the model is explicit and algorithmic, as in BETSI, reproducibility improves. Where it rests on the analyst's judgement, as in XPS fitting or choosing a BET range, the spread grows.
- Binaries 3.2 and 3.3 are at the level of convention. They become empirical only after a criterion is fixed, for example the d-count for an oxidation state.
- Disagreements between single-molecule and ensemble measurements mostly dissolve once scope is stated; see further candidates.

### Gaps
- The 2025 electron-diffraction study of partial charges was not checked.
- No independent replication of the XPS error rate was found.
- No systematic replication project for chemistry, comparable to psychology's, was found; see project registry entry 32.

## 4. Stability and synthesizability

### Takeaway
Computed stability does not define synthesizability. Claims of discovery from AI-scale prediction and autonomous synthesis were contested on novelty and on phase identification. "New material" needs explicit membership criteria.

### Cited findings: frontier binaries

#### 4.1 Does convex-hull stability decide synthesizability? (split)
- **Claim.** (a) A compound must lie on the zero-kelvin DFT convex hull to be synthesizable. (b) Every compound computed to lie on the hull can be made by accessible routes.
- **Negation.** (a) Compounds above the hull are made. (b) Some compounds on the hull resist synthesis.
- **Observings, claim side.** [convention] The hull is the standard filter in high-throughput screening.
- **Observings, negation side.**
  - [simulation on observed compounds] About half of roughly 29,900 known (ICSD) compounds are metastable in DFT, with a median of about 15 meV/atom above the hull and a 90th percentile of about 67 meV/atom, varying by chemistry (Sun et al., Sci. Adv. 2016, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1600225 [unverified]).
  - [simulation] An upper bound on the energy of synthesizable polymorphs, set by the amorphous state (Aykol et al., Sci. Adv. 2018, https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaq0148 [unverified]).
  - [simulation] Formation energies learned by machine learning classify stability poorly (Bartel et al., npj Comput. Mater. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00362-y [unverified]).
  - (b) [observing] β-C3N4 was predicted in 1989 to rival diamond in incompressibility (Liu and Cohen, Science 1989, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.245.4920.841 [unverified]) and has not been convincingly made as a bulk crystalline phase [unverified].
  - *Field adds:* finite-temperature entropy, kinetics, precursor routes and errors of the functional [model].
- **Why the field finds it hard.** "Synthesizable" is defined by human success, failures go unpublished, and DFT errors are as large as the ranges of metastability.
- **Standing in 2026.** (a) Settled negative. (b) Open.
- **What would settle it.** Recorded negative synthesis attempts, finite-temperature free energies, and kinetic models specific to each route.
- **Main proponents.** Metastability: G. Ceder, K. Persson, W. Sun, M. Aykol, C. Bartel. Claim: implicit in the practice of hull filtering.
- **Kind.** Explaining.

#### 4.2 Did the A-Lab make new compounds? (split)
- **Claim.** In 17 days of autonomous operation, the A-Lab synthesized 41 of 58 targeted inorganic compounds not previously reported (Szymanski et al., Nature 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06734-w [unverified]). Split: (a) the target compositions were obtained as the majority crystalline phase; (b) the predicted ordered structures were realized as new compounds.
- **Negation.** (a) The majority phases were not the targets. (b) The products were known compounds, or compositionally disordered versions of known structures, not new ordered compounds.
- **Observings, claim side.** [observing] Powder XRD of each product, with phase identification by machine learning, automated Rietveld refinement, and recipes revised by active learning [unverified].
- **Observings, negation side.** [reconstruction] A re-examination of the published patterns found poor automated refinements, unrecognized cation disorder, and several "new" phases that were already known. The critics judged the novelty claims unsupported and called for verification by experts (Leeman et al., PRX Energy 2024, https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXEnergy.3.011002 [unverified]). The A-Lab authors replied [unverified]; whether Nature attached a correction is also unverified.
- **Why the field finds it hard.** Powder XRD may not distinguish ordered from disordered cation arrangements, novelty depends on how much the databases cover, and automation lacks chemical judgement.
- **Standing in 2026.** Contested. By the critics' analysis, (b) leans to the negation; (a) is partly supported [unverified].
- **What would settle it.** Independent re-synthesis with synchrotron or neutron diffraction, electron diffraction to check ordering, and checks of the literature for novelty.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: G. Ceder, N. Szymanski. Negation: R. Palgrave, L. Schoop.
- **Kind.** Observing.

#### 4.3 Are GNoME's "stable" predictions new materials?
- **Claim.** Most of the ~381,000 structures GNoME placed newly on the convex hull, out of 2.2 million it predicted stable, are distinct new materials (Merchant et al., Nature 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06735-9 [unverified]).
- **Negation.** A substantial fraction are ordered variants of known compounds or alloys that would be disordered in reality, so they are not distinct materials.
- **Observings, claim side.** [simulation] Hull energies verified by DFT. [observing, as claimed] 736 structures realized independently by experiment [unverified].
- **Observings, negation side.** [reconstruction] Cheetham and Seshadri sampled the set and found many entries trivially related to known phases, or ordered stand-ins for disordered solid solutions. They proposed novelty, credibility and utility as tests (Chem. Mater. 2024, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.4c00643 [unverified]).
- **Why the field finds it hard.** "New material" has no agreed criterion for membership, and ordered models at zero kelvin meet disorder at synthesis temperatures.
- **Standing in 2026.** Contested, and largely a question of definition.
- **What would settle it.** Agreed criteria (a distinct structure type, configurational entropy accounted for, a credible synthesis), then an audit of a random sample.
- **Main proponents.** Claim: E. D. Cubuk, A. Merchant (Google DeepMind). Negation: A. K. Cheetham, R. Seshadri.
- **Kind.** Convention or definition, with an explaining component.

### Inferences
- Classification is the crux. "Stable", "synthesizable", "new" and "made" each need criteria, and in 4.2–4.3 the critics supplied criteria (novelty, credibility, utility) only after the fact.
- The recurring mismatch is between ordered structures modelled by DFT at 0 K and disordered products made at high temperature (4.2, 4.3, and the MatterGen candidate below).
- Negative synthesis results are rarely published, so "resists synthesis" is under-observed. As a result, binary 4.1(b) cannot yet be tested on a base of systematic evidence.

### Gaps
- Whether Nature attached a correction or editor's note to the A-Lab paper, and the full content of the A-Lab team's reply.
- No independent audit found of GNoME's 736 claimed experimental realizations.
- Validation of synthesizability classifiers learned by machine learning was not checked.
