# Podal pairs and dualities in chemistry: conjugates, mirror images, complements, hole-equivalences, dualities and symmetric spectra

Key: **Centre** = what the two stand about; **Exch.** = what passes between them; **Status** = *exact* (definition or symmetry), *approximate* (rule with breakdowns) or *conventional* (naming); **Obs** = what was measured (method, sample, conditions, quantity); **Model** = what theory or convention adds. **Labels**: OBS observing · REC reconstruction from observings · CONV convention or definition · MOD model construct · SIM simulation. "Listed DOI" = read on the cited page, not opened at the publisher; [unverified] = not checked this session. Collection stopped early when the shared search budget and hourly fetch limit ran out; the Gaps entries list what remains unchecked.

## Key gathered points

- **Four kinds of centre recur:**
  - *An exchanged unit:* one proton (conjugate acid/base), electrons (redox couple, anode/cathode), an electron pair (Lewis acid/base, nucleophile/electrophile), a hydrogen (H-bond donor/acceptor).
  - *A symmetry element:* a mirror plane (enantiomers) or an inversion centre (g/u parity, meso forms, centrosymmetric racemic crystals).
  - *A spectral or energy midpoint:* the nonbonding level α (Coulson–Rushbrooke), the 0–0 band (absorption/fluorescence mirror rule), the Rayleigh line (Stokes/anti-Stokes), the chemical shift (NMR doublet), an isosbestic wavelength, pKa, ½pKw.
  - *A path midpoint:* the transition state shared by a step and its microscopic reverse, e.g. the planar state between mirror-image amine pyramids.
- **Exact pairs** come from a definition (conjugates differ by one H⁺; reductive elimination is defined as the reverse of oxidative addition) or a symmetry (enantiomers under the parity-conserving electromagnetic Hamiltonian; Laporte rule and mutual exclusion in centrosymmetric species; equal Stokes/anti-Stokes shifts; ortho/para spin classes).
- **Approximate pairs break observably:**
  - Coulson–Rushbrooke pairing is exact only in Hückel/PPP-type models and fails with heteroatoms and odd rings, yet predicts dark lowest doublets in alternant radicals and low conductance through meta-linked benzene.
  - Mirror nuclei show analogue-state energy differences of tens to hundreds of keV, mainly Coulomb; in 2025 ⁷¹Kr and ⁷¹Br were reported to have different ground states.
  - The absorption/fluorescence mirror rule fails for oligo(p-phenylene)s.
  - Watson–Crick pairs coexist with transient Hoogsteen states (0.08–2.7 % populations, ~0.1–2.6 ms lifetimes).
  - Six of eight new sulfur-containing quasiracemates break the expected near-inversion packing.
- **Conventional pairs** need their reference rule stated: D/L, R/S and (+)/(−) are mutually independent; cis/trans and E/Z can disagree; likewise α/β, endo/exo, syn/anti, P/M. The SHE zero is a convention, and electrode signs flip between galvanic and electrolytic cells. The arbitrary D assignment was confirmed in 1951.
- **Open in October 2026:** molecular parity violation (predicted vibrational splittings mHz to ~1 Hz; best comparisons <8 Hz, i.e. 2×10⁻¹³, vibrational, and 0.08 ± 0.72 Hz, ~10⁻¹⁰, rotational; none seen); the mechanism of chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS); the origin of homochirality; mirror-life governance (2024 risk paper, 2025 moratorium recommendation, 2026 statements).
- **Descriptive dualities** (Legendre conjugates, direct/reciprocal lattices, Fourier pairs, wave/particle, VB/MO, normal/reversed-phase chromatography) are exact transforms or bases equivalent in principle. Observation usually lands on one side (intensities, interferograms, FIDs, ionization energies); the other is reconstructed.

## 1. Conjugate and complementary pairs

### Takeaway
Most complementary pairs in chemistry are defined by one exchanged unit, such as H⁺, electrons, an electron pair, a hydrogen or a base-pairing partner. The pairing is therefore exact by definition. What is approximate is everything quantitative attached to it (strength orders, shape complementarity, base-pairing fidelity), and these are the things measurements test.

### Cited Findings
**Proton pairs**
- *Conjugate acid–base pair:* the acid formed by protonating a base carries one more unit of positive charge than the base, whatever the absolute charges. Centre: one proton. Exch.: H⁺. Status: exact (definition). Label: CONV. Source: [Gold Book C01266, doi:10.1351/goldbook.C01266](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/C01266/plain).
- *Scales attached to the pair:* Obs: pKa from glass-electrode titration with least-squares fitting, UV-vis or fluorescence spectrophotometry (preferred below pKa ≈ 2 or above ≈ 11), and time-averaged NMR shifts. Model: pKa + pKb = pKw for any acid in water; pKw is 13.9965 at 25 °C (14.943 at 0 °C, 13.262 at 50 °C); at half-neutralization pH = pKa, the centre where acid and base are equally abundant. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Acid dissociation constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_dissociation_constant).
- *Strength inversion runs one way:* a strong acid has a weak conjugate base (HCl/Cl⁻), but a weak acid need not have a strong one (acetate, Kb ≈ 5.6×10⁻¹⁰). Label: OBS. Source: [Conjugate base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_base).
- *Amphiprotic* (water) and amphoteric (amino acid) species belong to two pairs at once; glycine's isoelectric point is the pH of equal cationic and anionic forms. Label: CONV + OBS. Source: [Acid dissociation constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_dissociation_constant).

**Electron pairs: redox couples, electrodes and step pairs**
- *Redox couple; oxidant and reductant:* oxidation (electron loss, rise in oxidation state) and reduction always occur together; the oxidant is itself reduced and the reductant oxidized; half-reactions balance the electrons. Status: exact (definition). Label: CONV. Source: [Redox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redox).
- *What is measured:* only whole-cell potentials, by voltmeter or electrometer across two half-cells (Daniell cell: Cu²⁺/Cu +0.34 V, Zn²⁺/Zn −0.76 V, cell +1.10 V). Single-electrode values are assigned by setting the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) to 0.00 V and tabulating reductions. Label: OBS (emf) + CONV. Sources: [Standard electrode potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_electrode_potential); [Galvanic cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_cell).
- *Anode and cathode:* the Gold Book defines the anode as the "Electrode at which oxidation takes place" ([A00370](https://www.old.goldbook.iupac.org/pdf/A00370.pdf)) and the cathode as the electrode of reduction ([C00905](https://old.goldbook.iupac.org/html/C/C00905.html)). The process is fixed but the sign is not: the anode is negative in a galvanic cell and positive in an electrolytic one ([Galvanic cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanic_cell)). Status: exact by process, conventional by sign. Label: CONV.
- *Disproportionation and comproportionation:* an intermediate oxidation state gives one higher and one lower (2 Sn²⁺ → Sn⁴⁺ + Sn, studied by Gadolin in 1788; 3 Cl₂ + 6 OH⁻ → 5 Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻ + 3 H₂O; 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂; Boudouard 2 CO → C + CO₂); comproportionation (symproportionation) builds the intermediate (Claus: 2 H₂S + SO₂ → 3 S + 2 H₂O). Centre: the intermediate state the products straddle. Status: exact (definition). Obs: product analysis. Label: CONV + OBS. Source: [Disproportionation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disproportionation) (listed DOI 10.1351/goldbook.D01799).
- *Oxidative addition / reductive elimination:* insertion of a metal complex into a covalent bond with formal two-electron loss on one metal, or one-electron loss on each of two ([Gold Book O04367](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/O04367/plain)); reductive elimination is defined as its reverse ([Gold Book R05223](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/R05223/plain)). Centre: the formal ±2 oxidation-state change. Exch.: the A–B bond. Status: exact by definition (formal bookkeeping). Label: CONV.
- *Microscopic-reverse pairs:* in a reversible reaction the mechanism one way is exactly the reverse of the other; reactions starting with photochemical excitation are excluded ([Gold Book P04850](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/P04850/plain)). Detailed balancing, the macroscopic counterpart, requires equal forward and backward traffic on every pathway at equilibrium ([Gold Book D01626](https://dev.goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/D01626)); equilibrium is dynamic, with equal non-zero rates, G stationary and ΔG° = −RT ln K ([Chemical equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_equilibrium)). Centre: the transition state common to both directions. Label: MOD (principle) + OBS (K, rate constants).
- *The transition state as a centre:* model: a first-order saddle point at the top of the reaction coordinate, populated negligibly for about one vibrational period; by the Hammond–Leffler postulate it resembles the higher-enthalpy side (early for exothermic, late for endothermic steps); transition-state theory dates from 1935 (Eyring; Evans and Polanyi). Obs: femtosecond pump–probe signals versus delay, from which reaction progress is reconstructed (Zewail, Nobel Prize 1999; listed DOI 10.1126/science.242.4886.1645). Label: MOD; OBS → REC. Sources: [Transition state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_state); [Femtochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femtochemistry).
- *Diels–Alder / retro-Diels–Alder:* with ΔH° and ΔS° both negative, the reverse reaction wins at high temperature (industrial cyclopentadiene from its dimer). Label: OBS. Source: [Diels–Alder reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diels-Alder_reaction).

**Electron-pair donors and acceptors**
- *Lewis acid and base:* an acceptor with a low-lying empty orbital and an electron-pair donor form an adduct (NH₃ + BMe₃ → NH₃·BMe₃); Lewis defined acids and bases this way in 1923. A Lewis base is usually a Brønsted base, but a Lewis acid need not be a Brønsted acid: CO is a very weak Brønsted base yet binds BF₃ strongly, and 2,6-di-tert-butylpyridine takes HCl but not BF₃ (sterics). The hard/soft classification dates from 1963; the ECW model (−ΔH = E_A·E_B + C_A·C_B + W) implies no single strength order. Status: pair exact by definition; strength orders approximate and partner-dependent. Label: CONV + OBS (adduct enthalpies) + MOD. Source: [Lewis acids and bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_acids_and_bases) (listed DOIs 10.1351/goldbook.L03508, 10.1351/goldbook.E02020).
- *Nucleophile and electrophile:* Ingold's terms (1933), replacing Lapworth's anionoid/cationoid (1925). Nucleophiles donate electron pairs as Lewis bases do, but nucleophilicity is kinetic (rates) while basicity is thermodynamic (equilibria). Ambident nucleophiles attack from two sites (SCN⁻ via S or N). Swain–Scott (1953) and Mayr–Patz (N, E, s) scales correlate rate constants; azide reacts about 3000 times faster than water with benzyl chloride. Label: CONV + OBS (rates) + MOD (linear free-energy relations). Source: [Nucleophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleophile).
- *Frustrated Lewis pairs (FLPs).* Source: [Frustrated Lewis pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frustrated_Lewis_pair).
  - An acid and a base held apart by steric bulk cannot form an adduct, but together they split H₂ heterolytically: PCy₃ + B(C₆F₅)₃ + H₂ → [HPCy₃]⁺[HB(C₆F₅)₃]⁻.
  - This was reported as reversible, metal-free H₂ activation by Welch, San Juan, Masuda and Stephan (2006, listed DOI 10.1126/science.1134230). "Frustrated radical pairs" followed in 2023 (Ju et al., listed DOI 10.1021/jacs.3c07070).
  - Label: OBS + MOD.
- *Umpolung (polarity inversion).*
  - IUPAC definition: any process that reverses the normal alternating donor/acceptor reactivity pattern that O or N heteroatoms impose along a chain ([Gold Book U06551](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/U06551/plain)). Its examples are R–C≡C–X used as a synthon for "R–C≡C⁺", and temporarily replacing N or O with P, S or Se.
  - Normal polarity makes 1,3- and 1,5-heteroatom patterns easy (aldol, Claisen, Michael) and 1,2-, 1,4- and 1,6-patterns hard ([Umpolung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umpolung)).
  - Tools include 2-lithio-1,3-dithianes as masked acyl anions (Seebach and Corey 1975, listed DOI 10.1021/jo00890a018; Seebach's 1979 review, listed DOI 10.1002/anie.197902393), cyanide in the benzoin condensation, N-heterocyclic carbenes (NHCs), and the thiamine carbene in pyruvate decarboxylase ([Umpolung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umpolung)).
  - NHCs act as catalytic acyl-anion equivalents ([Bugaut & Glorius 2012, Chem. Soc. Rev. 41:3511, doi:10.1039/C2CS15333E](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2012/cs/c2cs15333e)).
  - Centre: a carbon whose sign of polarity is swapped. Status: the "natural" alternation is a heuristic (approximate), and umpolung names a strategy. Obs: the constitution of the products.
  - Label: MOD + CONV.
- *HOMO/LUMO donor–acceptor exchange.*
  - In a normal-demand Diels–Alder reaction the diene HOMO pairs with the dienophile LUMO. In inverse demand the dienophile HOMO pairs with the diene LUMO, and substituents can switch which interaction dominates.
  - Label: MOD, tested by rates and regiochemistry. Source: [Diels–Alder reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diels-Alder_reaction).

**Hydrogen-bond and charge-transfer partners**
- *H-bond donor and acceptor.* Source: [Hydrogen bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bonds).
  - Definition: IUPAC 2011 (Arunan et al., Pure Appl. Chem. 83:1637, listed DOI 10.1351/PAC-REC-10-01-02) requires an X–H donor with X more electronegative than H, an acceptor atom or group, and evidence that a bond forms.
  - Obs: in the IR, the X–H stretching frequency drops; "improper" H-bonds shift the other way. In ¹H NMR the proton moves downfield (the acetylacetone enol proton sits near 15.5 ppm). Crystal structures show distances shorter than van der Waals sums (X–H ≈ 110 pm, H···Y ≈ 160–200 pm).
  - Centre: the shared hydrogen. Symmetric H-bonds such as bifluoride are the limit at which donor and acceptor become equivalent [details unverified].
  - Label: CONV + OBS.
- *Charge-transfer (donor–acceptor) partners:* the donor carries a partial positive and the acceptor a partial negative charge; complete transfer gives a salt. Model: CT bands are transitions between mixed neutral and ionic states (Mulliken–Hush), with a gap set by the donor's ionization potential, the acceptor's electron affinity and their Coulomb attraction; solvation lowers them. Obs: Benesi–Hildebrand association constants (iodine–aromatics, 1949, listed DOI 10.1021/ja01176a030); TTF–TCNQ (1973), the first purely organic near-metallic conductor; the purple iodine–starch band. Centre: the degree of ionicity between DA and D⁺A⁻. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Charge-transfer complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge-transfer_complex).
- *Cation and anion exchangers.*
  - Cation exchangers carry a negatively charged stationary phase and anion exchangers a positively charged one.
  - Label: CONV. Source: [Chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatography).

**Complementary sequences and shapes**
- *Watson–Crick pairing.*
  - G•C pairs form three H-bonds and A•T/U pairs two. A purine always pairs with a pyrimidine, because two purines clash and two pyrimidines sit too far apart. Stacking supplies most of the stability and pairing supplies the specificity; GC-rich DNA melts at higher temperature ([Base pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_pair)).
  - The strands run antiparallel. Chargaff observed A = T and G = C in DNA from any species, and Photo 51 (May 1952, taken by Gosling under Franklin) informed the 1953 model ([DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA)). Watson and Crick, Nature 171:737 (1953) [DOI unverified].
  - Centre: the duplex dyad. Exch.: A↔T, G↔C. Status: approximate (wobble G•U pairs at the third codon position, Hoogsteen pairs, mismatches).
  - Label: OBS (composition ratios, fibre diffraction) → REC (the double-helix model).
- *Hoogsteen pairs.*
  - The pyrimidine's Watson–Crick face binds the purine's N7 ("Hoogsteen") face, with the purine in the syn conformation. The first example was a 1963 crystal of 1-methylthymine with 9-methyladenine (listed DOI 10.1107/S0365110X63002437). G•C⁺ requires a protonated cytidine; reverse Hoogsteen pairs occur in tRNA, and triplexes use Hoogsteen pairing ([Hoogsteen base pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoogsteen_base_pair)).
  - Obs (2011): transient Hoogsteen pairs in canonical duplex DNA were detected by NMR relaxation dispersion, supported by steered molecular dynamics, at CA and TA steps ([Nikolova et al. 2011, Nature 470:498, doi:10.1038/nature09775](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v470y2011i7335d10.1038_nature09775.html)).
  - Obs (2014): ¹³C/¹⁵N R1ρ at 600 MHz and 25–26 °C showed them to be widespread ([Alvey et al. 2014, Nat. Commun. 5:4786, doi:10.1038/ncomms5786](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/ncomms5786)):
    - Populations: 0.08–2.73 % for A•T and 0.13–2.11 % for G•C⁺ (at pH 5.2–5.4).
    - Lifetimes: 0.12–2.57 ms and 0.40–2.08 ms respectively.
    - Mechanism: the purine rotates 180° from anti to syn.
    - Relative stability varies by about 2.1 kcal/mol across sequence steps.
  - Status: reported 2011; extended and reproduced 2014. Label: OBS + MOD (two-state exchange fit) + SIM (molecular dynamics).
- *Unnatural base pairs:* Hirao's s–y and Ds–Px (2002, listed DOI 10.1038/nbt0202-177) and Romesberg's hydrophobic d5SICS–dNaM (2012, listed DOI 10.1073/pnas.1205176109), replicated in semi-synthetic *E. coli* (2014, listed DOI 10.1038/nature13314), show complementarity resting on shape and hydrophobicity without H-bonds. Label: OBS. Source: [Base pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_pair).
- *Palindromic recognition sites:* many type II restriction sites are inverted-repeat palindromes reading the same 5′→3′ on both strands; EcoRI cuts G|AATTC (sticky ends) and EcoRV GAT|ATC (blunt); the enzymes are typically homodimers. First type II enzyme HindII, 1970 (listed DOI 10.1016/0022-2836(70)90149-X); Nobel Prize 1978 (Arber, Nathans, Smith). Centre: the site's dyad. Status: exact in sequence. Label: CONV + OBS. Source: [Restriction enzyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restriction_enzyme).
- *Host–guest complementarity:* H + G ⇌ HG with K = [HG]/([H][G]); highly selective binding is molecular recognition. Obs: NMR titrations (fast-exchange averaged shifts) and UV-vis titrations fitted for K; α-, β- and γ-cyclodextrins (6, 7, 8 glucose units) have cavities of about 5, 6 and 8 Å; crown ethers match ion size (12-crown-4 for Li⁺). Status: approximate; complementarity and preorganization are design heuristics (Fischer's 1894 lock-and-key [unverified]). Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Host–guest chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Host–guest_chemistry).

### Inferences
- In this family the "centre" is an exchanged unit rather than a point. Each member is the other plus or minus that unit, so the pairing is exact while strength needs separate scales (pKa, E°, N/E, K).
- Only microscopic-reverse pairs literally share one configuration, the transition state. By the Hammond–Leffler postulate, a thermoneutral identity reaction would put it midway; this is an inference from the postulate, not a measurement.
- An FLP is a pair kept from meeting that acts jointly on a third molecule and leaves a new complementary pair: H⁺ on the base and H⁻ on the acid.

## 2. Mirror and inversion pairs

### Takeaway
Enantiomers are exact mirror pairs because the electromagnetic Hamiltonian commutes with parity. Only chiral probes tell them apart, and the weak-force splitting predicted between them had not been observed in any molecule as of October 2026. Pairing about an inversion centre recurs at three scales: within molecules (g/u, meso), between molecules in crystals (racemic compounds, quasiracemates) and in spectra (Laporte rule, mutual exclusion). Most names for stereo-pairs are conventions whose reference rule must be stated.

### Cited Findings
**Enantiomers**
- *Definition and behaviour:* one of a pair of non-superposable mirror-image entities ([Gold Book E02069](https://dev.goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/E02069/plain)), not interconvertible by rotation or conformational change. They rotate plane-polarized light oppositely, a racemate is inactive, and separation needs a chiral environment (chiral precursor, catalyst or kinetic resolution); thalidomide's enantiomers were sedative and teratogenic respectively, and escitalopram is the active enantiomer of racemic citalopram ([Enantiomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer)). Centre: a mirror plane; the molecule has no improper axis Sₙ (σ = S₁, i = S₂) ([Molecular symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_symmetry)), and only pure-rotation groups (Cₙ, Dₙ, T, O, I) are chiral ([Point groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_groups_in_three_dimensions)). Exch.: the handedness of every stereogenic element. Status: exact under electromagnetism, since molecular electromagnetic Hamiltonians commute with parity ([Parity violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_violation)). Obs: polarimetry; achiral chromatography cannot separate a racemate ([Chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatography)); only chiral molecules show circular dichroism, ΔA = A_LCP − A_RCP ([Circular dichroism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_dichroism); equal and opposite enantiomer CD spectra [unverified]). Label: CONV + OBS.

**Parity violation, the predicted break in the mirror pair (open)**
- The weak interaction violates parity, shown in the beta decay of ⁶⁰Co by Wu et al. (results shared 4 January 1957). Label: OBS (nuclear physics). Source: [Parity violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_violation).
- *Prior state, c. 1999–2013:* Obs: the CHFClBr C–F stretch, by CO₂-laser saturated absorption with the enantiomers in separate Fabry–Perot cavities, differed by less than about 8 Hz (fractional about 2×10⁻¹³; Satterthwaite et al. quote 2.5×10⁻¹³), limited by impurity-linked pressure shifts. Model: about 2.4 mHz predicted (Δν/ν ≈ 8×10⁻¹⁷); heavy-metal complexes about 1 Hz; oxorhenium complexes several hundred mHz. Label: OBS (limit) + SIM. Source: [Tokunaga et al., arXiv:1309.5630](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.5630).
- *Planned route, c. 2019:* mid-IR Ramsey interferometry on buffer-gas-cooled beams with comb-referenced quantum cascade lasers; methyltrioxorhenium cooled to about 6 K rotational temperature as a test, no beam yet; N≡UHFI predicted near 20 Hz but unsynthesized; precision goal below 0.1 Hz. Label: OBS (apparatus) + MOD. Source: [Cournol et al., arXiv:1912.06054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.06054).
- *Differential rotational test, 2021/22:* Obs: the 3₀₃–2₁₂ line of (R)- and (S)-1,2-propanediol near 14.717 GHz (cryogenic buffer-gas cell, tunable microwave Fabry–Perot cavity, alternating samples) agrees within 0.08 ± 0.72 Hz (fractional about 9.7×10⁻¹¹). Model: predicted shifts are about 10⁻¹⁹ for light molecules like this and about 10⁻¹⁵ with heavy atoms near the stereocentre; parity violation is seen in atoms, not yet in molecules. Label: OBS (null at about 10⁻¹⁰). Source: [Satterthwaite et al. 2022, Symmetry 14:28, doi:10.3390/sym14010028](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10559601).
- *2026.* Source: [Aucar, Stroppa & Aucar, arXiv:2603.01885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01885).
  - Across a set of chiral molecules, computed parity-violation energy differences correlate positively with an electronic chirality measure. The authors suggest choosing molecules with a high value of that measure for detection (preprint, 2 March 2026).
  - Label: SIM. Status: open; this session found no molecular detection.
- No causal link from parity violation to biological homochirality has been demonstrated, and the Vester–Ulbricht hypothesis is described as speculation. Label: MOD (hypothesis; historically notable, unconfirmed). Source: [Homochirality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homochirality).

**Racemates; homochiral versus heterochiral association**
- *Racemates and their solid forms:* an equimolar, optically inactive mixture marked (±)-, rac- or RS ([Gold Book R05025](https://goldbook.iupac.org/R05025.html)). Solids are conglomerates (separate R and S crystals forming a eutectic; about 10 % of racemic chiral compounds), racemic compounds (ordered 1:1 crystals), pseudoracemates (solid solutions) or quasiracemates; Wallach's rule (racemic crystals tend to be denser) was supported by a 1991 database analysis (listed DOI 10.1021/ja00026a015); kryptoracemates also occur ([Racemic mixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racemic_mixture)). Racemic compounds are mostly inversion-related: >90 % crystallize in centrosymmetric space groups, 8–9.5 % in non-centrosymmetric ones ([Wells, Sahlstrom & Wheeler, CrystEngComm 2021](https://pubs.rsc.org/os/content/getauthorversionpdf/D1CE01214B); DOI 10.1039/D1CE01214B inferred from the manuscript ID [unverified]). Label: OBS (crystallographic statistics) + CONV.
- *Self-disproportionation of enantiomers:* on achiral silica a 66.6 % ee sample split into fractions from 8.1 % to >99.9 % ee (hexane/EtOAc 5:1; lost with a polar eluent), attributed to homochiral versus heterochiral aggregates. First noted 1983 (¹⁴C-nicotine, listed DOI 10.1016/S0021-9673(01)87863-8); named 2006 (Soloshonok, listed DOI 10.1002/anie.200503373). Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Enantiomer self-disproportionation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer_self-disproportionation).
- *Amplifying one hand:* Frank's 1953 model (autocatalysis plus mutual antagonism; listed DOI 10.1016/0006-3002(53)90082-1); Soai-type asymmetric autocatalysis (listed DOI 10.1021/ja953066g); stirred NaClO₃ crystallization; enantiopure ribose-aminooxazoline crystallized on spin-polarized magnetite (2023, listed DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adg8274). Bennu amino acids showed no chiral bias (Glavin et al., Nature Astronomy, Feb 2025, per the page). The origin of biological homochirality is open. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Homochirality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homochirality).
- *Chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS).* Source: [CISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality-induced_spin_selectivity).
  - Chirality affects the spin of electrons transmitted through or emitted from chiral molecules (Naaman's group; Ray et al. 1999, listed DOI 10.1126/science.283.5403.814).
  - Models based on spin–orbit coupling predict magnitudes orders of magnitude below experiment, and the mechanism is "not completely understood". A 2025 study reported voltage-controlled interfacial chirality in MoS₂ (listed DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adx2281).
  - Status: open. Label: OBS + MOD.

**Pasteur and resolution**
- *Pasteur's hand-sorting:* Pasteur sorted by hand the enantiomorphous crystals of sodium ammonium tartrate tetrahydrate, a spontaneous resolution ([Enantiomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer); [Sodium ammonium tartrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_ammonium_tartrate), which cites his 1849 Comptes rendus paper, 28:477; the usual date 1848 [unverified]). The racemic salt crystallizes as a monohydrate (listed DOI 10.1039/DT9810001268); distinct enantiomorphous crystals form near 25 °C ([Racemic mixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racemic_mixture)); conglomerate formation only below about 27 °C [unverified]. Label: OBS (crystal habit, optical rotation) → REC (molecular handedness).
- *Kinetic resolution:* in Pasteur's 1858 fermentation, *Penicillium glaucum* consumed (R,R)-tartrate and left (S,S), the first kinetic resolution; the first synthetic one followed in 1899 (Marckwald and McKenzie, listed DOI 10.1002/cber.189903202130). Model: selectivity s = k_fast/k_slow; yield at most 50 % unless the substrate racemizes (dynamic kinetic resolution); parallel kinetic resolution gives two non-enantiomeric products. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Kinetic resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_resolution).

**Quasi- and pseudo-enantiomers; quasiracemates**
- *Definitions:* quasi-enantiomers are distinct species sharing a large chiral moiety of opposite sense, e.g. (R)-2-bromobutane and (S)-2-chlorobutane ([Gold Book Q04997, from Pure Appl. Chem. 1996, 68, 2193, doi:10.1351/pac199668122193](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/Q04997/plain)); a quasi-racemic compound is their crystalline 1:1 association ([Gold Book Q05001](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/Q05001/plain)); they are used in parallel kinetic resolution ([Enantiomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer)). Status: approximate by definition. Label: CONV.
- *A packing rule reopened:* quasiracemates were generally held to crystallize with components nearly inversion-related (32 coformer-free amino-acid examples known). Obs: of eight new sulfur-containing amino-acid hydrogen oxalate quasiracemates (single-crystal XRD, CSD 5.42 searches, Avnir continuous symmetry measures), six depart strongly from centrosymmetric alignment, with no clear correlation to side-chain length, composition or geometry. An earlier outlier: Pasteur's tartaramide/malamide quasiracemate, with pseudo-glide symmetry (listed DOI 10.1002/anie.200704007, via [Racemic mixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racemic_mixture)). Label: OBS + MOD (inversion index). Source: [Wells et al.](https://pubs.rsc.org/os/content/getauthorversionpdf/D1CE01214B).

**Diastereomers and naming conventions**
- *Diastereomers.*
  - Diastereomers are stereoisomers that are not mirror images ([Enantiomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer)), and they differ physically ([Cis–trans isomerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cis–trans_isomerism)):
    - Boiling point of 1,2-dichloroethene: cis 60.3 °C, trans 47.5 °C (bond dipoles add in cis and cancel in trans).
    - Melting point: maleic acid 130 °C versus fumaric acid 286 °C; oleic acid 13.4 °C versus elaidic acid 43 °C.
    - Biological activity: cisplatin is an anticancer drug and transplatin is not.
  - Label: OBS.
- *cis/trans versus E/Z.*
  - E/Z ranks substituents by CIP priority (Z for *zusammen*, E for *entgegen*) and can disagree with cis/trans: trans-2-chlorobut-2-ene, with its methyl groups trans, is (Z). Syn/anti is used for conformers that interconvert readily.
  - Status: conventional. Label: CONV. Source: [Cis–trans isomerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cis–trans_isomerism).
- *D/L, R/S and (+)/(−).*
  - These are three independent systems, based respectively on correlation with glyceraldehyde, CIP geometry and the sign of rotation ([Enantiomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer)).
  - (+)-Glyceraldehyde was assigned D arbitrarily before 1951. Bijvoet's 1951 anomalous-dispersion study of (+)-sodium rubidium tartrate (listed DOI 10.1038/168271a0) confirmed the assignment. Other methods include ORD, VCD, chiral NMR shift reagents and Coulomb explosion imaging (listed DOI 10.1126/science.1240362). Source: [Absolute configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_configuration).
  - Status: a convention later tied to observation (settled 1951). Label: CONV → OBS.
- *endo/exo and syn/anti:* endo puts a substituent nearest (syn) to the longest bridge and exo furthest (anti), as in norbornanes; the terms carry over to Diels–Alder transition states ([Endo-exo isomerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endo-exo_isomerism); listed DOI 10.1351/goldbook.E02094). Alder's endo rule (endo TS usually favoured in normal-demand reactions despite crowding) is strong for maleic anhydride, weak for acrylates, and overridden by bulk or reversibility (furan gives exo); its leading explanation, secondary orbital interactions, was questioned computationally in 2007 (listed DOI 10.1002/jcc.20532) ([Diels–Alder reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diels-Alder_reaction)). Status: descriptor conventional; rule approximate; explanation reopened (2007). Label: CONV + OBS + MOD.
- *α/β anomers:* relative descriptors relating the anomeric centre to the anomeric reference atom ([Gold Book A00003](https://dev.goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/A00003/plain)); anomers are epimers at the hemiacetal carbon ([Anomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomer)). Obs (polarimetry): α-D-glucopyranose +112.2°, β +18.7°, equilibrium +52.7° (about 36:64 α:β); mutarotation discovered by Dubrunfaut, 1844 ([Mutarotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutarotation)). Interconversion via the open-chain form [unverified]. Label: CONV + OBS.
- *P/M helicity.*
  - A right-handed helix is P (plus) and a left-handed one M (minus) ([Gold Book H02763](https://mail.goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/H02763/plain)); Δ/Λ are used equivalently. IUPAC does not count helicity as axial chirality ([Helical chirality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helical_chirality)).
  - B-DNA is right-handed. Z-DNA, which forms in methylated segments, is left-handed and has been seen in living organisms ([DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA)).
  - Label: CONV + OBS.
- *Meso forms:* two or more stereocentres yet achiral, via an internal mirror plane, an inversion centre or an S₄ axis (1,4-difluoro-2,5-dichlorocyclohexane has two meso isomers, neither with a mirror plane). Meso-tartaric acid stands beside the optically active (R,R)/(S,S) pair; like a racemate it shows no rotation, but it is one compound. Label: CONV + OBS. Source: [Meso compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meso_compound) (listed DOI 10.1351/goldbook.M03839).
- *Nitrogen inversion:* NH₃ inverts through a planar transition state (barrier about 24.2 kJ/mol; about 3×10¹⁰ s⁻¹ at room temperature; tunnelling adds about 80-fold), first detected by microwave spectroscopy in 1934 (listed DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.45.234). Chiral amines RR′R″N therefore racemize, while P-chiral phosphines (about 132 kJ/mol) hold configuration and Tröger's base locks its N stereocentres. Centre: the achiral planar TS between two mirror-image pyramids. Status: exact symmetric double well. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Nitrogen inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_inversion).

**Inversion centre, parity and selection rules**
- *g/u labels.* In centrosymmetric point groups every irreducible representation splits into g and u. Longuet-Higgins' molecular symmetry group adds feasible permutations and the parity operation E*. Label: MOD. Source: [Molecular symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_symmetry).
- *Laporte rule:* in centrosymmetric systems electric-dipole transitions connect only g with u ([Gold Book L03454](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/L03454)); relaxed by vibronic coupling, by loss of the inversion centre (tetrahedral complexes) with orbital mixing, and for spin-forbidden bands by spin–orbit coupling. Obs: octahedral d–d bands ε ≤ 100 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹; spin-forbidden bands about 1 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ or less ([LibreTexts: Selection rules](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Inorganic_Chemistry/Inorganic_Chemistry_(LibreTexts)/11%3A_Coordination_Chemistry_III_-_Electronic_Spectra/11.03%3A_Electronic_Spectra_of_Coordination_Compounds/11.3.01%3A_Selection_Rules)). Status: exact in strict symmetry, approximate in vibrating molecules. Label: MOD + OBS.
- *Mutual exclusion:* with a centre of symmetry no normal mode is both IR- and Raman-active (dipole u, polarizability g). In CO₂ the symmetric stretch is Raman-only and the antisymmetric stretch and bends IR-only; some modes are silent in both (ethylene, benzene, PtCl₄²⁻; listed DOI 10.1021/ed060p625); water (C₂ᵥ, no inversion centre) has all three modes active in both. Status: exact for centrosymmetric species. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Rule of mutual exclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_mutual_exclusion).
- *Chiral crystals:* enantiopure chiral substances (e.g. proteins) can adopt only the 65 of 230 space groups that contain no improper operations (mirrors, glides, inversion); racemates are not so limited. Label: MOD, borne out by solved structures. Source: [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography).

**Mirror-image biology (2016–2026)**, all from [Chiral life concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_life_concept)
- Molecular steps: mirror DNA polymerase (2016, listed DOI 10.1038/nchem.2517); mirror T7 transcription (2022, listed DOI 10.1126/science.abm0646).

- Label: expert and governance positions, not observings.

### Inferences
- The mirror plane is a centre of description rather than a place. Without a chiral probe, every measurement returns the same value for both members. That is why parity-violation searches are differential and why resolutions need chiral partners or chiral crystals.
- Naming pairs straddle the geometry in different ways:
  - D/L and R/S name the same mirror relation by different reference rules.
  - cis/trans and E/Z name the same diastereomers by different reference groups.
  - P/M names a screw sense, which can be enantiomeric (P- versus M-helicene) or diastereomeric (B- versus Z-DNA, both built from D-deoxyribose; the true mirror image would be L-DNA). This last point is my own inference.
- Nitrogen inversion gives a literal straddle: two mirror-image pyramids on either side of a planar centre, detected spectroscopically.

## 3. Pairing theorems and hole-equivalences

### Takeaway
The Coulson–Rushbrooke pairing theorem is chemistry's cleanest case of two levels standing as each other's far side about a centre. In an alternant hydrocarbon, every bonding π level below the nonbonding level α has an antibonding partner the same distance above it, with the same coefficients except for a sign change on one sublattice. The theorem is exact within Hückel/PPP-type models. It is borne out approximately by radical spectra, MCD and the interference seen in single-molecule conductance, and it fails with heteroatoms and non-alternant rings. Electron/hole and dⁿ/d¹⁰⁻ⁿ equivalences are model-level symmetries between filled and empty states, and the half-filled shell is their turning point.

### Cited Findings
- *Statement of the theorem:* in an alternant system the atoms split into starred and unstarred sets with no same-set bonds. Bonding and antibonding orbitals are equally spaced about the nonbonding level (the SOMO keeps the non-interacting energy); paired orbitals have equal coefficient magnitudes, the same sign on starred and opposite signs on unstarred atoms, and equal two-electron integrals and dipole elements. Also called pseudoparity, particle–hole symmetry or the Coulson–Rushbrooke–Longuet-Higgins theorem. Coulson and Rushbrooke (1940, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 36:193–200) proved it for closed shells under neglect of differential overlap with nearest-neighbour resonance; Longuet-Higgins, Pople, Dewar and co-workers extended it to odd alternant radicals. Label: MOD (exact inside the model). Source: [Green & Hele 2024, J. Chem. Phys. 160:164110, arXiv:2312.09013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09013).
- *Mathematical core.* A 1959 abstract (Ohio State Knowledge Bank; author not named on the page; Brandeis affiliation) locates the key feature in an operator that flips the sign of unstarred coordinates and anticommutes with the Hamiltonian, and uses it to interpret the spectra and bond orders of alternant hydrocarbons. Label: MOD. Source: [OSU KB](https://kb.osu.edu/items/0cbd354c-6426-5e56-923f-3b091da9fffa).
- *Selection rules from pairing (plus/minus states).* Source: [Green & Hele 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09013).
  - Model: plus and minus states do not couple through the Hamiltonian, before or after configuration interaction. Transition dipoles are non-zero only between states of opposite pseudoparity and equal multiplicity. The ground state behaves as a minus state, and the lowest doublet D₁ is usually a minus state and dark.
  - Obs (literature spectra): D₁ absorbs very weakly.
  - SIM: the higher-level GMC-QDPT method gives allyl D₁ a small oscillator strength (0.005), a slight breaking of pairing beyond PPP.
  - Excitation energies in eV (experiment / ExROPPP / GMC-QDPT): allyl 1²B₂ 3.07/3.05/3.45; benzyl 1²A₂ 2.76/2.89/3.05; trityl 1²E 2.41/2.74/2.63. Root-mean-square doublet errors are 0.33 eV for ExROPPP and 0.48 eV for GMC-QDPT.
  - Scope: the rules hold strictly only for pure hydrocarbon radicals and qualitatively for chlorinated TTM/PTM radicals. They do not apply to non-alternant systems, and the paper notes that emissive radicals are generally non-alternant derivatives.
  - Status: reported 2024, extending the 1950s results. Label: OBS + SIM.
- *Conductance consequence:* with π levels symmetric about the Fermi level, each occupied orbital at −ε has an unoccupied mirror at +ε differing by a sign on one subset, so contacts on two atoms of the same subset make each pair cancel (destructive quantum interference). Obs (as cited): meta-connected benzene conducts orders of magnitude less than ortho or para (Sautet & Joachim 1988; Patoux 1997; Mayor 2003). Limits: odd systems put a nonbonding orbital at the Fermi level; heteroatoms and non-alternant rings break the theorem; contacts on different subsets are not covered. Label: MOD + OBS. Source: [Zhao, Geskin & Stadler, arXiv:1612.02266 (dated Nov 2018)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.02266).
- *Methods that lose the pairing.* Standard all-valence-electron methods based on neglect of differential overlap fail to reproduce pairing properties, while experimental data (absorption, MCD) support its significance for phenanthrene, biphenylene and other alternants. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Spanget-Larsen 1986, Croat. Chem. Acta 59:711](https://hrcak.srce.hr/177298?lang=en).
- *Sublattice particle–hole symmetry:* HOMO−j and LUMO+j share coefficients up to a sign on unstarred atoms, so the SOMO has nodes there and radicals linked through unstarred atoms have vanishing ground-state interaction (Hückel level). The PPP Hamiltonian is invariant under a sublattice-dependent particle–hole transformation, which labels states ±. Proposed use: alternant diradicals as optically addressable qubits. Label: MOD/SIM. Source: [Poh et al. 2024, arXiv:2403.09102](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09102).
- *Bonding, antibonding and nonbonding.*
  - Bonding orbitals concentrate density between the nuclei, antibonding orbitals outside them, and nonbonding orbitals neither strengthen nor weaken the bond ([Molecular orbital theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital_theory)).
  - The bonding/antibonding pair is symmetric about α only when overlap is neglected [unverified]. Label: MOD.
- *HOMO/LUMO as the frontier pair:* analogous to the valence-band maximum and conduction-band minimum; radicals have a SOMO ([HOMO and LUMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOMO_and_LUMO); listed DOI 10.1351/goldbook.S05765). Frontier-orbital theory (Fukui et al. 1952, listed DOI 10.1063/1.1700523) rests on three generalizations (occupied orbitals repel; opposite charges attract; occupied–unoccupied, especially HOMO–LUMO, interactions attract), which map onto Klopman–Salem terms ([FMO theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_molecular_orbital_theory)). Label: MOD.
- *Thermal versus photochemical complementarity:* model: 4n-electron electrocyclic reactions are conrotatory thermally and disrotatory photochemically, 4n+2 the reverse; thermal [2+2] is forbidden, photochemical [2+2] often allowed, thermal [4+2] allowed; HOMO→LUMO promotion reverses the phase relations. Obs: thermolysis of trans-1,2,3,4-tetramethylcyclobutene gives one diene isomer; photo-closure of (E,E)-2,4-hexadiene gives only cis-3,4-dimethylcyclobutene; Havinga's 1961 tachysterol work showed the opposite courses. History: 1965 JACS papers (listed DOIs 10.1021/ja01080a054, 10.1021/ja01087a034); Nobel Prize 1981 (Hoffmann, Fukui); Corey's 2004 priority claim (listed DOI 10.1021/jo049925d) is generally not credited (historically notable). Status: exact for concerted paths under orbital-symmetry conservation. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Woodward–Hoffmann rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodward–Hoffmann_rules).
- *Electron and hole:* a hole is the absence of an electron relative to the ground state, treated as a quasiparticle of charge +e, so a nearly full band is described by its few holes; the usage extends to Auger/X-ray spectroscopy and computational chemistry. Model: Peierls (1929) showed theoretically that nearly full and nearly empty zones give opposite Hall voltages; the measured Hall sign is the observable [specific measurements unverified]. The positron analogy is limited (holes have negative inertial mass). Label: MOD + OBS. Source: [Electron hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_hole) (listed DOIs 10.1016/0025-5408(70)90105-4, 10.1021/ed044p391).
- *Partial support for dⁿ ↔ d¹⁰⁻ⁿ.*
  - Double groups treat ions with one electron or one vacancy in a d or f shell together (3d¹/3d⁹, 4f¹/4f¹³), and for half-integer J the characters change sign under a 2π rotation ([Double group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_group)).
  - The tetrahedral splitting pattern is the inverse of the octahedral one, with Δt ≈ (4/9)Δo, so tetrahedral complexes are usually high-spin ([Crystal field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_field_theory); [Ligand field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand_field_theory)).
  - Label: MOD.
- *High spin and low spin; the spectrochemical line.*
  - Low spin results when the pairing energy is below Δ, and high spin when it exceeds Δ ([Crystal field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_field_theory)).
  - The spectrochemical series runs from weak-field π-donors (I⁻) to strong-field π-acceptors (CN⁻, CO) ([Ligand field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand_field_theory)).
  - Centre: the crossover where Δ equals the pairing energy (an inference). Label: MOD + OBS (magnetic moments, spectra).
- *Hole formalism details, all [unverified]:*
  - pⁿ and p⁶⁻ⁿ give the same LS terms (for example p² and p⁴: ³P, ¹D, ¹S), as do dⁿ and d¹⁰⁻ⁿ.
  - Fine-structure order inverts beyond half filling (Hund's third rule; C has a ³P₀ ground level, O ³P₂).
  - Orgel and Tanabe–Sugano diagrams use these correspondences.
  - These are standard textbook statements that were not checked here.
- *The half-filled shell:* Mn (3d⁵) has a ⁶S ground term and Cr (3d⁵4s¹) a ⁷S term. Exceptions to maximum multiplicity are documented (5-dehydro-m-xylylene, listed DOI 10.1002/anie.200352990; FeMo-cofactor assignments, listed DOI 10.1039/C4SC00337C; a 2024 heterometallic Fe–S cluster, listed DOI 10.1021/jacs.3c12157). Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Hund's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hund's_rule).
- *Kramers doublets [unverified].* Systems with an odd number of electrons keep at least twofold degeneracy in any electric field (time-reversal symmetry), and only a magnetic field lifts it. The Kramers page was off the allowlist, so this was not checked.
- *Ortho and para spin isomers.*
  - H₂ ([Hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen)): ortho has parallel nuclear spins (triplet) and para antiparallel ones (singlet). At room temperature and above, hydrogen is about 75 % ortho and 25 % para. Ortho lies 1.455 kJ/mol higher, and the exothermic ortho-to-para conversion can boil off liquid H₂, so catalysts (ferric oxide, activated carbon) are used during liquefaction. The page's "several minutes" conversion time sits oddly with the need for catalysts [unverified].
  - Two spin-½ particles combine into a triplet and a singlet ([Singlet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singlet_state)).
  - Water, Obs: the two spin isomers were separated by electrostatic deflection (Küpper's "electric prism") and reacted with cold, trapped N₂H⁺ ions; para-H₂O reacted about 25 % faster (Kilaj et al., Nat. Commun. 2018, doi:10.1038/s41467-018-04483-3; [Unibas news](https://chemie.unibas.ch/en/news/details-524/water-is-not-the-same-as-water-1/); [CFEL](https://www.cfel.de/news_archive/2018/news_2018/water_is_not_simply_water/index_eng.html)).
  - Water, Model: the difference is attributed to rotational-state-dependent long-range attraction, supported by simulations.
  - Status: a single 2018 observation; whether it has been reproduced was not checked. Label: OBS + SIM.

### Inferences
- Coulson–Rushbrooke pairing, electron/hole symmetry and the hole formalism share one structure: an exchange between filled and empty states about a midpoint (α, the Fermi level, the half-filled shell). In each case exactness rests on a model idealization (nearest-neighbour Hückel, a rigid band, free-ion LS coupling). The observable trace is a symmetry or rule in spectra (dark D₁ states) or a sign (the Hall voltage).
- The plus/minus rules turn the pairing theorem into a test that can be observed: dark lowest doublets in alternant radicals, versus brighter states in non-alternant derivatives.

- Kramers' theorem, Tanabe–Sugano and Orgel diagrams, Hund's third rule and the term equivalences were not checked, because of allowlist and fetch limits.
- Later pairing-theorem literature (McLachlan, Koutecký, Gutman) and Hele's 2021 SPIE review were not fetched.

## 4. Nuclear pairs

### Takeaway
Mirror nuclei, with Z and N exchanged, are near-chemistry pairs about the N = Z line. Isospin symmetry makes them nearly identical: their ground-state binding energies differ by MeV, mostly through the Coulomb energy, and analogue excited states differ in excitation energy (mirror energy differences, MEDs) by tens to hundreds of keV. Work in 2025 both reinforced Coulomb dominance in the sd shell up to T = 5/2 and reported a ground-state difference in the A = 71 mirror pair.

### Cited Findings
- *Isospin.* Protons and neutrons are treated as two states of one nucleon, which predicts that mirror nuclei share their quantum properties, especially ground states. Label: MOD. Source: [IFIC/PCUV news, 20 June 2025](https://news.pcuv.es/en/home-pcuv-institutos-pcuv-ific-led-experiment-challenges-one-principles-nuclear-physics).
- *⁷¹Kr and ⁷¹Br (2025):* Obs: ⁷¹Kr (Z = 36, N = 35) and ⁷¹Br (Z = 35, N = 36), studied at RIKEN RIBF, have different ground states. Model: shell-model calculations support this; the lead author's claim of a first-ever case for such close mirror nuclei comes from a press release. Status: new in 2025; reproduction not checked. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: Algora et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 134:162502, doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.162502 (via [IFIC/PCUV news](https://news.pcuv.es/en/home-pcuv-institutos-pcuv-ific-led-experiment-challenges-one-principles-nuclear-physics)).
- *²³Si and ²³F (2025):* Obs: ²³Si mass excess 23362.9(5.8) keV from the LEBIT 9.4 T Penning trap at FRIB (TOF-ICR against ²³Na⁺), about 20 times more precise than the earlier CSRe value; ²³F from AME2020. Derived: binding-energy difference 23.984(31) MeV, 181(31) keV above the USDC shell model; ²¹Al–²¹O deviates by −204(101) keV; the Thomas–Ehrman shift is about −350 keV for ²⁷S and ³¹Ar; deviations reach about 330 keV for N = 11–15. Conclusion: Coulomb is the largest isospin-breaking effect up to T = 5/2. Label: OBS (mass) + MOD. Source: [Maier et al. 2025, Phys. Rev. C 112:014329, doi:10.1103/14s5-17gj](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10628218); [arXiv:2503.03034](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03034v2).
- *sd-shell isobaric multiplets (2025):* fits of the isobaric-multiplet-mass-equation b and c coefficients for |Tz| = 1/2 doublets (A = 17–39) and |Tz| = 1 triplets (A = 18–38); b(A = 17) = −3.543 MeV, c(A = 18) = 352 keV; rms deviations 77–155 keV across interactions. Measured MEDs: ¹⁹Ne–¹⁹F 11/2⁺ −208.4 keV; ²¹Na–²¹Ne 1/2⁺ −370.37 keV; ¹⁸Ne–¹⁸O 3⁺ −816.8 keV and second 0⁺ −57.46 keV. Large MEDs go with high 1s₁/₂ occupancy. Label: OBS (literature data) + SIM. Source: [Sarma, Srivastava, Suzuki & Shimizu, arXiv:2410.00765v2, Jan 2025](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2410.00765).
- *Centre and exchange.*
  - The members of a mirror pair sit at Tz = ±(N−Z)/2 about Tz = 0; the ²³Si–²³F pair has Tz = ∓5/2 ([Maier et al.](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10628218)).
  - Exch.: proton ↔ neutron. Status: approximate, because charge symmetry and charge independence are broken.
- *Even–odd pairing.* Source: [Nuclear shell model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_shell_model).
  - Nucleons in a level pair up with opposite angular momentum, so even–even nuclei have 0⁺ ground states. In odd-A nuclei the last unpaired nucleon sets the spin and parity (¹⁷O is 5/2⁺).
  - Label: OBS (ground-state spins) + MOD.
- *Magic numbers.* Source: [Nuclear shell model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_shell_model).
  - At 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82 and 126 nucleons, binding is noticeably stronger than for the next nucleon. A spin–orbit term is needed to explain this (Mayer; Jensen 1949; Nobel Prize 1963).
  - Particle–hole complements about closed shells, such as ¹⁵N/¹⁵O as holes against ¹⁷O/¹⁷F as particles, are [unverified].

### Inferences
- Mirror pairs offer a graded symmetry between two sides: the size of the MED measures how far each side departs from identity with the other. The Thomas–Ehrman shift and the 1s₁/₂ correlation show that departures concentrate where the extra proton is weakly bound in an s orbital.
- Chemistry meets mirror nuclei mainly through isotopes used as probes, so their bearing on chemical classification is indirect.

## 5. Dualities in description

### Takeaway
Chemistry's descriptive dualities are either exact mathematical transforms (Legendre, Fourier, reciprocal lattice) or alternative model bases (VB versus MO; localized versus canonical orbitals) that are equivalent in principle. They split the observables: one side is measured (intensities, interferograms, FIDs, ionization energies) and the other is reconstructed.

### Cited Findings
- *Thermodynamic conjugates and Legendre transforms.*
  - The four potentials have natural variables U(S,V), H(S,p), F(T,V) and G(T,p), plus the particle numbers Nᵢ. Each is obtained from U by Legendre transforms that swap one member of a conjugate pair: (T,S), (p,V) or (μ,N). No natural-variable set contains both members of a pair. D dimensions give 2^D potentials; Maxwell relations come from cross-derivatives, and the Gibbs–Duhem relation follows ([Thermodynamic potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_potentials)).
  - At constant T and p, equilibrium is a stationary point of G at which the stoichiometry-weighted chemical potentials balance ([Chemical equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_equilibrium)).
  - Status: exact (mathematics). Label: MOD + OBS (calorimetry, equations of state).
- *Direct and reciprocal lattices:* the reciprocal lattice is the set of wavevectors of plane waves sharing the lattice's periodicity (its Fourier transform); the reciprocal of the reciprocal is the direct lattice; FCC and BCC are mutually reciprocal; the Brillouin zone is the reciprocal Wigner–Seitz cell ([Reciprocal space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_space)). Obs: spot intensity ∝ |F|², so phases are lost (the phase problem) and recovered by direct methods (data better than 1.4 Å) or MAD/SAD anomalous phasing ([X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography); Bragg 1912, listed DOI 10.1038/090410b0). Label: OBS (intensities) → REC (electron density) + MOD.
- *Fourier pairs in spectroscopy.* Source: [Fourier-transform spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier-transform_spectroscopy).
  - An interferogram (intensity versus path difference) and a spectrum are related by a Fourier cosine transform. The multiplex (Fellgett) advantage is about √m when detector noise dominates.
  - In FT-NMR, a free induction decay recorded in time is transformed into a frequency spectrum.
  - Label: OBS (time-domain signal) → REC (spectrum).
- *Wave and particle:* model λ = h/p (de Broglie 1924). Obs: electron diffraction from nickel (Davisson–Germer 1927, listed DOI 10.1103/physrev.30.705) and through thin films (Thomson–Reid, listed DOI 10.1038/119890a0); C₆₀ interference (1999, λ ≈ 2.5 pm, listed DOI 10.1038/44348); molecules of 25,000 Da by 2019; single-electron build-up of fringes (listed DOI 10.1119/1.16104). Label: OBS (fringes; single detection events) + MOD (the duality reading; Born's probability). Source: [Matter wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter_wave).
- *Valence bond versus molecular orbital theory:* VB began with Heitler and London (1927), with Pauling's resonance (1928) and hybridization (1930); MO theory followed (Hund, Mulliken; Lennard-Jones 1929, listed DOI 10.1039/TF9292500668). Simple MO gives H₂ equal covalent and ionic weight and the wrong dissociation products, where simple VB dissociates correctly; adding ionic structures to VB, or configurations to MO, brings the two to mathematical equivalence ([Valence bond theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_bond_theory); [Molecular orbital theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_orbital_theory)). MO handles paramagnetism (O₂) and ionization energies more directly. Debates recur (spin-coupled benzene, 1986, listed DOI 10.1038/323699a0; Pauling's reply, listed DOI 10.1038/325396d0). Label: MOD.
- *Water's lone pairs:* two equivalent "rabbit-ear" hybrids (about sp^2.3) or two inequivalent pairs, σ (2a₁, about sp^0.7) and π (1b₁, pure p). Both give the same total density and are related by a unitary transformation; the equivalent pairs suit H-bond directions and the anomeric effect, while the canonical orbitals are needed for photoelectron energies. Debated in 2014 (listed DOI 10.1039/C4RP00057A) and 2015 (listed DOI 10.1039/C4RP00245H). Status: exact equivalence for the total wavefunction, split in observables. Label: OBS (PES) + MOD. Source: [Lone pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_pair).
- *Complementary chromatographies:* normal phase (polar stationary phase, e.g. silica with toluene) versus reversed phase (C8/C18 with a polar mobile phase; hydrophilic analytes elute first), with elution orders reversed; HILIC's order is generally opposite to reversed phase, and the two are orthogonal and can be coupled; cation versus anion exchangers; enantiomers need a chiral stationary or mobile phase. Obs: retention times. Model: partition coefficients. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Chromatography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatography).

### Inferences
- Each duality turns on a hinge:
  - For Legendre transforms, the product of a conjugate pair, which has the dimensions of energy.
  - For Fourier pairs, reciprocal variables (time and frequency, path difference and wavenumber, and position and momentum through λ = h/p).
  - For VB and MO, a unitary rotation among the occupied orbitals.
- Observation usually lands on one side (measured intensities, time-domain signals, canonical-orbital ionization energies), so the other side is a reconstruction. This is the line between observing and explaining that the project draws.

### Gaps
- Dual polyhedra in cluster chemistry (closo deltahedra and Wade's rules; the icosahedral B₁₂H₁₂²⁻ against dodecahedral C₂₀H₂₀ as a vertex/face dual pair) [unverified]; no session source.
- Bohr's complementarity and Fourier-limited linewidths were not fetched.

## 6. Symmetric spectra and centres

### Takeaway
Spectral symmetries about a centre are either exact or approximate:
- *Exact:* Stokes and anti-Stokes shifts are equal about the Rayleigh line; first-order doublet lines sit symmetrically about the shift; an isosbestic point appears whenever its conditions hold.
- *Approximate, with diagnostic breakdowns:* the absorption/fluorescence mirror rule; second-order NMR patterns; and the anti-Stokes/Stokes intensity asymmetry, which is itself used as a thermometer.

### Cited Findings
- *Absorption/fluorescence mirror rule:* model: absorption from v = 0 of S₀ maps S₁'s vibrational levels and emission from v = 0 of S₁ maps S₀'s, so similar spacings give mirror-image spectra. Listed exceptions: emission after excitation to S₂ or higher (quinine sulfate), solvent interaction, broad bands in condensed phases, and charge transfer that redistributes the excited state ([IIIT virtual lab](https://mfs-iiith.vlabs.ac.in/exp/stokes-shift/theory.html)); excited-state reactions are named as the main cause ([NPTEL](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/102103044/module2/lec6/4.html)). Obs + SIM: oligo(para-phenylene)s break the rule strongly, explained by an anharmonic double-well potential for ring librations (ab initio Franck–Condon treatment matching experiment; [Heimel et al. 2005](https://repository.gatech.edu/entities/publication/435d5e77-c5a2-400a-a0c7-dd6c4b8963b9)). Related: excitation spectra generally equal absorption spectra ([Fluorescence spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescence_spectroscopy)); resonance fluorescence, at the exciting wavelength without relaxation, is the coincident case ([Gold Book R05335](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/R05335/plain)); relaxed fluorescence is displaced by the Stokes shift ([Vibronic transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibronic_transition)), which comes from vibrational relaxation and solvent reorganization, while anti-Stokes emission can arise from phonons or triplet–triplet annihilation ([Stokes shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stokes_shift)). Centre: the 0–0 (origin) band. Status: approximate. Label: OBS + MOD (Franck–Condon).
- *Stokes and anti-Stokes lines about the Rayleigh line:* equal shifts on either side, because both lines involve the same pair of vibrational levels; Stokes lines are stronger (the lower level is more populated, Boltzmann), and the intensity ratio serves as a thermometer. Observed 1928 by Raman and Krishnan (mercury lamp, photographic plates) and independently by Landsberg and Mandelstam (21 February 1928); Smekal (1923, listed DOI 10.1007/BF01576902) is cited as a precursor [role unverified]. Status: exact in position, asymmetric in intensity. Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Raman scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raman_scattering).
- *Symmetric and antisymmetric modes.*
  - In the symmetric stretch of CO₂ both bonds change equally and the carbon stays still; in the antisymmetric stretch one bond lengthens as the other shortens. A molecule has 3N−6 modes (3N−5 if linear). IR intensity follows the change in dipole and Raman intensity the change in polarizability ([Molecular vibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_vibration)).
  - Mutual exclusion is covered in section 2. The mode frequencies are [unverified].
- *NMR doublet about the chemical shift:* each line sits J/2 from the centre (7 Hz coupling → ±3.5 Hz); the n+1 rule gives Pascal-triangle intensities; J is quoted in Hz because it is field-independent; the simple rules need shift differences much larger than J, otherwise extra peaks and distorted intensities appear ([Proton NMR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_NMR)). A single J's sign is invisible, and vicinal J follows dihedral angle (Karplus) ([J-coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-coupling)). Status: approximate (first-order limit). Label: OBS + MOD.
- *EPR hyperfine patterns.*
  - M equivalent nuclei of spin I give 2MI + 1 lines with Pascal intensities (CH₃ gives 1:3:3:1). The resonance condition is hν = gμ_B·B₀, and g departs from the free-electron value through spin–orbit coupling.
  - Label: OBS + MOD. Source: [Electron paramagnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_paramagnetic_resonance).
- *Isosbestic point:* the wavelength, wavenumber or frequency at which total absorbance does not change during a reaction or physical change; simplest case, two species with equal molar absorption coefficients at constant total concentration. It does not prove a two-species equilibrium or a quantitative conversion, only unchanged stoichiometry without significant side reactions over the period observed ([Gold Book I03310](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/I03310/plain)). Obs: oxy- and deoxyhaemoglobin are isosbestic at 586 nm and near 808 nm (total haemoglobin measurable at any saturation); spectrophotometer checks use potassium dichromate (339, 445 nm), bromothymol blue (325, 498 nm) and congo red (541 nm); isosbestic points supported a clean conversion in Eschenmoser's corrin work, whereas A→B→C sequences generally lack one ([Isosbestic point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosbestic_point)). Centre: the wavelength where the two contributions balance. Status: exact under its conditions, diagnostic only. Label: OBS + MOD.
- *Acid–base and neutral centres:* pH = pKa at half-neutralization ([Acid dissociation constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_dissociation_constant)); the neutral point is pH = ½pKw, with Kw = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ at 24.87 °C, so neutral pH is about 7.47 at 0 °C and about 6.13 at 100 °C (derived from the page's table); pKw(D₂O) = 14.869 at 25 °C; ultrapure water conducts 0.055 μS/cm ([Self-ionization of water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-ionization_of_water)). Glycine's isoelectric point is the pH of equal cationic and anionic forms (pI = (pKa₁ + pKa₂)/2 [unverified]). Label: CONV (neutrality) + OBS.
- *Ammonia inversion doublet.*
  - The superposition of the two pyramidal forms splits the energy levels, which is used in masers. It was first detected by microwave absorption in 1934 ([Nitrogen inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_inversion)).
  - The line frequency (about 23.8 GHz) is [unverified]. Label: OBS + MOD.

### Inferences
- The exact cases are those in which one quantity fixes both sides: a single level spacing, a single coupling constant or a conservation law. The approximate cases depend on two separate surfaces or populations being alike, such as similar S₀ and S₁ curvature, thermal populations, or coupling that is weak relative to the shift difference.
- The Rayleigh line and an isosbestic wavelength are measured features. The 0–0 band and the chemical shift are usually inferred from the symmetry of the pattern.

### Gaps
- Lakowicz's *Principles of Fluorescence Spectroscopy* was not consulted. A fuller catalogue of mirror-rule breakdowns (excimers, ESIPT) is [unverified].
- The symmetric parabolas of Marcus theory and cyclic-voltammetry peak pairs were not checked.

- Pairs made by definition are exact, but they say little until a scale (pKa, E°) is attached.
