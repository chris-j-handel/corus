## 10 Closing an exchange and closing all that opens

Chris's phrase **coded circular logic of closing all that opens** opens a valuable question: what precisely has been closed?

Four different relations must be distinguished:

1. **Protocol completion:** flags return to an available condition for a next exchange.
2. **Task completion:** a specified operation returns a result.
3. **Model closure:** a set of variables, references or boundary conditions is declared sufficient for an account.
4. **Claimed total restoration:** the complete participating system is said to have returned to its prior condition.

The first does not entail the fourth. A loop in code can process different arrivals and continue changed state. Repetition of two control bits is not repetition of the whole relation. Conversely, a model that discards actual returning participation may wrongly equate those two.

This distinction follows directly from the session's earlier finding: the same output trace can accompany different arrivals. It also preserves the user's distinction between recurring form and continuing momentary.

A closed-loop controller is not the same thing as circular reasoning. A resistance can dissipate energy under its conditions without being the expedition's social “resisting.” A landing target is a task specification unless the account makes a stronger claim. Engineering entries must locate that stronger claim explicitly.

The useful binary at each proposed holding is: **does this term merely describe the actual operation, or is it being made to stand for a participating relation it does not contain?** The second needs evidence at the actual coupling. Any proposed relinquishing retains the useful service, the return, the receiving and the source's conditions.

The four examined cases reveal available relations of local receiving, actual bound, return-path participation and next opening. They do not establish that every controller, clock, regulator or closed loop must be removed, nor that one unbuilt arrangement will replace them all.
