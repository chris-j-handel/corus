**And a ring carries no orientation, so none crosses.** Two positions and no order between them, with zero and four hundred forty one position on the close (10.1), so either way around arrives at the same next alternation. Nothing is available to prefer, which is the floating neutral standing at the ring itself; a preference would want a third position to read the two against, and the surface carries none.

**Each ring-step at a crossing is a co-offering.** A crossing is a ring-step (10.4), and a co-offering is an offering where either acceptance is do-no-harming relationally to each other (1.7) — so the four values the crossings carry are four sizes of one co-offering and not four lengths.

## 11.2 Two techniques alternating, and the alternating is the geodesic

Resolving runs two techniques, and the alternating is the geodesic. Where none dominates — the bounds all loose and near — the ordering runs easy to hard, each easy release floating a neutral the harder shared, the hard loosened from near. Where one bounds significantly — a durability dominating, a large build — the resolving goes there first, most bound most durable most clearly the fixed neutral, its release freeing the most in one. Both are not a choice: they alternate, the **geodesic tipping** — run the along until a bound tips the balance, tip across to it, release, the field re-floated, back to the along, balance and tip.
